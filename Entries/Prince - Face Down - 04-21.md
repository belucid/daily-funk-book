Let's stick with Prince again today. He's always a funky choice.

In 1995, Prince showed NPG keyboardist Morris Hayes a scathing review of their album, *The Gold Experience*. In an attempt to cheer Prince up, Hayes launched into a tirade with a few choice lines. Two days later, Prince handed Hayes a finished song built from his angry words. That song was today’s track, “Face Down.” Prince was always listening. He filtered his life through a musical lens, and in this case he turned a friend’s venting into new music.

This song is as close as Prince gets to rapping. He’s not quite rapping, but he’s not singing either. "Face Down" plays like a gangsta rap parody run through a funk filter. Prince is having fun, skewering his critics, and amusing his friend. And it still grooves hard.

Prince died of an overdose on April 21, 2016. In the song he jokes about being buried face down so critics can kiss his ass. In fact, there was no such burial orientation decision to make. He was cremated, and his ashes are kept at Paisley Park in a custom urn modeled after the building.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The song that made Prince clean up his act"**: The most compelling angle here is the irony that Prince's most profane, gleefully vulgar track became the catalyst for his spiritual transformation. ### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Face Down" originated from NPG keyboardist Morris Hayes' expletive-laden rant after reading a negative review of *The Gold Experience*. Hayes told Prince that critics could "kiss our ass" and be buried "facedown." Two days later, Prince returned with the finished track, built from Hayes' exact language. — Source: [Diffuser.fm](https://diffuser.fm/prince-face-down/)
- The track contains a strings sample from "Killin' at the Soda Shop," an unreleased Jill Jones track with orchestration by Clare Fischer. Fischer and Prince collaborated for nearly three decades on over 40 pieces, yet never met in person. Prince deliberately avoided seeing even a photograph of Fischer. — Source: [Wikipedia (Emancipation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_(Prince_album)), [The Iconic Prince](https://theiconicprince.wordpress.com/2017/03/16/clare-fischer-prince-a-winning-combination/)
- The track also uses a "Dead like Elvis" vocal sample from Ninety-9's "Stained Glass." — Source: [Diffuser.fm](https://diffuser.fm/prince-face-down/)
- A music video was filmed at Paisley Park Studios in early 1997. It is described as the most playful and joyous video from the *Emancipation* era, a "pure comedy" despite the angry lyrics. The commercial single was ultimately cancelled due to EMI Records restructuring. — Source: [Diffuser.fm](https://diffuser.fm/prince-face-down/), [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/832987-The-Artist-Formerly-Known-As-Prince-Face-Down)
- On the Jam of the Year Tour, Prince would introduce "Face Down" by telling the audience: "Take your children outside. We're going to get funky for about five minutes." He played his One Eye bass guitar during performances. — Source: [Goldies Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/prince-tours/jam-of-the-year-tour/)
- The song's profanity became the direct trigger for Prince's eventual conversion to the Jehovah's Witness faith. Larry Graham (formerly of Sly and the Family Stone, and inventor of slap bass) and his family would leave the stage every time Prince performed "Face Down." When Prince finally asked why, Graham asked if he'd ever considered doing a show without profanity. This conversation led to "The Cross" being retitled "The Christ" and "Sexy M.F." being permanently dropped from setlists. Morris Hayes described the shift as "a seismic shift." — Source: [Diffuser.fm](https://diffuser.fm/prince-face-down/)
- Promo-only CD singles were released in 1997 with three versions: Album Version, X-Tended Rap Money Mix, and Instrumental Money Mix. A promo 12-inch vinyl also exists. — Source: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/832987-The-Artist-Formerly-Known-As-Prince-Face-Down)

#### Album
- *Emancipation* was a triple album with each disc containing exactly 12 tracks running precisely 60 minutes, for a total of three hours. This mathematical precision was a deliberate artistic statement. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_(Prince_album))
- It was Prince's third album released in 1996 alone (alongside *Chaos and Disorder* and the *Girl 6* soundtrack), making it one of the most prolific release years of his career. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_(Prince_album))
- The album title refers to Prince's freedom from his Warner Bros. contract after 18 years. He described his new distributor EMI as "hired hands, like calling a florist to deliver flowers," and crucially retained ownership of his masters. — Source: [Ultimate Prince](https://ultimateprince.com/prince-emancipation-album/)
- Warner Bros. had warned Prince against recording cover versions. In defiance, *Emancipation* included four covers, and he released one ("Betcha By Golly Wow!") as the lead single. — Source: [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/tribute-celebrating-25-years-of-prince-emancipation)
- The album was certified 2x Platinum in the US (666,666 units due to triple-disc counting) and reached #1 in Switzerland. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_(Prince_album))
- Disc three, where "Face Down" lives, has been described as the "dance/funk extravaganza" of the set. — Source: [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/emancipation-prince/ip90p0q82osnc)
- The album's legacy was shadowed by tragedy: Prince's infant son Boy Gregory died shortly after birth from Pfeiffer syndrome, his marriage to Mayte Garcia dissolved, and EMI collapsed as a label. — Source: [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/tribute-celebrating-25-years-of-prince-emancipation)
- A 2019 reissue on Sony Legacy made the album available on vinyl for the first time as a 6-LP set. — Source: [Goldies Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/albums/emancipation/)

#### Artist
- Prince (born Prince Rogers Nelson, June 7, 1958, Minneapolis, MN; died April 21, 2016) released *Emancipation* under his unpronounceable Love Symbol name ("The Artist Formerly Known As Prince"). — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician))
- Morris Hayes, the man whose rant spawned "Face Down," was Prince's longest-serving band member (1992-2012). He came to Prince's attention as the house band keyboardist at Prince's nightclub Glam Slam in Minneapolis. He served as musical director for Prince's Super Bowl XLI halftime show. — Source: [Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/morris-kevin-hayes-7785/), [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/everything-was-cool-once-keyboardist-morris-hayes-figured-out-which-prince-had-arrived/480052643)
- Clare Fischer's orchestral arrangement collaboration with Prince lasted from 1985 until Fischer's death in 2012, spanning nearly 100 songs. Prince acknowledged Fischer in the *Parade* liner notes: "With special thanks 2 Clare Fischer 4 Making Brighter the Colors Black and White." — Source: [The Iconic Prince](https://theiconicprince.wordpress.com/2017/03/16/clare-fischer-prince-a-winning-combination/)
- Larry Graham, whose quiet objection to "Face Down" changed Prince's life, is credited as the inventor of slap bass technique and was the bassist in Sly and the Family Stone before forming Graham Central Station. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Graham)

### Genre Suggestions

The current genre is **Minneapolis Sound**. Here are considerations:

1. **Minneapolis Sound** (current, recommended): "Face Down" fits squarely here. It's a Paisley Park production from Prince's mid-90s period, featuring the lean synth-driven sound, drum machine textures, and the NPG band that define the Minneapolis Sound entries in this book. The genre definition specifically notes that entries "focus on Prince's funkier deep cuts, plus the Prince-adjacent musicians and groups that made the scene." This is exactly that: a funky deep cut. — Source: Project genre definition.

2. **Classic Funk**: "Face Down" has a raw, rhythm-section-driven funk energy that could fit classic funk, but the genre definition draws the classic era as roughly 1968-1977. A 1996 track falls well outside that window. Not recommended.

3. **Psychedelic Funk**: The satirical, surreal humor and trickster persona could loosely connect to the psychedelic funk tradition, but the sound itself is far more Minneapolis than Parliament-Funkadelic. Not a strong fit.
