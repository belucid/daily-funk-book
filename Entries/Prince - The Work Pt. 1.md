### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Prince's Valentine to James Brown"** — The track's horn-stab signature, the multi-part song title ("Pt. 1"), and the "are you willing to do the work?" hook are all direct JB signifiers. Prince was famously put on the JB Lyceum stage as a ten-year-old by his stepfather and never forgot it. This angle lets the author frame the song as a grown Prince finally getting to answer the Godfather directly, on his own terms.
  (Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-the-work-pt-1/)

- **"Funk as evangelism"** — The Rainbow Children is Prince's Jehovah's Witness album, and "The Work" is the thesis statement: are you willing to do the work? In funk's tradition of preacher-as-bandleader, Prince turns the JB call-and-response into a literal altar call. Lets the entry sit at the crossroads of James Brown and Jimmy Cliff's gospel edge without getting theological.
  (Source: https://albumism.com/features/prince-the-rainbow-children-turns-20-anniversary-retrospective)

- **"The Napster experiment"** — Prince was one of the first major artists to use Napster as a legitimate release platform, dropping a shorter mix of "The Work Pt. 1" as a free download on April 6, 2001, months before the album arrived. This is a rare story of an artist treating the file-sharing war as an opportunity rather than a threat.
  (Source: https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/albums/the-rainbow-children/, https://chronologicalprincerecordingsindex.blogspot.com/2020/11/chronological-prince-recordings.html)

- **"Prince after the symbol"** — The Rainbow Children is Prince's first album credited as "Prince" again after years as the unpronounceable symbol. "The Work" is his first funk statement under his own name in a new decade. The writing theme can frame it as a new-name-same-groove moment.
  (Source: https://albumism.com/features/prince-the-rainbow-children-turns-20-anniversary-retrospective)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- "Prince called this album his return to funk, and the horn stabs are direct descendants of James Brown's playbook. Count how many times Prince answers those horn hits with a guitar lick, and notice when he breaks the pattern."
- "Around the two-minute mark Prince lays down a guitar solo and a Rhodes solo that move together like two hands on the same piano. One writer claims he cut them simultaneously in a single take. Listen for where the solos agree and where they pull apart."
- "The title is a question: are you willing to do the work? The album is Prince's Jehovah's Witness statement of faith. Listen to how the groove itself keeps asking the question, beat after beat, instead of ever really answering it."
- "Put this next to any James Brown mid-period side (think 'Super Bad' or 'Get Up Offa That Thing') and note which elements Prince copies and which he sneaks in from his own Minneapolis toolkit. The horns are pure JB, but where is the Prince?"

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- Prince released an earlier, shorter version of "The Work Pt. 1" (about 3m 36s) as a free download via Napster on April 6, 2001, roughly seven months before the album. It was one of the earliest uses of Napster by a major artist as a legitimate distribution channel rather than a piracy target. — Source: https://chronologicalprincerecordingsindex.blogspot.com/2020/11/chronological-prince-recordings.html
- The album version (4m 28s) is extended and segues into the next track, suggesting Prince designed it as structural connective tissue in the album's suite rather than as a standalone single. — Source: https://chronologicalprincerecordingsindex.blogspot.com/2020/11/chronological-prince-recordings.html
- Per ultimateprince.com, Prince reportedly played guitar and piano solos simultaneously in a single take on the track, embodying the "work ethic" the song celebrates. Not independently verified in Wikipedia or AllMusic. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-the-work-pt-1/
- The horn arrangement is described as a "Valentine to James Brown," with a tightly coiled horn signature, guitar riffs, and the multi-part song title all echoing JB's catalog conventions. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-the-work-pt-1/
- Larry Graham (ex-Sly & The Family Stone, Graham Central Station) plays bass on the track. Graham was also Prince's personal link to the Jehovah's Witness faith and the spiritual mentor behind Prince's 2001 conversion. So the man who invented slap bass is the bass player on Prince's first song about his new religion. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children
- A live version from the 2002 One Nite Alone... Tour was released on the Live at the Aladdin Las Vegas DVD (filmed December 15, 2002), billed in a medley as "Money Don't Matter 2 Night / The Work." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_at_the_Aladdin_Las_Vegas
- No "Pt. 2" of "The Work" was ever released. The "Pt. 1" title is a conceptual hook rather than a reference to an existing second half; sources speculate it refers to Prince handing the baton to listeners. — Source: https://500princesongs.com/132-the-work-pt-1/

### Album

- The Rainbow Children is Prince's 24th studio album and his first album credited to "Prince" after years of releasing as the unpronounceable Love Symbol. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children
- The album was recorded at Paisley Park in Chanhassen, Minnesota, between September 5, 2000 and June 19, 2001. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children
- Released November 20, 2001 via NPG Records with distribution by Redline Entertainment. A fan-club MP3 preview was made available on October 16, 2001 via npgmusicclub.com. — Source: https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/albums/the-rainbow-children/
- The album is structured as a suite with narrated interludes (spoken-word by "Mr. Hayes and Femi Jiya," with Prince's voice pitched down to create the "Wise One" narrator) and explores Prince's conversion from Seventh-Day Adventist to Jehovah's Witness. — Source: https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/albums/the-rainbow-children/
- Prince told audiences on the supporting tour: "For those of you expecting to get your 'Purple Rain' on, you're in the wrong house." — Source: https://albumism.com/features/prince-the-rainbow-children-turns-20-anniversary-retrospective
- Critical reaction was sharply divided. Mojo praised it as "a sprawling, instrumentally dazzling work"; Spin called it "fat-free, dirt-free, melody-free jazz Olestra" and gave it 2/10. Metacritic score: 54/100. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children
- Peaked at #109 on the Billboard 200 but hit #4 on Independent Albums, reflecting its NPG Records distribution outside the major-label system. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children

### Artist

- Prince Rogers Nelson was born June 7, 1958 in Minneapolis, Minnesota, and died April 21, 2016 at Paisley Park in Chanhassen from an accidental fentanyl overdose. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician)
- Prince was first put on stage with James Brown at age 10 when his stepfather hoisted him up during a JB concert. This moment is cited repeatedly as the formative encounter that shaped his ideas about bandleading, stage discipline, and funk. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-the-work-pt-1/
- The Rainbow Children was Prince's first album released after his name had reverted from the Love Symbol back to "Prince" in 2000, following the expiration of his Warner Bros. contract. — Source: https://albumism.com/features/prince-the-rainbow-children-turns-20-anniversary-retrospective
- Bassist Larry Graham was Prince's spiritual mentor in his conversion to the Jehovah's Witness faith during the recording period, and his bass playing anchors both "The Work Pt. 1" and the closer "Last December." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rainbow_Children

### Genre Suggestions

- **Minneapolis Sound (current)** — Defensible on authorship alone (Prince recording at Paisley Park with Minneapolis-sound alumni), but note that this track intentionally departs from the 1980s synth-and-LinnDrum signature that typically defines the Minneapolis Sound per the book's own definition. Keeping this tag is fine as an author-is-the-scene call.
- **Classic Funk** — A strong case. The track is deliberately styled as a 1970s James Brown throwback: horn stabs, syncopated guitar, and a rhythm section pocket. It sits outside the book's 1968–1977 classic-funk window, so this would require an author's-call framing. Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-the-work-pt-1/
- **Jazz-Funk** — Also defensible. The album at large leans heavily on live jazz instrumentation (Najee on soprano sax and flute, Fender Rhodes, extended improvisational passages), and "The Work" sits inside that broader suite. Source: https://albumism.com/features/prince-the-rainbow-children-turns-20-anniversary-retrospective

Recommendation: keep Minneapolis Sound, or switch to Classic Funk if the author wants to foreground the JB tribute angle.
