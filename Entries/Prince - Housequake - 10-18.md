On  October 17, 1986 Prince announced he was parting ways with his long time band, The Revolution. The next day, he was back in the studio, making today's song. "Housequake" was inspired by the the Southern California earthquakes Prince experienced in 1986. 

The track is a one-man-band power move, a redeclaration that Prince did not need anybody. In it, he says: “Everybody shut up. Listen to the band.” For Prince, the band was just him.

"Housequake" was the first track recorded for the shelved *Camille* project, an album Prince planned to release under a feminine alter ego. He later folded material from *Camille* and two other album projects into the *Sign o' the Times* double album. The Camille voice was achieved by pitch-shifting and tape speed manipulation, and it would show up again in songs for years.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The day after the earthquake"** — Prince recorded "Housequake" on October 18, 1986, the day after the public announcement that he had disbanded The Revolution. The earthquake metaphor runs double: the literal LA quakes that terrified him in July 1986, and the seismic shift in his own creative life. The entry could frame the track as Prince turning fear into command, channeling the loss of control into a one-man funk demolition.

2. **"The birth of Camille"** — "Housequake" was the first track recorded for what became the shelved *Camille* album, a project Prince planned to release pseudonymously under a feminine alter ego. The Camille voice (achieved by pitch-shifting and tape speed manipulation) would become one of his most distinctive creative tools. The entry could explore how an entire alternate identity was born in a single session, with the name possibly drawn from a 19th-century French intersex person.

3. **"Prince plays everything"** — With The Revolution freshly dismissed, Prince played every instrument on "Housequake" except horns. It was a deliberate one-man-band power move, a declaration that he didn't need anyone. Susan Rogers noticed he was "off, he was different" that day. The entry could frame the track as the sound of a control freak reasserting total control.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Housequake" was recorded on October 18, 1986 at Sunset Sound in Hollywood, the day after the public announcement of The Revolution's disbanding. Prince played every instrument except horns. — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-housequake/
- The track was one of Prince's earliest experiments with the "Camille" voice, a pitch-shifted androgynous persona achieved via the Publison Infernal Machine (a pitch-altering device) and by speeding up the tape. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_(album)
- Engineer Susan Rogers noted Prince was "off, he was different" during the session, and that he spent an unusually long time on the track, "which usually meant...that the song was one he considered especially important." — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-housequake/
- The bassline was inspired by a real earthquake: on July 8 and July 13, 1986, earthquakes measuring 6.0 and 5.9 struck while Prince was working at Sunset Sound in LA. According to ex-girlfriend Susannah Melvoin, Prince "did not like this earthquake. It scared the [expletive] out of him." The track was his way of commanding the very force that had terrified him. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-sign-o-the-times-earthquake/
- Prince refused to release "Housequake" as a single despite its obvious hit potential, as part of his growing creative friction with Warner Bros. — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-housequake/
- A 12" maxi-single version called "7 Minutes Mo'Quake" (7:15) was included on the B-side of the "U Got the Look" 12-inch single. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Got_the_Look
- "Housequake" has been sampled in at least 27 tracks, including MC Hammer's "They Put Me in the Mix" (1988), Bomb the Bass's "Beat Dis" (1988), Cash Money & Marvelous's "Ugly People Be Quiet (Remix)" (1988), and LeVert's "Rope a Dope Style." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Prince/Housequake/
- "Housequake" itself contains a sample of James Brown's "Hot Pants (She Got to Use What She Got to Get What She Wants)." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Prince/Housequake/

#### Album
- *Sign o' the Times* was originally conceived as part of three separate abandoned projects: *Dream Factory* (a band album), *Camille* (a pseudonymous solo album), and *Crystal Ball* (a triple album). Warner Bros. forced Prince to cut *Crystal Ball* from three LPs to two, resulting in the final double album. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_o%27_the_Times
- The album peaked at #6 on the Billboard 200, #4 on the UK Albums Chart, and #1 in Switzerland. It was certified platinum by the RIAA within four months. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_o%27_the_Times
- *Sign o' the Times* is widely regarded as Prince's greatest album. It won the Pazz & Jop critics' poll in 1987, was nominated for the Grammy for Album of the Year, and was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2017. Rolling Stone ranked it #45 on its 2020 list of the 500 Greatest Albums. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_o%27_the_Times
- A rare pressing of the shelved *Camille* album (which included "Housequake") sold at auction for $58,787 in 2016. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_(album)
- The 2020 Super Deluxe Edition reissue included 63 previously unreleased tracks and a complete live concert from Utrecht, Netherlands (June 20, 1987). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_o%27_the_Times

#### Artist
- Prince broke up The Revolution over dinner with Lisa Coleman and Wendy Melvoin on October 7, 1986. He later called Bobby Z and fired him by phone. The public announcement came on October 17. The next day, he started recording "Housequake." — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-revolution-break-up/
- The Revolution's final show with Prince was September 9, 1986, in Japan, where Prince ended the Parade tour by smashing his instrument onstage. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-revolution-break-up/
- Prince had previously experimented with pitch-shifted vocals on the 1984 B-side "Erotic City," but the Camille persona as a distinct creative identity began with these late-October 1986 sessions. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_(album)
- The name "Camille" may have been inspired by Herculine Barbin, a 19th-century French intersex person who used the alias Camille and was the subject of the 1985 film *Mystere Alexina*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_(album)
- Prince informed Warner Bros. that his image would not appear on the Camille album cover and that he would not acknowledge the album as his own work. Warner Bros. was reportedly unwilling to release an album not attributed to Prince. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_(album)
- The *Sign o' the Times* concert film (1987) included "Housequake" performed as a medley with "Little Red Corvette." The original footage, shot in Rotterdam and Antwerp, was deemed unsatisfactory, so Prince reshot the performances at Paisley Park Studios. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_o%27_the_Times_(film)

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Minneapolis Sound** (current value, recommended) — "Housequake" is a textbook Minneapolis Sound track: lean, high-tempo, drum-machine-driven, synth-heavy funk with new wave energy. It was recorded by Prince as a solo artist during the exact period that defined the Minneapolis Sound's peak. The Camille vocal trick is quintessentially Prince's Minneapolis innovation. This is the strongest fit.
   - Source: Genre definition in On Genres & Regions.md; all sources confirm the track's sonic identity.

2. **Classic Funk** — The track draws heavily on James Brown (it samples "Hot Pants"), uses call-and-response party vocals, and has the horn stabs and percussive intensity of classic funk. However, the drum machine, pitch-shifted vocals, and 1987 release date place it outside the classic funk era (1968-1977 per the book's definition).
   - Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Prince/Housequake/ (James Brown sample); genre definitions.

3. **Psychedelic Funk** — The Camille alter ego, the surreal vocal manipulation, and the invented-persona framing have psychedelic funk overtones. However, "Housequake" lacks the cosmic mythology and extended storytelling of Parliament-Funkadelic, making this a weaker fit than Minneapolis Sound.
