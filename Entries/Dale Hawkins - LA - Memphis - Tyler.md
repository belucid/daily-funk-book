No `open:` items were present in the original entry. One open ambiguity surfaced during research:

- Praguefrank's Country Discography labels the title track as "instrumental," but multiple other reviews (Sessiondays, AVClub, Light in the Attic) describe Hawkins as the "buoyant master of ceremonies" delivering spoken/sung lyrics about each city ("Ain't no bum trip, man"). ### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A song that is its own travelogue":** The title track literally narrates the album's making — Hawkins flying between Ardent in Memphis, Joe Osborn's basement in L.A., and a Tyler, Texas studio to assemble what is now considered the foundational country-funk record. The song is the album's table of contents disguised as a horn-driven groove.
- **"The architect of swamp rock becomes the architect of country funk":** Hawkins invented the "Susie Q" sound in 1957, then a decade later helped invent (in retrospect) the genre that Light in the Attic would name 43 years later when "L.A. Memphis Tyler Texas" became the opening track of *Country Funk 1969-1975*. A single artist sits at the birth of two posthumously named genres.
- **"The producer who finally produced himself":** By 1969 Hawkins had spent most of the decade as an A&R man and producer (Jewel/Paula, ABNAK, RCA), turning other people into hits. This album is what happens when a producer with a Rolodex containing Ry Cooder, James Burton, Taj Mahal, Dan Penn, Spooner Oldham, and the Memphis Horns finally cuts his own record.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The whole song is a MC's introduction to a record you haven't heard yet — Hawkins names each city as the band shifts under him. Listen for the moment the groove changes feel as he announces each location, and see if you can hear three different studios in one two-minute-forty-four-second song.
- Hawkins shouts "Ain't no bum trip, man" — count how many times he addresses the listener directly. This is an album-opener written like a stage patter. Is the song trying to convince you of something?
- Ry Cooder, James Burton, Taj Mahal and the Memphis Horns are somewhere in this mix. Pick one instrument that wouldn't belong on a 1957 "Susie Q" session, and try to identify who's playing it.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The title track works as the album's mission statement: Hawkins literally introduces the three recording cities to the listener in MC style "à la Bob Wills," with the lyric "Ain't no bum trip, man." — Source: https://www.avclub.com/remembering-when-country-music-wasn-t-so-white-or-so-co-1798236313
- Co-written with Randy Fouts. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Funk_1969%E2%80%931975
- Recorded at Ardent Recording Studio in Memphis with Wayne Jackson (of the Memphis Horns) and Taj Mahal on percussion/harmonica, per the most detailed session log available. — Source: http://countrydiscoghraphy2.blogspot.com/2016/06/dale-hawkins.html
- Chosen by Light in the Attic as Track 1 of *Country Funk 1969-1975* (released July 24, 2012), the compilation that effectively named and defined the country-funk micro-genre. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Funk_1969%E2%80%931975
- Described by reviewers as having "a Levon Helm-esque drum beat" with Ry Cooder's slide guitar and James Burton's Telecaster work — Source: https://therisingstorm.net/dale-hawkins-l-a-memphis-and-tyler-texas/

#### Album

- The album was Hawkins' first studio LP in eleven years (since 1958's *Oh! Suzy-Q* on Chess) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins
- Recorded across three cities and three studios in 1969: Joe Osborn's basement in Los Angeles (with James Burton, Ry Cooder, Joe Osborn on 8-string bass, Paul Humphrey on drums); Ardent Studios in Memphis (with Wayne Jackson, Taj Mahal, Dan Penn, Spooner Oldham); and Steve Wright/Brians Recording Studios in Tyler, Texas (with the local garage band Mouse & The Traps — Bugs Henderson, Ronnie Weiss, David Stanley, Linda Waring). — Source: http://countrydiscoghraphy2.blogspot.com/2016/06/dale-hawkins.html
- Hawkins developed the concept while under contract to Bell Records as a producer and arranged the multi-city sessions himself. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins
- Cover photography by Joel Brodsky, the photographer best known for shooting The Doors' iconic "young lion" Jim Morrison portraits. — Source: https://sessiondays.com/2025/04/25/1969-dale-hawkins-l-a-memphis-tyler-texas/
- Sold poorly on release in 1969 but became a "lost classic" — Mojo magazine ranked the 2007 Rev-Ola reissue No. 8 in its Best Reissues of the year. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins ; https://therisingstorm.net/dale-hawkins-l-a-memphis-and-tyler-texas/
- Two 45s were lifted from the sessions: Bell 807 (July 1969, "Back Street" / "Little Bitty Rain Cloud") and Bell 827 (September 1969, "Heavy On My Mind" / "Joe") — the title track itself was never issued as an A-side single. — Source: http://countrydiscoghraphy2.blogspot.com/2016/06/dale-hawkins.html ; https://www.45cat.com/record/nc377477us

#### Artist

- Delmar Allen "Dale" Hawkins was born August 22, 1936 (some sources say 1938) on Goldmine Plantation, Richland Parish, Louisiana, and died February 13, 2010 in Little Rock, Arkansas of colon cancer. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/dale-hawkins-4310/
- His 1957 single "Susie Q" — co-written by Hawkins with James Burton playing the signature riff — reached #27 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #7 R&B, and was later named one of the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's "500 Songs That Shaped Rock and Roll." Creedence Clearwater Revival's 1968 cover made the song famous to a second generation. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Q_(song)
- His cousin Ronnie Hawkins led The Hawks, the band that later backed Bob Dylan and went on to become The Band. — Source: https://64parishes.org/entry/dale-hawkins
- Hawkins had a remarkable second career as a producer/A&R man before this album: he hosted *The Dale Hawkins Show* teen dance party on WCAU-TV Philadelphia (1960-61), produced The Uniques' "Not Too Long Ago" and the Five Americans' "Western Union," ran ABNAK Records in Tyler, Texas (1966), and became RCA's West Coast A&R head (1968-71). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/dale-hawkins-4310/
- Hawkins is in the Rockabilly Hall of Fame and the Louisiana Music Hall of Fame. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hawkins

### Region Suggestions

Two strong cases — leaving the field empty for the author to decide:

- **US - Texas:** Tyler appears in both the album title and the song title, Hawkins had been living in Tyler since 1966 and ran ABNAK Records there, and the Texas sessions provided the rhythm section (Mouse & The Traps) for several album tracks. The title track itself was tracked in Memphis per Praguefrank, but Tyler is the city Hawkins was living in when he conceived the project.
