### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Funk's verdict on Watergate"** — While the nation was gripped by the Senate hearings in the summer of 1973, James Brown's band answered the scandal with a shrug and a punchline: you can keep the politics, just give me my money. An angle on funk as working-class political commentary that is cynical, comic, and danceable all at once. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death_(album)
- **"A 14-second song that became a hip-hop institution"** — The same title shows up on the album as two throwaway interludes and one full jam, yet the riff went on to anchor "Self Destruction" and a dozen other rap records. A meditation on how a B-side scrap outlived its moment. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/36545-The-JBs-Doing-It-To-Death , https://www.whosampled.com/Fred-Wesley/You-Can-Have-Watergate-Just-Gimme-Some-Bucks-and-I'll-Be-Straight/
- **"Whose record is it?"** — Released on the album as "The J.B.'s" but on the single as "Fred Wesley & The J.B.'s," with James Brown as sole writer-producer-arranger. A look at authorship and credit inside the Brown machine. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4346692-Fred-Wesley-The-JBs-If-You-Dont-Get-It-The-First-Time-Back-Up-And-Try-It-Again-Party-You-Can-Have-Wa

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The album hides this song as two 14-second teases before it finally lets it stretch out for six and a half minutes. When you reach the full version, listen for what makes those earlier fragments feel like a promise being kept.
- The title is a one-line political joke, but the band never winks. Listen to how the groove stays dead serious while the words shrug off the biggest scandal in the country — where does the comedy actually live?
- This is James Brown's band without Brown out front. Listen for Fred Wesley's trombone and Maceo Parker's sax trading the lead, and ask what changes when the horns, not the Godfather's voice, carry the song.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The title is a topical jab at the Watergate scandal, which dominated US news in 1973; the band's stance is comic indifference — keep the scandal, just pay me. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death_(album)
- The track has been heavily sampled in hip-hop, including the landmark anti-violence posse cut "Self Destruction" by the Stop the Violence Movement (1989), plus records by Special Ed ("The Mission"), Just-Ice ("Flavor"), Zhigge, and DJ Magic Mike. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Fred-Wesley/You-Can-Have-Watergate-Just-Gimme-Some-Bucks-and-I'll-Be-Straight/
- James Brown is credited as sole writer, producer, and arranger on the track. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4936407-The-JBs-Doing-It-To-Death

#### Album

- *Doing It to Death* was recorded at two sessions: tracks 1 & 2 at International Studios in Augusta, Georgia on January 29, 1973, and the rest at A&R Studios in New York City on June 2, 1973. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death_(album)
- The album is built around the ten-minute title cut "Doing It to Death (Parts 1 & 2)," whose single reached #1 on the Billboard R&B chart and #22 on the Hot 100. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death
- It was released on People Records, the James Brown-owned imprint distributed through Polydor (catalog PE 5603). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4936407-The-JBs-Doing-It-To-Death

#### Artist

- The J.B.'s were James Brown's backing band, debuting on the 1970 single "Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine"; early line-ups included Bootsy and Catfish Collins, with Fred Wesley as trombonist and musical director. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown
- The *Doing It to Death* personnel included James Brown (vocals/organ), Fred Wesley (trombone), Maceo Parker (alto sax/flute), Jimmy Nolen (guitar), Fred Thomas (bass), and John "Jabo" Starks (drums). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death_(album)

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Southeast (current value):** Best fit and recommended to retain. James Brown was based in Augusta, Georgia; the J.B.'s were his Augusta/Southeast-rooted band, and two album tracks were cut at International Studios in Augusta, GA. The Southeast is the artist's home base even though the full version of this track was recorded at A&R Studios in New York City. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_It_to_Death_(album) , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown
