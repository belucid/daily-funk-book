### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"House pianist steps to the mic"**: Latimore was the in-house keyboard man at TK/Glades before he was a star. Frame the entry around the arc of the sideman who out-hustled the acts he backed, with this title track as a statement of forward motion from a guy who'd paid his dues on other people's records. ([Henry Stone Music](https://henrystonemusic.com/get-to-know-latimore-soul-and-blues-music-pioneer/))
- **"Miami Sound, minus the disco"**: 1976 is peak TK Records, the year KC and the Sunshine Band owned the charts. Use this track to show the other Miami that Henry Stone built: slower, Rhodes-soaked, blues-grounded funk that shared session players and a label roof with disco without chasing the four-on-the-floor. ([Wikipedia, TK Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TK_Records))
- **"Tennessee blues in a Miami suit"**: Latimore was a Tennessee kid raised on country and Baptist church choir, now singing over a polished Florida string section. The tension between his deep-soul delivery and the sophisticated Rhodes/strings arrangement is the whole record. ([Wikipedia, Latimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician)))

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Listen for the Fender Rhodes. Latimore is singing like a deep-soul bluesman but playing like a jazz cat. Where does the keyboard push against the vocal, and where does it get out of the way?
- The arrangement leans on strings and horns to sweeten the groove. Try to pinpoint the moment the horns answer a vocal line. Is this a funk record dressed up as soul, or a soul record built on a funk rhythm section?
- Latimore wrote the song himself. Listen to the lyric as forward-looking advice, then ask whether the groove sounds like someone moving on or someone still working something out.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- Title track of Latimore's fourth Glades LP, self-penned by Benjamin Latimore, produced by Steve Alaimo. Source: [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/509852-Latimore-Let-Me-Live-The-Life-I-Love-It-Aint-Where-You-Been)
- Not released as an A-side; it was issued as the B-side of the "Let Me Live the Life I Love" 7" on Glades in 1977. Source: [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/509852-Latimore-Let-Me-Live-The-Life-I-Love-It-Aint-Where-You-Been)
- The LP's charting singles were "Somethin' 'Bout 'Cha" (#7 R&B, #37 Pop) and a slow reworking of KC & the Sunshine Band's "I Get Lifted" (#30 R&B). The title track itself was not a charting single. Source: [BSN Pubs Glades discography](https://www.bsnpubs.com/florida/glades/glades.html)
- Sampled by Get Down Edits on "Where You've Been." Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/690681/Get-Down-Edits-Where-You've-Been-Latimore-It-Ain't-Where-You-Been/)

#### Album
- Catalog: Glades 7509, released 1976. Album peaked at #181 Billboard Pop / #47 Black Albums (January 1977). Source: [BSN Pubs Glades discography](https://www.bsnpubs.com/florida/glades/glades.html)
- Personnel included George "Chocolate" Perry (bass), Ron "Tubby" Ziegler (drums), Ron Peterson (guitar), Richie Puente (percussion), with horn and string arrangements by Mike Lewis, the core TK session crew. Sources: [Dusty Groove](https://www.dustygroove.com/item/18001/Latimore:It-Ain-t-Where-You-Been), [Funk My Soul](https://www.funkmysoul.gr/albums-no-review/latimore-1976-it-aint-where-you-been/)
- The album's slow version of "I Get Lifted" is a blues-soul reinterpretation of the same tune KC & the Sunshine Band had cut as a disco hit on the same TK family of labels. Source: [Dusty Groove](https://www.dustygroove.com/item/18001/Latimore:It-Ain-t-Where-You-Been)
- A deep cut from the record, "Let Me Go," was later sampled by Jay-Z on "Intro: A Million and One Questions / Rhyme No More" (In My Lifetime, Vol. 1, 1997). Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/3911/Jay-Z-Intro:-A-Million-and-One-QuestionsRhyme-No-More-Latimore-Let-Me-Go/)

#### Artist
- Born Benjamin Latimore, September 7, 1939, in Charleston, Tennessee. Raised on country radio, Baptist church choir, and blues. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Got his Miami start as pianist at the Knight Beat in the Sir John Hotel in Overtown before Steve Alaimo brought him to Henry Stone. Sources: [Standard Hotels, Fertile Soul](https://www.standardhotels.com/culture/florida-miami-soul-history-overtown-johnnys-records), [Henry Stone Music](https://henrystonemusic.com/get-to-know-latimore-soul-and-blues-music-pioneer/)
- Doubled as TK Records' house pianist while cutting his own sides for Glades, so he's on records he didn't sing on. Source: [Henry Stone Music](https://henrystonemusic.com/get-to-know-latimore-soul-and-blues-music-pioneer/)
- Biggest hit: "Let's Straighten It Out" (1974), #1 R&B, #31 Hot 100. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 2017. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))

### Genre Suggestions

Genre field is currently empty. Suggestions, in order of fit:

- **Classic Funk**: The rhythm section is straight mid-70s funk: tight drums, locked bass, syncopated guitar chops, horn stabs. Production is the TK Miami house sound, squarely in the classic funk tradition.
- **Jazz-Funk**: Latimore's Fender Rhodes work is "jazzy piano lines" over the groove, and contemporaneous reviewers flagged him as a "sophisticated cat" instrumentally, pointing toward jazz-funk's Rhodes-forward, harmonically richer territory. ([Dusty Groove](https://www.dustygroove.com/item/18001/Latimore:It-Ain-t-Where-You-Been))
- **Deep Funk**: Weaker case. The record is polished with strings and horns rather than raw or regional, so deep funk is probably not the best fit.

Recommendation: **Classic Funk** as primary, with **Jazz-Funk** as the alternative if the Rhodes foregrounding becomes the theme.
