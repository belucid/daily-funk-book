### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **Retro funk winks and nods, "tobacco road" (existing field):** Strong and on point. The album title deliberately invokes Piedmont/Carolina tobacco country, and the whole project is a modern act of homage to 1970s deep/garage funk. This track's "Part 1 & 2" title is itself a retro wink at the old 45-single convention of splitting a long jam across two sides.
- **"Straight to tape" — analog authenticity as a statement:** For the first time in his career Rashad recorded live, straight to tape, with an all-star retro-funk band. The angle: what is gained (and lost) when a modern artist forces himself into the constraints of the medium that made the music he loves.
- **A Raleigh punk kid returns to funk:** Rashad grew up in Raleigh's punk and metal scene before circling back to the funk his R&B-DJ father raised him on. The track's manic energy is arguably where those two worlds meet — punk aggression inside a funk pocket ("garage funk," his own term).

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The title says "Part 1 & 2." Listen for the seam — the moment the track shifts gears from one part into the next. Can you hear where a vintage 45 would have flipped from the A-side to the B-side?
- This was cut live, straight to tape, in a single room. Listen for the small human imperfections a click-track session would have scrubbed out. What tells you no one fixed this in the box afterward?
- Rashad calls his sound "garage funk." Pick the one instrument that carries the "garage" grit here rather than the polish. Is it the guitar, the drums, or his vocal?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The track is titled "Part 1 & 2," a deliberate nod to the classic funk convention (James Brown, etc.) of splitting one long groove across the two sides of a 7" single; here it runs a continuous 6:10. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/3724125-Boulevards-Carolina-Funk-Barn-Burner-On-Tobacco-Road
- It was issued as an advance single (June 6, 2024) ahead of the June 28, 2024 album, pitched by press as a "hip-shaking" attempt to bottle the energy of a live Boulevards show. — Source: https://www.gratefulweb.com/articles/boulevards-shares-new-single-do-it-like-a-maniac/

#### Album

- For the first time in his career, Rashad recorded live and straight to tape, tracking with producer Sergio Rios (of Orgone) at Killion Sound in Los Angeles. — Source: https://waltermagazine.com/art-and-culture/in-a-groove-a-new-album-from-boulevards/
- The band is a who's-who of the modern retro-funk revival: Dan Hastie (keys), Dale Jennings (bass), Sam Halterman (drums), with guitar from Sergio Rios and JJ Whitefield, plus background vocals from Kendra Morris. Mixed by Adrian Quesada (Black Pumas). — Source: https://boulevardscowboy.bandcamp.com/album/carolina-funk-barn-burner-on-tobacco-road
- The album title frames the record around North Carolina tobacco country ("Barn Burner on Tobacco Road"), positioning it as a specifically Carolina take on funk. — Source: https://waltermagazine.com/art-and-culture/in-a-groove-a-new-album-from-boulevards/

#### Artist

- Boulevards is the solo project of Jamil Rashad, a lifelong Raleigh, NC native; his father was an R&B radio DJ who exposed him early to jazz, blues and R&B. — Source: https://waltermagazine.com/art-and-culture/boulevards/
- Before funk, Rashad was immersed in Raleigh's punk and metal scene as a teen — an influence he credits for the tightness and power in his songwriting. — Source: https://waltermagazine.com/art-and-culture/boulevards/
- He was signed to Brooklyn's Captured Tracks and cites a 2015 Hopscotch Festival set at CAM Raleigh (~300 people) as the launch of his local fan base. — Source: https://waltermagazine.com/art-and-culture/boulevards/

### Genre Suggestions

- **Nu-Funk (existing field):** Best fit and correct. Per the book's definition, Nu-Funk is a mid-1990s-onward retro funk revival of bands making new records "in the spirit" of classic funk's 45-era feel — exactly what this 2024, straight-to-tape, deep-funk homage is, made by a contemporary US artist within that small international scene. Keep it.
- **Deep Funk (secondary):** Sonically the album chases the deep/garage-funk aesthetic of obscure regional 45s. But per the book's definition Deep Funk is a *commercial fate* (delayed rediscovery of overlooked originals), not a sound — so it does not technically apply to a brand-new release. Mentioned only to flag the aesthetic lineage.
- **Funk Rock (distant third):** Rashad's punk/metal roots and the track's aggressive energy give it a rock edge, but the core is funk revival, not rock fusion. Nu-Funk remains the clear choice.
