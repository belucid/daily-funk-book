Discogs credits the original 45 only to Lee Fields (Producer, Written-By); no member names are listed for the backing band on any source I found. The band appears to have been a studio/road backing unit for Fields in the mid-1970s and is not documented as an independent act.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- "The B-side that outran the A-side": "The Bull Is Coming" is the instrumental flip of "Funky Screw," yet it became the more celebrated, sample/DJ-coveted half. An angle on how crate-diggers, not the original market, decide what a record is "about."
- "Little JB cuts a private press": Fields modeling himself on James Brown ("Little JB") while issuing a self-controlled regional 45 on his own Angle 3 imprint — the DIY economics of 1970s funk before the soul revival rescued him decades later.
- "Rescued by reissue": A 1974 obscurity pressed in tiny numbers, then re-pressed by Germany's Tramp Records in 2013 in a run of 500 — the literal mechanism of "deep funk" recognition in action.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is an instrumental. Listen for what stands in for Fields's voice — which instrument carries the "lead" line where a vocal would normally sit, and when does the title ("the bull is coming") feel illustrated by the arrangement?
- Compare the groove here to a James Brown band instrumental (e.g., a JB's track). Fields was nicknamed "Little JB" — what in the rhythm section and horn stabs earns that comparison, and what doesn't?
- At roughly 3 and a half minutes the track stays tight and repetitive. Track how little changes across the runtime and ask why DJs and samplers prized exactly that loopable steadiness.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "The Bull Is Coming" is the instrumental B-side of Fields's "Funky Screw" 45; the backing on both sides was by The Devil's Personal Band — Source: https://soundsoftheuniverse.com/sjr/product/lee-fields-funky-screw
- Original duration is 3:33, and Lee Fields is credited as both writer and producer of the single — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/550809-Lee-Fields-Funky-Screw
- The single became one of Fields's most sought-after records among funk collectors, prompting a 2013 reissue limited to 500 copies worldwide on Tramp Records (TR151) — Source: https://www.redeyerecords.co.uk/vinyl/43630-tr151-lee-fields-funky-screw

#### Album

- The track was not released on an album in 1974; it exists as the B-side of the "Funky Screw" 7" single on Angle 3 (cat# 1004) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/564947-Lee-Fields-Funky-Screw
- It later appears on digital compilations/deluxe collections of Fields's early work (e.g., a "Let's Talk It Over (Deluxe Edition)" digital listing includes "The Bull Is Coming") — Source: https://www.amazon.com/The-Bull-Is-Coming/dp/B00AT0OEXU

#### Artist

- Lee Fields was born Elmer Lee Fields on April 26, 1950, in Wilson, North Carolina — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Fields
- Nicknamed "Little JB" for his James Brown–inspired style and moves; his debut single was "Bewildered" b/w "Tell Her I Love Her" on Bedford Records in 1969 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Fields
- Angle 3 was effectively Fields's own imprint; his debut LP "Let's Talk It Over" appeared on Angle 3 in 1979. His career was revived from the late 1990s via Desco Records (Gabriel Roth / Phillip Lehman, later of Daptone) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Fields

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk (current value — keep):** This is the textbook case. A 1974 regional private-press 45 that sold poorly, then was rediscovered by crate-diggers and reissued (Tramp, 500 copies). The book defines deep funk as "a commercial fate... overlooked on release and only found their audience later." Source: Matter/On Subgenres.md ; reissue scarcity: https://www.redeyerecords.co.uk/vinyl/43630-tr151-lee-fields-funky-screw
- **Classic Funk (secondary):** Sonically it sits squarely in the 1969–1977 classic-funk window as a James Brown–derived instrumental groove; but the book reserves Classic Funk for major-label / well-distributed commercial-pipeline records, which this private press is not — so Deep Funk is the better fit.
- **Nu-Funk (consider only for the reissue framing):** Not applicable to the 1974 recording itself, which predates the mid-1990s revival scene; mentioned only because Tramp Records is a hub of that scene. Recommend against.

Recommendation: keep **Deep Funk**.
