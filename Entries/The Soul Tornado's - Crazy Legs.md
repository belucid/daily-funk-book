### Open Research Questions

Two discrepancies surfaced during research that the author should be aware of:
- **Recording location.** The Jazzman reissue bio places the group and their "local producer" in Akron, Ohio. A separate search summary (NTS/RYM-sourced) states the band "recorded a number of songs in July 1969 in Detroit, MI." I could not get a primary source to confirm Detroit; the Akron origin is solid, the Detroit recording claim is unverified. Treat "recorded in Detroit" as unconfirmed.
- **Release year.** Discogs lists the Westwood 45 as 1970. The all-night recording session is consistently dated to 1969 (teenage years). Likely recorded 1969, pressed/released 1970. I set `year` to 1970 (release) per Discogs.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"One all-night session."** The whole legend of this band fits in a sentence: teenage brothers cut enough music for three 45s in a single overnight session, then largely vanished. A tight hook about how much funk can come from one sleepless night.
- **"The misspelling that became a brand."** The band is the Soul Toronados (after the Oldsmobile Toronado), but their records went out under a scatter of spellings: Tornado's, Tornadoes, Toronadoes, Toranadoes. This very 45 reads "The Soul Tornado's." A fun angle on how loose small-label pressings were with their own artists' names.
- **"Schooled by the masters."** The Heller home in Akron was a crash pad and soul-food stop for touring organ giants like Jimmy Smith and Brother Jack McDuff. The screaming Hammond on "Crazy Legs" is the sound of kids who learned at the feet of the greats.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Lock onto Bobby Heller's Hammond: it sits pitched high and almost screaming over the groove. Decide whether the organ or his brother Charles's drums is really driving this track.
- These were teenagers cutting a record in one overnight session. Listen for the looseness, the raw room sound, and ask what that rough-edged energy gives you that a polished studio band might lose.
- This is a Hammond-funk 45, not a James Brown horn machine. Listen to how the organ takes the role horns would play on a bigger record, and judge whether it carries the weight.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Crazy Legs" b/w "Bobby's Mood," both sides clocking 2:56, was issued on Westwood Record Co. (catalog B-1017), pressed by the Columbus, Ohio plant Musicol and published by Ermal Publishing (BMI). Writing credits go to Bobby Heller and Cy Merriman. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1052731-The-Soul-Tornados-Crazy-Legs
- It became a prized deep-funk rarity: original copies have changed hands roughly between $33 and $132 (median around $65), and DJ Keb Darge featured the group on his "Beams Presents The Keb Darge Experience" rare-funk comps. — Sources: https://www.popsike.com/SOUL-TORNADOS-Crazy-Legs-RARE-private-funk-45-Tornados/230627885075.html , https://www.discogs.com/release/949955

#### Album
- No original album exists. "Crazy Legs" was one of three 45s the group cut; all of their output was gathered for the first time on Jazzman's 2005 reissue "The Complete Recordings," presented as three 7" singles in a sleeve. — Source: https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings

#### Artist
- The band was built around Akron, Ohio twin brothers Bobby Heller (Hammond organ) and Charles Heller (drums), with James "Boots" Smith on guitar and Bruce Martin on bass. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/301037-Soul-Toronados , https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings
- The Heller family home in Akron was a regular stop for touring organ and jazz greats including Jimmy Smith and Brother Jack McDuff, who would rest, eat home-cooked soul food, and informally mentor the kids. — Source: https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings
- As teenagers in 1969 they signed with a local producer and recorded enough material for three 45s in one all-night session. The records went out under several spellings of the band name across the Burt, Magic City, and Westwood labels. — Source: https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings
- The name is a nod to the Oldsmobile Toronado; the "Soul Tornado's" spelling on this Westwood 45 is one of several variant spellings the group's records carried. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/301037-Soul-Toronados

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk (strongest).** This is a textbook deep-funk story: an overlooked private/regional 45 that found its audience decades later through crate-digging DJs (Keb Darge, Jazzman) and rare-groove comps. The book's own definition centers deep funk on "delayed recognition," which fits exactly. — Sources: project `Matter/On Subgenres.md`; https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings
- **Soul Jazz (secondary).** The track is organ-led Hammond funk played by kids mentored by Jimmy Smith and Jack McDuff, the organ-combo lineage at the heart of soul jazz. It leans rawer and more groove-locked than club soul jazz, but the instrumentation and pedigree are right. — Source: https://jazzmanalbums.bandcamp.com/album/the-complete-recordings
- **Classic Funk (weak).** It carries classic-era funk vocabulary but fails the book's classic-funk test of major-label pipeline, distribution, and chart presence. Mentioned only to be set aside.

Recommendation: Deep Funk.
