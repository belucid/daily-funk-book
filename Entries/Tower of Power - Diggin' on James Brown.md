By the time they recorded today’s "Diggin’ on James Brown" in 1995, Tower of Power had survived a revolving door of lead singers, outlived funk’s commercial decline, and carried the dark footnote that one of their former frontmen, Rick Stevens, was convicted in a 1976 triple murder case. *Souled Out* featured yet another lineup, but even after all the trials, literal and figurative, writing a funky love letter to James Brown was a worthy pursuit.

Singer Brent Carter handled lead vocals elsewhere on the album, but Emilio Castillo, the band’s co-founder, stepped out from behind the saxophone to deliver this tribute himself. Castillo explained that James Brown is the godfather who made a horn-driven funk band like Tower of Power possible.

The admiration was reportedly mutual. Castillo said James Brown told him in 1994 that he and his band regularly listened to Tower of Power albums.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"When the student tips his hat to the teacher"** — Tower of Power was the quintessential Oakland horn-funk band, and James Brown was the godfather who made the language possible. But this isn't just a one-way tribute: James Brown personally told Emilio Castillo in 1994 that he and his band regularly listened to Tower of Power albums. The track becomes a story of mutual respect between generations.

2. **"Emilio steps to the mic"** — On an album where Brent Carter handles lead vocals across every other track, Castillo himself takes the mic for the James Brown tribute. The bandleader and co-founder, who usually stays behind his saxophone, steps forward for this one song. That personal gesture says something about what Brown meant to him.

3. **"A funk band refuses to die"** — By 1995, Tower of Power had been through more than a dozen lead singers, a former vocalist convicted of triple murder, and the commercial decline of funk. Souled Out was their first album in a new decade with yet another new lineup. Writing a love letter to James Brown at that point is a declaration that the groove is still worth chasing.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Diggin' on James Brown" is one of only a handful of tracks on Souled Out where Emilio Castillo sings lead instead of the album's lead vocalist Brent Carter. Castillo, as co-founder and bandleader, rarely stepped to the mic, making this a personal statement. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_Out_(Tower_of_Power_album)
- The song was written by Emilio Castillo, Stephen "Doc" Kupka, and Ken Kessie, who was an engineer and producer at Can-Am Studios in Tarzana, California. Kessie died in August 2012. — Source: https://secondhandsongs.com/work/228116/all
- In live performance, Tower of Power expanded the song into a full James Brown medley, weaving in "It's a New Day," "Mother Popcorn," "There It Is," and "I Got the Feelin'" before reprising the original. This version was captured on the 2021 release "50 Years of Funk & Soul: Live at the Fox Theater." — Source: https://open.spotify.com/track/6D9pwKYEWRbNYb4CWxOmAv
- The melody of "Diggin' on James Brown" was parodied in the 2002 Baby Einstein video "Baby Newton: All About Shapes" as a children's song called "I Know My Shapes," written and performed by Jack Moss. The tune was later replaced with an original composition in 2004 releases, likely to avoid copyright issues. — Source: https://the-true-baby-einstein.fandom.com/wiki/I_Know_My_Shapes
- Hal Leonard published an official jazz combo transcription of the track, with drum parts transcribed by David Garibaldi himself. — Source: https://www.halleonard.com/product/2500859/tower-of-power-diggin-on-james-brown

#### Album
- Souled Out was released August 15, 1995 on Epic Records. It marked the debut of both lead vocalist Brent Carter and drummer Herman Matthews. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_Out_(Tower_of_Power_album)
- Jeff Lorber co-produced the album with Emilio Castillo, and also played Clavinet on two tracks ("Taxed to the Max" and "Do You Wanna"). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_Out_(Tower_of_Power_album)
- Recording took place across four studios: The Complex and Stagg Street Studio in Los Angeles, JHL Sound in Pacific Palisades, and Wings West Recording in Connecticut. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_Out_(Tower_of_Power_album)
- Herman Matthews, the album's drummer, is a distant cousin of former Tower of Power vocalist Rick Stevens. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_Out_(Tower_of_Power_album)

#### Artist
- Tower of Power was originally called "The Motowns." They changed their name to Tower of Power specifically to get booked at Bill Graham's Fillmore Auditorium in San Francisco. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- Emilio Castillo was born in Detroit and moved to Fremont, California at age 11. His father caught him and his brother trying to steal a T-shirt when he was 14 and told them to pick something to keep them off the street. Both chose music. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Castillo
- James Brown himself told Castillo in 1994 that he and his band regularly listened to Tower of Power albums, offering front-row seats to a sold-out show. This encounter happened roughly a year before "Diggin' on James Brown" was released. — Source: https://jesusrockstheworld.wordpress.com/2020/04/06/interview-with-emilio-castillo-tower-of-power/
- Bassist Rocco Prestia cited James Jamerson and the musicians from James Brown's ensembles (particularly Bootsy Collins) as his primary influences. His fingerstyle technique, built on ghost notes and percussive muting, is considered one of the most influential bass approaches in funk. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Prestia
- Prestia had a liver transplant (2002/2014, sources differ on timing), open heart surgery (2006), and a kidney transplant. He remained on the Tower of Power payroll after his health forced him to stop touring in 2018, and he died on September 29, 2020, at age 69. — Source: https://deadline.com/2020/10/francis-rocco-prestia-dies-bassist-for-tower-of-power-was-69-obituary-1234590046/
- Former vocalist Rick Stevens, who sang "You're Still a Young Man," was convicted of murder in 1976 for a triple shooting connected to drug deals. He served 36 years in prison, was paroled in 2012, reunited briefly with the band in 2013, and died of cancer at 77. — Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/after-36-years-prison-tower-power-s-rick-stevens-returns-n573001
- The Tower of Power horn section became one of the most sought-after session units in popular music, recording with artists including Otis Redding, Elton John, Aerosmith, Bonnie Raitt, Eric Clapton, and Aaron Neville. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- On January 12, 2017, drummer David Garibaldi and bassist Marc Van Wageningen were struck by an Amtrak train while crossing Embarcadero Street in Oakland before a performance. Both survived and fully recovered. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- Brent Carter, the lead vocalist on Souled Out, later also sang lead for the Average White Band and performed the Maurice White vocal parts in Maurice Hines' Broadway musical "Hot Feet." — Source: https://www.thesoundcafe.com/post/brent-carter-friends-have-work-to-do

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** — Tower of Power is one of the defining horn-driven funk bands of the 1970s, and while this track is from 1995, it is stylistically rooted in the classic funk vocabulary: tight horn arrangements, a locked-in rhythm section, syncopated bass, and a groove-first approach. The song is a tribute to James Brown, the progenitor of funk, and musically it operates in that classic tradition rather than in any fusion, acid jazz, or contemporary reimagining. The book's definition emphasizes "the music that defined the core vocabulary," and Tower of Power is a textbook example, even when recording outside the 1968-1977 window.

2. **Funk Rock** — Tower of Power has been described as "the quintessential horn-driven funk-rock band" by AllMusic. Their sound blends funk grooves with rock energy, and the guitar work on their recordings often leans into rock territory. If the book's definition of Funk Rock accommodates horn-heavy bands with rock guitar textures, this could fit. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tower-of-power-mn0000013393/biography

3. **Nu-Funk** — This is a 1995 recording, which places it in the era when nu-funk was emerging. However, Tower of Power is not really a "revival" act in the nu-funk sense; they are an original-generation funk band that simply kept recording. This label would be misleading. Not recommended.

**Recommendation:** Classic Funk is the strongest fit. The track is a classic funk band doing what they do best, paying tribute to the genre's founder.
