Bill Withers' soul standard "Lean on Me" is so well known that, like Otis Redding with "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay," he has become synonymous with his biggest hit. But even at his most soulful, Withers was not shy about dropping a few funky burners onto his 1970s records.

The rhythm section on today's track is the muscle from Charles Wright & the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band: James Gadson, Melvin Dunlap, Benorce Blackmon, and Ray Jackson. This was a fully formed, hit-making funk unit, and from the first track on *Still Bill*, "Lonely Town, Lonely Street," you can hear the shift in Withers' sound. This album carries a more funk infused feel compared to the earlier, more pure soul of *Just As I Am*, which was backed by a Booker T. Jones-led ensemble of Stax players.

We lost one of my favorite voices, Bill Withers, on this day in 2020. I don't know when or where you're reading this, but I'm tempted to say there ain't no sunshine today because he's gone.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The man who made toilets for 747s"** — Withers was 33 and still installing lavatories at a Boeing aircraft parts factory when "Ain't No Sunshine" charted. By the time *Still Bill* dropped in 1972, he was a star, but that blue-collar gravity never left his voice. "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" is the sound of a man who knows exactly what loneliness in a big city feels like because he lived it working factory shifts in LA.

2. **"The band that left the Watts 103rd"** — The rhythm section on this track (James Gadson, Melvin Dunlap, Benorce Blackmon, Ray Jackson) all defected from Charles Wright & the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band to back Withers. This was a funk supergroup hiding in plain sight, and "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" is the first thing you hear them play on *Still Bill*.

3. **"Opening track as thesis statement"** — "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" opens *Still Bill*, the album that also contains "Lean on Me" and "Use Me". It's a deliberate choice to lead with a tough, funky minor-key groove before the hits arrive. The sequencing tells you Withers sees himself as a funk artist first, not a balladeer.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" opens *Still Bill*, setting the tone for the album before the massive hits "Lean on Me" and "Use Me" arrive. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill
- The track was released as the B-side of the "Friend of Mine" single in 1973 on Sussex Records (catalog SUX 257), drawn from the *Live at Carnegie Hall* album. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6679805-Bill-Withers-Friend-Of-Mine-Lonely-Town-Lonely-Street
- Bobbi Humphrey covered the track on her 1972 Blue Note album *Dig This!*, recorded just months after *Still Bill*'s release. Her version opens with a reflective flute intro over Horace Ott's string charts before morphing into a gritty funk groove. The session featured Ron Carter on bass and Alphonse Mouzon on drums. — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/705338/Bobbi-Humphrey:Dig-This
- The Geto Boys sampled "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" on their track "It Ain't" from the 1993 album *Till Death Do Us Part*, slowing the bluesy shuffle down into a heavier hip-hop context. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1690/Geto-Boys-It-Ain't-Bill-Withers-Lonely-Town,-Lonely-Street/
- Joe Bonamassa covered the track on his 2012 album *Driving Towards the Daylight*, stretching it to seven minutes with extended electric guitar work. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/cover/177013/Joe-Bonamassa-Lonely-Town-Lonely-Street-Bill-Withers-Lonely-Town,-Lonely-Street/
- Sheryl Crow, with Citizen Cope and drummer Steve Jordan, recorded a cover as a tribute released on April 6, 2020, just days after Withers' death on March 30, 2020. Crow said Withers once told her "I am a stonemason now and I am good at it!" when she urged him to record again. — Source: https://www.noise11.com/news/sheryl-crow-releases-bill-withers-cover-of-lonely-town-lonely-street-with-citizen-cope-20200406
- Al Jarreau recorded a version on an album titled *Lonely Town Lonely Street*, which consists entirely of Bill Withers covers, though there is some question about whether the material was legitimately authorized. — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/41445/Al-Jarreau:Lonely-Town-Lonely-Street
- Bill Withers performed the song on Soul Train in 1972. — Source: https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0849284/

#### Album
- *Still Bill* reached #1 on Billboard's Soul Albums chart and #4 on the Billboard 200. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill
- The album was recorded at The Record Plant in Los Angeles. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill
- The backing band on *Still Bill* was essentially the rhythm section of Charles Wright & the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band: James Gadson (drums), Melvin Dunlap (bass), Benorce Blackmon (guitar), and Ray Jackson (keys). They left Wright's band around 1970-71 to work with Withers. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Wright_%26_the_Watts_103rd_Street_Rhythm_Band
- Melvin Dunlap, the bassist, grew up in Cleveland, Ohio, and had been the touring bassist for The O'Jays before moving to LA, where he worked in a factory (like Withers) before joining the Watts band. — Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2026/02/bill-withers-still-bill-sussex-1972/
- The label, Sussex Records (catalog SXBS 7014), was founded by Clarence Avant, known as "The Godfather of Black Music." The label folded in 1975 when the IRS padlocked the offices over $62,000 in unpaid taxes and auctioned off all assets. Many master tapes disappeared. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Records
- Sussex also released Sixto Rodriguez's two albums, which became cult classics in South Africa and Australia, the subject of the documentary *Searching for Sugar Man*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Records

#### Artist
- Bill Withers (born William Harrison Withers Jr., July 4, 1938, Slab Fork, West Virginia; died March 30, 2020, Los Angeles) was the youngest of six children. His father was a miner; his mother was a maid. His father died when he was 13. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers
- Withers had a childhood stutter that shaped his life. He joined the Navy at 17 partly because of it, serving nine years and finally receiving adequate speech therapy. He said: "I figured out that my stutter was a fear of the perception of the listener. So I started having fun, trying to lower my opinion of other people and raise my opinion of myself." — Source: https://www.stutteringhelp.org/content/singer-withers-overcame-stuttering
- At age 31, Withers was still making toilet seats for Boeing 747s at a factory in LA when "Ain't No Sunshine" broke into the top 10. The cover of his debut album *Just as I Am* shows him holding his lunch pail at the factory, and the photo sessions were done during his lunch breaks. — Source: https://www.washingtonpost.com/arts-entertainment/2020/04/04/bill-withers-aint-no-sunshine/
- He was signed to Sussex Records by Clarence Avant. His debut *Just as I Am* (1971) was produced by Booker T. Jones with Stephen Stills on guitar. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers
- Withers won three Grammy Awards (Best R&B Song for "Ain't No Sunshine" in 1972, "Just the Two of Us" in 1982, and "Lean on Me" in 1988 for Club Nouveau's cover). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers
- He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2015 and the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2005. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers
- Withers' first marriage was to actress Denise Nicholas (1973-1974); his second was to Marcia Johnson (1976), with whom he had two children. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers
- Withers effectively retired from music after 1985's *Watching You, Watching Me*, walking away from the industry at 47 and never recording again. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently set to `Classic Funk`.

1. **Classic Funk** (current, recommended) — This is the right call. "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" was released in 1972 on a major-distributed label (Sussex via Buddah), from an album that hit #1 on the Soul Albums chart and #4 on the Billboard 200. It's within the core classic era (1968-1977), and the track's identity is funk-first: a locked-in rhythm section groove with Gadson's drums and Dunlap's bass driving the pocket. It features a vocal chorus and standard radio-friendly length. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill

2. **Deep Funk** (alternative) — While the album was massively successful, "Lonely Town, Lonely Street" itself was never a charting single. It was a B-side to "Friend of Mine" in 1973 and is essentially an album deep cut that has gained recognition through crate-digging and DJ play over the decades. It fits the "delayed recognition" criterion of deep funk, though the album's fame complicates this label. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6679805-Bill-Withers-Friend-Of-Mine-Lonely-Town-Lonely-Street

3. **Soul Jazz** (stretch) — The Jive Time Records review describes the track as "tough folk-funk, augmented by violins." The live versions lean jazzier, and Bobbi Humphrey's near-immediate jazz flute cover on Blue Note suggests the track was heard by jazz musicians as jazz-adjacent material. However, the studio version is firmly in funk territory. Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2026/02/bill-withers-still-bill-sussex-1972/
