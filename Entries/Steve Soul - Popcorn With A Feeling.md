### Open Research Questions

The entry had no `open:` items. These questions came up during research and are still open:

- **Who is Steve Soul?** Discogs says Steve Soul is Stephen B. Myers, a DJ from Birmingham, Alabama, who made two James Brown "call and song" interview singles. The Discogs profile is flagged "Needs Vote" and I could not find a second source. Searches for Stephen/Steve Myers with the Birmingham Black radio stations (WENN, WJLD) and the Birmingham Black Radio Museum came up empty. A Gripsweat/eBay listing calls the record "James Brown (as Steve Soul)", which reads Steve Soul as a Brown pseudonym. That is likely wrong, because the interview voice on the companion records is a DJ, not Brown. Next steps: the Birmingham Black Radio Museum (https://thebbrm.org) archives, Billboard/Cash Box "Vox Jox" columns from 1969–70, and the Hip-O Select *Singles Vol. 6* liner notes by Alan Leeds/Harry Weinger. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/1256151-Steve-Soul ; https://gripsweat.com/item/222642934702/nm-1970-james-brown-as-steve-soul-popcorn-with-a-feeling-45
- **Which side was the A-side?** Discogs lists "Popcorn With A Feeling" as side A (matrix SK-13134, the lower number) and "Soul President" as side B. 45cat titles the record "Soul President / Popcorn With A Feeling", and so do the Super Soul Sound blog and the "Sweet" Charles Sherrell discography, which all treat "Soul President" (the novelty break-in) as the plug side. Gripsweat lists "Popcorn with a Feeling b/w Soul President." The Hip-O Select compilation includes only "Popcorn With A Feeling", which suggests UMG's archive treats it as the A-side. Not resolved. Sources: https://www.45cat.com/record/4512551 ; https://supersoulsound.com/jbsuperfan/1970-federal-45-steve-soul-soul-presidentpopcorn-with-a-feeling-instrumental/ ; https://www.sweetcharlessherrell.com/discography-1.html
- **Is it the same recording as the *It's a Mother* track?** It very likely is. The writer credits are identical (Brown, Kellum, Stubblefield, Nolen, Pinckney), and the timings are 2:55 on the 45, 2:56 on the LP and 2:58 on the Hip-O CD. Apple Music's digital *Singles Vol. 6* credits the track to James Brown as "Popcorn With A Feeling (Instrumental)", the same title as the LP version. No source states outright that it is the same master. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/popcorn-with-a-feeling-instrumental/1463715205?i=1463715548
- **Exact release date:** No month or day was found. See Date Suggestions.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The band wore a different name."** This is a James Brown band instrumental that had already been on a James Brown LP, reissued under the name of a DJ who doesn't play on it. It opens up how Brown used King's subsidiary labels and side names to get more product onto the radio.
2. **"Break-ins and the radio DJ as artist."** "Soul President" and the earlier "A Talk With The News" are Dickie Goodman-style records that build fake interviews from snippets of Brown's hits. They point toward the DJ-as-recording-artist idea that hip-hop later made central, and the funky flip is what diggers actually want.
3. **"Popcorn, extra butter."** In 1969–70 Brown turned one dance craze into a whole run of records: "The Popcorn", "Mother Popcorn", "Lowdown Popcorn", "Let a Man Come In and Do the Popcorn", "Mashed Potato Popcorn" and this one. The entry could look at how a single groove idea got franchised. It ties in with the existing "Mother Popcorn (Pt. 1) - 01-01" entry.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Douglas Wolk called the flute on this track "really unfortunate." Listen for when the flute enters. Does it sink the groove or lift it?
2. Imagine you bought this 45 thinking Steve Soul was a new artist. What gives away, within the first ten seconds, that this is the James Brown band?
3. Focus on Clyde Stubblefield's drums and Jimmy Nolen's chicken-scratch guitar. With no vocals to lead, which of the two drives the track more?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- The A-side is an instrumental credited to James Brown, Alfonzo "Country" Kellum, Clyde Stubblefield, Jimmy Nolen and St. Clair Pinckney. It is effectively a band composition, unusual for a Brown production of this period. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/663336-Steve-Soul-Popcorn-With-A-Feeling
- The same title, with the same writers, is track 6 on James Brown's *It's a Mother* (King 1063, August 1969) as an instrumental. So the Steve Soul 45 almost certainly recycled a James Brown band recording that had already come out on an LP. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother
- Douglas Wolk's WFMU essay on Brown's Popcorn craze singles out "Popcorn With A Feeling" for its "really unfortunate flute part." — Source: https://db.wfmu.org/LCD/25/popcorn.html
- The flip, "Soul President", is a break-in novelty in the style of Dickie Goodman. — Source: https://supersoulsound.com/jbsuperfan/1970-federal-45-steve-soul-soul-presidentpopcorn-with-a-feeling-instrumental/ ; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyKAfK91b3c
- Discogs tags the single with the styles Funk, Novelty and Break-In. The master has about 484 "wants" against 110 "haves", which shows strong collector demand. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/663336-Steve-Soul-Popcorn-With-A-Feeling
- Cover versions: Brazilian arranger Waltel Branco's "Tema De Zorra" is circulated as a version of "Popcorn With A Feeling". This rests on one DJ playlist and a YouTube upload, so it needs more checking. The Soul Surfers also recorded the tune for a 2019 "Tribute to J.B.'s" single. — Source: https://serato.com/playlists/djtbird/laboratori-22-jamesmas-2020-edition ; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdUhOSYRS9M ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/popcorn-with-a-feeling/1878026080?i=1878026081

### Album

- No original album. In 2008 the track was collected on Hip-O Select/Polydor's *The Singles, Volume 6: 1969–1970* (disc 2, track 5). It is credited there to Steve Soul and placed in sequence between "Ain't It Funky Now" and "Part Two (Let A Man Come In And Do The Popcorn)". The compilers treated it as part of Brown's official singles chronology. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1981909-James-Brown-The-Singles-Volume-6-1969-1970
- *It's a Mother* (King, August 1969), the LP that also carries the instrumental, was recorded between June 24, 1967 and June 12, 1969 at the Apollo Theater, King Studios (Cincinnati) and Criteria Studios (Miami). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother

### Artist

- Discogs lists Steve Soul's real name as Stephen B. Myers, "DJ from Birmingham, Alabama, that released two singles with fake 'call and song'-interviews with James Brown." (Single source, flagged "Needs Vote".) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/1256151-Steve-Soul
- The first Steve Soul single was "James Brown – A Talk With The News" b/w "Shades Of Brown (Pt. 2)" (King 45-6216, released February 15, 1969). On it, "James Brown's answers are short parts of his songs." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3820704-Steve-Soul-James-Brown-A-Talk-With-The-News
- Federal was the King subsidiary where James Brown started in 1956. By 1970 Brown was using it as an outlet for his productions. The release is labeled "A James Brown Production" and was distributed by Starday-King Records, Inc. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5482025-Steve-Soul-Popcorn-With-A-Feeling ; https://www.discogs.com/label/1543806-Federal-5

### Field Verification

- **label: Federal.** Discogs gives Federal 45-12551, and so do 45cat, Gripsweat and the Sherrell discography. Discogs also lists Golo and Dynatone as publishers and Starday-King as distributor. No conflicting source was found.
- **year: 1970.** Discogs master and releases say 1970, as do Gripsweat ("©1970") and Super Soul Sound. Counter-claim: the "Sweet" Charles Sherrell discography lists F 12551 under **1969** (https://www.sweetcharlessherrell.com/discography-1.html). The catalog sequence brackets the turn of 1969–70, so a late-1969 release is possible. 1970 is kept because it is the majority view and the copyright year. The LP version of the same instrumental is 1969 (*It's a Mother*).
- **track length: 2m 55s.** The 45 label/Discogs says 2:55. Other sources: Wikipedia *It's a Mother* LP 2:56; Discogs Hip-O *Singles Vol. 6* CD 2:58; Apple Music *It's a Mother* 2:56 (176.76 s); Apple Music *Singles Vol. 6* 2:56 (176.48 s); Deezer *It's A Mother* 2:55 (175 s). The 45 label time is used.
- **album: (empty).** Single-only release as Steve Soul. Compiled in 2008 on *The Singles, Volume 6: 1969–1970*. The recording also appears on *It's a Mother* (1969) under James Brown's name.
- **Credit discrepancy:** Discogs credits the Hip-O CD track to Steve Soul. Apple Music's digital edition of the same compilation credits it to James Brown as "Popcorn With A Feeling (Instrumental)".
- **Writer-credit discrepancy:** Discogs and Wikipedia list five writers (Brown, Kellum, Stubblefield, Nolen, Pinckney). The French IdRef catalogue lists only J. Nolan, C. Stubblefield and A. Kellum (1970). — https://www.idref.fr/198741421/id

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (primary). This is a 1969–70 James Brown band instrumental from the peak Popcorn era, the core vocabulary that the book's "Classic Funk" definition describes. It was released through King/Federal distribution and its sister track was on a King LP.
2. **Deep Funk.** As a Steve Soul 45 it was a commercial non-event: no chart placement was found, it was released on a fading subsidiary, and it was found later by collectors (484 Discogs wants; Super Soul Sound calls the flip "uncut deep groove funk"). It matches the book's "delayed recognition" definition, but the same music was on a James Brown LP, which weakens the case.
3. (Weak) **Soul Jazz.** The flute-led instrumental leans toward soul-jazz texture, but the rhythm section is pure JB funk. Not recommended.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - Ohio** (written to field). Federal/King was based in Cincinnati, and King Studios in Cincinnati is one of the *It's a Mother* recording locations. Defensible on label and probable studio. — https://www.discogs.com/label/1543806-Federal-5 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother
2. **US - Southeast.** If the Discogs identification is right, the credited artist, Steve Soul, was a Birmingham, Alabama DJ. Brown himself was based in Augusta, Georgia. The exact studio for this track is unconfirmed, and Criteria Studios in Miami is another possibility.
3. **US - Florida.** Only if the track turns out to be a Criteria (Miami) session. There is no evidence for that yet.
