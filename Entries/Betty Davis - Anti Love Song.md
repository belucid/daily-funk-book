Today’s song, "Anti Love Song," is from Betty Davis’ self-titled debut album, released on April 2, 1973, by Just Sunshine Records. Many listeners knew her as Miles Davis’ ex-wife, and the woman who named his *Bitches Brew* album. Her own debut was a funky declaration of independence.

Gregg Errico is the credited producer, but Betty Davis was in charge. She wrote and arranged every song on the album, and hand-picked the band. She brought in Sly Stone’s rhythm section: Larry Graham on bass and Errico on drums. She also discovered young talent, using teenage guitarist Neal Schon and the future Pointer Sisters on backing vocals.

The album is a funk masterpiece, but it failed to make a commercial impact. It didn't crack the *Billboard* 200 and peaked at #54 on the US R&B Albums chart. In 1973, the establishment wasn't ready for a sexually aggressive female artist. Davis paved the way for future stars like Madonna and Janet Jackson, but at the time she was condemned, boycotted, and banned. The NAACP criticized the album’s sexual content and pressured radio stations to not play her records. The first single, “If I’m in Luck I Might Get Picked Up,” was banned in some cities.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The Bay Area dream team Betty assembled.** Betty wrote and arranged every song, then handpicked her band: Sly Stone's rhythm section (Larry Graham and Greg Errico), Santana's young guitarist Neal Schon, and the unsigned Pointer Sisters plus Sylvester on backing vocals. A 28-year-old Black woman directing a roomful of Bay Area heavyweights in 1972 is the actual story; the "anti love" lyric is the spark, but the architecture is Betty.
- **The "anti" in funk.** Funk is a music of yes — yes to the body, yes to the groove, yes to the night. Betty opens her debut by saying no. The song is a refusal letter set to a slap-bass riff, and that rhetorical move (using the funk vocabulary to push back rather than pull close) is what makes the track land harder than a love song would.
- **The Mrs. Miles Davis problem.** In 1973 most listeners knew Betty as "Miles's ex-wife who suggested the name Bitches Brew." She spent this album refusing to be a footnote, and "Anti Love Song" is the cleanest distillation of that refusal: power balanced, equal weight, neither party owns the other.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Lock in on Larry Graham's bass for the first 30 seconds. Notice how he leaves space between thumps where a busier bassist would crowd in. Then ask: is Betty answering the bass, or is the bass answering Betty?
- Betty hangs back from the microphone and sings most of this song almost under her breath. Track the moments she lets the volume up. What is she reacting to: the lyric, the band, or both?
- Neal Schon is 19 years old and playing wah guitar behind a singer twice as famous as he is. Listen for the moments his guitar steps forward, and the moments it disappears entirely. Is he serving the song or auditioning?

#### Album

- Released April 2, 1973, on Just Sunshine Records, an independent label. — Source: https://albumism.com/features/betty-davis-eponymous-debut-album-betty-davis-album-anniversary
- Recorded over roughly three weeks at Wally Heider Studios in San Francisco, sessions running 1972 through early 1973. — Sources: https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/essays/she-wailed-electric-on-betty-davis ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/betty-davis-mw0000459304
- Greg Errico produced. Betty Davis wrote and arranged every track on the album herself. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis_(album)
- Charted at #202 on the Billboard 200 (Bubbling Under) and #54 on the US Top R&B Albums chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis_(album)
- The full personnel reads like a Bay Area summit: Larry Graham and Doug Rauch on bass; Errico and Willie Sparks on drums; Neal Schon and Doug Rodrigues on guitar; Pete Sears, Richard Kermode, and Merl Saunders on keys; the Pointer Sisters, Sylvester, and others on backing vocals. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis_(album)
- The NAACP publicly condemned Betty over the album's sexually explicit material, pressuring radio stations to boycott her records. The first single, "If I'm in Luck I Might Get Picked Up," was banned in some cities. — Sources: https://albumism.com/features/betty-davis-eponymous-debut-album-betty-davis-album-anniversary ; https://hazlitt.net/blog/we-dont-need-another-black-woman-rock-n-roll-how-betty-davis-challenged-music-industry
- Reissued on CD and LP by Light in the Attic in 2007 (LITA 026) with three previously unreleased bonus tracks: "Come Take Me," "You Won't See Me in the Morning," and "I Will Take That Ride." Further reissues followed in 2017 and 2023. — Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/betty-davis

#### Artist

- Born Betty Gray Mabry, July 26, 1944, in Durham, North Carolina. Family relocated to Homestead, Pennsylvania (greater Pittsburgh), where her father worked at a steel mill. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- Died February 9, 2022, in Homestead, Pennsylvania, aged 77. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- Married Miles Davis in September 1968, divorced 1969. She is widely credited with introducing Miles to Jimi Hendrix and Sly Stone, the rock and funk listening that shaped his electric turn. She is reported to have suggested the title "Bitches Brew" for the 1970 album. — Sources: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis ; https://souljazzreview.wordpress.com/2014/05/16/betty-davis-anti-love-song/
- Before recording her own albums, she wrote songs for The Chambers Brothers ("Uptown (To Harlem)," 1967) and demoed material for The Commodores at Motown. When Berry Gordy required her to sign over publishing, she pulled the songs and recorded them herself later. — Source: https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/essays/she-wailed-electric-on-betty-davis
- Recorded just three commercially released albums between 1973 and 1975, then largely retired from the industry by the end of the decade. The 2017 documentary "Betty: They Say I'm Different" reintroduced her to a wider audience. — Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (strong recommendation).** The book defines Classic Funk as funk released through the major-label commercial pipeline between roughly 1969 and 1977 where the track's primary identity is funk rather than a fusion. Betty Davis charted on Billboard's Top R&B Albums (#54), was distributed nationally on a real (if independent) label, and the rhythm-section identity here is straight funk rather than rock or jazz. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis_(album)
- **Funk Rock (reasonable alternative).** AllMusic tags the album "Funk, Psychedelic Soul, Soul," and other reviewers (Guitar Player, Soul Jazz Review) emphasize the heavy electric-guitar attack and Neal Schon's wah lines. If the author wants to stress the rock-leaning guitar layer over the funk pocket, Funk Rock is defensible. — Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/betty-davis-mw0000459304 ; https://www.guitarplayer.com/players/betty-davis-vinyl-treasures

I'd shelve it unless the author specifically wants to emphasize Sly's shadow over the album.

Strongest suggestion: **Classic Funk**.
