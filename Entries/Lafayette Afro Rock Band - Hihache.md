### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The most sampled band you never heard."** Hihache is one of the most sampled breaks in recorded music (300+ songs per WhoSampled tallies), yet the album flopped on release and the band remained obscure in their American homeland. The gap between commercial failure and posthumous ubiquity is the story.
- **"American funk with a French passport."** A band of Long Island musicians who decided the US funk market was too crowded, emigrated to Paris's Barbès district, and there cut the records that would later be mined by US hip-hop. Funk as a transatlantic round trip.
- **"The break is the song."** Hihache opens with a clean, generic-sounding 4/4 drum pattern that became raw material for producers. Frame the entry around the idea of a recording whose cultural meaning was rewritten by sampling decades after it was made.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The track opens with a bare drum pattern before the band enters. Try to count how many seconds the drums play alone — that isolated opening is exactly what generations of hip-hop producers looped.
- You have almost certainly heard this groove rebuilt in another song. As you listen, ask which later track it most reminds you of, then check WhoSampled to see if your ear was right.
- Listen for the interplay between the percussion (congas) and the trap kit. Where does the "Afro" in Afro-Rock actually live in this arrangement — the drums, the horns, or the bass?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Hihache" is one of the most sampled breaks in music history, sampled in 300+ tracks by artists including Biz Markie, LL Cool J, Wu-Tang Clan, Naughty by Nature, Coolio, Flying Lotus, Aphex Twin and The Avalanches — Source: http://www.vinylmeplease.com/magazine/lafayette-afro-rock-band/
- The track was written, and on some accounts produced, by Leroy Gómez — the saxophonist who soon after became famous as the lead vocalist of disco act Santa Esmeralda ("Don't Let Me Be Misunderstood") — Source: https://www.factmag.com/2014/02/04/sample-the-funk-10-legendary-samples-and-the-stories-behind-them/4/
- The opening is a clean, almost generic 4/4 drum figure — part of why it proved so endlessly reusable as a breakbeat — Source: http://www.vinylmeplease.com/magazine/lafayette-afro-rock-band/

#### Album

- *Soul Makossa* takes its title and title track from a cover of Manu Dibango's international hit; the album initially failed to chart and only gained stature decades later through sampling — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Afro_Rock_Band
- The original French pressing was on Musidisc (cat. 30 CV 1269); a Spanish pressing on Marfer (distributed for Musidisc) and US release via Editions Makossa followed — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1242582-Lafayette-Afro-Rock-Band-Soul-Makossa
- Both of the band's most-mined tracks live a couple of records apart: "Hihache" on *Soul Makossa* and "Darkest Light" on *Malik* (1975) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Afro_Rock_Band

#### Artist

- The band formed in Roosevelt, Long Island, NY in 1970 as the Bobby Boyd Congress, named for vocalist Bobby Boyd — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Afro_Rock_Band
- Deciding the US funk scene was too saturated, they relocated to France in 1971, settling in Paris's Barbès district, where producer Pierre Jaubert took them on — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Afro_Rock_Band
- The collective recorded under a string of names — Ice, Soul Congress, Crispy & Co., Captain Dax — making them a discographer's puzzle; they broke up in 1978 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Afro_Rock_Band

### Genre Suggestions

- **Afrobeat (recommended).** Discogs styles the album "Afrobeat, Soul, Funk." Per the book's definition, Afrobeat is the broad label for funk rooted in Africa / Afro-funk; the "Afro" in Afro-Rock Band and the conga-driven, polyrhythmic texture fit this best — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/141725-Lafayette-Afro-Rock-Band-Soul-Makossa
- **Deep Funk.** Strong case under the book's "delayed recognition" definition: the album flopped on release and was rediscovered decades later by crate-digging DJs/producers. The defining trait of deep funk — delayed recognition through sampling — describes Hihache's whole afterlife — Source: http://www.vinylmeplease.com/magazine/lafayette-afro-rock-band/
- **Classic Funk.** Defensible by era (1973) and by being a straightforward funk groove rather than a fusion, but it lacks the commercial-pipeline/mainstream-American footprint the book ascribes to classic funk, so it ranks third.
