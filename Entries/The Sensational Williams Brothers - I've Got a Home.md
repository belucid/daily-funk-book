### Open Research Questions

No author-supplied `open:` lines were present in this entry. Open items found during research:

- **Release-year discrepancy.** iHeart lists the album as "June 1978" (https://www.iheart.com/artist/the-sensational-williams-brothers-30000225/albums/ive-got-a-home-82291064/). The JazzDisco Savoy catalog index lists MG 14493 as 1979 (https://www.jazzdisco.org/savoy-records/catalog-14400-series/album-index/). Discogs master and release both list 1979 (https://www.discogs.com/master/2513329-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ive-Got-A-Home, https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ives-Got-A-Home). The neighboring Sensational Williams Brothers album "Mama Prayed For Me" (MG 14462) is 1978, so the 14493 catalog number sitting later in the sequence supports a 1979 release. I have used 1979, but the album may have been recorded in late 1978 with a 1979 street date — that would explain the iHeart "June 1978" tag if it reflects a session date or a re-release.
- **Catalog-number variation.** JazzDisco lists "MG 14493" while Discogs lists "SL 14493." Both are likely Savoy's prefix variations for the same release (MG vs. SL for stereo LPs in Savoy's 14400 gospel series).
- **Leonard Williams' birth/death.** One unsourced search snippet gave Leonard's birth as July 1, 1951, but no authoritative source confirms this. Wikipedia's `The Williams Brothers (gospel group)` article does not provide his dates. Left noted but not used.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"When gospel got funky on purpose."** Use this entry to push back on the "after-the-fact label" framing in your gospel-funk definition. The Williams Brothers were a Mississippi quartet with deep Southern church roots, and yet the credits on this LP read like a funk session: electric bass, electric guitar, kit drums, and a four-piece horn section with dedicated horn arrangements by Darryl Dixon, Dave Watson, and Rick Freistat. This wasn't a band tripping into funk; it was a quartet choosing to put a horn line on a hymn.
- **"The family business."** The personnel of "I've Got a Home" — Doug on lead vocals, Melvin on lead guitar and vocals, Leonard on bass, cousin Henry Green on vocals, with founding member Maurice Surrell on drums — is the family band their father Leon "Pop" Williams organized in a Smithdale backyard in 1960. Twenty years later they're still playing every instrument in the room. The entry can frame the funk groove as a family heirloom that grew with the brothers.
- **"Mississippi to the world via New Jersey."** Savoy was a Newark/New Jersey gospel powerhouse by 1979, but the music on it was being made in a Mississippi musical lineage that also produced the Jackson Southernaires (brother Frank), the Mississippi Mass Choir (also Frank), and later Malaco's gospel boom. Frame the track as one moment in a network of Smithdale-rooted musicians who reshaped Black gospel commerce.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The track is barely 3:30 long. Listen for how the band uses the back half: after the verses establish the "I've got a home" hook, the horn section punches in around the choir. What part of the arrangement signals "funk" and what part signals "quartet gospel"? Which side wins by the fade?
- Melvin Williams plays a right-handed guitar left-handed (per https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Williams_(musician)). Try to pick his lead guitar out of the mix — the inverted string layout often gives left-handed-on-right-handed players a particular phrasing on bends and pull-offs. Does it sound like a Pop Staples guitar voice, or something else?
- Listen across the bar line between the lead vocal (Doug or Melvin) and Henry Green's background tenor. Gospel quartet harmonies are call-and-response; funk drumming wants the one. Where do those two impulses collide on this track?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The Discogs master classifies "I've Got a Home" itself under genre "Funk / Soul" with styles "Gospel, Rhythm & Blues, Funk" — even Discogs' own catalogers refused to file this as straight gospel. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/2513329-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ive-Got-A-Home
- The track is the shortest on the LP (3:30) and is the album opener. The rest of the record sprawls — "Softly And Tenderly" at 6:15, "He Did Nobody Wrong" at 5:20 — so "I've Got a Home" reads like a deliberately tight, radio-length funk statement at the top of an otherwise expansive gospel set. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/2513329-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ive-Got-A-Home

#### Album

- The credits on the album include a four-piece horn section (Darryl Dixon and Dave Watson on sax, Doug Rothauser and Rick Freistat on trumpet, Peter Baerga on trombone) with horn arrangements credited to Dixon, Watson, and Freistat — substantial funk-session firepower for a gospel record on Savoy. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ives-Got-A-Home
- Drums are handled by Maurice L. Surrell, Sr., who Mississippi sources name as one of the original non-family members the Williams brothers played with as kids in Smithdale. He's been with the project from the beginning. — Source: https://www.mswritersandmusicians.com/mississippi-musicians/the-williams-brothers and https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ives-Got-A-Home
- The album was produced by John Daniels, a gospel producer who ran "The John Daniels Gospel Series" mostly through New Birth Records and worked across Savoy, New Birth, and Glori. He is essentially the gospel-funk producer-of-record for this period of the Williams Brothers' catalog. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/2775322-John-Daniels-8 and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SxRwWn54qUw
- Executive producer is Fred Mendelsohn, the longtime Savoy Records executive who shepherded the label's gospel catalog from the 1960s onward. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ives-Got-A-Home

#### Artist

- The group formed in 1960 in Smithdale, Mississippi, when patriarch Leon "Pop" Williams (Nov 24, 1908/1909 – Sep 6, 1989) overheard his sons and a cousin singing in the backyard and put them together as a group. The early band name was "The Little Williams Brothers." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Williams_Brothers_(gospel_group) and https://malaco.com/artist/gospel/the-williams-brothers/
- Founder Pop Williams also organized the Southern Gospel Singers and Big Four Gospel Singers, and is considered the architect of the Smithdale-to-Jackson gospel pipeline that also fed the Jackson Southernaires. — Source: https://malaco.com/artist/gospel/the-williams-brothers/
- The Williams Brothers' brother Frank (Franklin Delano Williams, Jun 25, 1947 – Mar 22, 1993) was an early Williams Brothers member who left to join the Jackson Southernaires, became director of gospel promotions at Malaco in 1979, and founded the Mississippi Mass Choir in 1988 — a direct lineage from a Smithdale household to one of the most successful gospel acts of the late 20th century. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Williams
- Melvin Williams (born Jul 21, 1953) plays a right-handed guitar left-handed without restringing it, which is unusual enough to be noted in his Wikipedia entry. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Williams_(musician)
- In 1991 Doug and Melvin Williams launched Blackberry Records, which their sources describe as the first African-American-owned record label in Mississippi. — Source: https://www.mswritersandmusicians.com/mississippi-musicians/the-williams-brothers
- Henry Green, the cousin and original non-family member who's on this 1979 record, sang with the group for more than six decades. He died at his home in Smithdale on February 4, 2024, age 80. — Source: https://journalofgospelmusic.com/quartet/rip-henry-green-gospel-quartet-singer-member-of-the-williams-brothers/ and https://www.wjtv.com/entertainment-news/member-of-the-williams-brothers-dies/

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is **Gospel Funk** and I'd keep it as the primary tag. The track is a textbook example of the book's definition: a gospel quartet absorbing the funk vocabulary of its era — full horn section, electric bass and guitar, kit drums, tight 3:30 runtime — to "make a joyful noise for the Lord."

- **Gospel Funk (primary).** Strongest fit. Lead vocals, lyrics, and harmonic structure are gospel quartet; rhythm section, horn arrangements, and groove are funk. Source: Discogs styles "Gospel, Rhythm & Blues, Funk" (https://www.discogs.com/master/2513329-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ive-Got-A-Home) and the personnel listing on https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-2-Ives-Got-A-Home.
- **Deep Funk (secondary).** Defensible. The Sensational Williams Brothers' late-70s Savoy LPs (especially "Mama Prayed For Me" and this one) sit firmly in the crate-digger zone now — eBay listings explicitly label them "Gospel Soul Funk Breaks" and they trade as funk records among collectors. This satisfies the book's "delayed recognition" criterion. Source: https://www.ebay.com/itm/306093815752 and the album's Discogs page.
- **Classic Funk** is a weaker fit; the track is inside the 1969–1977 era only by a year or two and its commercial channel was the gospel market, not mainstream funk radio.
