David Garibaldi was born on November 4, 1946. His drumming defines today’s track, Tower of Power’s "Squib Cakes." Among drummers, Garibaldi is a legend. His ghost-note layering, syncopation, and Latin-inflected funk became a masterclass that generations have studied. *Rolling Stone* ranked him #46 on its “100 Greatest Drummers of All Time” list, and *Modern Drummer* readers voted him the top R&B/Funk drummer six straight years from 1980 to 1985.

The *Back to Oakland* album is often singled out as essential listening for drummers. It opens and closes with “Oakland Stroke,” two short tracks that bookend the record. Oakland Stroke became the name for Garibaldi's drumming style, and the band's signature rhythmic feel.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The shape of a groove"** — The title "Squib Cakes" was slang Chester Thompson and Mic Gillette invented to describe the shape of a woman's hips and the way she walks. The song itself is an instrumental, so the suggestive name is all implication, no words. This opens a fun angle on how funk instrumentals carry sensuality through feel rather than lyrics.

2. **"The drummer's holy text"** — *Back to Oakland* was voted by Modern Drummer magazine as one of the most important recordings for drummers, and David Garibaldi's performance on "Squib Cakes" is a key reason why. His ghost-note layering, beat displacement, and Latin-inflected patterns on this track became a masterclass that generations of drummers studied. This theme frames the track through the lens of rhythm education and influence.

3. **"From 'Check This Out' to a classic"** — Chester Thompson wrote the song shortly after joining Tower of Power in 1973. It was first performed under the working title "Check This Out" before Gillette and Thompson's slang gave it a permanent name. This theme traces how a brand-new member's contribution became one of the band's most enduring signatures.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Rocco Prestia's bass line is a masterclass in muted sixteenth notes, turning his fingers into a second percussion section. Can you hear where his bass and Garibaldi's kick drum lock together, and where they deliberately pull apart?"

2. "Chester Thompson builds his keyboard solo across several minutes, starting restrained and ending ferocious. At what point does the solo cross from jazz into funk? Is there a single moment where it tips over?"

3. "This is nearly eight minutes of instrumental funk with no vocals at all. At what point, if any, do you miss a singer? What takes the place of a voice in holding your attention?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The title "Squib Cakes" was slang that Chester Thompson and trumpeter Mic Gillette invented to describe women, specifically "the shape of their hips and the way they walk." Thompson confirmed this in the liner notes of *What Is Hip? The Tower of Power Anthology*. — Source: https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/towerofpower/squib-cakes-t945.html
- The track was originally performed under the working title "Check This Out" at a 1973 concert, shortly after Thompson joined the band. — Source: https://www.wolfgangs.com/music/tower-of-power/audio/20052098-8189.html
- "Squib Cakes" has been sampled by a wide range of artists across genres, including Cypress Hill ("Checkmate" and "Lightning Strike"), The Brand New Heavies ("Dream Come True"), Original Concept ("Runnin' Yo' Mouth"), The Orb, De La Soul, The Charlatans ("Patrol," Dust Brothers mix), Wagon Christ, and Plug. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Tower-of-Power/Squib-Cakes/sampled/
- Poncho Sanchez covered "Squib Cakes" on his 2005 Concord album *Do It!*, giving it a Latin jazz treatment. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/cover/355691/Poncho-Sanchez-Squib-Cakes-Tower-of-Power-Squib-Cakes/
- The Time (Prince's side project) sampled "Squib Cakes" on "Release It," connecting Tower of Power directly to the Minneapolis Sound. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/110029/The-Time-Release-It-Tower-of-Power-Squib-Cakes/

#### Album
- *Back to Oakland* peaked at #26 on the Billboard 200 and #13 on the Billboard Top Soul LPs chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The album was voted by Modern Drummer magazine as one of the most important recordings for drummers to listen to. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- It was recorded at two locations: Record Plant in Sausalito, California, and Kaye/Smith Studios in Seattle, Washington. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The album's cover photograph by Bruce Steinberg was shot at the San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The album opens and closes with the "Oakland Stroke," a 53-second intro and a 1:08 reprise that bookend the record and gave a name to the band's signature rhythmic feel. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The single "Don't Change Horses (In the Middle of a Stream)" reached #26 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #22 on R&B. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- Warner Bros. Records catalog number: BS 2749. — Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/tower-of-power/back-to-oakland.p/

#### Artist
- Tower of Power was formed after Emilio Castillo and Stephen "Doc" Kupka met at the Alameda County Fair on July 4th weekend, 1968. Castillo hired Kupka after a home audition, on the advice of his father. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- Castillo's father got him into music after catching Emilio and his brother stealing. He challenged them to find something constructive, which led both brothers to pick up instruments. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Castillo
- The band was originally called The Motowns, then changed its name to Tower of Power specifically to get booked at Bill Graham's Fillmore Auditorium in San Francisco. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- David Garibaldi was ranked #46 on Rolling Stone's "100 Greatest Drummers of All Time" list. He won Modern Drummer's R&B/Funk category six consecutive years (1980-1985). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Garibaldi_(musician)
- Rocco Prestia pioneered a muted sixteenth-note fingerstyle bass technique that became foundational to funk bass playing. His approach created a percussive, staccato tone that was widely imitated. — Source: https://www.guitarworld.com/features/rocco-prestia-tower-of-power-interview
- Chester Thompson, who wrote "Squib Cakes," later spent 26 years as Santana's keyboardist (1983-2009), co-writing and co-producing the Grammy-winning *Blues for Salvador* (1987) and contributing to *Supernatural* (1999), which sold over 30 million copies. — Source: https://santana.com/chester-thompson/
- Mic Gillette, who co-coined the term "Squib Cakes," died of a heart attack on January 17, 2016, at age 64. He had been a child prodigy, reading music and playing trumpet by age four. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mic_Gillette
- Rocco Prestia died on September 29, 2020, at age 69. — Source: https://www.guitarworld.com/news/legendary-tower-of-power-bassist-rocco-prestia-dies-aged-69
- The band has had over 60 musicians tour or record with them since 1968. Oakland declared May 31 "Tower of Power Day" in 2018. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power
- David Garibaldi announced his retirement from Tower of Power on January 15, 2024. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Garibaldi_(musician)

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current value) — Strong fit. Tower of Power is one of the defining classic funk bands of the 1970s, with major label distribution on Warner Bros., chart presence, and radio play during funk's peak commercial era (1968-1977). "Squib Cakes" comes from their most commercially successful period. This is the most natural home for this track. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power

2. **Jazz-Funk** — Also a strong case. "Squib Cakes" is a 7:49 instrumental with extended improvisation, especially Chester Thompson's organ solo. The track has more in common with jazz-funk's longer, soloist-driven format than with classic funk's vocal-hook-driven singles. AllMusic tags the album as both Funk and Soul, but the track itself plays like jazz musicians in a funk pocket. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/back-to-oakland-mw0000652809

3. **Deep Funk** — Weak case, not recommended. While "Squib Cakes" was never released as a single, it comes from a well-distributed Warner Bros. album that charted at #26 on the Billboard 200. It doesn't fit the "overlooked on release, rediscovered later" profile of deep funk.
