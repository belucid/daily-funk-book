### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The man who paid his own way with someone else's hit."** Pena's debut sold almost nothing, but he wrote "Jet Airliner," and Steve Miller's 1977 cover funded the rest of his life. The forgotten funk record sits in the shadow of a song that became a classic-rock staple. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena
- **"A case for blind virtuosity across genres."** Pena was nearly blind from birth, a Perkins School graduate who moved from blues and folk to flamenco to, eventually, self-taught Tuvan throat singing. "I'm Gonna Make It Alright" is one stop on an astonishingly wide musical road. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena
- **"Deep funk hiding on a folk-rock record."** The album is filed under Folk Rock / Soul, yet track two erupts into a dirty, Meters-style funk groove. A case for digging past a record's genre label to find the one track that doesn't fit. Source: https://musicmaven.wordpress.com/2007/09/04/music-passion-paul-pena/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This album is shelved as folk rock, but track two does not behave like it. Listen for the moment the groove locks into a dirty New Orleans-style pocket — what makes this feel closer to The Meters than to a singer-songwriter LP?
- Pena plays and sings nearly everything himself across this record. Listen to how his deep, weathered vocal sits against the syncopated rhythm section — is he leading the groove or riding it?
- Knowing Pena was almost completely blind from birth and would later teach himself Tuvan throat singing off shortwave radio, listen for the sheer range of vocal texture and control here. Where do you hear the ear that could later master Kargyraa?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "I'm Gonna Make It Alright" is the second track on the LP (side A, track 2), described by collectors as coming on "like a prime-time Meters with a dirty funk sound" — the funkiest moment on an otherwise folk/soul record. Source: https://musicmaven.wordpress.com/2007/09/04/music-passion-paul-pena/
- The track is exactly 4:07 on the original Capitol pressing; the 2011 digital reissue lists it slightly differently (~4:11 on Apple Music), a common artifact of digital remastering. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/685160-Paul-Pena-Paul-Pena ; https://music.apple.com/us/song/im-gonna-make-it-alright/716296565

#### Album
- The album sat out of print for roughly 40 years before being officially reissued in digital (mp3) form on Amazon and iTunes around 2011. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena_(album)
- Critically respected despite poor sales: AllMusic rated it 3.5 stars and Robert Christgau gave it an A− in his seventies retrospective. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena_(album)
- The personnel is a notable session crew: steel guitar by Jeff "Skunk" Baxter (later of Steely Dan and the Doobie Brothers) and congas/percussion by Jumma Santos (Jim Riley, who played with Miles Davis). Recorded November 1971 at Intermedia Sound, Boston. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1731849-Paul-Pena-Paul-Pena

#### Artist
- Pena wrote "Jet Airliner"; Steve Miller covered it on 1977's *Book of Dreams* and the royalties from that hit "helped support Pena for the rest of his life." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena
- Of Cape Verdean descent, born with congenital glaucoma and nearly blind from birth, Pena attended the Perkins School for the Blind. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena
- In 1969 he opened for Frank Zappa and the Grateful Dead at Philadelphia's Electric Factory and played the Newport Folk Festival. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena
- In 1984 he heard Tuvan throat singing on shortwave radio and taught himself Khoomei, Sygyt, and Kargyraa; his mastery is the subject of the 1999 documentary *Genghis Blues*, which won the Sundance Audience Award and earned an Academy Award nomination. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk** (strongest case): The defining trait of deep funk in this book is delayed recognition — a track overlooked on release that found its audience later via crate-diggers. This album sold poorly, went out of print for ~40 years, and the funk cut "I'm Gonna Make It Alright" is exactly the kind of obscure-album funk gem collectors prize ("prime-time Meters with a dirty funk sound"). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pena_(album) ; https://musicmaven.wordpress.com/2007/09/04/music-passion-paul-pena/
- **Classic Funk** (secondary): Released 1972 on a major label (Capitol) within the 1969-1977 classic era, with funk as the track's primary identity. The weakness: classic funk in this book emphasizes commercial distribution/chart presence, and this album had neither — pushing it toward deep funk. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1731849-Paul-Pena-Paul-Pena
- **Funk Rock** (weaker): The parent album is filed as Folk Rock / Soul and Pena's roots are bluesy/rock; but this specific track leans more straight-funk than rock fusion, so deep funk fits better. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/685160-Paul-Pena-Paul-Pena

Recommendation: **Deep Funk**, with Classic Funk as the alternative.
