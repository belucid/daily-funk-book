Ask most people to name the cities associated with funk, and they are likely to mention Detroit, Minneapolis, Dayton, New Orleans, Memphis, or even Los Angeles and London long before Durham, North Carolina. But during funk’s heyday, most mid-sized Southern cities had a scene of their own, often centered on a few clubs, bars, and tiny record labels.

The Black Experience Band formed in Durham in 1970 to back the Communicators, a five-man vocal group. They later split from the Communicators to work with another vocal act, the Modulations, then spent a short period as a self-contained band before breaking up. Their final single appeared in 1976 on Microtronics Records.

Today’s song, “The Road,” is about as deep as deep funk gets. It is Mariana Trench deep. The instrumental B-side was written by the band’s trombonist, James “Butch” Barton. The A-side was the politically charged “Has Time Really Changed.” The single was on Tri Oak Records, a short-lived label that released only two singles, both in 1974.

“The Road” was rescued from obscurity by the British label Jazzman, which included it on the 2007 anthology *Carolina Funk: Funk 45s from the Atlantic Coast*. The collection brought together twenty-two rare regional funk singles selected by Jazzman Gerald and North Carolina record collector Jason Perlmutter.

### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. Questions surfaced during research:

- **Was "The Road" the politically-charged track, or "Has Time Really Changed"?** Sources disagree. Bull City Soul (Durham local-music history) attributes the "too political, less airplay" reaction to the A-side, "Has Time Really Changed." A funk-o-logy forum thread instead implies the B-side "The Road" carries the late-60s, raw funk character. Note: "The Road" is essentially an instrumental B-side (writer James "Butch" Barton), so the "too political" framing most plausibly belongs to the vocal A-side. Recommend treating the political-airplay angle as describing the single as a whole / the A-side, not "The Road" specifically. Sources: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/communicators.html ; http://funk-o-logy.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1552
- **Album:** No album/LP was ever issued. "The Road" exists only as a 1974 7" B-side and on later compilations. The `album` field is correctly left empty. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/683049-The-Black-Experience-Band

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The B-side that outlived the A-side."** The single's selling point on release was the vocal A-side; decades later, crate-diggers prize the raw instrumental B-side enough to anthologize it. A theme about how deep-funk reputation inverts a record's original commercial logic.
- **"Bull City funk."** Durham, North Carolina is not on funk's usual map (Detroit, Dayton, New Orleans, Memphis). A theme framing this as evidence funk took root in small Southern cities through working bar-bands and tiny local labels (Duplex, Tri Oak, Microtronics).
- **"Too political for the radio."** Use the airplay-suppression story (whether attached to the A-side or the single overall) to explore how Black social commentary in early-70s funk met resistance even at the local-radio level.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is a 1974 recording that sounds like 1968. Listen for the wah-wah guitar, raw open cymbals, and the heavy, "sticky" bass line, then ask yourself what specifically dates it earlier than its release year.
- "The Road" is the instrumental flip of a vocal single. Listen for the spots where you can almost hear the absent vocal — where is the band leaving room for a singer who never arrives?
- Compare the pocket here to Kool & the Gang's early instrumentals. The band was said to be working in that style; can you hear the Kool & the Gang influence, and where does this little North Carolina band go its own way?

#### Artist

- The Black Experience Band formed around 1970 in Durham, North Carolina to back the Communicators, a five-man harmony group (Tommy Clegg, Izell Gooch, Sylvester Howell, James Jenkins, Cager Perry). — Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/communicators.html ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/683049-The-Black-Experience-Band
- After splitting from the Communicators, the band backed The Modulations, then played as a self-contained group before breaking up; it issued one final single in 1976 on Microtronics Records. — Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/communicators.html ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/683049-The-Black-Experience-Band
- The Communicators' 1973 debut "One Chance" (b/w "Is It Funky Enough?") was written by bassist Willie Hill and guitarist Willis Norman; first issued locally on Duplex Records (1304), its regional success led Sylvia Robinson's Turbo label to pick it up for national distribution in 1974. — Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/communicators.html
- Per Bull City Soul, the "Has Time Really Changed" single was considered "too political" by radio stations and got much less airplay than the debut. — Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/communicators.html

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk (current — keep).** Textbook fit per the book's definition: an obscure 1974 regional/private-label funk 45 overlooked on release and rediscovered decades later by crate-diggers (anthologized on Jazzman's "Carolina Funk," 2007). The defining trait of delayed recognition is exactly this record's story. — Source: https://jazzmanfunkcompilations.bandcamp.com/album/carolina-funk-funk-45s-from-the-atlantic-coast ; On Subgenres definition (deep funk = delayed recognition)
- **Classic Funk (secondary).** It was recorded and released in 1974, squarely in the 1969–1977 classic era, and its primary identity is funk (not a fusion). However, it lacks the mainstream distribution/chart presence the book ties to classic funk, so Deep Funk remains the better fit. — Source: Discogs release; On Subgenres definitions
- **Psychedelic Funk (long shot).** Sources cite a Funkadelic stylistic influence and heavy wah-wah; not a strong case, but worth noting if the author hears a P-Funk lean. — Source: https://www.heavyweightfunk45s.com/2017/03/12/communicators-black-experience-band/
