There was never any question in my mind that this journey would begin with James Brown. Spoiler alert: it will end with him too, on New Year’s Eve. Everything in the year between is the branching funk tree that sprouted at the feet of the Godfather.

Today’s song is “Mother Popcorn (Pt. 1).” The Part 1 is there because Brown was recording long funk jams, but the 45 rpm 7-inch single only held about 4 minutes per side. The song would be split across two sides, labeled Part 1 and Part 2.

During Brown’s commercial peak, funk albums gathered up recent singles for listeners who wanted a long-playing record. *It’s a Mother* leans into the Part 1 and Part 2 format, sequencing six of these split tracks back to back so the longer jams don't require a record flip.

"Mother Popcorn" owes a lot to Brown's earlier hit, "Cold Sweat," but it has a busier, more rhythm-driven sound. When Brown uses "mother" in his lyrics, he's not referring to his absentee mother, who left him when he was four years old. It's a euphemism for a big booty.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is currently empty.

1. **"The first word of a new language"** — Mother Popcorn is widely cited as a turning point where James Brown shifted from song-based funk to rhythm-as-the-entire-point. Robert Christgau called it the moment Brown "began to concern himself more and more exclusively with rhythmic distinctions." This frames the entry as the starting point, not just of the book, but of the idea that groove could carry everything.

2. **"Two takes on a mother"** — The first version of the song, "You Got to Have a Mother for Me," was recorded January 13, 1969 at RCA Studios in Los Angeles with the same lyrics but a completely different instrumental arrangement, and was rejected. The final version was cut months later at King Studios in Cincinnati. The contrast between the two recordings shows how much the groove, not the words, made the song.

3. **"Last call for Pee Wee"** — Mother Popcorn was one of the last arrangements Pee Wee Ellis wrote for James Brown before leaving the band in 1969. Ellis co-wrote 26 songs with Brown, including "Cold Sweat" and "Say It Loud." This was the end of a partnership that built the vocabulary of funk.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The first version of the song was recorded on January 13, 1969 at RCA Studios in Los Angeles under the title "You Got to Have a Mother for Me." It had the same lyrics but a completely different instrumental backing and was rejected for release as a single. This rejected version was finally issued on the 1988 compilation album *Motherlode*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Popcorn
- The song entered the Billboard Hot 100 on June 8, 1969 at #80, peaked at #11 six weeks later on July 20, and hit #1 on the R&B chart. It was the highest-charting of Brown's series of "Popcorn" recordings that year. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Popcorn
- Critic Robert Christgau singled out "Mother Popcorn" as "the turning point" in Brown's music where he "began to concern himself more and more exclusively with rhythmic distinctions." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Popcorn
- The song shares a beat structure and even a guitar riff with Brown's 1967 hit "Cold Sweat," but a faster tempo and more agitated 16th-note horn movement give it a more frenetic quality. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Popcorn
- Prince sampled "Mother Popcorn" for "Gett Off" (1991). Other artists who sampled it include Spoonie Gee, Double Dee & Steinski, Donald D, Pizzicato Five, and Coldcut. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/James-Brown/Mother-Popcorn/sampled/
- Aerosmith covered the song on their 1978 album *Live! Bootleg*, and the Blues Brothers included it in a medley with "Do You Love Me" on the 1980 album *Made in America*. Frank Black also covered it. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Popcorn

#### Album
- *It's a Mother* was James Brown's 26th studio album, released August 1969 on King Records (catalog KSD-1063). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother
- The album was recorded across multiple sessions and studios from June 1967 to June 1969, including the Apollo Theater (NYC), King Studios (Cincinnati), and Criteria Studios (Miami). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother
- It peaked at #26 on the Billboard Top LPs and #3 on the R&B/Soul LP chart during a 22-week run. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Mother
- AllMusic rates it 4 out of 5 stars; the Rolling Stone Album Guide gave it 3 out of 5. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/its-a-mother-mw0000851853

#### Artist
- James Joseph Brown was born May 3, 1933 in a one-room shack in Barnwell, South Carolina, and died December 25, 2006 (Christmas Day) at age 73 from congestive heart failure. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown
- Brown was raised in Augusta, Georgia by his great-aunt Honey, who ran a brothel. He grew up in poverty during the Great Depression. — Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/James-Brown-American-singer
- At the 1969 Newport Jazz Festival, Brown performed an 8-minute version of "Mother Popcorn," and after a lengthy ovation, returned for an encore of the same song. Though both Down Beat and Rolling Stone called the festival a "disaster," Brown's set was considered an "unequivocal triumph." — Source: https://www.wolfgangs.com/music/james-brown/audio/20020023-3134.html?tid=4889051
- Pee Wee Ellis, co-writer of "Mother Popcorn," joined Brown in 1965 and co-wrote 26 songs with him, including "Cold Sweat" (1967) and "Say It Loud, I'm Black and I'm Proud" (1968). Ellis described the fusion of his jazz background with Brown's R&B roots as "a new and distinctive black culture." He left in 1969, making "Mother Popcorn" one of their final collaborations. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pee_Wee_Ellis
- Clyde Stubblefield, the drummer on "Mother Popcorn," perfected the ghost-note-driven style that drummer David Garibaldi credits with inventing the vocabulary of funk drumming. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Stubblefield
- The circumstances of Brown's death on Christmas Day 2006 have been questioned. In 2019, CNN and other journalists suggested Brown may have been murdered. The case was reviewed by Atlanta prosecutors but closed in 2020 without action, with key evidence reportedly missing. — Source: https://allthatsinteresting.com/james-brown-death

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current, recommended) — This is the right call. "Mother Popcorn" is a #1 R&B hit from 1969 on a major label (King Records), with wide radio play and chart presence. It sits squarely in the 1968-1977 window defined by the book's Classic Funk genre. The track was a commercial hit that defined the core vocabulary of funk.

2. **Deep Funk** — Not appropriate. While the track is deeply funky, it was a massive commercial success (#1 R&B, #11 Pop), which disqualifies it from the "delayed recognition" definition of Deep Funk in this book.

3. **Psychedelic Funk** — Not appropriate. There's nothing surreal, hallucinatory, or P-Funk-adjacent about this track. It's straight-ahead, rhythm-first funk.
