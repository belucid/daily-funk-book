### Open Research Questions

The entry had no `open:` items. I found these questions during research and could not settle them:

- **Who actually plays on it?** The two sources disagree.
  - The Discogs entry for *Funky Good Time: The Anthology* (1995, Harry Weinger, compilation producer) lists New York session players for this track: Gordon Edwards (bass), Charlie Brown (guitar), Michael Gipson (trombone), Lew Soloff (trumpet) and Tasha Thomas (vocals). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/106625-The-JBs-Funky-Good-Time-The-Anthology
  - A reviewer of the anthology credits the track to "THE LAST WORD (Fred Wesley, James Brown, Maceo Parker etc)." Source: https://markattheflicks.blogspot.com/2021/03/funky-good-time-anthology-by-jbs.html
  - Discogs lists James Brown, Fred Wesley, Pee Wee Ellis and Maceo Parker as members of The Last Word. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/227924-The-Last-Word
  - The anthology credits are the most detailed and come from the label's own archive, so I would trust them most. Approach for the author: check the printed booklet of Polydor 31452 7094-2, if a copy is at hand.
- **Exact release date.** Neither Discogs nor any other source I found gives a day.
  - The runout etching on the US single reads "34735 PD-14226-AS-1A 2-8-74," which points to mastering at Sterling Sound on February 8, 1974. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
  - One reviewer says it was a "February 1974 US 45-single." Source: https://markattheflicks.blogspot.com/2021/03/funky-good-time-anthology-by-jbs.html
  - 45cat (https://www.45cat.com/record/pd14226) and AllMusic both returned HTTP 403, so I could not check them. A Billboard "New Releases" listing from Feb–Mar 1974 would settle it.
- **Did it chart?** I found no evidence that it reached the Billboard Hot 100 or the soul chart. My searches came up empty, which does not prove it never charted.
- **Title.** The full title on the record is "Keep On Bumpin' Before You Give Out Of Gas." The entry's `track` field uses the shortened "Keep on Bumpin'". That field is not editable, so the author should decide whether to keep the short form.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The band with a hundred names."** The J.B.'s were also billed as The New Dapps, Maceo & the Macks, The James Brown Soul Train, A.A.B.B., The First Family and The Last Word (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s). This single shows Brown flooding the market with pseudonymous product in early 1974. Even the band name works as a joke: the Godfather always gets the last word.
- **"The J.B.'s that weren't."** If the anthology credits are right, this "J.B.'s" record was cut by New York jazz and session players: Lew Soloff of Blood, Sweat & Tears and Gordon Edwards, later of Stuff. Many of the same players were on the David Matthews-arranged studio-band side of *Hell* (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_(James_Brown_album)). That opens the question of what made something a James Brown sound: the players, or the boss.
- **"Funk as fuel economy."** The title came out in early 1974, at the height of the oil crisis and gas lines. Brown's own records at the time were openly topical; the same anthology era produced "You Can Have Watergate Just Gimme Some Bucks And I'll Be Straight." The gas-crisis reading is my inference; I found no source tying the title to the oil embargo.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The credited players are New York session pros, not the touring J.B.'s. Listen to the horns and bass. Does this sound like the J.B.'s, or like a very good imitation of them?
- Tasha Thomas, who later played Aunt Em in *The Wiz* on Broadway, is credited on vocals. Listen for when the voices come in. Are they the star of the record, or one more rhythm instrument?
- Play this back-to-back with its B-side, "Funky & Some." Which side would you have flipped the record over for?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The track was recorded at Sound Ideas Studios in New York City on January 17, 1974. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/106625-The-JBs-Funky-Good-Time-The-Anthology
- The anthology credits list Gordon Edwards (bass), Charlie Brown (guitar), Michael Gipson (trombone), Lew Soloff (trumpet) and Tasha Thomas (vocals). These are New York session players rather than the core touring J.B.'s. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/106625-The-JBs-Funky-Good-Time-The-Anthology
- Writing and arranging are credited to James Brown and Fred Wesley; Brown produced and Bob Both engineered. Bob Both is also credited (tambourine) on the studio-band side of *Hell* in 1974. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_(James_Brown_album)
- Gordon Edwards, Charlie Brown, Lew (Lou) Soloff and Michael Gibson are all in the David Matthews-arranged studio band on *Hell*. That suggests "Keep On Bumpin'" came out of the same New York session pool. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_(James_Brown_album)
- The US label credit reads "James Brown – The Hitman – The Godfather Of Soul." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
- The US promo 45 had the A-side in mono on one side and stereo on the other. Discogs notes say a pressing by Monarch is supposed to exist as well. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4555651-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas
- The single was issued in the US, UK (Polydor 2066 429), Netherlands and Brazil. The Brazilian 7" was cut at 33⅓ RPM. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/557070-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some, https://www.discogs.com/release/4962815-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some, https://www.discogs.com/release/18870259-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas
- It was revived for the UK rare-groove market on *Up For The Down Stroke (Uncut Funk Volume 1)* (1992) and *From The Funk Side* (1994). Its widest reissue was the 1995 anthology *Funky Good Time*, track 2-7. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1881969-Various-Up-For-The-Down-Stroke-Uncut-Funk-Volume-1, https://www.discogs.com/master/36564-The-JBs-Funky-Good-Time-The-Anthology
- I found no sampling record. WhoSampled returned HTTP 403, so this is unverified, not confirmed absent.

#### Album

- There is no parent album; the track is a non-LP single. The B-side, "Funky & Some," later gave its name to a 1993 compilation, *Funky & Some (Some Of James Brown's Funkiest People)*. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/227924-The-Last-Word
- In 1976 it appeared on a Brazilian 7" EP, *Discoteca Alucinante N.º 2*, next to James Brown, Lyn Collins and Clydie King. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/24039422-James-Brown-Lyn-CollinsLast-Word-Clydie-King-Discoteca-Alucinante-N%C2%BA-2

#### Artist

- "The Last Word" was one of several alternate billings for the J.B.'s. Others included The New Dapps, The James Brown Soul Train, Maceo and the Macks, A.A.B.B. and The First Family. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
- The Last Word released only one single (PD 14226). Everything else under the name is a compilation appearance. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/227924-The-Last-Word
- Lew Soloff was Blood, Sweat & Tears' trumpeter from 1968 to 1973, so he came to this session straight from a rock band. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lew_Soloff
- Gordon Edwards later led Stuff, the New York session supergroup with Richard Tee, Cornell Dupree, Eric Gale and Steve Gadd. He also played on Brown's *The Payback* and *Reality*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Edwards_(musician)
- Tasha Thomas played Aunt Em in the original Broadway production of *The Wiz* and had a 1979 hit with "Shoot Me (With Your Love)." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasha_Thomas

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (primary).** It came out in 1974, inside the 1969–1977 classic window, on Polydor, a major label, with worldwide distribution (US, UK, Netherlands, Brazil). It was produced by James Brown and arranged by Fred Wesley. The track's identity is pure funk. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/367088-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
- **Deep Funk.** I found no evidence that it charted. It went out under a one-off pseudonym, and its later life came through UK rare-groove compilations (*Uncut Funk*, *From The Funk Side*) and the 1995 anthology. That fits the book's "delayed recognition" definition, even though it was on a major label. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1881969-Various-Up-For-The-Down-Stroke-Uncut-Funk-Volume-1, https://www.discogs.com/master/1650885
- **Jazz Funk (weaker).** This only holds if the session-player credits are right. Players like Lew Soloff and Gordon Edwards came from jazz-rock and session work. But the track is a short, radio-length groove, not an improvisation vehicle, so it fits jazz funk less well than classic funk.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - New York (written to field).** The track was recorded at Sound Ideas Studios, New York City, with New York session musicians. The lacquer was cut at Sterling Sound in New York, and Polydor Incorporated was New York-based. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/106625-The-JBs-Funky-Good-Time-The-Anthology
- **US - Southeast.** This works if the author tags by the J.B.'s and James Brown's home base. Brown's operation was centered in Augusta, Georgia, and many J.B.'s sessions were cut at International Recording, Augusta (per the same anthology notes). It is defensible if the author treats The Last Word as "the J.B.'s" regardless of who was in the room.
- The enumerated list needs no new region.
