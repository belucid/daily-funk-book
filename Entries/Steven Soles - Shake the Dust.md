### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The session man's gospel detour."** Soles spent the mid-'70s in Bob Dylan's Rolling Thunder Revue and co-founded the Alpha Band with T Bone Burnett. Then in 1980 he made a born-again record for a Costa Mesa church label — and its funkiest cut got rediscovered four decades later by crate-diggers. The hook: how a hard-touring rock pro ended up in the country-funk canon by way of Jesus music.
- **"Named after the fact."** "Country Funk" did not exist as a genre when Soles cut this; the 2012-2021 Light in the Attic comps invented the label retroactively. "Shake the Dust" opens Volume III, so it is a perfect lens on how a 2021 compilation can reframe a forgotten 1980 gospel LP track as funk.
- **"Elvis's rhythm section plays church."** The Promise is anchored by Jerry Scheff (bass) and Ron Tutt (drums) — Elvis Presley's TCB Band — plus Alpha Band's David Mansfield. The angle: the groove on a tiny Christian label record is the same pocket that backed the King.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Soles spent years as a sideman in Bob Dylan's band; one reviewer calls this track's vibe "a great '70s Dylan feel." Listen past the gospel lyric — where do you hear Dylan in the phrasing and the loose, rolling band?
- This is the song a 2021 crate-digging compilation chose to *open* a "country funk" record. Listen for the moment that earns it that label: is it the bass, the clavinet-era groove, or the drum pocket? Decide whether you'd file it under country, funk, or gospel.
- The rhythm section here (Jerry Scheff, Ron Tutt) backed Elvis Presley. Listen to the drums and bass and ask whether you can hear that pedigree under the church-flavored melody.

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- "Shake the Dust" is the opening track on Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume III (1975-1982)* on both vinyl and CD — the song that introduces the whole comp. — Source: https://www.gratefulweb.com/articles/light-attic-announces-country-funk-volume-iii-1975-1982
- The title references Jesus's instruction to the apostles to "shake the dust off your feet" (Matthew 10:14) — a gospel lyric that landed, decades later, on a secular funk compilation. — Source: https://real80sccm.com/2026/02/11/steven-soles-the-promise-1980/
- One CCM reviewer calls it "the best track on the album" and says it "has a great 70's Dylan feel," singling it out for David Mansfield's playing. — Source: https://real80sccm.com/2026/02/11/steven-soles-the-promise-1980/

### Album
- *The Promise* (1980) was Soles' first solo album of original material and his first overtly Christian record, released on Maranatha! Music — the label born out of Calvary Chapel in Costa Mesa, California, a hub of the Jesus Music movement. — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/word/maranatha/maranatha.html
- The backing band pairs Alpha Band bandmate David Mansfield (guitar, violin, dobro) with Elvis Presley's TCB Band rhythm section: Jerry Scheff (bass) and Ron Tutt (drums). — Source: https://real80sccm.com/2026/02/11/steven-soles-the-promise-1980/
- AllMusic gave *The Promise* three stars and noted Soles' solo albums were critically acclaimed "if little heard." — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/promise-mw0000920458

### Artist
- Bob Dylan personally asked Soles to join the 1975-76 Rolling Thunder Revue; he appears on *Desire*, *Street Legal*, and *Bob Dylan at Budokan*, and played the character "Ramon" in Dylan's 1978 film *Renaldo and Clara*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soles
- After Rolling Thunder, Soles co-founded the Alpha Band with T Bone Burnett and David Mansfield, releasing three albums in 1977-78. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soles
- Soles went on to a major production/songwriting career (Roy Orbison, John Mellencamp, Elvis Costello, Counting Crows, k.d. lang) and has won two Grammy Awards and an Academy Award. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soles

### Genre Suggestions

- **Country Funk (current — keep).** The single strongest justification is canonical: Light in the Attic literally placed this track as the *opening cut* of *Country Funk Volume III*, the compilation series the book's own definition credits with naming the genre. By the book's own logic this is a textbook country-funk entry. — Source: https://www.gratefulweb.com/articles/light-attic-announces-country-funk-volume-iii-1975-1982
- **Gospel Funk (secondary case).** *The Promise* is an overtly Christian album on the Jesus-Music label Maranatha! Music, and the title quotes Matthew 10:14. The book defines gospel funk as "gospel music that absorbed the funk vocabulary of its era" — which fits this song almost as precisely as country funk. A genuine toss-up between the two. — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/word/maranatha/maranatha.html
- **Deep Funk (weaker, contextual).** The "delayed recognition" trait fits — a 1980 private-ish Christian LP track ignored for 40 years until crate-diggers resurfaced it. But Country Funk captures the discovery story more specifically, so this is a distant third.

Recommendation: **keep Country Funk**, with **Gospel Funk** as the most defensible alternative the author should weigh.

- **US - West (chosen).** Soles is Los Angeles-based; Maranatha! The closest enumerated value is **US - West**. — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/word/maranatha/maranatha.html
