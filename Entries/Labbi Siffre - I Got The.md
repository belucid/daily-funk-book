### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is empty.

1. **"The beat that cleared its conscience."** In the late 1990s, Siffre, who is gay, would only let Dr. Dre and Eminem sample the track once the homophobic and misogynist lines were gone. They sent him a clean version, he approved it, and the explicit version kept the lyrics anyway. This frames the track as a groove with a moral backbone, and the story behind the most famous riff in it.
2. **"Chas & Dave were the rhythm section."** The bass and guitar parts the world knows from "My Name Is" were played by Chas Hodges and Dave Peacock, the cockney "rockney" duo, and the drummer was King Crimson's Ian Wallace. The angle is the surprise of very English, very un-funky names making a London funk record that ended up in Detroit hip-hop.
3. **"Six and a half minutes, not four bars."** Most people know a four-bar loop. The full track builds a long, slow bridge "from those two-note basslines" (Soul-sides) and keeps shifting direction. The angle is to get the reader to hear the whole song and not just the sample.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

The `listening mission` field is empty.

1. "You know this riff from Eminem's 'My Name Is.' Listen to the first minute and decide which part really sells it: Dave Peacock's bass, Chas Hodges' guitar, or Labi's electric piano?"
2. "Listen for the bridge, which builds slowly out of a two-note bassline. When does it stop sounding like a loop and start sounding like a song?"
3. "This is a London record from 1975. What in the drums and horns tells you it isn't from Detroit or New York?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The opening riff became the backbone of Eminem's 1999 breakthrough "My Name Is." That single was Eminem's first Billboard top 40 hit (No. 36) and won him his first Grammy (Best Rap Solo Performance) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Name_Is
- "My Name Is" credits itself with an "interpolation" (a replayed version) of "I Got The," not a direct sample. Wikipedia says the hook played by Hodges and Peacock "was heavily repeated throughout the single in a replayed sample" — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Name_Is ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remember_My_Song
- Siffre refused to clear the sample until the anti-gay and anti-women lines were taken out. He told New Humanist in 2012: "Dissing the victims of bigotry ... is lazy writing. Diss the bigots, not their victims. I denied sample rights till that lazy writing was removed" — Source: https://americansongwriter.com/eminems-my-name-is-brought-70s-funk-pioneer-labi-siffre-back-to-the-mainstream-but-the-sample-almost-didnt-happen ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Got_The...
- He approved only the clean version. "I should have stipulated 'all versions,'" he said later. To Rolling Stone he added: "I don't know whether I would react the same today as I reacted then" — Source: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/labi-siffre-resented-eminem-dr-dre/ ; https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-holdovers-song-crying-laughing-loving-lying-labi-siffre-1234939657/
- Jay-Z sampled a different part of the song two years before Eminem, on "Streets Is Watching" (In My Lifetime, Vol. 1, 1997). Miguel also sampled it, for "Kaleidoscope Dream" — Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-holdovers-song-crying-laughing-loving-lying-labi-siffre-1234939657/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Got_The...
- The song plays in the Better Call Saul episode "Bagman" (season 5, episode 8, aired April 6, 2020) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Got_The... ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagman_(Better_Call_Saul)
- It was only issued as a single years after the sampling made it famous. In 2003 it came out as one side of a Stateside split 7" with All The People's "Cramp Your Style" (SS 2228), taken from the compilation *$tateside Sampled*. In 2006 it got its own Stateside 7" (SS 2232, B-side "The Vulture") — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1732477-Labi-Siffre-All-The-People-I-Got-The-Cramp-Your-Style ; https://www.discogs.com/release/698784-Labi-Siffre-I-Got-The
- Soul-sides' O-Dub singles out "that now-infamous bridge section which builds so slowly yet surely from those two-note basslines" — Source: https://soul-sides.com/2014/02/labi-siffre-remember-his-songs.html
- Chas Hodges on royalties from the Eminem record: "We ain't had any yet, but someone's chasing it up." Seen in a search snippet only; the page would not render for a full read — Source: https://www.musicradar.com/news/chas-and-dave-played-on-a-classic-eminem-track-and-no-one-knew-about-it ; see also https://www.nme.com/blogs/wtf-of-the-day-chas-hodges-of-chas-dave-played-guitar-on-eminems-my-name-is-2382847

#### Album

- The band is a surprising cast of British session players. Co-producer and lead guitarist was Big Jim Sullivan, who played on around 750 charting singles including 54 UK No. 1s. Drums on "I Got The" were by Ian Wallace of King Crimson. Bass was Dave Peacock and guitar Chas Hodges, months before they became famous as Chas & Dave — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1965796-Labi-Siffre-Remember-My-Song ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Jim_Sullivan ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Wallace_(drummer)
- The album did not chart. It "lingered in obscurity for many years" until the Eminem sample, which led to the 2006 reissue — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remember_My_Song
- It was recorded at The Music Centre in Wembley. The original LP sleeve thanks "Refreshments: Peggy, Lawrence and Albert" and "Transport: Big Barn" — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1965796-Labi-Siffre-Remember-My-Song
- Demon Music calls it "a gritty departure from the singer-songwriter's trademark sweeter sound." Siffre recorded it almost back to back with his other 1975 album, *Happy*. Demon's 3CD set *1975* is out 27/11/2026 — Source: https://www.demonmusicgroup.co.uk/?p=23786
- None of Siffre's albums came out on a US label in the 1970s. Rolling Stone says none was released in the US until the streaming era — Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-holdovers-song-crying-laughing-loving-lying-labi-siffre-1234939657/ ; https://soul-sides.com/2014/02/labi-siffre-remember-his-songs.html

#### Artist

- Born Claudius Afolabi Siffre on 25 June 1945 in Hammersmith, London, to a Yoruba Nigerian father and a mother of Barbadian descent. In the 1960s he played jazz guitar in the house band at Annie Ross's club in Soho — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labi_Siffre ; https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/celebrating-hf/heritage-trails/black-music-history-trail/labi-siffres-birthplace-goldhawk-road
- He wrote "It Must Be Love," which Madness took to No. 4 in the UK in 1981 (Siffre plays violin in their video), and the anti-apartheid anthem "(Something Inside) So Strong," which won an Ivor Novello award — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labi_Siffre
- Kanye West built "I Wonder" (*Graduation*) on Siffre's "My Song." *The Holdovers* (2023) used "Crying Laughing Loving Lying" — Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-holdovers-song-crying-laughing-loving-lying-labi-siffre-1234939657/
- Siffre and Peter Lloyd, his partner from 1964, moved to Amsterdam in 1969 because Siffre saw the UK as "uncivilized" in its homophobia. Siffre gave up music in 1998 to care for Lloyd after Lloyd's stroke — Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-holdovers-song-crying-laughing-loving-lying-labi-siffre-1234939657/
- He is also a published poet; his three collections include *Nigger* and *Blood on the Page* — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labi_Siffre
- In February 2026, at 80, he announced *Unfinished Business*, his first new album in 28 years — Source: https://www.officialcharts.com/chart-news/labi-siffre-new-album-unfinished-business-2026-when-bbc-radio-2-piano-room/ ; https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/labi-siffre/folk-icon-labi-siffre-to-release-first-new-album-in-28-years

### Field Verification

- **album: Remember My Song.** Discogs master and Wikipedia agree. No conflicts.
- **label: EMI. This has a conflict.**
  - The original UK LP is EMI EMC 3065 (https://www.discogs.com/release/1965796-Labi-Siffre-Remember-My-Song).
  - The main Labi Siffre Wikipedia article says he "signed with EMI Records" in 1975 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labi_Siffre).
  - The **Remember My Song** Wikipedia article says the album "was released on the Pye International record label." That contradicts the physical record and looks like a mistake (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remember_My_Song).
  - Demon Music says he recorded it "for new label Parlophone" (https://www.demonmusicgroup.co.uk/?p=23786). Parlophone is an EMI imprint and holds the reissue rights now; the 2006 remaster and streaming versions are on Parlophone UK (https://www.deezer.com/album/342870). The 1975 sleeve itself says EMI.
  - Use EMI for the original release.
- **year: 1975.** Discogs, Wikipedia, Rolling Stone and Demon all agree. The only oddity is Apple Music, which shows a 1973 release date for the album. That is a metadata error.
- **track length: 6m 36s.** The original LP gives 6:36 (Discogs). The 2006 remaster on Apple, Spotify and Deezer runs 6:35. A 2001 compilation version on Deezer runs 6:24 (384 s) (https://www.deezer.com/track/3527318). The 2006 radio edit runs 3:51 to 3:52. I used the original LP time.
- **region: UK.** Siffre was born in London, it was recorded in Wembley, and the label was British. No conflicts.
- **Personnel discrepancy (useful for the riff mission).**
  - Discogs credits Siffre with the electric piano on A1 and Ian Wallace with the drums.
  - Wikipedia's "My Name Is" article describes the borrowed riff as "bass and guitar ... played by Chas & Dave."
  - Far Out says "the electronic piano riff and bassline."
  - So sources disagree about whether the hook is Hodges' guitar or Siffre's electric piano, layered over Peacock's bass. Listen before writing about it.
- **First single release: sources disagree.** American Songwriter, Far Out and Wikipedia all say 2003. Discogs shows a 2003 Stateside split 7" (SS 2228) and a 2006 solo Stateside 7" (SS 2232). Both dates are right; they are two different releases.

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is empty.

1. **Deep Funk (recommended).** The book defines this as "a commercial fate": tracks overlooked on release and later found by crate-digging DJs looking for hip-hop samples, including tracks on well-known labels that came from "a poorly selling record with limited distribution." That describes this track exactly. The album was on EMI but didn't chart, never had a US release, and was revived by Jay-Z and Dr. Dre samples (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remember_My_Song ; https://soul-sides.com/2014/02/labi-siffre-remember-his-songs.html).
2. **Classic Funk.** It is from 1975, inside the book's 1969 to 1977 window, and came out on a major label. The track's main identity is funk: Discogs lists the styles as Soul and Funk, and Demon calls it "tight funk-soul grooves." It lacks the hit-making and charting part of the definition, though, which is why Deep Funk fits better.
3. **Not Brit-Funk.** The book excludes brit-funk. That scene (Light of the World, Level 42, Linx) is late 1970s and 1980s, so this 1975 London session record sits outside it. It's worth saying so in case a reader or editor asks.

### Region Suggestions

1. **UK (recommended, written to field).** Siffre was born and based in London, it was recorded at The Music Centre in Wembley, and it was an EMI UK release (https://www.discogs.com/release/1965796-Labi-Siffre-Remember-My-Song).
2. **Nigeria (not recommended).** His father was Yoruba Nigerian, but nothing about the recording or the scene ties the track to Nigeria (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labi_Siffre).
3. **US - Michigan (not recommended).** It is tempting because of the Eminem (Detroit) connection, but that is the afterlife of the track, not where it came from.
