Issues raised by this research that the author should resolve:

- **Track title mismatch.** No source spells the title "Funk & Some". Discogs (original 45 and the 2011 CD) and Spotify/Apple/Deezer use **"Funky & Some"**. 45cat and AllMusic use **"Funky And Some"**. The `track` field is locked for me, so the author should consider changing it to "Funky & Some". Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some , https://www.45cat.com/record/pd14226 , https://www.allmusic.com/song/funky-and-some-mt0033462048
- **When was the B-side recorded?** The BnF catalogue (from the *Funky Good Time* anthology notes) dates only the A-side: 1974-01-17, Sound Ideas, New York. The Second Disc says the four bonus tracks on the 2011 *Lost Album* (with "Funky & Some" as track 13) come "from the same sessions" as that album. AllMusic dates those sessions to February 1, 1972. If that is right, "Funky & Some" may be a 1972 J.B.'s/Fred Wesley recording held back and used as a 1974 B-side. Neither source says so outright for this track. The Hip-O Select liner notes by Alan Leeds and Harry Weinger are the likely resolution, but I could not access them online. Sources: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb38311854k/5 , https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album/ , https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-lost-album-mw0002245841
- **Who plays on it?** No source lists personnel for this track. The Discogs artist page gives The Last Word's members as James Brown, Fred Wesley, Pee Wee Ellis and Maceo Parker, which is a collective attribution, not session credits. https://www.discogs.com/artist/227924-The-Last-Word
- **Chart history.** No Billboard or Cash Box chart entry was found for the A-side or the B-side. A Billboard search turned up only an unrelated "You Got To Keep On Bumpin'" by Kay-Gees. This is treated as "no evidence of charting", not confirmed absence.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The band with a hundred names."** The Last Word was one of many aliases James Brown put on the J.B.'s (Maceo and the Macks, The First Family, A.A.B.B., the James Brown Soul Train...). The entry can ask why Brown kept rebranding the same players, and what that did to their careers. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
- **"The B-side that outlived its A-side."** "Keep On Bumpin'" got the anthology slots in 1976, 1995 and 2000. The flip later had a 1993 collector compilation named after it (*Funky & Some — Some Of James Brown's Funkiest People*, Funky People FP-6901, where it opens side A) and landed as the closing track of a 2011 archival CD. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/2907844-Various-Funky-Some-Some-Of-James-Browns-Funkiest-People , https://www.discogs.com/release/3454429-The-JBs-Fred-Wesley-The-Lost-Album-Featuring-Watermelon-Man
- **"Leftovers from a lost album."** If the B-side really came from Fred Wesley's shelved 1972 jazz-album sessions, this is a jazz trombonist's abandoned dream turned into throwaway 45 filler. Use it carefully until the session date is confirmed.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This B-side may come from 1972 sessions, while its A-side was cut in January 1974. Play the two back to back. Do they sound like the same band on the same day?
- "Funky & Some" has no hook to sing along to. Pick the one element that keeps you listening for three minutes: the horn line, the guitar scratch, or the drum break that collectors prize.
- Brown credited this band as "The Last Word" instead of the J.B.'s. With your eyes closed, could you tell this from a J.B.'s single? What, if anything, gives it its own identity?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Originally the B-side of Polydor PD 14226 (US, 1974), backing "Keep On Bumpin' Before You Give Out Of Gas". Writing and arranging credits go to James Brown and Fred Wesley. Brown is the producer and Bob Both the engineer. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
- The original label carries Brown's billing "James Brown - The Hitman - The Godfather Of Soul". Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
- Lacquers were cut at Sterling Sound, with both sides' runouts etched "2-8-74" (February 8, 1974). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
- Issued in the UK and the Netherlands as Polydor 2066 429, with both sides intact. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/367088-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some ; https://www.45cat.com/record/2066429
- A 1993 US compilation of James Brown-produced side projects took its title from this track: *Funky & Some (Some Of James Brown's Funkiest People)*, Funky People FP-6901. The same label's *Nothing But Funk (12 JB Produced Funk Instrumentals 1967-1977)*, FP-6902, includes it too. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/2907844-Various-Funky-Some-Some-Of-James-Browns-Funkiest-People ; https://www.discogs.com/release/1505462-Various-Nothing-But-Funk-12-JB-Produced-Funk-Instrumentals-1967-1977
- Officially reissued in 2011 as bonus track 13 on Hip-O Select's *The Lost Album Featuring Watermelon Man*, credited to The J.B.'s & Fred Wesley rather than The Last Word. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3454429-The-JBs-Fred-Wesley-The-Lost-Album-Featuring-Watermelon-Man ; https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album/
- A fan blog calls the single "super power funk from the JB's" with "deep breaks on both cuts". Source: https://supersoulsound.com/jbsuperfan/1974-polydor-45-the-last-word-keep-on-bumping-before-you-give-out-of-gasfunky-and-some/

#### Album

- No original album. The closest thing is the 2011 *Lost Album*. It holds Fred Wesley's shelved jazz LP, which Brown offered him and then cancelled. It was recorded with New York session players including Randy and Michael Brecker, Joe Farrell, Eddie Daniels, Steve Gadd and Ron Carter. Brown plays drums on "Watermelon Man". Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album/ ; https://jambands.com/news/2011/12/08/the-jb-s-and-fred-wesley-release-lost-album/
- AllMusic gives the *Lost Album* release date as December 6, 2011 and its session date as February 1, 1972. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-lost-album-mw0002245841

#### Artist

- "The Last Word" was one of the J.B.'s many alternate billings, alongside Fred Wesley and the J.B.'s, the James Brown Soul Train, Maceo and the Macks, A.A.B.B. and The First Family. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
- The Discogs artist page lists James Brown, Fred Wesley, Pee Wee Ellis and Maceo Parker as members. The only other credited release is a 1976 Portuguese/Spanish-market Polydor EP, *Discoteca Alucinante N.º 2*. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/227924-The-Last-Word
- The A-side, "Keep On Bumpin'", was recorded 1974-01-17 at Sound Ideas, New York, about a month before Fred Wesley & the J.B.'s cut "Damn Right I Am Somebody" in the same studio (1974-02-19). Source: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb38311854k/5

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty.

1. **Classic Funk** (primary suggestion). It was issued on a major label (Polydor) in 1974, at the centre of the classic era, by Brown's own band under an alias, with Brown and Wesley writing and arranging. That is core funk vocabulary, not a fusion. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2789819-The-Last-Word-Keep-On-Bumpin-Before-You-Give-Out-Of-Gas-Funky-Some
2. **Deep Funk.** It fits the book's "commercial fate" definition. It was the B-side of a single that never charted, and its following was built afterwards by collector compilations: the 1993 Funky People LPs that named a collection after it, and German deep-funk compilations that took up its A-side (Scissor Records' *I'll Name That Tune In One — Deepfunk From The Vault*). Collectors also prize its "deep breaks". Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/2907844-Various-Funky-Some-Some-Of-James-Browns-Funkiest-People , https://www.discogs.com/release/1526506-Various-Scissor-Records-Presents-Ill-Name-That-Tune-In-One-Deepfunk-From-The-Vault , https://supersoulsound.com/jbsuperfan/1974-polydor-45-the-last-word-keep-on-bumping-before-you-give-out-of-gasfunky-and-some/
3. **Jazz Funk** (weak, conditional). Only relevant if the track is confirmed to come from Wesley's 1972 New York jazz sessions with players like Gadd and Carter. At three minutes, with a 45-style structure, it doesn't fit the book's jazz-funk traits on its own.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - New York** (written to the field). The single's documented session (the A-side) was at Sound Ideas, New York. The 2011 compilers place "Funky & Some" with the *Lost Album* sessions, which were also New York-based and used New York studio players. The lacquers were cut at Sterling Sound in NYC, and Polydor's US operation was New York-based. Sources: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb38311854k/5 , https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album/
2. **US - Southeast.** A defensible alternative if region should follow the band's home rather than the studio. Brown and the J.B.'s were based in Augusta, Georgia, where many J.B.'s sessions of the period took place (International Studio, Augusta). Source: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb38311854k/5
3. The book's other J.B.'s-family entries should use one consistent rule (studio vs. home base). The parallel "Keep On Bumpin'" entry should match whatever the author picks here.
