---
Zach Schonfeld, who wrote a book about *Ghetto: Misfortune’s Wealth*, called it "an album that nobody has heard _of_, but everybody has heard." Released on Stax’s Enterprise subsidiary late in 1973, *Ghetto* arrived just as Stax’s distribution deal with CBS was coming apart and the label was sliding toward bankruptcy. The album sold poorly, and even though 24-Carat Black toured into 1975, the band had little label support and worse finances. The final blow came when most of the group spent time in jail over an unpaid hotel bill.

The label’s troubles were not the only thing working against the record. _Ghetto_ was a funk-opera about the problems of Black poverty by Dale Warren, a conservatory-trained violinist and Motown string arranger who had orchestrated Isaac Hayes’ *Hot Buttered Soul*. Warren imagined a funky answer to the rock operas of the era, then recruited a band of teenagers from the Cincinnati area to help realize it. The result was a staged theatrical production and a 57-minute concept album. Warren refused to cut the long tracks down for radio, leaving stations with a bleak mix of singing, spoken word, string orchestration, and funk that did not fit anywhere obvious.

That same obscurity later made the album irresistible to hip-hop producers tired of sampling the same familiar funk records. Jay-Z, Nas, Dr. Dre, Naughty by Nature, Pusha-T, Kendrick Lamar, and others found material in *Ghetto*, turning a commercial failure into a secret sourcebook.

Today’s song, "24-Carat Black (Theme)," closes the album like it's music for the end credits of the stage production. After all the narration, orchestration, and heavy themes, the finale strips things down to a lean jazz-funk groove.

#### Album

- *Ghetto: Misfortune's Wealth* was released in 1973 on Enterprise (Stax subsidiary), catalog ENS-1030. Multiple sources say "late summer" or "fall" 1973; no specific street date is published — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/stax/enterprise.html and https://staxrecords.com/spotlight/24-carat-black/
- Eight thematic "synopses" / tracks, 57:26 total runtime, no edited single ever released to radio — Warren refused to cut anything for airplay — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- The sessions were tracked over three marathon weeks at Morgan Sound Theatre in Ypsilanti, Michigan, not at Stax's Memphis studio — Source: https://www.buckeyebeat.com/24caratblack.html and https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- The album is widely called "the most sampled album never heard." Hip-hop borrowers include Eric B. & Rakim ("In the Ghetto," 1990), Digable Planets, Jay-Z (with Memphis Bleek, "Can I Live II" samples "Mother's Day"), Nas, Dr. Dre, Naughty by Nature ("Poverty's Paradise"), Pusha-T, Kendrick Lamar ("Fear" on *DAMN.* uses Princess Hearn's voice from a Warren-era recording), Metro Boomin — Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html and https://www.whosampled.com/album/The-24-Carat-Black/Ghetto:-Misfortune'S-Wealth/
- Despite the sampling income, surviving members reportedly received only "three-figure royalty checks," and a 33 1/3 book by Zach Schonfeld about the album highlights the injustice — Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html and https://mocastore.org/products/1036072
- A second LP, *Gone: The Promises of Yesterday*, was compiled from 1974 Universal Studios (Chicago) sessions that sat in keyboardist Bruce Thompson's basement for 35 years before Numero Group released them in 2009. A third album, *III*, followed in 2020 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-Carat_Black

#### Artist

- The band started in 1965 as The Ditalians, a Cincinnati quartet of Sawyer Junior High School friends (Tyrone Steels, James "Sonny" Talbert, brothers Larry and Willie Cottrell), reportedly named because a founder had some Italian ancestry — Source: https://www.buckeyebeat.com/24caratblack.html
- NBA Hall of Famer Oscar Robertson, a Cincinnati basketball star, helped boost the early band's profile and provided Nashville studio connections — Source: https://staxrecords.com/spotlight/24-carat-black/
- Dale Warren (the producer/composer/arranger) was born September 27, 1943 in Detroit and died February 3, 1994 in Gwinnett County, Georgia at age 50. He was a conservatory-trained violinist, nephew of Berry Gordy's wife Raynoma, Motown staff arranger from 1961, and the arranger behind Isaac Hayes' *Hot Buttered Soul* (1969). He also conducted the Wattstax '72 Orchestra and composed the opening instrumental for the 1972 Wattstax concert — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Warren
- Warren met The Ditalians, renamed them, and married lead vocalist Princess Hearn (who joined with her sister Patricia and friend Kathleen Dent) in 1972 while rehearsals for the album were beginning. He left a previous wife to do so — Source: https://staxrecords.com/spotlight/24-carat-black/ and https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- The band's touring history included arrests for unpaid hotel bills in Henderson, Kentucky, encounters with Elvis Presley, KKK confrontations, and onstage stunts involving a casket — Source: https://333sound.com/nine-reasons-why-i-chose-to-write-a-book-about-24-carat-blacks-ghetto-misfortunes-wealth/
- After Stax's 1975 collapse, four members (William Talbert, Tyrone Steels, Ernest Lattimore, Gregory Ingram) formed Shotgun, who landed eight R&B chart hits between 1977-1982, including "Don't You Wanna Make Love" (#35 R&B, 1979) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-Carat_Black
- 24-Carat Black were inducted into the Cincinnati Black Music Walk of Fame — Source: https://www.cincyblackmusicwalkoffame.org/artists/24-carat-black

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is `Deep Funk`, which is the strongest fit. Alternatives worth considering:

- **Deep Funk (recommended).** The book defines deep funk as "less a sound than a commercial fate" — tracks overlooked on release that found their audience later through crate-diggers, DJs and dance floors. *Ghetto: Misfortune's Wealth* is the textbook case: a commercial disaster on Enterprise in 1973 that became "the most sampled album never heard." Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html
- **Jazz-Funk.** The (Theme) specifically reads more jazz-funk than the rest of the album: orchestral, long-form, soloist-friendly, light on vocal hooks — exactly the "longer track lengths, room for extended improvisation, rhythm section built to support soloists" definition the book uses. Source: https://www.funkretrospect.com/stories/24-carat-blacks-ghetto-misfortunes-wealth-the-progressive-soul-funk-concept-album (describes it as "the jazzy groover")
- **Soul Jazz.** A weaker option, but the strings + electric piano + sax soloing has soul-jazz DNA. The book's definition leans this toward earlier, smaller-ensemble work, so jazz-funk is the better second choice.

- **US - Ohio (recommended, set).** The band formed in Cincinnati in 1965 as The Ditalians at Sawyer Junior High. The Cincinnati Black Music Walk of Fame inducted them as a Cincinnati band.
