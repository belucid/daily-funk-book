### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in the entry. Two factual ambiguities surfaced during research and are flagged below.

1. **Chart peak discrepancy (R&B).** Wikipedia states the single peaked at **#67 Pop / #11 R&B**. Promotional and blog sources (Fine Gold Music, AllMusic-derived bio text quoted in search results) claim **#1 R&B / #67 Pop**. The "#1 R&B" figure appears only in promotional/secondary copy and is almost certainly inflated; the Pop peak of #67 is consistent across all sources. I could not access a primary Billboard archive page (Billboard.com returns to search only; AllMusic album pages 403 to WebFetch) to settle the R&B peak conclusively. Best current resolution: **#67 Pop, ~#11 R&B** per Wikipedia. Author should verify the R&B peak against a Joel Whitburn / Billboard R&B singles reference before printing a number.

2. **Songwriter credit discrepancy.** The Discogs 7" single master credits the writer simply as "Ripple" (band). The Discogs album master credits four named writers — **Dennis Taylor, Jack Hill, Peter Joyner, Robert Beavers** — none of whom are listed Ripple members. This may be a Discogs metadata error, a publishing/administration credit, or the actual composers. Unresolved; author may wish to check label copy / BMI-ASCAP registration.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The title that names the whole book."** The entry's body already leans on this: a 1973 record whose title — "I Don't Know What It Is, But It Sure Is Funky" — is a plainspoken thesis statement for funk itself, and for a book that keeps asking what funk is. An angle about naming the un-nameable groove.
- **"Funk financed by the Sultan of Smut."** GRC (General Recording Corporation) was the Atlanta label of Michael Thevis, a pornography magnate later convicted of murder and arson. Great music built on "highly questionable finances" — the tension between a joyful, innocent-sounding hit and the criminal empire that pressed it.
- **"The hook that outlived the song."** The "oh-la oh-la-ay" chant became folk-cultural property, resurfacing in the Electric Slide (Marcia Griffiths' "Electric Boogie") and Kid 'n Play, while the band itself faded — a study in how a four-bar phrase can outrun its authors.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The whole record is an argument by demonstration — nobody on it defines "funk," they just play it. Listen for the exact moment the groove locks in and decide: what specifically makes this "sure is funky" and not merely soul or R&B?
- Wait for the "oh-la oh-la-ay" chant. You've heard this melody your whole life via the Electric Slide. Does hearing it in its 1973 home change how it lands?
- This is a nine-piece band, not a drum machine. Track the interplay between the congas (Walter Carter) and the drum kit (Brian Sherrer) — where do the two percussionists agree, and where does one push against the other?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The single's signature "oh-la oh-la-ay" vocal line was later folded into Marcia Griffiths' "Electric Boogie (Electric Slide)" and Kid 'n Play's 1988 "Rollin' with Kid 'n Play." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_(band)
- The riff/hook was also sampled by Special Ed ("I Got It Made") and Kid 'n Play, both Billboard Top-20 acts, per the current rights holder. — Source: https://www.finegoldmusic.com/sure-is-funky
- Released as GRC 45 catalog #1004, backed with "Dance Lady Dance." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1052925-Ripple-I-Dont-Know-What-It-Is-But-It-Sure-Is-Funky-Dance-Lady-Dance
- The album cut (3:28) is tighter than the single's plug-side timing listed on Discogs (3:43); the entry uses the album version length. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/169408-Ripple-Ripple and https://www.discogs.com/master/354469-Ripple-I-Dont-Know-What-It-Is-But-It-Sure-Is-Funky-Dance-Lady-Dance

#### Album

- The self-titled LP *Ripple* (GRC GA-5005, 1973) was produced by Dee Ervin, with arrangements by Dave Ferguson, Dee Ervin and the band; engineered in part by Glenn Meadows (later a renowned Nashville mastering engineer). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/598558-Ripple-Ripple
- The single was produced by Dave Ferguson (a band member — trumpet/flugelhorn) rather than by the album's producer Dee Ervin. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1052925-Ripple-I-Dont-Know-What-It-Is-But-It-Sure-Is-Funky-Dance-Lady-Dance

#### Artist

- Ripple was an interracial nine-piece band that originated in Michigan and relocated to Atlanta; they later opened for Parliament-Funkadelic on the "Mothership Connection" tour. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_(band)
- After GRC they signed to Salsoul, releasing *Sons of the Gods* (1977) and scoring the disco-era favorite "The Beat Goes On and On." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_(band)
- GRC (General Recording Corporation) was founded in Atlanta by Michael Thevis — a pornography kingpin known as the "Sultan of Smut," later convicted of murder and arson — who financed GRC, Aware and Hotlanta Records plus the Sound Pit studio; the label folded after his arrest. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Thevis and https://dereksmusicblog.com/2017/05/21/the-rise-and-fall-of-a-musical-empire/
- Listed members: Keith Samuels (guitar, lead vocals), Simon Kenneth Carter (bass, vocals), Brian Sherrer (drums/timbales/percussion), Walter Carter (congas, percussion, vocals), Dave Ferguson (trumpet, flugelhorn, percussion), William Hull (tenor sax, flute, percussion), Curtis Reynolds (organ, piano, vibraphone, vocals), Victor Burks (keyboards, vocals), Barry Lee (guitar). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_(band) and https://www.discogs.com/release/598558-Ripple-Ripple

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (primary suggestion).** Fits the book's definition precisely: a 1973 track whose primary identity is funk (not funk fused with rock/jazz/Latin), released into the commercial pipeline (charting single, national R&B/Pop presence, PFunk tour support) during the 1969–1977 classic era. - **Deep Funk (alternate).** Weaker but arguable: despite the chart showing, Ripple became a crate-digger and sampler favorite (Electric Slide, Kid 'n Play, Special Ed), and the deeper LP cuts circulate as rare-groove. However, "delayed recognition" is the defining trait of Deep Funk, and this track was a hit on release — so Classic Funk is the better fit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_(band)
