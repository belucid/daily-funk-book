### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in the entry. One question surfaced during research and is resolved below:

- **Which Lonnie Smith is this?** This is Dr. Lonnie Smith, the Hammond B-3 organist (1942-2021), not Lonnie Liston Smith, the Richmond-born pianist (b. 1940). "Sizzle Stick" is from Dr. Lonnie Smith's 1976 Groove Merchant LP *Keep On Lovin'*. Confirmed via Discogs personnel and Wikipedia. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_On_Lovin%27
- **One naming note for the saga theme:** the LP is credited simply to "Lonnie Smith" on the sleeve (the "Dr." was not yet a fixture on his Groove Merchant covers), which is exactly the kind of detail that fed decades of mix-ups with Lonnie Liston Smith. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/849554-Lonnie-Smith-Keep-On-Lovin

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The entry's existing `writing theme` is "saga of Lonnie Smith and Lonnie Liston Smith." Noted and kept in mind. Suggestions:

- **Two Lonnie Smiths, one decade of confusion.** Dr. Lonnie Smith (organ) and Lonnie Liston Smith (piano) were both Black jazz keyboardists making funk-leaning fusion records in the mid-1970s, with near-identical names. This track is a perfect hook for untangling them, especially since the sleeve says only "Lonnie Smith." Opens a friendly "here's how to tell them apart" angle.
- **The organist who unplugged the organ.** The B-3 was Dr. Lonnie Smith's signature, yet on *Keep On Lovin'* he sets it aside for the Fender Rhodes and a glossy fusion band. A theme about an artist chasing the 1970s commercial sound, and whether the gamble paid off.
- **A young Joe Lovano in the horn section.** Before he was a jazz titan, Lovano was an anonymous section player blowing tenor on a funk date. A "spot the future legend in the credits" angle.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Sizzle Stick" was written by guitarist Lance Quinn and arranger/conductor Brad Baker, not by Smith himself; runs 5:57. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_On_Lovin%27
- The track was paired with "Filet-O-Sole" on a 2018 reissue 7" single, a sign of its later crate-digger/DJ appeal. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/12776108-Lonnie-Smith-Sizzle-Stick-Filet-O-sole

#### Album
- On *Keep On Lovin'* Dr. Lonnie Smith abandons his trademark Hammond B-3 entirely for Fender Rhodes and keyboards, fully embracing 1970s fusion. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/keep-on-lovin-mw0000853605 (AllMusic review noting he "forsakes his signature Hammond B-3 for the Fender Rhodes")
- The horn section includes a young Joe Lovano on tenor sax, roughly a decade before his first album as a leader, alongside Brewer-Brecker-circle session aces Randy Brecker (trumpet) and Barry Rogers (trombone). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_On_Lovin%27
- Bass duties go to Motown Funk Brother Bob Babbitt (who also wrote "Filet-O-Sole") and a young Will Lee. Recorded at Mediasound, NYC, August 1976, produced by Groove Merchant founder Sonny Lester. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/849554-Lonnie-Smith-Keep-On-Lovin
- The LP was distributed by PIP Records, a division of Pickwick International. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/849554-Lonnie-Smith-Keep-On-Lovin

#### Artist
- Dr. Lonnie Smith was born July 3, 1942, in Lackawanna, NY (a Buffalo suburb), and died September 28, 2021, in Fort Lauderdale, FL, of pulmonary fibrosis, age 79. Source: https://www.npr.org/2021/09/28/1041372956/dr-lonnie-smith-master-of-the-hammond-organ-dies-at-79
- He rose to notice in the 1960s in George Benson's quartet, then recorded for Blue Note; he was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2017. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Lonnie_Smith
- Not to be confused with pianist Lonnie Liston Smith (b. December 28, 1940, Richmond, VA), leader of the Cosmic Echoes, who played with Pharoah Sanders and Miles Davis. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonnie_Liston_Smith
