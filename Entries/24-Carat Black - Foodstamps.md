### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is currently empty. Candidates:

- **"The five-second loop that paid for the album."** "Foodstamps" supplied the stop-start drum break that Digable Planets dropped into "Rebirth of Slick (Cool Like Dat)" — a Top-15 Billboard hit and a 1994 Grammy winner for Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group. Frame the entry as the most cosmically inverted economy in funk: a track buried on a 1973 commercial flop becomes a 1993 Grammy-bait hook, and the players who recorded it (per Schonfeld's book) saw only "three-figure royalty checks." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebirth_of_Slick_(Cool_Like_Dat) and https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html
- **"The drummer who built jazz-rap."** Make Tyrone Steels the protagonist. He started as a Sawyer Junior High School friend in The Ditalians in 1965, played percussion on *Ghetto*, then went on to form Shotgun with four other 24-Carat Black alumni and score eight R&B chart hits. The "Foodstamps" break is his uncredited contribution to the entire 1990s jazz-rap moment. Source: https://staxrecords.com/spotlight/24-carat-black/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotgun_(funk_band)
- **"The instrumental amid the opera."** *Ghetto* is a narrated, sung concept album about Black poverty — and yet "Foodstamps," the song whose title most directly names the album's subject matter, is a brassy, highly funky instrumental with no vocal storytelling at all. Lean into what it means that the most political-sounding title on a political record refuses to use words.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Foodstamps" is track 6 of 8 on *Ghetto: Misfortune's Wealth*, running 6:27 on the Bandcamp listing (6:26 per WeFunk's DJ database) — a highly funky instrumental built around a brass arrangement and a rhythm-section pocket. Source: https://24-caratblack.bandcamp.com/album/ghetto-misfortunes-wealth and https://www.wefunkradio.com/song/1289_cv/24-carat+black/foodstamps
- The track's drum break — a stop-start solo by 24-Carat Black percussionist Tyrone Steels — was lifted "virtually unchanged" by Digable Planets and used as the bridge in "Rebirth of Slick (Cool Like Dat)," which won Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group at the 36th Grammy Awards (1994). Source: https://staxrecords.com/spotlight/24-carat-black/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebirth_of_Slick_(Cool_Like_Dat)
- Discrepancy: Wikipedia's "Rebirth of Slick" article credits only Art Blakey's "Stretching" as a sample; WhoSampled, Riot Fest, Stax Records' own site, and multiple music-press sources document the "Foodstamps" break as the source of the famous bridge. The Stax and WhoSampled lineage is the consistent one. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/2657/Digable-Planets-Rebirth-of-Slick-(Cool-Like-Dat)-The-24-Carat-Black-Foodstamps/ and https://riotfest.org/2018/08/17/digable-planets-reachin-samples/
- Beyond Digable Planets, WhoSampled documents at least three other tracks that sample "Foodstamps": Psyche Origami's "Wharf Song," Homeboy Sandman's "Name," and others on the WhoSampled list — small but enduring afterlife. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-24-Carat-Black/Foodstamps/sampled/
- Writer credit listed as Dale Warren / The 24-Carat Black. Source: https://www.wefunkradio.com/song/1289_cv/24-carat+black/foodstamps
- The track has been re-extracted by beat-makers as a standalone drum break. There are dedicated YouTube uploads titled "Foodstamps Drum Break HQ .Wav" and "Foodstamps (Drum Break - Loop)" specifically packaged for sample use. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mm0xCGOkhHY and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ERHAIz5GZ8c

#### Album

- *Ghetto: Misfortune's Wealth* was released on Enterprise (Stax subsidiary), catalog ENS-1030, in 1973. BSN Pubs' Enterprise discography and Stax Records both date it to 1973 without a specific street day; FUNKretrospect specifies "fall of 1973"; some streaming services default to "January 1, 1973" but that is platform-default behavior for year-only metadata. Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/stax/enterprise.html and https://www.funkretrospect.com/stories/24-carat-blacks-ghetto-misfortunes-wealth-the-progressive-soul-funk-concept-album
- Eight tracks / "synopses," 57:26 total runtime, recorded over three marathon weeks at Morgan Sound Theatre in Ypsilanti, Michigan — not Stax Memphis. Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood and https://www.buckeyebeat.com/24caratblack.html
- The album was a commercial failure and Stax never edited a radio single from it — Warren refused. Despite this, the album became "the most sampled album never heard," with credits including Jay-Z, Nas, Dr. Dre, Kendrick Lamar ("Fear" on *DAMN.*), Eric B. & Rakim, Naughty by Nature, Pusha-T, and Digable Planets. Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html and https://www.whosampled.com/album/The-24-Carat-Black/Ghetto:-Misfortune'S-Wealth/
- A book-length treatment of the album by Zach Schonfeld appeared in 2020 in Bloomsbury's 33 1/3 series, documenting the band's exploitation and the album's hip-hop afterlife. Source: https://333sound.com/nine-reasons-why-i-chose-to-write-a-book-about-24-carat-blacks-ghetto-misfortunes-wealth/

#### Artist

- 24-Carat Black began as The Ditalians, a 1965 Cincinnati quartet of Sawyer Junior High School friends including Tyrone Steels (the drummer behind the "Foodstamps" break). Source: https://www.buckeyebeat.com/24caratblack.html
- After Stax's 1975 collapse, Steels, William Talbert, Ernest Lattimore, Larry Austin, Gregory Ingram, and William Gentry formed Shotgun, which landed eight R&B chart hits between 1977 and 1982, signed to ABC Records. Steels co-wrote much of the catalog. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotgun_(funk_band) and https://www.feenotes.com/database/artists/steels-tyrone/
- Producer Dale Warren (b. September 27, 1943, Detroit; d. February 3, 1994, Gwinnett County GA) was a conservatory-trained violinist, Motown staff arranger from 1961, nephew of Berry Gordy's first wife Raynoma, and the orchestrator of Isaac Hayes' *Hot Buttered Soul*. He conducted the Wattstax '72 Orchestra. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Warren
- Warren left his previous wife to marry 24-Carat Black lead vocalist Princess Hearn in 1972, during rehearsals for the album. Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- 24-Carat Black were inducted into the Cincinnati Black Music Walk of Fame. Source: https://www.cincyblackmusicwalkoffame.org/artists/24-carat-black

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is `Deep Funk`, which is the strongest single label. Alternatives:

- **Deep Funk (recommended, current).** The book defines deep funk as "less a sound than a commercial fate" — tracks overlooked on release that found their audience later via crate-digging and sampling. "Foodstamps" is the canonical example: a commercial flop in 1973, then resurrected as a Grammy-winning hip-hop break in the 1990s, then re-extracted as a standalone drum break for further sampling. Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html
- **Classic Funk.** As a 1973 release on a major-label subsidiary (Enterprise/Stax) by a touring band, it sits inside the 1969-1977 classic-funk window, and the track's primary identity is funk (not jazz-funk or soul). The only reason classic funk is not the strongest label is the album's commercial failure — the "delayed recognition" trait of deep funk is what wins.
- **Jazz-Funk.** Weaker for "Foodstamps" specifically than for "(Theme)." "Foodstamps" is shorter, more rhythm-section-forward, and less about extended improvisation. The brass is arranged rather than soloed.
