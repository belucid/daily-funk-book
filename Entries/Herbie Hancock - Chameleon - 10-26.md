Herbie Hancock’s Mwandishi band opened for the Pointer Sisters at the Troubadour in LA in the summer of 1973. It did not go well. By October 26 of the same year, he was releasing a new album with a new band.

In his autobiography *Possibilities*, Hancock recalls watching the crowd go wild for the Pointer Sisters’ funky sound while barely tolerating his own avant-garde jazz. The experience was humbling. Hancock’s response was to form a new band and head in a new direction.

The working title of today’s track was “Caesar Salad,” but the final title, “Chameleon,” became a metaphor for Hancock himself. He's an artist who never stopped changing colors. He was a classically trained pianist as a child. After his early Blue Note records, Miles Davis brought him into the second great quintet, where he played acoustic piano in one of the most important bands in jazz. He later pushed into the avant-garde with Mwandishi, using analog synthesizers to expand the sound of jazz. After the Troubadour wake-up call, he helped bring jazz-funk into the commercial mainstream with his new band, The Headhunters. A decade later, he changed colors again with the electronic smash hit “Rockit.”

### Open Research Questions

When did Mwandishi open for the Pointer Sisters at the Troubadour in LA?

**Resolution: best estimate is summer 1973; exact calendar date not nailed down from web sources.** The most-cited primary essay (Bob Belden's "Chameleon: The Life and Music of Herbie Hancock," hosted on Hancock's official site) places the show in the "summer of 1973." Other sources confirm it happened after Sextant's release (March 30, 1973) and during the chart run of "Yes We Can Can" (the Pointer Sisters single broke that summer), narrowing the window to roughly June-August 1973. A Newsweek piece by Maureen Orth dated August 20, 1973 references the Pointers' breakthrough Troubadour engagement as having occurred "three months ago" (around May 1973), but the Mwandishi opening slot may have been a separate later booking. The Hollywood Bowl's "Head Hunters at 50" essay also says only "summer of 1973." Bob Gluck's 2012 book *You'll Know When You Get There: Herbie Hancock and the Mwandishi Band* (University of Chicago Press) is the most thorough scholarly treatment of the band and the most likely place to find a precise date, but I could not access its full text via the web. ### Writing Theme Suggestions

Current writing theme: **"Jazz artists 'selling out'"**

This is a strong theme and well supported by the research. Here are some alternatives:

1. **"The bassline that was almost a Caesar Salad"** — The track's working title was "Caesar Salad," per producer David Rubinson. The name change to "Chameleon" became a metaphor for Hancock himself, an artist who kept adapting. This angle lets you open with a fun, disarming fact and then pivot into Hancock's shape-shifting career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)

2. **"When a synthesizer replaced the bass player"** — The iconic 12-note bassline was played on an ARP Odyssey synth, not a bass guitar. This was one of the first times a synthesizer served as the bass instrument in a funk/jazz context, and it helped launch a revolution in how funk bands thought about the low end. The angle gives you a way to talk about technology, groove, and the strange alchemy of making a machine feel funky. — Source: https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2015/04/21/the-iconic-sounds-of-synthesis-herbie-hancocks-chameleon-bassline/

3. **"The Pointer Sisters made him do it"** — Hancock's Mwandishi band opened for the Pointer Sisters at the Troubadour in LA and watched the crowd go wild for them while barely responding to his avant-garde jazz. The experience was a turning point that led directly to Head Hunters. The angle frames Chameleon as the product of a humbling night, not a cynical calculation. — Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/2017/01/08/essay-chameleon-the-life-and-music-of-herbie-hancock/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

No listening mission currently set.

1. **"The bassline is a synthesizer, not a bass guitar. Can you hear it? Listen to the texture of those low notes and ask yourself: does it groove harder because it's a machine, or in spite of it?"** — Draws attention to the ARP Odyssey bassline, which many listeners assume is a bass guitar. Invites a judgment call about feel and technology.

2. **"Chameleon runs nearly sixteen minutes. Where does the composition end and the jam begin? Try to find the seam."** — Invites structural listening in a track that blends tight composition with extended improvisation, a hallmark of jazz-funk.

3. **"Hancock solos twice: once on the ARP Odyssey synth, once on the Rhodes piano. Which solo hits you harder, and what does that say about the instrument or the player?"** — Focuses attention on two distinct solo sections and invites comparison between the two keyboard voices.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The working title for "Chameleon" was "Caesar Salad," changed before release. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)
- The signature 12-note bassline was played by Hancock on an ARP Odyssey synthesizer, not by bassist Paul Jackson on bass guitar. This was one of the earliest prominent uses of a synth as a bass instrument in funk/jazz. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)
- The track is built on a two-chord vamp: i-IV in Bb Dorian (Bbm7 and Eb7). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)
- The single edit reached #42 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #18 on the Hot Soul Singles chart in 1974, a remarkable feat for a 15-minute jazz-funk instrumental. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)
- "Chameleon" has been sampled over fifty times in hip-hop, with early samples by Spoonie Gee (1987), and later by 2Pac, DJ Quik, Beck, Organized Konfusion, Kool G Rap, and Digital Underground. — Source: https://kileylarsen.substack.com/p/50-years-ago-herbie-hancocks-funk ; https://www.whosampled.com/Herbie-Hancock/Chameleon/
- The track has become standard repertoire in small jazz ensembles worldwide, with notable covers by Maceo Parker, Buddy Rich, Stanley Jordan, Gov't Mule, and Umphrey's McGee. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon_(composition)

#### Album
- Head Hunters became the first jazz album to be certified platinum, and was the biggest-selling jazz album of all time until George Benson's Breezin' surpassed it in 1976. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters
- The album went gold (500,000 copies) within six months of release, a feat that took Miles Davis's Kind of Blue 35 years to achieve. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters
- Head Hunters was inducted into the Library of Congress National Recording Registry in 2007 as "culturally, historically or aesthetically important." — Source: https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/head-hunters.pdf
- The album received a perfect 10/10 from Pitchfork and 5/5 from AllMusic, DownBeat, and Jazzwise. Rolling Stone ranked it #254 on their 2020 Greatest Albums list. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters
- The album cover was designed by Victor Moscoso, featuring Hancock wearing a mask based on Baoulé cultural designs from Côte d'Ivoire. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters
- Black college radio, particularly Howard University's station, was instrumental in breaking the album to a wider audience. — Source: https://kileylarsen.substack.com/p/50-years-ago-herbie-hancocks-funk
- Recording took place August-September 1973 at Wally Heider Studios and Different Fur Trading Co. in San Francisco, not in New York or Los Angeles. — Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/head-hunters/
- Harvey Mason was a session drummer too busy to tour, so Mike Clark replaced him for live performances and all subsequent Headhunters recordings. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Headhunters

#### Artist
- Herbie Hancock performed Mozart's Piano Concerto No. 26 with the Chicago Symphony Orchestra at age 11 (February 5, 1952). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- Hancock graduated from Grinnell College in Iowa with degrees in both electrical engineering and music (1960). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- The epiphany that led to Head Hunters came from opening for the Pointer Sisters at the Troubadour in West Hollywood. Hancock watched the crowd go wild for them and realized his avant-garde Mwandishi band had lost the ability to connect with audiences. He later wrote that his discomfort revealed "jazz snobbery, where funk was somehow lower on the food chain." — Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/2017/01/08/essay-chameleon-the-life-and-music-of-herbie-hancock/
- Hancock became a practitioner of Nichiren Buddhism with Soka Gakkai International in 1972, one year before recording Head Hunters. He has cited the practice as central to his creative reinventions. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- His sister Jean was killed in the crash of Delta Air Lines Flight 191 in 1985. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- Hancock disclosed a past crack cocaine addiction in the 1990s in his 2014 memoir Possibilities. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- David Rubinson, the album's co-producer, was the one who recommended Paul Jackson as bassist for the new band. — Source: https://jazzjournal.co.uk/2019/12/09/groove-master-paul-jackson/

### Genre Suggestions

Current genre: **Jazz-Funk**

1. **Jazz-Funk (recommended, keep current value)** — This is the textbook definition of jazz-funk per the book's genre descriptions: funk played by jazz musicians whose earlier careers were in traditional jazz, with longer track lengths, room for extended improvisation, and a rhythm section built to support soloists. Hancock came from hard bop and Miles Davis's quintet, and Chameleon features a 15-minute runtime with extended solos over a funk groove. Every major source classifies this as jazz-funk or jazz fusion. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/head-hunters-mw0000649551

2. **Classic Funk** — A secondary case could be made. The single edit charted on the Hot 100 and Hot Soul Singles, it was played in dance clubs, and the groove is as deep and funky as anything from the classic funk era (1968-1977). However, the extended runtime, improvisational structure, and Hancock's jazz pedigree make Jazz-Funk the stronger fit.

3. **Psychedelic Funk** — A weaker case. The synthesizer textures and 15-minute voyage have a psychedelic quality, and the album cover features African mask imagery. But the track lacks the surreal lyrics, alter egos, and hallucinatory storytelling that define psychedelic funk in this book.
