### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Covering the boss's little sister."** This "You're The One" is not a McGriff original. Sly Stone wrote and produced it as the debut single for Little Sister, a vocal trio led by his younger sister Vet Stewart, on his new Stone Flower label. It reached #4 R&B and #22 pop in early 1970. Within months a Philadelphia organist was cutting it as an instrumental. The angle is how fast Sly's songbook became the funk standard repertoire, even for his family's side projects.
2. **"Organ jazz chases the jukebox."** *Soul Sugar* is almost all fresh hits: the Archies' "Sugar, Sugar", James Brown's "Ain't It Funky Now", Stevie Wonder's "Signed, Sealed, Delivered", Aretha's "Spirit in the Dark", and Sly's song. Producer Sonny Lester was known for commissioning arrangements of recent pop hits for McGriff. The theme is the soul-jazz business model in 1970, and whether it produced art anyway. AllMusic's Ankeny says this is a contender for McGriff's funkiest record.
3. **"From beat cop to the Hammond."** McGriff was a military policeman in Korea and then a Philadelphia police officer before he turned to the organ full time. The theme follows the man rather than the song: the working-class, church-rooted "blues organist" (his own term) moving into funk.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Little Sister's original is a vocal call-and-response built around a chanted hook. Listen for how McGriff's band rebuilds that vocal hook for instruments. Who sings the "words": the horns, the organ, or the guitar?
2. Ignore the organ for the first minute and listen only to Marion Booker's drums and Richard Davis's electric bass. Does this rhythm section sound like a jazz band playing funk, or a funk band with a jazz organist sitting in?
3. Play Little Sister's "You're The One (Part I)" and then McGriff's version back to back. Which one feels more like a party, and what does the organ add or take away that the voices can't?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Written by Sylvester Stewart (Sly Stone). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/35332-Jimmy-McGriff-Soul-Sugar
- The original was the debut single by Little Sister (Stone Flower 45-9000, 1970), written and produced by Sly Stone and issued in two parts. Atlantic distributed Stone Flower. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_the_One_(Little_Sister_song); https://www.discogs.com/master/140487-Little-Sister-Youre-The-One
- Little Sister's original reached #22 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #4 on Billboard's Best Selling Soul Singles. It debuted on the Hot 100 at #86 on Feb. 28, 1970. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_the_One_(Little_Sister_song)
- The Three Degrees also covered the song, as a Roulette single (R-7097, produced by Richard Barrett), which *Record World* made a "Pick of the Week" on Dec. 26, 1970. The song was being covered across soul, girl-group and organ jazz within a year. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_the_One_(Little_Sister_song)
- McGriff's version is the shortest kind of soul-jazz cover (about 3 minutes, B2 on the LP). It is a radio-length cut, not a jam vehicle. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/35332-Jimmy-McGriff-Soul-Sugar

#### Album

- *Soul Sugar* (Capitol ST-616) was produced by Sonny Lester, engineered by Malcolm Addey, and recorded at Bell Sound Studios, New York. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/573313-Jimmy-McGriff-Soul-Sugar
- Personnel: McGriff (organ), Murray Watson (trumpet), Cliff Davis (tenor sax, flute), Johnny Beard/Board (baritone sax), Horace Ott (electric piano), Larry Frazier (guitar), Richard Davis (bass), Marion J. Booker (drums), Lawrence Killian (congas, tambourine). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Sugar; https://www.freshsoundrecords.com/jimmy-mcgriff-albums/4699-soul-sugar-digipack-edition.html
- Bassist Richard Davis also played on Eric Dolphy's *Out to Lunch!* and Van Morrison's *Astral Weeks*. The same bassist moved between avant-garde jazz, folk-rock mysticism and McGriff's Capitol funk. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Davis_(bassist)
- jazz.com describes the band as driven by Marion Booker's "funk drumming" and Richard Davis's "dry, busy, and pocketed electric bass". — Source: https://www.jazz.com/encyclopedia/mcgriff-jimmy-james-harrell
- AllMusic (Jason Ankeny, 4/5) says *Soul Sugar* "looms large in Jimmy McGriff's vast catalog" and "demands serious consideration" as his funkiest record. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Sugar (quoting AllMusic)
- The original liner notes said the album brought McGriff "into the jazz-rock scene with a new band" and that it "will leave you begging for more long after you've worn it out." — Source: https://www.freshsoundrecords.com/jimmy-mcgriff-albums/4699-soul-sugar-digipack-edition.html
- Blue Note's bio calls *Soul Sugar* "a similar outing" to *Electric Funk*, McGriff's early jazz-funk record with arranger/electric pianist Horace Ott, who is also on *Soul Sugar*. — Source: https://www.bluenote.com/?p=1611
- The album was not issued on CD until a 2007 Groove Hut digipak, paired with *Groove Grease*. — Source: https://www.freshsoundrecords.com/jimmy-mcgriff-albums/4699-soul-sugar-digipack-edition.html; https://www.discogs.com/master/2376292

#### Artist

- James Harrell McGriff was born April 3, 1936, in Germantown, Philadelphia, and died May 24, 2008, in Voorhees Township, NJ, of complications of multiple sclerosis. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff; https://inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20080528_Jazzman_Jimmy_McGriff__72__soulful_master_of_the_organ.html
- He was a military policeman in the Korean War and then a Philadelphia police officer for about two to three years before committing to music. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff; https://www.jazz.com/encyclopedia/mcgriff-jimmy-james-harrell
- His cousins included saxophonist Benny Golson and soul singer Harold Melvin. Seeing Richard "Groove" Holmes in Camden, NJ, inspired him to take up the Hammond. — Source: https://www.bluenote.com/?p=1611
- Jimmy Smith was a childhood friend. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff
- His 1962 instrumental "I've Got a Woman" (Sue) reached #20 on the Billboard Hot 100. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff
- He called himself a "blues organist" and said his playing came from the church. He released more than 80 albums. — Source: https://www.jazz.com/encyclopedia/mcgriff-jimmy-james-harrell
- He briefly owned The Golden Slipper club in Newark in the late 1960s/early 1970s, where *Black Pearl* was recorded live. — Source: https://www.jazz.com/encyclopedia/mcgriff-jimmy-james-harrell
- In 1970 alone he released *Electric Funk* (Blue Note), *The Dudes Doin' Business* (Capitol, with Junior Parker) and *Soul Sugar* (Capitol). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: Soul Jazz.

1. **Soul Jazz (keep).** McGriff is a defining organ soul-jazz player, and the book defines soul jazz as organ-led groups bridging hard bop and funk. Discogs tags the album "Soul-Jazz". — https://www.discogs.com/master/35332-Jimmy-McGriff-Soul-Sugar
2. **Jazz Funk (strong alternative).** Discogs also tags it "Jazz-Funk". The book defines jazz-funk as "bigger bands, denser arrangements, and a more overtly funk-forward rhythm section". *Soul Sugar* is a nine-piece band with horns, electric piano, guitar and congas, a Sly Stone/James Brown repertoire, funk drumming (jazz.com), and liner notes pitching McGriff "into the jazz-rock scene with a new band". Against this, the tracks are short, radio-length covers rather than extended improvisations, which the book counts as a jazz-funk trait. — https://www.jazz.com/encyclopedia/mcgriff-jimmy-james-harrell; https://www.freshsoundrecords.com/jimmy-mcgriff-albums/4699-soul-sugar-digipack-edition.html
3. **Deep Funk (weak).** The album was out of print on CD until 2007 and is collected by funk diggers. It came from a major label (Capitol), though, and there is no evidence of delayed rediscovery through samples, so this fits poorly.

My recommendation: keep Soul Jazz if the book needs organ-trio-lineage entries. Jazz Funk is arguably the more accurate description of this particular band and sound.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - Northeast (set).** McGriff was born in Germantown, Philadelphia, worked the Philadelphia/Camden/Trenton/Newark organ circuit, and lived in Voorhees, NJ. This is the artist's regional identity. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGriff; https://www.bluenote.com/?p=1611
2. **US - New York.** The session was recorded at Bell Sound Studios in New York City with New York session players (Richard Davis, Horace Ott), produced by NYC-based Sonny Lester. This is defensible if region follows the recording location. — https://www.discogs.com/release/573313-Jimmy-McGriff-Soul-Sugar
3. **US - California (weak).** The song was written and first recorded in the Bay Area/LA Sly Stone orbit. That supports a footnote, not the tag.
