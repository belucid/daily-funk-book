### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items. The research raised these questions, which are still unresolved:

- **Who actually plays on "Use Me"?** Most of the album was cut with New York session players (the Breckers, Joe Farrell, Eddie Daniels, Steve Gadd, Ron Carter). The press coverage names "Watermelon Man" as the only true J.B.'s track ([The Second Disc](https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album), [jambands.com](https://jambands.com/news/2011/12/08/the-jb-s-and-fred-wesley-release-lost-album/)). However, Spotify, Apple Music and Deezer credit "Use Me" to **The J.B.'s** alone while the neighboring tracks are credited to "The JB's and Fred Wesley." The 1972 French single also credits only "The J.B's" ([Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/1479296-The-JBs-Use-Me)). Ian McCann's *Record Collector* review says the funk intensifies "when The JBs appear" on "JB Shout" and "Use Me" ([Record Collector](https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/the-lost-album-featuringwatermelon-man)). This suggests "Use Me" may be a J.B.'s band recording and not one of the New York session tracks. Steps taken: Discogs release credits (no personnel listed per track), Second Disc, jambands, Apple, Record Collector, and a search of Fred Wesley's blog (the post at funkyfredwesley.com/2011/12/lost-album-found-and-on-sale-now.html now returns 404). **Suggested next step:** check the 12-page booklet of the Hip-O Select CD (compiled by Alan Leeds and Harry Weinger), or Wesley's memoir *Hit Me, Fred* (Duke University Press, 2002).
- **When was "Use Me" recorded?** AllMusic gives a single session date of Feb 1, 1972 at A&R Studios. But Bill Withers' original appeared on *Still Bill* in 1972 and was released as a single in August/September 1972 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_Me_(Bill_Withers_song))). "Get On the Good Foot" and "Everybody Plays the Fool" are also mid-to-late-1972 hits. A Feb 1, 1972 date for every track is therefore unlikely. The sessions probably ran over several months, ending before the LP test pressing (Discogs "released" 1972-11-16). Unresolved.
- **Was People PE 616 ever pressed in the US?** The Second Disc's track notes say tracks 7 and 9 came out on People PE 616 in 1972. A reader comment there disputes it, and Discogs describes the French Polydor single as the "French picture sleeve release of otherwise unissued People PE 616" ([Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/1479296-The-JBs-Use-Me)). Discogs' only PE 616 entries are 2006 unofficial "Blow Your Head / Mindpower" pressings ([Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/master/1065531-Fred-Wesley-The-JBs-James-Brown-Blow-Your-Head-Mindpower)). 45cat (which would settle it) returns 403.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is empty.

1. **"The album James Brown wouldn't let out."** Wesley's first real album under his own name was cut, test-pressed and then shelved at Brown's own label. "Use Me" escaped only on a French 45 and waited 39 years for a US release. The song's title gives the hook: what it meant to be Brown's indispensable, underpaid, overused sideman.
2. **"Two July 4th babies."** Fred Wesley (b. July 4, 1943) and Bill Withers (b. July 4, 1938) share a birthday. The entry could frame the cover as a dialogue between two Southern-born, self-made musicians: one a sideman, one a reluctant star.
3. **"Covering the hit while it's still hot."** Withers' "Use Me" was at No. 2 on both the Hot 100 and the soul chart in fall 1972, and the J.B.'s covered it almost immediately. This opens a look at how the J.B.'s and Brown's operation turned current radio hits ("Back Stabbers," "Everybody Plays the Fool," "Alone Again (Naturally)") into horn-driven funk instrumentals.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

The `listening mission` field is empty.

1. Withers' original is built on Ray Jackson's clavinet. Listen for what takes over the clavinet's job in the J.B.'s version: is it the guitar, the bass or the horns?
2. This is an instrumental cover of a song famous for its lyric. Listen to how the horn section "sings" the melody. Which phrase most clearly stands in for Withers' voice?
3. Play this right after "Sweet Loneliness" or "Secret Love" from the same album. Can you hear the shift from the New York session-player jazz tracks to the J.B.'s funk band?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Use Me" was never issued in the US in 1972. Its only contemporary release was a French Polydor picture-sleeve 7" (2066 287), backed with "Everybody Plays The Fool." Discogs describes it as the release "of otherwise unissued People PE 616." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1479296-The-JBs-Use-Me
- The French single credits David Matthews as arranger and James Brown as producer. It lists "Use Me" at 3:43. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1479296-The-JBs-Use-Me
- The arranger David Matthews (b. March 4, 1942, Sonora, Kentucky) was Brown's arranger/bandleader from 1970. He later became a CTI staff arranger for George Benson, Grover Washington Jr. and Esther Phillips, and leader of the Manhattan Jazz Quintet. He is not Dave Matthews of the Dave Matthews Band. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Matthews_(keyboardist)
- Streaming services credit "Use Me" to "The J.B.'s" alone. Every other track on the album is credited to "The JB's and Fred Wesley" or "Fred Wesley & The J.B.'s." — Source: https://open.spotify.com/track/6jfGopqie2ZA79psyiSzWc ; https://music.apple.com/us/album/use-me/1444035009?i=1444035881
- *Record Collector*'s Ian McCann singled out "Use Me" and "JB Shout" as the moments when the J.B.'s appear and "the funk intensifies." — Source: https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/the-lost-album-featuringwatermelon-man
- Bill Withers' original peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100. Michael Jackson's "Ben" and Chuck Berry's "My Ding-a-Ling" kept it from No. 1. It also reached No. 2 on the soul chart and went gold. Wikipedia's list of covers includes Fred Wesley & The J.B.'s alongside Grace Jones, Mick Jagger, Isaac Hayes, D'Angelo and Esther Phillips. Its drum break was sampled by Kendrick Lamar ("Sing About Me, I'm Dying of Thirst"). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_Me_(Bill_Withers_song)
- The B-side "Everybody Plays the Fool" was a fresh 1972 hit too: The Main Ingredient's version reached No. 3 on the Hot 100 that fall. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody_Plays_the_Fool

#### Album

- The album was Wesley's "true solo album" under his own name. It was "consigned to the shelves" at Brown's People Records, and only a couple of singles leaked out. It was finally issued by Hip-O Select in 2011 with six tracks never heard before. — Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album
- Despite the J.B.'s billing, most of the album features New York studio players: Randy and Michael Brecker, Joe Farrell, Eddie Daniels, Steve Gadd and Ron Carter. James Brown himself plays drums on "Watermelon Man." — Source: https://jambands.com/news/2011/12/08/the-jb-s-and-fred-wesley-release-lost-album/
- A People Records LP test pressing (PE 5603) exists, with the same nine-track running order as the 2011 CD's original album. Discogs lists its date as 1972-11-16. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8494316-Fred-Wesley-The-JBs-Untitled
- "Seulb" is "blues" spelled backwards. The reviewer compares the jazz tracks to CTI and Mainstream records of the era. — Source: https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/the-lost-album-featuringwatermelon-man
- Apple Music says the session was "shelved shortly after recording." It highlights Wesley's jazz arrangements of pop hits such as "You've Got a Friend," "Everybody Plays the Fool" and "Back Stabbers." — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/the-lost-album/1444035009
- The 2011 CD was compiled by Alan Leeds and Harry Weinger and mastered by Seth Foster at Sterling Sound. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3454429-The-JBs-Fred-Wesley-The-Lost-Album-Featuring-Watermelon-Man

#### Artist

- Fred Wesley was born July 4, 1943 in Columbus, Georgia, and raised in Mobile, Alabama. He switched from trumpet to trombone around age 12. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley
- Wesley rejoined Brown in December 1970 to lead the newly formed J.B.'s. The group had come together in March 1970 after Brown's previous band walked out over pay. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
- "Doing It to Death" (1973), credited to Fred Wesley & the J.B.'s, was a No. 1 R&B hit and went gold. That was roughly a year after this shelved album. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley ; https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album
- After leaving Brown in 1975, Wesley led the Horny Horns for Parliament-Funkadelic. He joined the Count Basie Orchestra in 1978 and wrote the memoir *Hit Me, Fred: Recollections of a Sideman* (2002). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is empty.

1. **Jazz Funk (primary suggestion).** The album was conceived as Wesley's jazz record. It was arranged by David Matthews, later a CTI staff arranger, and mostly cut with jazz players (the Breckers, Ron Carter, Joe Farrell). *Record Collector* likens it to CTI and Mainstream releases. The book defines jazz-funk by the players, the musical traits and the era, and this record fits all three. Sources: https://jambands.com/news/2011/12/08/the-jb-s-and-fred-wesley-release-lost-album/ ; https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/the-lost-album-featuringwatermelon-man
2. **Classic Funk.** If "Use Me" is indeed a J.B.'s band recording, as the streaming credits and McCann's review suggest, it sits squarely in the 1969–77 James Brown funk machine. It is a short, radio-length cover of a current pop hit, produced by Brown. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1479296-The-JBs-Use-Me
3. **Deep Funk.** The book defines deep funk by "delayed recognition." That fits a track that was unissued in the US, appeared only on a French 45, and reached most listeners via a 2011 archival release. It is weaker because the J.B.'s were a major-label act and this was not a crate-digger find. Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2011/11/07/hip-o-select-gets-on-the-good-foot-with-lost-fred-wesley-album

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - New York (set).** AllMusic places the sessions at A&R Studios, New York. The album's players were New York studio musicians, and People Records was run from Brown's New York operation. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-lost-album-mw0002245841 (via search snippet); https://jambands.com/news/2011/12/08/the-jb-s-and-fred-wesley-release-lost-album/
2. **US - Southeast.** Fred Wesley was born in Columbus, Georgia and raised in Mobile, Alabama. James Brown's base was Augusta, Georgia. This is a fair choice if the author weights the artist's roots over the recording location. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley
3. **US - Ohio.** Wikipedia gives the J.B.'s origin as Cincinnati, Ohio (King Records' home), where the band formed in 1970. It is weaker for a 1972 New York-made record. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
