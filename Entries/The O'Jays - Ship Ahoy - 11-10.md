---
Alex Haley’s *Roots* was not published until 1976. Three years earlier, the O’Jays put listeners on a slave ship with “Ship Ahoy,” trying to convey the Middle Passage in a way popular music hadn't attempted. Before the first vocal arrives, you hear creaking timbers, crashing waves, thunder, and a cracking whip.

Kenny Gamble and Leon Huff traveled to Ghana to visit the coastal slave castles. Gamble told *Mojo* magazine that they stood in the castle's Door of No Return as they were conceiving the song: “We stood in the Door Of No Return that led you right to the slave ship and that was it for you, you were done.”

The song was originally planned for the soundtrack of the 1973 blaxploitation film _Shaft in Africa._ When it did not make it into the film, they used it to open their album *Ship Ahoy*, released on November 10, 1973.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"A song rejected by Shaft in Africa"** — Gamble and Huff originally wrote "Ship Ahoy" for the 1973 _Shaft in Africa_ soundtrack. When it did not make the film, they repurposed it as the anchor of a concept album about the Middle Passage. The Shaft rejection turned out to be a gift: freed from the soundtrack's action-movie needs, the song could stretch to nearly ten minutes and sit at the literal and emotional center of the album. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)

2. **"Three years before Roots"** — Alex Haley's _Roots_ was not published until 1976. "Ship Ahoy" put listeners inside a slave ship in 1973, personalizing the Middle Passage in a way no pop record had before. The writing theme would explore how this song beat mainstream literature to the punch on the most intimate telling of the transatlantic slave trade. Source: https://www.popmatters.com/ojays-ship-2496020277.html

3. **"Sound design as history lesson"** — Before the first vocal arrives at around 2:30, the track lays out a soundscape of creaking timbers, crashing waves, and a cracking whip. This is unusual for pop records, and it turns the listener into a captive below deck before a single word is sung. Kenny Gamble described standing at Ghana's "Door of No Return" as the imaginative starting point. The theme frames the track as an audio documentary in song form. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-ojays/ship-ahoy

4. **"The full boil of Philly Soul"** — "Ship Ahoy" lets MFSB, Norman Harris, and the Sigma Sound house style work on a long canvas. The same machinery that polished radio ballads was pointed at something brutal, and the Philly Sound's lush strings turn into mourners instead of seducers. A theme about what happens when a hit factory takes on hard subject matter. Source: https://dereksmusicblog.com/2015/01/18/the-ojays-ship-ahoy-3/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

The current listening mission is strong and specific ("It starts as a slow burn with sound effects and two bass notes that could be from a country song. By 2:30 it is fully funky. When does the switch happen?"). Alternatives in the same spirit:

1. "Nothing happens for almost two and a half minutes, and that is the point. Count the sound effects you hear before the vocals come in: waves, timbers, whips, wind. What does the track make you picture before anyone sings a word?"

2. "Listen for the moment the strings stop being pretty. Around the midpoint the Philly Sound orchestration, the same strings you know from 'Me and Mrs. Jones,' turns from velvet into a warning. When does it flip for you?"

3. "Eddie Levert is singing as one of the captives, not about them. Pay attention to the pronouns. How does the song feel different when the narrator is below deck rather than looking back from dry land?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The song was originally written by Gamble and Huff for the soundtrack to the 1973 film _Shaft in Africa_, then repurposed as the centerpiece of the O'Jays album when the producers decided it was too heavy for a movie cue. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The opening sound design includes crashing waves, creaking ship timbers, thunder, and a cracking whip before any vocal enters. The first sung line does not arrive until roughly two and a half minutes in. Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2013/03/14/review-the-ojays-ship-ahoy-40th-anniversary-edition/
- Kenny Gamble told _Mojo_ magazine that he and Leon Huff traveled to Ghana and stood in the "Door of No Return" at the slave castle as they were conceiving the song: "We stood in the Door Of No Return that led you right to the slave ship and that was it for you, you were done." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- PopMatters argued the track "personalized the 'voyage' in ways that few black popular artifacts had previously done so, some three years before the publication of Alex Haley's _Roots_." Source: https://www.popmatters.com/ojays-ship-2496020277.html
- The arrangement is credited to Norman Harris, one of the core MFSB arrangers; the ten-minute runtime let the Philadelphia International house sound operate in a mode usually reserved for jazz or classical music. Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2013/03/14/review-the-ojays-ship-ahoy-40th-anniversary-edition/
- "Ship Ahoy" has been sampled by multiple hip-hop artists, including DJ Paul & Juicy J ("Psychopathic Lunatic"), G. Dep feat. Carl Thomas ("It's All Over"), Cormega feat. Ghostface Killah ("Tony/Montana"), and Yung Wun feat. Cassidy ("One More Day in the Hood"). Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-O'Jays/Ship-Ahoy/
- A reggae version of the song was recorded by British reggae group The Cimarons. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-O'Jays/Ship-Ahoy/

#### Album

- _Ship Ahoy_ is The O'Jays' seventh studio album and was released on November 10, 1973 on Philadelphia International Records (catalog KZ 32408). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The album reached No. 11 on the Billboard Pop Albums chart and No. 1 on the R&B Albums chart. It was certified Gold by the RIAA on January 21, 1974 and Platinum on August 11, 1992. It was the highest-selling R&B album on Billboard's 1974 year-end chart. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The cover art by James Barkley depicts the three O'Jays inside the hold of a slave ship alongside illustrated captives. The back cover shows a painting of an illuminated slave ship at sea. Barkley was better known for children's book illustrations and a 1972 U.S. postage stamp of Denali. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The album produced two Top 10 pop singles: "For the Love of Money" (No. 9 Pop, No. 3 R&B) and "Put Your Hands Together" (No. 10 Pop, No. 2 R&B). The title track was not released as a single. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- It was recorded at Sigma Sound Studios in Philadelphia, with MFSB as the backing orchestra. Personnel included Anthony Jackson on bass, Leon Isley Brooks on keyboards, and arrangers Norman Harris, Bobby Martin, and Lenny Pakula. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The New York Times praised the album in 1974 as "Gamble and Huff at their creative best." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)

#### Artist

- The O'Jays formed in 1958 in Canton, Ohio, while the members were at Canton McKinley High School. The original five were Eddie Levert, Walter Lee Williams, William Powell, Bobby Massey, and Bill Isles. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- The group first called themselves The Triumphs, then The Mascots, and renamed themselves The O'Jays in 1963 in tribute to Cleveland radio DJ Eddie O'Jay, who championed the group early on. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- By the time of _Ship Ahoy_, the group was a trio (Levert, Williams, Powell) after Massey and Isles left in 1972. Source: https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/the-ojays-1958-2019/
- William Powell was diagnosed with cancer in 1975 and stopped touring in 1976; he died May 26, 1977 at age 35. He sang on _Ship Ahoy_. Source: https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/william-powell-january-20-1942-may-26-1977-ojays
- The O'Jays signed to Gamble and Huff's Philadelphia International Records in 1972; _Ship Ahoy_ was their second PIR album, following _Back Stabbers_ (1972). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- The O'Jays were inducted into the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2004 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2005. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current) — Defensible. The track came out in the core classic era (1973), on a major commercial label, via a group that had Top 10 pop hits. The groove that kicks in around 2:30 is MFSB at full stride, with a horn-driven funk pocket. However, "Ship Ahoy" is an unusual classic-funk choice: the first third of the song is pure mood and sound design, and the song never had a single release. It is an album-oriented track, which is more the terrain of jazz-funk or psychedelic funk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)

2. **Psychedelic Funk** — Reasonable alternative. The sound-effects opening, cinematic arrangement, long runtime, and surreal storytelling sit near psychedelic funk territory as the book defines it ("funk's groove with psychedelic music's surreal lyrics, colors and sounds... invented worlds, and hallucinatory storytelling"). The Middle Passage setting is arguably a kind of historical dreamscape built out of sound design. Source: Book definition in `Matter/On Genres & Regions.md`

3. **Jazz-Funk** — Weak but not crazy. The extended form, orchestral writing, and willingness to let the groove breathe for minutes at a time echo jazz-funk habits (longer tracks, room for arrangement). But the players are not jazz musicians, and there is no extended improvisation, so this is a stretch.

**Recommendation**: Classic Funk is defensible as filed. Psychedelic Funk would be the only genuinely interesting re-tag. Keeping Classic Funk also makes the sibling "For the Love of Money" entry consistent with this one (both are from _Ship Ahoy_).

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - Northeast** (current) — Strong case. The album was recorded at Sigma Sound Studios in Philadelphia and released on Philadelphia International Records. MFSB, the house band, was a Philly outfit. The Philly Sound is a Northeast phenomenon. This also keeps the entry consistent with "For the Love of Money - 10-19.md", which is tagged the same. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)

2. **US - Ohio** — Reasonable alternative. The O'Jays formed in Canton, Ohio in 1958 and spent their first decade as an Ohio group. The book's regions section says "each choice is meant to represent the track's origins and be defensible," and one could argue the O'Jays' origin should anchor them. The sister entry's research notes considered US - Ohio before settling on US - Northeast.

**Recommendation**: Keep US - Northeast. The album is a Philadelphia International / Sigma Sound product, and the track's production is as Philly as the Philly Sound gets.
