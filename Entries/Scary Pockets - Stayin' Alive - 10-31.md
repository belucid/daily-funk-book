Released on Halloween in 2022, today’s song is Scary Pockets’ cover of the Bee Gees’ 1977 disco hit "Stayin’ Alive."

Even if you don't know much disco, you know this song. The original dances to a 104 BPM four-on-the-floor pulse. To make it funky, Scary Pockets slows it down, brings the vocal back to earth, and replaces the straight-ahead beat with funk syncopation and swagger. It's a good case study in the differences between funk and disco production.

Episode 289 of the *Switched on Pop* podcast shows how Scary Pockets arranged this version in about 90 minutes before recording it live in the same room.

#### Track
- The cover was released October 31, 2022, Halloween day, as a digital single on the band's own Pockets, Inc. label. For a band literally named "Scary" Pockets, a Halloween drop is a nice bit of calendar theater. Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/stayin-alive-feat-lizzy-mcalpine-single/1649972578
- Credits on the take: Ryan Lerman and Will Graefe on guitar, Jack Conte on keys, Travis Carlton on bass, RJ Kelly on drums, Lizzy McAlpine on vocals. Travis Carlton is the son of session legend Larry Carlton, carrying serious LA studio pedigree into the pocket. Source: https://switchedonpop.com/episodes/scary-pockets-lizzy-mcalpine
- Scary Pockets arranges and records each cover on the fly in roughly 90 minutes. The Switched On Pop podcast built a whole episode around watching the Stayin' Alive session happen in real time. Source: https://switchedonpop.com/episodes/scary-pockets-lizzy-mcalpine
- The cover runs 3:09, slightly shorter than the Bee Gees' 1977 original (4:45), because the band trims the repetition and leans into groove density over disco-length buildup. Sources: https://open.spotify.com/track/5yH1Dmshfq3sVD2VKvroaB and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stayin%27_Alive
- Writer credits remain Barry, Robin and Maurice Gibb. The song's original chart run was extraordinary: #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 for four weeks starting February 4, 1978, and one of six consecutive Bee Gees #1 singles, tying a Beatles record at the time. The Scary Pockets cover lets Funk-a-Day wave at that history without actually including disco. Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/stayin-alive-feat-lizzy-mcalpine-single/1649972578 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stayin%27_Alive

#### Artist
- Scary Pockets was founded in Los Angeles in early 2017 by keyboardist Jack Conte, co-founder and CEO of Patreon, and guitarist/arranger Ryan Lerman. The two met in high school at The Branson School in Marin County, California. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets
- The origin story is that Conte, buried in Patreon work and no longer playing, pitched the band to Lerman the day after Lerman's 30th birthday dinner. Lerman's mission statement: "make the most amount of music in the least amount of time and have the most amount of fun." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets
- Before Scary Pockets, Lerman toured the world as bassist for Ben Folds, guitarist for Michael Bublé, and musical director for John Legend. Source: https://bassmusicianmagazine.com/2021/05/interview-with-bassist-ryan-lerman/
- Jack Conte's other band is Pomplamoose, the YouTube-native duo with his wife Nataly Dawn. The Patreon crowdfunding company grew directly out of his experience trying to fund Pomplamoose videos. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Conte and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets
- The band's first video was a funk cover of Ed Sheeran's "Shape of You," released in March 2017, and they posted weekly covers for more than six years straight. On December 11, 2023, Scary Pockets announced they were ending the weekly covers format. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets
- The rotating-cast model echoes Steely Dan's studio philosophy: pick the right players per song, not a fixed lineup. Source: https://www.culturesonar.com/scary-pockets-redefining-covers/
- Guest vocalist Lizzy McAlpine is a Philadelphia-born singer-songwriter, born September 21, 1999, who studied at Berklee before breaking big with her 2022 album "Five Seconds Flat" and the TikTok-viral single "Ceilings." She later signed to RCA Records and released "Older" in 2024. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzy_McAlpine

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Nu-Funk.** The strongest fit by the book's own definitions. Nu-funk is defined as "a modern funk revival scene that emerged in the mid-1990s, shaped by DJs and bands capturing the feel of rare funk 45s while making new records in that spirit," and is explicitly international with "standout artists across the US, Europe, and Australia." Scary Pockets is a 2017-founded LA band whose whole mission is to reinterpret pop songs in a vintage funk idiom with live studio musicians. That is nu-funk in its cleanest form. Source: On Genres & Regions.md and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets
2. **Funk Rock.** A secondary but plausible reading, especially since the original "Stayin' Alive" sits on the disco/rock border and Scary Pockets lean on electric guitar hooks. Weaker because the book notes that most funk rock entries come from the 1980s-90s surge and expect a rock backbone Scary Pockets don't really prioritize. Source: On Genres & Regions.md
3. **Jazz Funk.** Arguably defensible because Ryan Lerman's sideman résumé (Ben Folds, John Legend, Michael Bublé) and the rotating cast of LA session jazz players give the takes a jazz-chops feel. But the book defines jazz funk as a 1970s phenomenon played by jazz musicians who crossed over, so this is a stretch. Source: On Genres & Regions.md

My strong recommendation: **Nu-Funk**. It is the definitional bullseye and also gives the book an anchor entry for a genre that otherwise skews toward smaller/obscurer acts.
