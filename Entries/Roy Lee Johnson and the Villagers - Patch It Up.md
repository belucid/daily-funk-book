### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. One item surfaced during research worth flagging:

- **Studio discrepancy.** Sources disagree on the exact Muscle Shoals studio. The James Brown-style funk press notes and Ace/reissue copy describe the album as recorded at Muscle Shoals; one retailer description names FAME Studios specifically, while other coverage references Muscle Shoals Sound Studio. The credited personnel (Roger Hawkins, Barry Beckett, Pete Carr, Jimmy Johnson as producer) are the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section ("the Swampers"), who by 1973 ran Muscle Shoals Sound Studio. Both studios sit in Muscle Shoals, Alabama, so the `region` conclusion (US - Southeast) is unaffected. I could not find a primary liner-note scan confirming the exact room. Source: https://goner-records.com/products/roy-lee-johnson-the-villagers-s-t

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The man who wrote Mr. Moonlight."** Roy Lee Johnson penned "Mr. Moonlight" in high school; the Beatles covered it on Beatles for Sale / Beatles '65 in 1964. A funk obscurity by a songwriter who quietly shaped the British Invasion is a strong frame. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson
- **"The last gasp of Stax."** This 1973 LP is often called "an unsung monster from the waning hours of the Stax empire" — funk made as the label slid toward bankruptcy. The track as a document of an institution's final creative surge. Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/roy-lee-johnson-and-the-villagers-1
- **"The band that ended with a death."** The Villagers disbanded after 21-year-old bassist Michael James died unexpectedly. "Patch It Up" as the opener of the only album by a group cut short. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The press calls this a "relentless opener" with "a strong James Brown influence." Listen to the first thirty seconds: what specific JB devices (the chicken-scratch guitar, the horn stabs, the one-chord vamp) does the band borrow, and where does Johnson's own voice break from the template?
- This is the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section playing funk rather than soul ballads. Focus on Roger Hawkins and bassist Michael James in the pocket — how does a section famous for Aretha and Wilson Pickett change its feel when the brief is James Brown?
- At barely 2:30 the track is over fast. Notice how little fat there is. What gets left out compared to a longer funk workout, and does the brevity make it hit harder?

### Album

- The self-titled LP (Stax STS-3020, 1973) was recorded in Muscle Shoals, Alabama with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section: Roger Hawkins (percussion), Barry Beckett (clavinet), Pete Carr (guitar), bassist Michael James, plus the Memphis Horns and Muscle Shoals Horns. Produced by Jimmy Johnson. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/12130511-Roy-Lee-Johnson-The-Villagers-Roy-Lee-Johnson-The-Villagers
- The album is described as "an unsung monster from the waning hours of the Stax empire," a relentlessly funky gem inspired by James Brown. — Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/roy-lee-johnson-and-the-villagers-1
- The band broke up after 21-year-old bassist Michael James died unexpectedly, making this their only album. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson
- A 1972 single "The Dryer" (Parts I & II) preceded the album; both parts appear on the LP. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/611650-Roy-Lee-Johnson-The-Villagers-The-Dryer

### Artist

- Roy Lee Johnson wrote "Mr. Moonlight" in high school; first recorded by Piano Red (as Dr. Feelgood and the Interns), it was famously covered by the Beatles on Beatles for Sale (1964, UK) / Beatles '65 (US), establishing Johnson as an influence on the British Invasion. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson
- Johnson began as a teenager with The Brassettes around 1955; after a 1965 Atlanta talent-contest win his early sides appeared on small labels, and he played in Piano Red's band the Interns. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson
- **Artist-name discrepancy:** the entry's locked `artist` field reads "Ray Lee Johnson," but every authoritative source (Discogs, Wikipedia, Stax credits, all streaming platforms) names him **Roy** Lee Johnson. This appears to be a typo in the entry title/field. I did not alter the locked field; the author should correct "Ray" to "Roy." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lee_Johnson

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk** — The track is the album's most overtly James Brown-influenced cut, a tight one-chord funk vamp from a Stax-era rhythm section. This is the best single fit. Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/roy-lee-johnson-and-the-villagers-1
- **Deep Funk** — Its status as a sought-after crate-digger / DJ 45-era club classic since the late '80s, on an obscure-but-collectible Stax LP, fits the Deep Funk collector lineage. Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/roy-lee-johnson-and-the-villagers-1
- **Gospel Funk** — A weaker option; the gospel-trained Chalmers/Rhodes backing vocals and church-rooted Stax house style give it a gospel edge, but the track itself is secular JB funk, so this is a stretch versus the first two.
