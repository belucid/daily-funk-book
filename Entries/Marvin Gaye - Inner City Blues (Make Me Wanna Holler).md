### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were found in the original entry. All standard research areas have been addressed below.

One unresolved question: sources disagree on the single's exact release date. Wikipedia and Classic Motown cite **October 14, 1971**, while several Discogs/RYM listings and The Vinyl Factory cite **September 16, 1971**. Most sources also note that the song "took its bow on both the Billboard Hot 100 and the Best Selling Soul Singles chart" the week of **October 9, 1971**, which lines up better with an October release. I lean toward October 14, 1971 as the most defensible date because it's the figure used by both Motown's own site and Wikipedia.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **Funk that does its hollering quietly.** The track is a protest record built from a whisper rather than a shout, with bongos, congas, a wah-clavinet sized riff and a vocal Marvin layered against itself. The "make me wanna holler" comes from inside the groove, not over the top of it. An angle that leans into how funk's mellow side can carry a heavier political payload than its loud side.

2. **The janitor with the title.** Co-writer James Nyx Jr. was a Motown janitor and elevator operator who'd been on Jobete's books for years with mostly shelved songs. He brought Marvin the words from a newspaper headline about Detroit's "inner city," talked taxes, the moon program, and the ghetto, then ended up with a co-credit on one of the most important records of the decade. A great hook for the unsung-collaborator entry.

3. **The closer that doubles back.** "Inner City Blues" is the last song on *What's Going On*, and in its final minute it reprises the title track's melody before sliding into a dark fade. An angle on Marvin Gaye's instinct for the album as a single thought, treating the whole record as one long song that needed a circular ending.

4. **A football tryout, a recording session, and a record that changed Motown.** The same thread that ran through Marvin's Detroit Lions tryout (Mel Farr and Lem Barney, both Lions players, ended up singing on *What's Going On*) also sets up the social conscience that explodes by the album's last track. A people-and-grief origin angle.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Listen for what's missing. Most protest funk shouts. This one whispers. Where, exactly, in the arrangement does the anger live, the bongos, the bassline, the doubled vocal, or somewhere else?"

2. "Around 4:30 the song stops being a song and quotes the album's opening theme. Catch the moment Marvin loops back to 'What's Going On' and decide: is this a victory lap or a sigh?"

3. "Try to count Bob Babbitt's bassline. It walks where you'd expect a funk bass to lock. How does the groove still feel locked when the bass keeps moving?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The title came from a newspaper. Co-writer James Nyx Jr. saw a headline about Detroit's "inner city" one morning and that gave him the song's name; before that, Marvin had the tune but no words. — Source: https://classic.motown.com/story/marvins-inner-city-blues/
- The album version closes the record by reprising the melody of the album's opening title track "What's Going On" in its final minute, then segues into a dark fade. The single edit cut this reprise out to keep the running time under three minutes. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_Blues_(Make_Me_Wanna_Holler)
- It hit No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles chart for two weeks and No. 9 on the Hot 100, completing a run in which three consecutive singles from *What's Going On* spent nine total weeks at No. 1 R&B. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/marvin-gaye-inner-city-blues-song/
- The song ranks No. 256 on Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Songs of All Time. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_Blues_(Make_Me_Wanna_Holler)
- Marvin Gaye plays the piano on the track himself. The Funk Brothers fill in around him: Bob Babbitt on bass, Eddie "Bongo" Brown and Bobbye Hall on bongos, with arrangements by David Van De Pitte and members of the Detroit Symphony Orchestra adding strings and horns. — Source: https://classic.motown.com/story/marvins-inner-city-blues/
- A music video was finally made in 1994, more than two decades after release. The Hughes Brothers (then-recent directors of *Menace II Society*) shot it over five days in Harlem for the album's reissue. They reportedly told police they were shooting a documentary so they could film firefighters putting out a real fire. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_Blues_(Make_Me_Wanna_Holler)
- The track has been covered widely across genres including jazz (Sarah Vaughan, Grover Washington Jr., Reuben Wilson, Placebo) and soul/rock (The Impressions, The Chi-Lites, Joe Cocker, John Mayer, Angela Winbush, H.E.R.). — Source: https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/marvin-gaye-inner-city-blues-versions
- It's been heavily sampled in hip-hop, including by A Tribe Called Quest ("Youthful Expression," 1990), Big Daddy Kane ("W.G.O.N.R.S."), and Freddie Gibbs ("Listen"). — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Marvin-Gaye/Inner-City-Blues-(Make-Me-Wanna-Holler)/

#### Album

- *What's Going On* was Marvin Gaye's first album as credited producer, and the first Motown album to credit the in-house Funk Brothers session musicians by name. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Going_On_(album)
- Berry Gordy initially refused to release the title single, calling it "too political." Marvin responded by going on strike on his other Motown material until Gordy gave in. The single hit No. 1 R&B within a month. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Gaye
- The album was tracked extremely fast, the bulk of it cut at Hitsville U.S.A. between March 17 and 30, 1971, with additional sessions at Golden World, United Sound (Detroit) and The Sound Factory (Los Angeles). It came out May 21, 1971. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Going_On_(album)
- Marvin's signature multi-tracked lead vocal sound, layering himself in harmony with himself, came from a happy accident. An engineer accidentally mixed two of his lead vocal takes together, and Marvin liked it enough to keep it as a method. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What%27s_Going_On_(album)
- *What's Going On* sits at No. 1 on Rolling Stone's 2020 update of the 500 Greatest Albums of All Time. — Source: https://www.rs500albums.com/50-1/1

#### Artist

- In 1970, devastated by the death of his duet partner Tammi Terrell from brain cancer, Marvin briefly tried to give up music to play in the NFL. Detroit Lions coach Joe Schmidt let him try out at running back, tight end, wide receiver, and fullback, then turned him down as "too much of a liability." — Source: https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/13464184/marvin-gaye-tryout-nfl-detroit-lions
- The Lions tryout had a real musical payoff. Two Lions players, Mel Farr and future Hall of Famer Lem Barney, became close friends with Marvin and ended up singing the famous party-chatter intro on the title track of *What's Going On*. — Source: https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/13464184/marvin-gaye-tryout-nfl-detroit-lions
- Co-writer James Nyx Jr. came up through Motown literally, working as a janitor and elevator operator at Hitsville while writing on the side for Jobete. *What's Going On* was his late-career breakthrough, with co-credits on three songs including "Inner City Blues." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Nyx_Jr.
- Marvin Gaye was shot and killed by his father, Marvin Gay Sr., on April 1, 1984, the day before his 45th birthday, at his parents' Los Angeles home. His father later pleaded no contest to voluntary manslaughter. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Gaye

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Classic Funk**.

1. **Classic Funk (current — keep).** *What's Going On* sits squarely in the 1969–1977 window the book defines as classic funk, was a major-label release with massive chart presence, and the track's primary identity is funk rather than soul, jazz, or R&B. The mellow groove, conga-and-bongo bed, Bob Babbitt's bassline, and the looping wah-clavinet riff all live in the funk tradition. The song's social commentary doesn't change its musical genre.

2. **Psychedelic Funk (alternative).** The Wikipedia and AllMusic style tags both flag *What's Going On* as "psychedelic soul"; the track's cloudy, multi-tracked vocals, hazy mix, and dreamy fade carry psychedelic-funk DNA even if it's not in the Parliament-Funkadelic tradition. Worth noting but probably too narrow a fit given the book's "psychedelic funk" sits more comfortably with George Clinton's universe.

3. **Soul Jazz (long-shot).** The track's mellow, organ-and-piano-driven feel and chord-rich bed have soul-jazz cousins (Reuben Wilson covered it on Blue Note within a year), but the song itself is too vocal-led and groove-centered to live there. Not recommended.

**Recommendation:** Keep **Classic Funk**.

### Region Suggestions

Current value: **US - Michigan** (set during this research).

1. **US - Michigan (recommended — set).** Recorded at Hitsville U.S.A. in Detroit, released on the Detroit-based Tamla label, played by Motown's Detroit-based Funk Brothers, and arranged with the Detroit Symphony Orchestra. The track's home is Detroit by every relevant measure except where Marvin grew up.

2. **US - DC (alternative).** Marvin Gaye was born and raised in Washington, D.C. His sense of "the inner city" was likely shaped as much by D.C. as by Detroit. But the song was conceived around a Detroit newspaper headline (per James Nyx Jr.), recorded by a Detroit team, and released by a Detroit label. US - DC is defensible only if the book's region rule is read to track artist origin first.

3. **US - Midwest (broader fallback).** Could work as a more general regional tag, but US - Michigan is more specific and equally defensible. Not recommended.

**Recommendation:** Keep **US - Michigan**.
