Today’s song is Patrice Rushen’s “Shout It Out,” from the album *Shout It Out*. That makes it a title track, a song that shares its name with the album. So what makes a good title track?

In the early LP era, albums often gathered an artist’s recent singles, and the album title usually pointed buyers toward a big hit single they already knew. As the industry became more album-oriented, the title track took on a different role. When an album includes one, it often acts as a mission statement.

We have seen examples of title tracks laying out the album’s mission: Prince’s “Musicology” (April 20), the O’Jays’ “Ship Ahoy” (November 10), and Herb Alpert’s “Rise” (October 20). Yesterday's song offered a different case. Lenny Kravitz’s *Mama Said* takes its title from the chorus of “Always on the Run,” but the song and album do not share a name.

“Shout It Out” does not open Rushen’s album. That job goes to “The Hump” (September 30). But “Shout It Out” is sequenced second, and it still feels like the mission statement. Rushen had already proved herself as a pianist, composer, and arranger. Here, she steps out from behind the keys and up to the mic, starting to claim her voice as a singer too.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Shout It Out" is the album's title track, second on side one, and runs 6:14 on the LP (6:18 on streaming). It was written solely by Patrice Rushen, unlike the co-written "The Hump." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- The track has a sung chorus but no vocal verses, an unusual song-form choice that gives the band most of the real estate. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- Tom Scott plays both tenor saxophone and the lyricon on this track. The lyricon is the world's first commercially available wind synthesizer, and Scott was its best-known early adopter. Later in 1977 he would play lyricon on Steely Dan's "Peg," and in 1982 on Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean." — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Scott_(saxophonist), https://www.aerophoneacademy.com/2308352/episodes/16814883-special-guest-tom-scott-lyricon-pioneer-jazz-fusion-icon-part-1
- The title track has been sampled by Cool Hand Flex and Chris Energy for "Like Dis." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/588691/Cool-Hand-Flex-Chris-Energy-Like-Dis-Patrice-Rushen-Shout-It-Out/
- Personnel on "Shout It Out" (the track): Patrice Rushen (lead vocals, electric piano, clavinet, tambourine, arrangements); Charles Meeks (electric bass, backing vocals); James Gadson (drums); Bill Summers (percussion); Tom Scott (tenor sax, lyricon); Maxine Waters (backing vocals). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)

#### Album

- Shout It Out was released February 21, 1977 on Prestige (catalog P 10101), Rushen's third and final Prestige LP before moving to Elektra. It was recorded in 1976 (the source of the AllMusic "1976" year tag). — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album), https://www.allmusic.com/album/shout-it-out-mw0001879018
- Shout It Out is the first Rushen album to feature her as lead vocalist. Earlier Prestige records were instrumental. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- Executive producer: Orrin Keepnews, co-founder of Riverside Records. Co-producers: Rushen, Reggie Andrews, Tommy Vicari. Mastered by Bernie Grundman at A&M Studios in Hollywood. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- The session cast spans three of the 1970s' most important bandleader-support units at once: Al McKay from Earth, Wind & Fire on guitar, Bill Summers from the Headhunters on percussion, and Tom Scott from the L.A. Express on horns and lyricon. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_McKay, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Summers_(musician), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Scott_(saxophonist)
- Rushen co-produced the record at 22, a rare credit for a young woman at a major jazz label in 1976. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)

#### Artist

- Patrice Louise Rushen was born September 30, 1954 in Los Angeles. She was enrolled at USC's Thornton School of Music at four and giving classical recitals by six. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Rushen
- She signed to Prestige at 19, after her high-school combo took first prize at a Monterey Jazz Festival student competition. — Source: https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/patrice-rushen-1954/
- Rushen was the first woman to serve as music director of the Grammy Awards (46th–48th ceremonies) and the first in 43 years to direct the Emmy Awards. — Source: https://patricerushen.com/full-length-bio
- She chaired USC Thornton's popular music program from 2014 to 2024 and is a three-time Grammy nominee. — Source: https://patricerushen.com/full-length-bio

### Genre Suggestions

`Jazz-Funk` is the right call. Per the book's definition, jazz-funk is "funk played by jazz musicians whose earlier careers were in more traditional jazz styles," with longer track lengths and room for extended improvisation. The title track runs 6:14, features a jazz soloist (Tom Scott) on both sax and lyricon, and sits on a funk rhythm section. Rushen came up through Prestige as a straight jazz player.

Alternatives considered:

- **Classic Funk.** The groove and sung chorus are as funky as many classic-funk entries from 1977. But the track's length, solo focus, and jazz-trained producer/performer all push it out of the classic-funk lane by the book's definition.
- **Soul Jazz.** Reserved for the earlier, smaller-ensemble side of the jazz-to-funk arc. Shout It Out is the full-boil big-band version, not the simmer. Not a fit.

Recommendation: keep `Jazz-Funk`.
