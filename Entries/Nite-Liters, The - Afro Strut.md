### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. Questions surfaced during research and their resolution:

- **Is "Afro-Strut" an album or only a single?** Both. The track first circulated as a 7" single (RCA Victor 74-0591, A-side, b/w "(We've Got To) Pull Together") and also appears as a track on the 1972 LP *Instrumental Directions* (RCA Victor LSP 4580). Confusingly, Discogs shows an Argentine pressing of that same LP (catalog LSP 4580, master 182738) that was **retitled** *Afro-Strut* for the South American market. There is no separate US album called *Afro-Strut*; the canonical US album title is *Instrumental Directions*. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/10978604-The-Nite-Liters-Afro-Strut (Argentina pressing, master_id 182738) vs https://www.discogs.com/master/182738-The-Nite-Liters-Instrumental-Directions.
- **1971 vs 1972 for the single?** Discogs dates the single and album masters to 1972, and the New Birth Wikipedia article says the "Afro-Strut" single was released in 1972. Some retailer/blog listings say 1971. I used 1972 as the canonical value; see Field Verification for the discrepancy.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The house band that outgrew its own name."** The Nite-Liters were the instrumental engine assembled by Harvey Fuqua and Tony Churchill; they were then folded into the vocal supergroup New Birth, to the point that the same players were credited under different names depending on the track. "Afro-Strut" is the sound of the band as a standalone instrumental unit before the identity dissolved. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Birth_(band)
- **"A B-side with a second life."** A modest 1972 chart single became a crate-digger staple sampled by UGK, Above the Law, Positive K, N2Deep and others — the track earned more cultural weight in 1990s hip-hop than it did on release. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Nite-Liters/Afro-Strut/
- **"Funk from the doo-wop man."** The track is produced and co-written by Harvey Fuqua, founder of the 1950s doo-wop group The Moonglows and a Motown architect — a study in how a first-generation R&B figure adapted to the funk era. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Fuqua

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is an instrumental with no lead vocal to hang onto. Track the guitar-and-horn "strut" figure that gives the tune its name — where does the band restate that hook, and where do they let the rhythm section take over?
- At barely 2:50 this is a tight, radio-length instrumental. Listen for how little fat there is: count how many bars pass before the main riff returns, and ask whether it feels like a jam or a pop single in disguise.
- You have almost certainly heard this drum-and-guitar break chopped up in a rap song. Listen for the four-bar loop that hip-hop producers lifted, then imagine it under a rapper's verse. Which section would you have sampled?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Afro-Strut" has been sampled in more than ten songs, including UGK feat. Mr. 3-2's "Stoned Junkee," Above the Law's "Another Execution," Positive K's "It's All Over," and N2Deep's "Mack Daddyz." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Nite-Liters/Afro-Strut/
- The single reached No. 49 on the Billboard Pop chart and No. 24 on the R&B chart per the New Birth Wikipedia article (see Field Verification — this chart claim is not corroborated by a second source). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Birth_(band)
- The track was written by guitarist Charles (Charlie) Hearndon and Harvey Fuqua, and produced by Fuqua III Productions. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1052948-The-Nite-Liters-Afro-Strut-Weve-Got-To-Pull-Together

#### Album

- *Instrumental Directions* (the album carrying "Afro-Strut") was cut at RCA's Studio C in New York City and peaked at No. 198 on the Billboard Top LPs chart and No. 41 on the Top Soul LPs chart in 1972. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_Directions
- The same album was released in Argentina under the title *Afro-Strut* (RCA Victor LSP 4580), and its all-instrumental program also included covers of "Theme from Shaft," "MacArthur Park," and "Wichita Lineman." Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/10978604-The-Nite-Liters-Afro-Strut

#### Artist

- The Nite-Liters formed in 1963 in Louisville, Kentucky, founded by Tony Churchill and Harvey Fuqua, and functioned as the instrumental core that was combined with vocal groups to create New Birth. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Birth_(band)
- Early Nite-Liters member Johnny Graham went on to join Earth, Wind & Fire. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Birth_(band)
- Producer/co-writer Harvey Fuqua founded the doo-wop group The Moonglows in the 1950s and was a key figure at Motown, running the Harvey and Tri-Phi labels (which signed The Spinners and Jr. Walker & the All Stars) before his funk-era work. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Fuqua
