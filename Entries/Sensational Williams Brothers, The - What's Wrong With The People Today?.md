### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **Funk smuggled into the sanctuary.** This is a gospel quartet from rural Mississippi getting genuinely funky in 1974 — the title track is a social-commentary groove ("what's wrong with the people today?") rather than a straight praise number. A hook about gospel acts absorbing the funk vocabulary of their secular peers.
- **The Don Robey pipeline.** Songbird was a Duke-Peacock gospel subsidiary that ABC absorbed in 1973 when Robey sold out; this LP (SBLP-248, 1974) lands right in that transition. An angle on how Houston's blues/R&B empire quietly fed a Mississippi gospel group into a major-label distribution machine.
- **Before the dynasty.** This 1974 record is the group's debut LP, decades before the Williams Brothers became Stellar-Award gospel royalty and founded Blackberry Records (Mississippi's first Black-owned label, 1991). A "where it started" frame for an act most listeners only know from their 1980s–90s peak.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The lead vocal is Douglas Williams (still a teenager in 1974). Listen to how the rhythm section sits in a secular funk pocket while the harmonies stay pure quartet gospel — where exactly does this stop sounding like church and start sounding like a soul 45?
- This is a "message" song, not a praise song. Track the lyric: how long before the brothers turn social complaint back toward the Lord, and what does that pivot tell you about gospel's relationship to the funk era?
- Compare the groove here to the album's straighter hymns like "At The Cross" or "Yes, Jesus Loves Me." What does the band add (or strip away) to make the title track feel like funk rather than gospel?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The title track is the LP's opener and a social-commentary number rather than a praise song; it was written by Doug, Leon ("Pop") and Leonard Williams. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5244826-The-Sensational-Williams-Brothers-Whats-Wrong-With-The-People-Today
- Lead vocal is credited to Douglas Williams, who was only about 17–18 in 1974 (b. Sept 3, 1956). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Williams_(musician)
- The track has been adopted by deep-funk / gospel-funk crate diggers; it appears on collector compilations and is filed by Discogs under Funk/Soul with a Gospel style. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/3035528-The-Sensational-Williams-Brothers-Whats-Wrong-With-The-People-Today

#### Album

- This was the group's debut full-length album (1974), even though they had been performing since 1960. — Source: https://www.mswritersandmusicians.com/mississippi-musicians/the-williams-brothers
- Produced by Lee Young (jazz drummer and brother of Lester Young, later an ABC/Motown executive); pressed at the Columbia Records plant in Terre Haute, Indiana; published by Lion Publishing Co. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5244826-The-Sensational-Williams-Brothers-Whats-Wrong-With-The-People-Today
- The album sits on Songbird (SBLP-248), the gospel subsidiary of Houston's Duke-Peacock empire that Don Robey sold to ABC in 1973 — so this 1974 LP is one of the first under ABC's ownership. — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/abc/songbird.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Bird_Records

#### Artist

- Founded in 1960 in Smithdale, Mississippi by patriarch Leon "Pop" Williams, who reportedly heard his kids singing in the backyard with broken brooms as microphones. — Source: https://www.mswritersandmusicians.com/mississippi-musicians/the-williams-brothers
- Early on they recorded and toured as the "Little Williams Brothers" / "Sensational Williams Brothers"; they were among the first to fold contemporary gospel into the hard-quartet tradition. — Source: https://malaco.com/artist/gospel/the-williams-brothers/
- In 1991 the group founded Blackberry Records, described as Mississippi's first Black-owned record label; the group has earned 17+ Stellar Awards and a 1999 International Gospel Music Hall of Fame induction. — Source: https://www.mswritersandmusicians.com/mississippi-musicians/the-williams-brothers ; https://malaco.com/artist/gospel/the-williams-brothers/
- Founder Pop Williams died in a car accident on September 6, 1989. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Williams_Brothers_(gospel_group)

### Genre Suggestions

- **Gospel Funk (current value — keep).** Strongest fit and matches the book's definition exactly: a gospel act getting seriously funky on a track. Discogs classifies the release as Funk/Soul (genre) with Gospel (style). Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/3035528
- **Deep Funk (alternate).** The track's commercial fate fits: an overlooked gospel-LP cut later prized by crate-diggers and rare-groove DJs, on a poorly-distributed gospel subsidiary. If the author wants to emphasize the rediscovery story over the praise content, Deep Funk is defensible. Source: book definition in `Matter/On Subgenres.md`; collector framing at https://recordshack.org/shop/format/lp/the-sensational-williams-brothers-whats-wrong-with-the-people-today-abc-songbird-lp/
- **Classic Funk (weaker).** The 1974 date sits inside the 1969–77 classic window, but the track's primary identity is gospel-with-funk, not mainstream funk in the commercial pipeline, so Gospel Funk remains the better label.

Recommendation: keep **Gospel Funk**.
