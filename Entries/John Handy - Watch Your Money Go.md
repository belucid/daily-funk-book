### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The sellout that wasn't"** — Handy was a Mingus sideman and avant-leaning Monterey hero who, in his mid-40s, cut unapologetic jazz-funk. AllMusic sniffed at Carnival ("commercial settings", 2 stars: https://www.allmusic.com/album/carnival-mw0001882130), but the groove holds up. An angle on how the jazz police treated crossover, and who turned out to be right.
2. **"Watch the formula go"** — This track is the deliberate follow-up to his surprise 1976 smash "Hard Work" (Billboard 200 #43, jazz #4 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Work_(album)): same producer, same strut, even an edited 7" aimed at radio. A case study in what happens the second time a jazz artist chases a hit. Pairs naturally with the book's "Hard Work" entry.
3. **"A money song from a man who watched it go"** — A wry instrumental-with-attitude about money, recorded for a label (ABC Impulse!) that would itself be absorbed by MCA within two years. Money anxiety as a funk lyric tradition (cf. O'Jays "For the Love of Money", entry 10-19).

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "This is the same sax player who stunned Monterey in 1965 with a 20-minute avant-garde 'Spanish Lady.' Can you hear that adventurous player sneaking out anywhere in these five minutes, or has the pocket fully tamed him?"
2. "Drummer James Gadson played on hundreds of soul hits. Focus only on his hi-hat and snare for one full minute — what is he doing to keep a one-chord vamp from going stale?"
3. "The single edit cut this from 5:58 to 3:43. As you listen, decide which two minutes you would have cut — then ask what the album version buys with the extra time."

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- Written by John Handy himself and produced by Esmond Edwards; track personnel: Handy (alto sax), James Gadson (drums), Rudy Coleman (bass), Mike Hoffmann (guitar), George Spencer (keyboards), Tom Nicholas (congas) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival and https://www.sessiondays.com/2019/04/1977-john-handy-carnival/
- Released as a 7" single on ABC Impulse! (IM-31012), backed with "Make Her Mine", in an edited 3:43 version (album cut runs 5:58) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/317451-John-Handy-Watch-Your-Money-Go-Make-Her-Mine
- The promo 7" carried "Watch Your Money Go" on both sides — a mono edit on the A-side and a stereo edit on the B-side, "Promotional Copy - Not For Sale" — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2757359-John-Handy-Watch-Your-Money-Go
- Drummer on this track, James Gadson, is one of the most-recorded session drummers in soul/funk (Bill Withers, Watts 103rd St. Rhythm Band) — credit verified at Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival

### Album

- Carnival (ABC Impulse! AS-9324, 1977) was recorded at A&R Recording Studios, New York and ABC Recording Studios, Los Angeles, produced by Esmond Edwards — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_(John_Handy_album)
- The session cast is an LA studio-royalty roll call: Lee Ritenour and Larry Carlton on guitars, Motown's James Jamerson on bass ("Love's Rejoycing"), Funk Brothers conga player Eddie "Bongo" Brown, and Paulinho Da Costa on percussion — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival
- AllMusic gave Carnival just 2 stars: "Handy is mostly featured here in commercial settings, singing two songs and interacting with an electric rhythm section" — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/carnival-mw0001882130
- It was Handy's follow-up to Hard Work (1976), his surprise crossover hit that reached No. 43 on the Billboard 200 and No. 4 on the Billboard jazz chart — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Work_(album)

### Artist

- John Richard Handy III was born February 3, 1933 in Dallas, Texas, and is still living (93 as of 2026); he moved to the Bay Area at 15 and attended McClymonds High School in Oakland and San Francisco State — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Handy and https://www.allaboutjazz.com/musicians/john-handy
- He played alto sax (plus clarinet and tenor) on Charles Mingus's landmark 1959 albums, including Mingus Ah Um, recorded May 5–12, 1959 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingus_Ah_Um
- His quintet's September 18, 1965 Monterey Jazz Festival set — two long pieces, "If Only We Knew" and "Spanish Lady" — became the celebrated Columbia LP "Recorded Live at the Monterey Jazz Festival" (1966) and earned two Grammy nominations; legend says Columbia's John Hammond offered him a contract as he walked offstage — Source: https://montereyjazz.org/news/timeless-since-1958/if-only-we-knew-john-handys-iconic-monterey-moment/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_Live_at_the_Monterey_Jazz_Festival
- Handy taught at Stanford and UC Berkeley, and received SFJAZZ's Beacon Award in 2009 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Handy
- He was still performing at 90, profiled by San Francisco Classical Voice in a feature titled "At 90, Saxophonist John Handy Still Puts In the Hard Work" — Source: https://www.sfcv.org/articles/feature/90-saxophonist-john-handy-still-puts-hard-work

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Jazz Funk (strong recommendation)** — Fits the book's definition on all three axes: a player whose earlier career was traditional jazz (Mingus sideman, Monterey 1965), funk grooves with jazz soloing on top, and squarely 1970s. Discogs styles the single "Jazz-Funk" outright. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/317451-John-Handy-Watch-Your-Money-Go-Make-Her-Mine, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Handy
2. **Soul Jazz (weaker)** — Discogs genres include "Jazz, Funk / Soul", but the book's definition reserves soul jazz for earlier, smaller-ensemble organ-trio-era material; this 1977 electric-rhythm-section record is the "full boil" the book files under jazz-funk. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/165987-John-Handy-Carnival
3. **Classic Funk (edge case)** — It's 1977 major-label funk with a radio edit, but the track's primary identity is a jazz soloist over a funk rhythm section, which the book's classic-funk definition explicitly excludes (funk fused into jazz). Mention only to dismiss.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - California (recommended, written to field)** — Handy moved to the Bay Area at 15, was schooled in Oakland and at SF State, made his name at Monterey, taught at Stanford and UC Berkeley, and remains an SF jazz institution (SFJAZZ Beacon Award 2009). The album was also partly recorded in Los Angeles with an LA session crew. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Handy, https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival
2. **US - Texas (defensible)** — Handy was born in Dallas and lived there to age 15. If the book tags by birthplace, this wins; but his entire musical identity formed in California. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Handy
3. **US - New York (weak)** — Part of Carnival was cut at A&R Studios, New York, and his Mingus years were a NYC sojourn (1958–62), but neither defines this track's origins. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/785594-John-Handy-Carnival
