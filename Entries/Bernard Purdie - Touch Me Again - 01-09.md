Bernard "Pretty" Purdie, the most-recorded drummer in America by 1974, had spent his career playing on other people's records. The soundtrack to the pornographic movie *Lialeh* was his first project as composer, producer, and bandleader.

The songwriting on today’s track, "Touch Me Again," is credited to Purdie, with lyrics by Barron Bercovichy. Lead vocals are by the relatively unknown singer Sandi Hewitt.

The *Lialeh* LP was pressed by Bryan Records in 1974, but both the movie and the record quickly vanished. Decades later, funk collectors paid four or five figures for original copies. The soundtrack is mostly instrumental, with little hint that it's an X-rated film score. “Touch Me Again” is the track that leans into the film’s intended sexy mood, yet it works well as a standalone slow funk groove.

Light in the Attic restored the *Lialeh* recordings and reissued the soundtrack on CD and vinyl in 2003. Thanks to that work, it's now available to stream.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The bandleader as accompanist.** "Touch Me Again" is the only proper vocal feature on the soundtrack, and on the one track named in the writing theme worth chasing, Purdie pulls back. The most-recorded drummer in America makes himself small so a singer almost no one knew can carry the room. The hook is the boss stepping aside on his own record. (Source: http://madshoesmusicology.blogspot.com/2012/03/bernard-pretty-purdie-lialeh-1974.html)

- **A case for slow funk.** Most of Lialeh runs hot. "Touch Me Again" pulls the tempo back to a slow, sensual ballad pocket where the kit is mostly hi-hat and rim, the flute "flitters," and Sandi Hewitt opens with "Don't make me the family way." The hook is what funk does when it stops trying to make you dance and starts trying to make you lean in. (Source: http://madshoesmusicology.blogspot.com/2012/03/bernard-pretty-purdie-lialeh-1974.html)

- **The soundtrack that outclassed the film.** Bryan Records pressed Lialeh in 1974, the movie disappeared, the LP disappeared with it, and decades later collectors paid hundreds for original copies of music that had nothing to do with the X-rated reason it was made. "Touch Me Again" is the track that most plays into the film's intended mood, and yet it survives perfectly well as a standalone slow groove. The hook is how funk survives the project that paid for it. (Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack and https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/10-incredible-leftfield-funk-45s-that-no-one-is-looking-for)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Sandi Hewitt's opening line is "Don't make me the family way." Listen for what Purdie's kit does behind that line and across the next minute. Where does he leave space for her, and where does he push?

- This is the only vocal feature on a Purdie-led instrumental soundtrack. Picture the band cutting "Lialeh" or "Hap'pin'" five minutes earlier in the same session. How does the room change when a singer walks up to the mic on track A2?

- A flute drifts in and out across the track, almost as a counter-voice to Hewitt. Track when it enters and leaves. What does it carry that the horns or organ aren't doing?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Touch Me Again" is the second track on side A of Lialeh, sitting between the instrumental title cut and "Conscious." It runs 4:25, making it one of the shorter tracks on the LP. Source: https://www.deezer.com/us/track/3184889541
- The track is credited to Bernard Purdie (music) with lyrics by Barron Bercovichy. Lead vocals are by Sandi Hewitt; backing vocals are by Ken Lowry, Martha Stewart, and Stormy Winters. Source: https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0143378/soundtrack
- Hewitt's delivery on the opening line, "Don't make me the family way," was described in one critical review as slithering "out of her mouth like her subsequent encouragements to touch, a female serpent nudging the fallen fruit toward your foot." The arrangement pulls the band back so a flute can flitter across the texture. Source: http://madshoesmusicology.blogspot.com/2012/03/bernard-pretty-purdie-lialeh-1974.html
- "Touch Me Again" has been sampled at least once in the hip-hop era: Aesop Fables built "Smokers Anthem" on top of it. WhoSampled also notes a couple of additional flips. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Bernard-Purdie/Touch-Me-Again/ and https://www.whosampled.com/sample/136184/Aesop-Fables-Smokers-Anthem-Bernard-Purdie-Touch-Me-Again/
- This is the only proper vocal feature on the soundtrack. The other six tracks are instrumentals built around Purdie's New York jazz session band. Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/bernard-purdie/lialeh/

#### Album

- The full soundtrack is seven Purdie originals across about 33 minutes, the most concentrated Purdie-as-composer statement anywhere in his catalog. Tracklist (original 1974 Bryan LP): A1 "Lialeh" / A2 "Touch Me Again" / A3 "Conscious" / A4 "Easy" / B1 "All Pink On The Inside" / B2 "Pass Me Not" / B3 "Hap'pin'." Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/bernard-purdie/lialeh/
- The horn section reads like a New York jazz session call sheet: Jimmy Owens and Charles Sullivan on trumpets, Garnett Brown on trombone, Seldon Powell on tenor and flute, Arthur "Babe" Clarke on baritone. Horace Ott did most of the arrangements. Source: https://www.amazon.com/Lialeh-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack/dp/B00009NQOW

#### Album

- Bryan Records (catalog BRS-102) pressed the LP in 1974 and the label essentially vanished afterward. Original copies became one of the most coveted blaxploitation-era LPs on the collector market. Recent sales data shows prices from roughly $600 into four figures. Source: https://www.ebay.com/itm/Bernard-Purdie-Lialeh-1974-Original-Bryan-Records-BRS-102-LP-N-/254599839495 and https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/10-incredible-leftfield-funk-45s-that-no-one-is-looking-for
- Light in the Attic gave the album its first reissue in 2003 (CD and 180-gram LP, catalog LITA 003) with liner notes by Egon, then of Stones Throw and Now-Again. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack
- Purdie cut Lialeh while concurrently working with Curtis Mayfield, Aretha Franklin, the Last Poets, the Rolling Stones, and James Brown, which puts the record in one of the busiest stretches of his already absurd session calendar. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack
- AllMusic files the soundtrack under soul jazz, soundtracks, and jazz-funk, which captures the hybrid character of the band's playing as well as anything. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0000694751

#### Artist

- Bernard Lee Purdie was born June 11, 1939 in Elkton, Maryland, the eleventh of fifteen children, and started keeping time on tin cans before he had a kit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Purdie
- For years Purdie arrived at sessions and set up two placards on either side of his kit reading "You done it" and "You done hired the hit-maker, Bernard 'Pretty' Purdie." A bass player named Jimmy Tyrell had told him to make the signs as a memory aid for producers. Source: https://www.musicradar.com/artists/he-would-often-arrive-at-sessions-with-two-banners-which-he-placed-either-side-of-his-kit-while-recording-reading-you-done-hired-the-hit-maker-from-the-purdie-shuffle-to-its-extended-jazz-chords-analysing-steely-dans-babylon-sisters and https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/03/give-the-drummer-some-bernard-purdie/
- Purdie became Aretha Franklin's musical director in 1970 and held the chair for five years, including the night her sheet music fell off the stand and he improvised the now-iconic "Rock Steady" pocket on the spot. Source: https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2019/03/give-the-drummer-some-bernard-purdie/
- The "Purdie shuffle," triplets layered with ghost notes against a half-time backbeat, has been studied as a textbook groove and shows up on Steely Dan's "Babylon Sisters" and "Home at Last." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Purdie
- He has long claimed to have overdubbed drums on early Beatles or Tony Sheridan recordings issued for the US market in 1964. Researchers consider the Sheridan-related claim plausible and the Beatles-proper claim unsupported. Either way, the legend is part of his persona. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_performed_on_Beatles_recordings
- He was inducted into the Modern Drummer Hall of Fame in 2013 and in 2024 received the inaugural Lifetime Achievement Award at the UK Drum Show in Liverpool. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Purdie

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Deep Funk**. Three options have a real case for "Touch Me Again":

- **Deep Funk (keep)** — Strongest fit. The book's definition of deep funk is "less a sound than a commercial fate," and Lialeh is a textbook example: an obscure 1974 LP on a one-album label, attached to a film almost no one could legally see, rediscovered decades later by crate-diggers and reissued by Light in the Attic. "Touch Me Again" specifically went unsampled and unfeatured for decades, so the rediscovery framing applies even more cleanly here than to the title track. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack
- **Classic Funk (alternate)** — Reasonable. Unlike the instrumental jazz-funk title track, "Touch Me Again" is a vocal-led slow funk ballad (Hewitt lead, three backing singers). It sits closer to the vocal funk vocabulary than to the instrumental jazz-funk tradition. The catch: the book's classic-funk definition leans on commercial-pipeline music with major-label distribution, which Lialeh decisively was not.
- **Jazz-Funk (weaker than for the title track)** — The personnel argue for jazz-funk on the album as a whole, but on this specific track the band pulls back, the soloists step aside, and the song lives or dies on the vocal. The jazz session-player DNA is still present (especially in the flute), but the track doesn't really showcase it.
- **Soul Jazz (weakest)** — AllMusic does tag the album under soul jazz, but per the book's working definition, soul jazz is a smaller-ensemble, earlier-era style. Lialeh as a whole is bigger and later. And "Touch Me Again" is a vocal feature, not an instrumental organ-trio side. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/lialeh-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0000694751

Recommendation: keep **Deep Funk**. The rediscovery story is the strongest framing for "Touch Me Again" specifically.
