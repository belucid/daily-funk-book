### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current `writing theme` is: *"Some deep funk wasn't even released until after the band was rediscovered."* That is exactly the story of this album and is a clean fit. Possible alternatives or refinements:

- **"The basement that saved an album."** Lean directly into Bruce Thompson, the keyboardist who held onto the master reels for 35 years in a south-Chicago basement until Numero Group stumbled on them while hunting for an unrelated Chocolate Sunday 45. Of more than 20 songs the band attempted at Universal Studios in Chicago in late 1974, only six tracks survived intact when the reels were transferred — the rest literally flaked off the tape. The current theme generalizes about deep funk; this version names the people and the accident. Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- **"A love song Warren wrote in 1965, recorded in 1974, released in 2009."** *Gone* isn't really sequel material — it's a vehicle for tainted love songs Dale Warren had written as far back as his 1965 Shrine/Motown days. "The Best Of Good Love Gone" is the opener and arguably the cleanest distillation of that 44-year delay between writing and release. The track also opens the record, which is a useful editorial frame. Source: https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- **"Not a sequel."** *Ghetto: Misfortune's Wealth* is a concept album about Black poverty; *Gone* is a record of love songs with Princess Hearn's vocals over wood blocks and "haunted voices." Use the contrast — same band name, same arranger, completely different emotional territory — to ask what a "second album" even means when it arrives 35 years late. Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "The Best Of Good Love Gone" is the opening track of *Gone: The Promises of Yesterday* and runs 5:40 — Source: https://24-caratblack.bandcamp.com/album/gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- Numero Group describes the track as a "moody and meandering burner with a heavy Afro-jazz undercurrent," featuring Princess Hearn's vocals over wood blocks and "haunted voices" — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- The track has been sampled at least once in the post-release era — German producer Knowsum used it for "Homo Ludens" — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/634834/Knowsum-Homo-Ludens-The-24-Carat-Black-The-Best-of-Good-Love-Gone/ and https://www.whosampled.com/The-24-Carat-Black/The-Best-of-Good-Love-Gone/
- The song is reportedly a re-working of a love song Dale Warren had originally written as far back as 1965 during his time at Shrine Records (D.C.) and Motown — meaning the composition predates the 1974 recording by nearly a decade and predates the 2009 release by 44 years — Source: https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday

#### Album

- *Gone: The Promises of Yesterday* was released July 28, 2009 by The Numero Group, catalog NUM025 (CD)/NUM025LP (vinyl) — Source: https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday and https://24-caratblack.bandcamp.com/album/gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- The album is six tracks, ~37 minutes total: "The Best Of Good Love Gone" (5:40), "I Want To Make Up" (6:39), "I Don't Love You" (2:17), "I'll Never Let You Go" (5:11), "Gone The Promises Of Yesterday" (5:57), "I Begin To Weep" (11:49) — Source: https://24-caratblack.bandcamp.com/album/gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- The recordings were tracked at Universal Studios in Chicago in late 1974 by a partly replaced lineup (the original group had fractured after *Ghetto*'s commercial failure). Bruce Thompson — a Wurlitzer demonstrator Warren had recruited from the Dixie Square Mall outlet in Harvey, IL, and former Raw Umber member — was Warren's protégé and de facto bandleader for the sessions — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- Warren abandoned the project when the studio invoice arrived; the master reels stayed in Bruce Thompson's basement on the south side of Chicago for 35 years — Source: https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- Numero Group discovered the tapes "only by complete fluke, while on the hunt for a Thompson-produced Chocolate Sunday 45." Of 20+ songs, only six tracks survived the transfer — most reels physically deteriorated; on the final transfer pass the tape fell apart as it ran — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- Numero positions the record as explicitly *not* a sequel to *Ghetto*: gone are the urban sermons, replaced by "dusky, sensuous re-workings of tainted love songs" Warren had originally written at Shrine Records in D.C. and Motown beginning in 1965 — Source: https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday

#### Artist

- 24-Carat Black formed in 1971 in Cincinnati, Ohio, evolving out of the Sawyer Junior High teen group The Ditalians (founded 1965). Princess Hearn joined as lead singer at age 17 with her sister Patricia Hearn and friend Kathleen Dent after a local talent show — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-Carat_Black and https://www.cincyblackmusicwalkoffame.org/artists/24-carat-black
- Producer/arranger Dale Warren (b. September 27, 1943, Detroit; d. February 3, 1994, Gwinnett County, GA) was a conservatory-trained violinist, the arranger behind Isaac Hayes' *Hot Buttered Soul*, and conductor of the Wattstax '72 Orchestra. He never lived to see the *Gone* recordings released — he died 15 years before Numero issued them — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Warren
- Warren married lead vocalist Princess Hearn during the *Ghetto* era — she sang on both the 1973 album and the 1974 *Gone* sessions, then left music after the band collapsed to take a receptionist job at a Cincinnati real-estate company — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-Carat_Black
- A third archival release, *III*, followed in 2020 on Numero, again from previously unheard material — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-Carat_Black

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is `Deep Funk`, which is the strongest fit. Alternatives:

- **Deep Funk (recommended, current).** The book defines deep funk as "less a sound than a commercial fate" — overlooked on release, found later via DJs and sampling. This album takes that definition to its extreme: the music wasn't even released at the time. It sat in a basement for 35 years and was rescued by Numero Group, the textbook deep-funk reissue label. Source: https://www.popmatters.com/24-carat-black-2649015816.html and https://numerogroup.com/products/24-carat-black-gone-the-promises-of-yesterday
- **Jazz-Funk.** Numero specifically describes "The Best Of Good Love Gone" as having "a heavy Afro-jazz undercurrent." The album is long-form, instrumentally textured, light on radio-ready vocal hooks, and built around Warren's orchestral instincts — a reasonable secondary tag. The book's jazz-funk definition (longer track lengths, room for improvisation, rhythm section supporting soloists) fits this track better than most of the album. Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/24-carat-black-misfortune-misunderstood
- **Soul Jazz.** A weaker option, but the orchestral/string instincts and the love-song lineage (Warren's Shrine/Motown writing from 1965) pull toward soul. The book uses soul jazz mostly for smaller-ensemble earlier work, so this is a softer match than the two above.
