Most listeners know Aretha Franklin as a master interpreter of other's songs. Today's "Rock Steady" is different. It's an Aretha original, written, arranged, and steered by Aretha herself. Bassist Chuck Rainey has said the released version is actually her demo, recorded on February 15, 1971, at Criteria Studios in Miami before producers Jerry Wexler and Tom Dowd arrived. 

"Rock Steady" was the B-side of "Oh Me Oh My (I'm a Fool for You Baby)," but it found its own audience and outperformed the A-side, reaching #9 on the *Billboard* Hot 100 and #2 on the soul singles chart.

### Open Research Questions

**Open: How did "Oh Me Oh My (I'm a Fool for You Baby)" perform on the charts?**

Resolution: "Oh Me Oh My (I'm a Fool for You Baby)" peaked at **#73 on the Billboard Hot 100** and **#9 on the Billboard Best Selling Soul Singles (R&B) chart**. So while it was the intended A-side of the Atlantic 2838 single released October 11, 1971, it was dramatically out-performed by its own B-side, "Rock Steady" (#9 Hot 100, #2 Soul). The classic case of a B-side eating the A-side's lunch. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Steady_(Aretha_Franklin_song) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Me_Oh_My_(I%27m_a_Fool_for_You_Baby)

Useful context the author may want for the entry: Aretha's "Oh Me Oh My" was itself a cover of a Lulu song (same title) written by Jim Doris. Lulu's 1970 original peaked at #22 on the Billboard Hot 100, so even with Lulu's head start and Aretha's star power, the Aretha A-side underperformed both the Lulu original and its own B-side. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Me_Oh_My_(I%27m_a_Fool_for_You_Baby)

Side note on A/B discrepancy: Wikipedia's two articles contradict each other on which side was A and which was B. The "Rock Steady" article and multiple vinyl collector sources (Classic 45s, Dusty Groove, 45cat, Sessiondays) all support the entry body's claim that "Rock Steady" was the B-side. The "Oh Me Oh My" Wikipedia article says the opposite and should probably be treated as the erroneous source. The clearest signal: the B-side-becomes-the-hit story only makes sense if Rock Steady was the B-side, which matches what Atlantic's catalog numbering and the chart trajectory both suggest.

Separate ambiguity noted during original research: whether the track should be tagged by Aretha's Detroit base (US - Michigan), the Miami studio where it was cut (US - Florida), or Atlantic's New York home (US - New York). Resolution proposed under Region Suggestions.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Aretha the funk composer."** Most listeners know her as an interpreter of other people's songs. Rock Steady is one of the rare tracks she wrote, arranged, and steered herself, including the scratch piano part the band followed. A theme that reframes her as a songwriter, not just a voice. (Source: https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2012/08/09/stories-behind-the-songs-chuck-rainey/)

2. **"The demo that became the hit."** Chuck Rainey says the released version is actually a demo cut before Wexler and Dowd arrived at Criteria, and the first take at that. A theme built around the happy accident of spontaneity: the room caught lightning before anyone tried to produce it. (Source: https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2012/08/09/stories-behind-the-songs-chuck-rainey/)

3. **"What a bass player hears."** Rainey's "patting" technique, borrowed from Bill Doggett's Hammond left hand, gives the bassline its weight. A theme that zooms in on one musician's craft choice and lets it open up the whole track.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Focus on Chuck Rainey's bass. He 'patted' the strings with the heel of his hand to mimic an organist's left-hand pulse. Can you hear the slight flam, the percussive thump arriving a hair before the note?"

2. "This was the first take, cut as a demo before the producers walked in. Listen for the looseness, the little ragged edges the band never got to polish out. Does it sound like a demo to you, or does that freshness sound like the whole point?"

3. "Count the rhythm section: Purdie on drums, Rainey on bass, Cornell Dupree on guitar, Donny Hathaway on organ, Dr. John on percussion. Pick one player and follow them the whole way through. What's the single decision they make that most defines the groove?"

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- Rock Steady was released as the B-side of "Oh Me Oh My (I'm a Fool for You Baby)" and still climbed to #9 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #2 on the Hot Soul Singles chart on its own strength. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Steady_(Aretha_Franklin_song)
- Dr. John plays percussion on the track, alongside Robert "Pops" Popwell. — Source: https://stereogum.com/2010954/aretha-franklin-rock-steady-samples/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- Chuck Rainey says the released recording is a demo, cut as the first take before producers Jerry Wexler and Tom Dowd even arrived at Criteria. — Source: https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2012/08/09/stories-behind-the-songs-chuck-rainey/
- Rainey developed his "patting" bass technique in 1962 while working with organist Bill Doggett, trying to mimic a Hammond's left-hand pulse through the Leslie. — Source: https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2012/08/09/stories-behind-the-songs-chuck-rainey/
- Sampled by EPMD on "I'm Housin'" (1988), Public Enemy on "Miuzi Weighs A Ton" (1987), and Scritti Politti on "Wood Beez (Pray Like Aretha Franklin)" (1984), among more than 100 documented samples. — Source: https://stereogum.com/2010954/aretha-franklin-rock-steady-samples/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- Covered as reggae by The Marvels in 1971, and later by Prince (Indigo Nights, 2007) and Hall & Oates (Our Kind of Soul, 2004). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Steady_(Aretha_Franklin_song)
- Written by Aretha alone, with producer Arif Mardin calling the session "a dream come true." — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/aretha-franklin/rock-steady
- Recorded February 15, 1971 at Criteria Studios, Miami. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Steady_(Aretha_Franklin_song)

### Album
- Young, Gifted and Black was recorded across a long span, August 12, 1970 through February 16, 1971, at Atlantic Studios (NYC) and Criteria (Miami). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young,_Gifted_and_Black
- It was Aretha's 18th studio album, peaked at #11 on the Billboard 200 and #2 on the R&B chart, and won her the 1972 Grammy for Best Female R&B Vocal Performance. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young,_Gifted_and_Black
- Rolling Stone placed it at #388 on the 2020 edition of the 500 Greatest Albums list. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young,_Gifted_and_Black
- The title track is Aretha's adaptation of the Nina Simone / Weldon Irvine composition. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young,_Gifted_and_Black

### Artist
- Aretha Louise Franklin was born March 25, 1942 at 406 Lucy Avenue, Memphis, Tennessee, and was raised in Detroit, where her father C. L. Franklin pastored New Bethel Baptist Church. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretha_Franklin
- She died August 16, 2018 at her home in Detroit, of advanced pancreatic cancer of the neuroendocrine type, at age 76. — Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/aretha-franklin-queen-of-soul-respect-singer-dead-at-76-today-2018-08-16/
- Rock Steady is one of relatively few originals Aretha composed solo, in a catalog built largely on definitive reinterpretations. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Steady_(Aretha_Franklin_song)
