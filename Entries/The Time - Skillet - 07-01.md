As one of my favorite bands, The Time were always going to show up in this book. Today’s pick finds them in an unexpected decade and an unexpected genre: the funk-rock track “Skillet”, from their lesser-known 1990 album *Pandemonium*.

Guitarist Jesse Johnson, born June 1, 1960, wrote “Skillet” with bandmates Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, with no involvement from Prince. By 1990, Johnson had stepped out from under Prince’s purple shadow and had released three solo albums. The guitar sound here is all him.

The track inspired the album cover: all seven band members stand inside a cast-iron skillet on a burner. Photographer Jeff Katz shot it with practical effects in the pre-Photoshop era.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

Current writing theme: "out of time, out of sound"

1. **"Cooking up something different"**: The entire Pandemonium album is built around a cooking metaphor, from the album cover (band standing in a flame-encircled cast-iron skillet) to the "Cooking Class" skit that leads directly into "Skillet" with the chant "We don't need no microwave!" This track is literally the dish the album is named after visually. The cooking conceit gives you a fun framework to talk about The Time reinventing themselves.

2. **"Jesse Johnson's skillet"**: "Skillet" is one of two tracks on Pandemonium (alongside "Blondie") that Jesse Johnson wrote and used to inject his funk-rock guitar style into the album. By 1990, Johnson had released three solo albums and was an accomplished artist in his own right. This track is essentially Johnson's stamp on the reunion, a reminder that The Time was more than Prince's puppet show.

3. **"The reunion album nobody expected"**: Pandemonium was only made because Warner Bros. shelved the Morris Day/Jerome Benton project Corporate World and demanded the original seven members reunite as a condition for financing Prince's Graffiti Bridge film. The band that was never supposed to get back together made their most collaborative and best-selling album. "Skillet" represents the non-Prince side of that reunion, a track written entirely by band members.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Skillet" was written by Harris (Jimmy Jam), Johnson (Jesse), and Lewis (Terry), making it entirely a band composition with no Prince involvement. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemonium_(The_Time_album))
- The track directly inspired the album's iconic cover art: the seven band members standing in a flame-encircled cast-iron skillet with utensils and condiments orbiting around them. The cover was shot by photographer Jeff Katz and created practically, before digital manipulation was common. — Source: [Goldie's Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/related-artists/pandemonium/), [Rock and Roll Globe](https://rockandrollglobe.com/funk/are-you-ready-to-party-up-in-here-pandemonium-at-30/)
- "Skillet" follows a comedic skit called "Cooking Class" where the band chants "We don't need no microwave!" The cooking metaphor runs throughout the album as a unifying concept. — Source: [Rock and Roll Globe](https://rockandrollglobe.com/funk/are-you-ready-to-party-up-in-here-pandemonium-at-30/)
- Morris Day uses the track to parody contemporary R&B trends, reportedly mocking Neneh Cherry and The Good Girls. — Source: [Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/music/Morris+Day+and+The+Time/_/Morris+Day+&+The+Time+-+Skillet)
- The track was mixed "in The Skillet for more sizzle, pure hiss and true distortion," a studio named after the track/concept. — Source: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/pandemonium-mw0000203698)
- A French comedy group, Lol Et Le Groupe, covered "Skillet" as the theme for the TV show "Les Nuls, L'émission." — Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/cover/700118/Lol-Et-Le-Groupe-Les-Nuls,-L'%C3%A9mission-(G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rique)-The-Time-Skillet/)

#### Album
- Pandemonium was originally going to be a different album called Corporate World, written entirely by Prince for just Morris Day and Jerome Benton. Warner Bros. shelved it and demanded the original seven members reunite as a condition for financing Prince's film Graffiti Bridge. — Source: [Goldie's Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/related-artists/pandemonium/)
- Prince wrote and performed all instruments on five of the fifteen tracks ("Jerk Out," "Donald Trump (Black Version)," "Chocolate," "Data Bank," "My Summertime Thang"). The remaining nine tracks were co-written by The Time members themselves. — Source: [Goldie's Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/related-artists/pandemonium/)
- "Jerk Out," the lead single, was originally recorded at Sunset Sound in 1982 for Ice Cream Castle but was shelved for nearly a decade before being reworked for Pandemonium. It became The Time's biggest hit: #1 R&B, #9 Pop. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemonium_(The_Time_album))
- The album opens with "Dreamland," a recording of the band's introduction from their October 2, 1987 Minnesota Black Music Awards reunion performance, their first time together in years. — Source: [Goldie's Parade](https://goldiesparade.co.uk/discography/related-artists/pandemonium/)
- Pandemonium was certified Gold (500,000 units) by the RIAA and outsold all previous Time albums. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemonium_(The_Time_album))
- Dutch saxophonist Candy Dulfer appears on the album. She was 20 years old at the time and had just released her Grammy-nominated debut Saxuality. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemonium_(The_Time_album))
- Prince co-wrote "Donald Trump (Black Version)" according to biographer Matt Thorne, though he is not credited. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemonium_(The_Time_album))
- The album was recorded across six studios: Flyte Tyme Productions, Jungle Love Studios, Paisley Park Studios, Sunset Sound, The Skillet, and The Time Studios. — Source: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/pandemonium-mw0000203698)

#### Artist
- The Time was created by Prince in 1981, inspired by the film The Idolmaker, as an outlet for funk-pop material while he explored other directions. The band grew from an existing Minneapolis group called Flyte Tyme. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))
- By 1990, every original member had become a successful producer or artist in their own right. Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis had become one of the most successful songwriting/production teams of the 1980s and 1990s (Janet Jackson, etc.). Jesse Johnson had three solo albums. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))
- The original project name "Corporate World" became ironic: Prince's fictional band had become real industry players who no longer needed him. — Background knowledge, but no source found.
- Jellybean Johnson, the band's drummer, died on November 21, 2025, at age 69, just two days after his birthday. — Source: [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/jellybean-johnson-the-time-prince-dead-obituary-1235471010/)
- A notorious backstage food fight erupted during the 1982 Controversy Tour due to tensions between The Time and Prince. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))
- Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis were fired (or left) in 1983 after missing a show due to a blizzard while producing the S.O.S. Band in Atlanta. Prince later clarified that Morris Day had requested his advice about their absence. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))
- Jerome Benton's official role in the band was "valet/dancer," not a traditional musician credit. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))
- The band later rebranded as "The Original 7ven" due to a trademark dispute with the Prince Estate. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))

### Genre Suggestions

Current genre: Funk Rock

1. **Funk Rock** (keep current): This is the strongest fit. "Skillet" is built around Jesse Johnson's heavy guitar riffs fused with a funk rhythm section. The track has been listed on funk rock compilations and multiple sources describe it as "funk-rock" or even "funk metal." The On Genres definition notes that "most funk rock entries come from the style's surge in popularity in the 1980s and 1990s," which fits Pandemonium's 1990 release perfectly. — Sources: [Rock and Roll Globe](https://rockandrollglobe.com/funk/are-you-ready-to-party-up-in-here-pandemonium-at-30/), [4thefunk.com](http://4thefunk.com/TheTime/story6.html)

2. **Minneapolis Sound**: The Time is one of the defining acts of the Minneapolis Sound, and Pandemonium was recorded at Paisley Park and Flyte Tyme in Minneapolis. However, "Skillet" specifically leans harder into rock guitar than the synth-driven, drum-machine-inflected Minneapolis Sound template. The genre fits the artist better than it fits this particular track. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band))

3. **Classic Funk**: This would be a stretch. While The Time has deep roots in classic funk, this 1990 track falls outside the 1968-1977 era defined in the genre notes, and its rock guitar emphasis pushes it away from classic funk's core sound.

**Recommendation**: Funk Rock is the right call. It's exactly what the entry body describes as an unexpected genre choice for The Time, which makes it editorially interesting.
