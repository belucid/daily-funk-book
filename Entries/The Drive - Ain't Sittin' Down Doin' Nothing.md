### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is currently empty.

1. **"The two weeks that ended an era."** ElectricJive reports that Henry Sithole and Bunny Luthuli died in the Tzaneen car accident *just two weeks after* finishing their last album. *Drive Unlimited* came out two years earlier, and "Ain't Sittin' Down Doin' Nothin'" — a Luthuli composition that is literally a manifesto of *keep moving* — becomes unbearably poignant when read against that two-week window. The theme is about the cruel acceleration of the late-'70s SA jazz scene: a band on the brink of overseas dates, cut down on a Transvaal road. Source: http://electricjive.blogspot.com/2016/11/a-tribute-to-henry-sithole-and-bunny.html
2. **"South Africa's first soul-jazz band."** Multiple SA jazz writers (Pan-African Music, sisgwenjazz, LondonJazzNews) describe The Drive as exactly that. The angle is the *invention* of a local idiom — Heshoo Beshoo's horn section plus Bunny Luthuli's guitar walking out of hard bop and into Soweto's dancefloor on their own terms, under apartheid radio censorship. Source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/the-drive-can-you-feel-it/
3. **"Bheki Mseleku at twenty."** Drive Unlimited was likely one of Mseleku's first studio sessions — he had only just moved to Johannesburg in 1975 to play organ for Spirits Rejoice. The track sits at the genesis of a player who would eventually be Mercury-nominated and Verve-signed. The theme is "where the giants started." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheki_Mseleku

### Listening Mission Suggestions

The `listening mission` field is currently empty.

1. The horn section in The Drive is the same horn section as the Heshoo Beshoo Group — the Sithole brothers, Henry on alto and Stanley on tenor. Listen for how the two saxophones move together: are they playing the same line, or are they answering each other? Try to hear the moment one brother hands off to the other.
2. Bunny Luthuli wrote this track and plays guitar on it. He will be dead in less than two years. Listen specifically for his guitar — when does it lead, when does it lay back behind the horns, and what do you hear it doing in the last minute?
3. This is a 1975 South African band on RCA, recorded in Johannesburg under apartheid. Compare the rhythm section's pocket to a contemporaneous American funk track you know. Where does Tony Sauli's bass sit relative to Nelson Magwaza's drums, and how is that different from, say, Jamerson-and-Benjamin or Bootsy-and-Jab'o?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Bunny Luthuli wrote "Ain't Sittin' Down Doin' Nothin'" — he is also the band's founder and guitarist. The title is essentially a band motto. Source: http://www.flatinternational.org/template_volume.php?volume_id=400
- The track was rescued from obscurity by Strut's *Next Stop Soweto 4* compilation in March 2015, forty years after its original 1975 release on RCA Victor in South Africa. Source: https://nextstopsoweto.bandcamp.com/track/aint-sittin-down-doin-nothing
- Tracklist durations differ very slightly between the original 1975 LP (6:06 per Discogs master 2617514) and the 2015 Strut reissue (6:02 on Bandcamp/Spotify). A small but real source-to-source discrepancy. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/2617514-The-Drive-2-Drive-Unlimited and https://open.spotify.com/track/78ccO3abWNbCBt0NRhfHb9

#### Album

- *Drive Unlimited* (RCA Victor, RCL 1200, May 1975) was The Drive's fourth record. Its tracklist mixes Bunny Luthuli compositions, Henry Sithole compositions, Stanley Sithole's "Spiritual Prayer," and three pieces by a then-twenty-year-old Bheki Mseleku — including the closer "I Have A Dream." Source: http://www.flatinternational.org/template_volume.php?volume_id=400
- The album was produced by David Thekwane and engineered by Nino Rivera at the RCA-owned Manley van Niekerk Studios in Johannesburg. Sources: http://www.flatinternational.org/template_volume.php?volume_id=400 and https://pan-african-music.com/en/the-drive-can-you-feel-it/
- The Discogs master is categorised as Jazz / Funk / Soul with styles Jazz-Funk, Soul-Jazz, and Modal — so multiple credible sources resist a strict "Afrobeat" classification for this material. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/2617514-The-Drive-2-Drive-Unlimited
- *Drive Unlimited* was reissued on vinyl in 2022 by Canadian label We Are Busy Bodies, the same label that reissued the 1975 *Can You Feel It* in 2021. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/23086922-The-Drive-Drive-Unlimited

#### Artist

- The Drive was formed in 1971 when guitarist Adolphus "Bunny" Luthuli pulled the Sithole brothers (Henry on alto, Stanley on tenor/baritone) and drummer Nelson Magwaza out of the Heshoo Beshoo Group to enter the Alco Best Band Competition at Jabulani Stadium in Soweto. They won first prize. Source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/heshoo-beshoo-armitage-road-feature
- The band recorded for Atlas City in 1974 and then made six further albums for RCA across the 1970s — they were, by most accounts, the most-touring soul-jazz act in South Africa during the period. Source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/heshoo-beshoo-armitage-road-feature
- On May 5, 1977, Bunny Luthuli and Henry Sithole were killed in a car accident in the Tzaneen area of northern Transvaal — only two weeks after recording the band's final album, and right as international touring opportunities were being arranged. Sources: http://electricjive.blogspot.com/2016/11/a-tribute-to-henry-sithole-and-bunny.html and https://pan-african-music.com/en/the-drive-can-you-feel-it/
- Surviving members went on to seed the next generation of South African jazz: Bheki Mseleku in particular (born 3 March 1955, Durban) became Mercury-Prize-nominated and recorded for Verve, having first cut his teeth on this album and in Spirits Rejoice in 1975. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheki_Mseleku
- *Drive Unlimited*'s lineup is a literal who's-who of '70s SA jazz: Henry Sithole (alto sax), Stanley Sithole (tenor/baritone sax), David Sithole (trumpet), Bunny Luthuli (guitar), Bheki Mseleku (synth/electric piano/Hammond), Tony Sauli (bass), Nelson Magwaza (drums/percussion/congas). Source: http://www.flatinternational.org/template_volume.php?volume_id=400

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently `Afrobeat`. Per `Matter/On Genres.md`, Afrobeat in this book is "a broad label for any funk rooted in Africa." That is defensible on grounds of geography alone, but the *musical* content of "Ain't Sittin' Down Doin' Nothin'" sits closer to American soul-jazz/jazz-funk than to Fela's Afrobeat lineage, and the band itself is described by every source as a "soul-jazz" group.

1. **Jazz-funk** — Best musical fit. Per the book's own definition, jazz-funk is "funk played by jazz musicians whose earlier careers were in more traditional jazz styles" with "longer track lengths" and "room for extended improvisation." The Drive came directly out of the hard-bop-adjacent Heshoo Beshoo Group, and the album opens with a 7:23 instrumental — exactly that profile. Discogs styles: Jazz-Funk. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/2617514-The-Drive-2-Drive-Unlimited
2. **Afrobeat** (current) — Defensible only on the book's "any funk rooted in Africa" expansion. Stylistically a stretch: there is no Tony-Allen-style polyrhythm, no Yoruba percussion language, no Fela-style horn unison/call-and-response over a slow-burn vamp. Source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/the-drive-can-you-feel-it/
3. **Deep Funk** — Plausible secondary case. The Drive's catalog is the archetypal "delayed-recognition" story: regional African pressings rescued by crate-diggers (Strut's *Next Stop Soweto*) and later by reissue labels (We Are Busy Bodies). Per the book, deep funk is "less a sound than a commercial fate" — and this fate is exactly that. Source: https://strut-records.co.uk/products/various-artists-next-stop-soweto-4-zulu-rock-afro-disco-mbaqanga-1975-1985

Recommendation: switch to **Jazz-funk**, possibly retain Afrobeat as a secondary tag if the book supports multi-value genre. The field is locked, so this is a suggestion only.
