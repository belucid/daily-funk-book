They say imitation is the sincerest form of flattery. They also say good artists copy and great artists steal. If both sentiments are true, Herb Alpert should feel flattered, and you can decide whether Tom Browne is a good trumpet player or a great one.

Today’s song is “Forever More” from Browne’s 1980 album *Love Approach*, and you will quickly hear more than an echo of yesterday’s “Rise.” Both tracks place a languid, deeply melodic trumpet line over insistent handclaps and a groove balanced on the edge of disco.

But “Forever More” arrived a year later, after the disco backlash had hit with full force. Just before the 1m 30s mark, the arrangement breaks down to drums alone, then adds back in the bass, reminding listeners how funky the underlying groove is and how little the track depends on disco’s trademark strings and four on the floor beat.

We hear what you did there, Mr. Browne.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **Existing theme — "Similarity to yesterday's Rise by Alpert":** Well supported (see Open Research Questions). "Forever More" is a mellow, trumpet-forward instrumental that leans on the same slow-burn, atmospheric-groove template Herb Alpert rode to a No. 1 with "Rise" in 1979. The entry sits at 10-21, one day after the Herb Alpert "Rise" entry (10-20), so the "day after" pairing is deliberate and works.
- **"The Jamaica, Queens jazz-funk circle":** Nearly everyone who shaped this track came out of a few square miles of southeast Queens — Tom Browne (Jamaica), writer Don Blackman (Jamaica), arranger Weldon Irvine (St. Albans), keyboardist Bernard Wright (Jamaica). The album's hit even shouts out the neighborhood ("Funkin' for Jamaica (N.Y.)"). "Forever More" is a quiet product of that hyper-local scene.
- **"The physics major who out-funked the funk bands":** Browne earned a physics degree (Kingsborough) while gigging the NYC jazz scene, then, as a jazz trumpeter on a producers' label (GRP), delivered a No. 1 R&B album. An angle about the "wrong" background producing the right groove.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Forever More" was written by Don Blackman and arranged by Weldon Irvine — Browne is the trumpet voice but not the composer. Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/889788 (credits) / https://somehowjazz.com/tom-browne-forever/
- Critics hear it as a near-homage to Herb Alpert's "Rise" (1979): "tracks like 'Forever More' ape the sound that Herb Alpert had perfected the previous year." Source: https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/album/tom-browne-love-approach/
- The instrumental had an odd second life as broadcast-day open/close music on NBC affiliate WCMH-TV in Columbus, Ohio, in the mid-1980s. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Approach
- It became crate-digging fodder for West Coast rap: sampled on records including B.G. Knocc Out & Dresta's "Compton Hoe" (1995), YGM Geez's "Streets of Compton" (1993), and others. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Tom-Browne/Forever-More/sampled/

#### Album

- *Love Approach* hit No. 1 on both the US R&B Albums and US Jazz charts and reached No. 18 on the Billboard 200. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Approach
- The lead single, "Funkin' for Jamaica (N.Y.)," topped the US R&B chart for three weeks in October 1980 and remains Browne's signature hit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkin%27_for_Jamaica_(N.Y.)
- The session bench is remarkable for a "producers' label" jazz-funk date: Marcus Miller (bass), Omar Hakim and Buddy Williams (drums), Bobby Broom (guitar), Bernard Wright (keys), produced by Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen. Recorded 1979–80 at A&R Studios, NYC. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Approach

#### Artist

- Tom Browne earned a degree in physics (Kingsborough Community College) while studying trumpet at the Manhattan School of Music and working the NYC jazz scene. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- His first professional gig, in 1975, was with keyboardist/arranger Weldon Irvine — the same Irvine who arranged "Forever More" five years later. Source: https://www.jazzapedia.com/artists/tom-browne
- Browne grew up in Jamaica, Queens, shaped by Count Basie and James Brown — the neighborhood he immortalized on "Funkin' for Jamaica (N.Y.)." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (current value) — strongly recommended.** The track is funk played by a jazz trumpeter on a jazz-fusion producers' label (GRP), foregrounding a melodic horn lead over a funk groove — the textbook definition in the book's own subgenre notes ("funk played by jazz musicians"). *Love Approach* topped the Jazz chart. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Approach , Discogs styles list "Jazz-Funk, Funk, Disco" https://www.discogs.com/master/158930-Tom-Browne-Love-Approach
- **Classic Funk — secondary/weak.** The album sat squarely in the 1980 commercial pipeline (major distribution, No. 1 R&B, radio), which fits the "central sound in mainstream American music" spirit, but the book draws Classic Funk as roughly 1969–1977 and reserves it for tracks whose primary identity is funk rather than funk-fused-with-jazz. That era and identity test argues against it here.
- Verdict: keep **Jazz-Funk**; it is unambiguous.
