*Blood Sugar Sex Magik* was released on September 24, 1991, the same day Nirvana released *Nevermind*. It is hard not to treat that date as a pivotal point, with a decade's defining sounds arriving all at once.

Legendary producer Rick Rubin helped strip away the metal-leaning guitars of the Chili Peppers' previous record, *Mother’s Milk*, and pushed the band toward something rawer and leaner, with funk-rock back at its center. The bassist Flea did not think the iconic bassline was anything special at first. His bandmates overruled him, and Rubin convinced Flea to simplify it further for the final recording.

As a bonus listen, try "Bedrock Anthem," Weird Al Yankovic's 1993 Flintstones-themed parody of "Give It Away" and "Under the Bridge." Does Weird Al keep it funky?

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The jacket in the closet"** — The song's philosophy of radical generosity came from a single moment: Anthony Kiedis admiring a jacket in Nina Hagen's closet and her immediately giving it to him, telling him "if you have a closet full of clothes and you try to keep them all, your life will get very small." The entry could frame "Give It Away" as a song built on one small act of giving, and explore how a personal philosophy became a funk-rock anthem.

2. **"Born in someone else's band"** — The riff that became "Give It Away" was originally written during jam sessions for H.A.T.E., a short-lived side project featuring Flea, Frusciante, and Fishbone's Angelo Moore. When H.A.T.E. disbanded, the riff came home. The entry could explore how the song's most recognizable element was a transplant, and Flea himself nearly discarded the bassline, calling it "just another bassline."

3. **"The day the '90s started"** — Blood Sugar Sex Magik dropped on September 24, 1991, the exact same day as Nirvana's Nevermind. Two albums from two bands, released simultaneously, both reshaping rock music. The entry could frame "Give It Away" as the funkier side of alternative rock's big bang.

#### Album
- Blood Sugar Sex Magik was recorded at The Mansion in the Hollywood Hills, a reportedly haunted estate that Rick Rubin owned. The band lived in the house during recording (except Chad Smith, who found the place too creepy). Frusciante said "there are definitely ghosts in the house" but described them as "very friendly." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Sugar_Sex_Magik
- The album was released on September 24, 1991, the exact same day as Nirvana's Nevermind. Both albums reshaped the rock landscape of the 1990s. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Sugar_Sex_Magik
- About 25 songs were written during the sessions, with 17 making the final cut. The album runs 73 minutes and 56 seconds. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Sugar_Sex_Magik
- The album has sold over 14 million copies worldwide and is certified 7x Platinum in the US. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Sugar_Sex_Magik
- Rick Rubin stripped back the heavy metal guitar layering of the previous album Mother's Milk, pushing the band toward a rawer, more melodic funk-rock sound. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/blood-sugar-sex-magik-mw0000265082

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Funk Rock** (current value) — This is the correct and strongest fit. "Give It Away" is built on Flea's funk bass and Chad Smith's hard-hitting drums, layered with Frusciante's distorted, Hendrix-influenced guitar. AllMusic categorizes the album under "Funk Metal" and "Dance-Rock" alongside alternative rock. The song's DNA is a funk groove driving a rock song, which is the definition of funk rock. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/blood-sugar-sex-magik-mw0000265082

2. **Psychedelic Funk** — A secondary consideration. The recording includes a Jew's harp, backward-sounding guitar effects from Frusciante, and a music video with the band painted silver in a desert landscape. The sonic textures and the surreal, incantatory repetition of the lyrics ("give it away" 68 times) carry a psychedelic quality. However, the song lacks the narrative mythology and cosmic worldbuilding that typically define psychedelic funk in this book's framework. Source: https://stereogum.com/2271681/the-alternative-number-ones-red-hot-chili-peppers-give-it-away/columns

3. **Classic Funk** — A weaker case, but worth noting. The song's core is a funk groove, and the philosophy of giving echoes James Brown-era positivity. However, "Give It Away" sits firmly in the early '90s alternative rock explosion, not the 1968-1977 classic funk window, and its rock guitar textures place it outside the classic funk sound. Not recommended.
