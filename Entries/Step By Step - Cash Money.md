### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **The family band from the block.** Johnnie Gee formed Step By Step at age nine with his brothers Jerome and Dewitt plus neighborhood kids in Milwaukee. By their mid-teens they were opening for Jackie Wilson and James Brown. The entry could frame "Cash Money" as the sound of a group who grew up onstage together, their tightness born from literal kinship.

2. **Brunswick's last roll of the dice.** By the time Step By Step signed to Brunswick in 1975, the label's owner Nat Tarnopol had just had a fraud conviction overturned on appeal, and the label's golden era with Jackie Wilson and the Chi-Lites was fading. Step By Step's album landed right next to Jackie Wilson's *Nobody But You* in the Brunswick catalog (BL-754211 and BL-754212). The entry could position "Cash Money" as a footnote from a label running out of time.

3. **Milwaukee's invisible funk.** Milwaukee rarely appears on the funk map, yet Step By Step were a genuine 12-piece ensemble with horns, harmonies, and a rhythm section deep enough to hold their own on a Brunswick Funk compilation decades later. The entry could make the case for the city as an overlooked pocket of Midwest funk.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Cash Money" is described as 'midtempo' and 'undulating, bass-heavy funk.' At nearly six minutes, it has room to breathe. Listen for how the horns and bass trade the spotlight. Who's really driving this track: the rhythm section or the horn charts?

2. Step By Step were a 12-piece ensemble, and that's a lot of voices to fit in one groove. Listen to how the arrangement keeps the track from sounding cluttered. What's playing, and just as importantly, what's staying out of the way?

3. This is a band of teenagers and twenty-somethings who grew up performing together in Milwaukee clubs. Does the track feel young and hungry, or polished and mature? What gives it away?

#### Track

#### Album
- The album *I Always Wanted To Be In The Band* was Step By Step's only release and saw little commercial success upon its original release. — Source: [Soul Brother Records](https://soulbrother.com/feature/step-by-step-i-always-wanted-to-be-in-a-band/)
- The title track later became a highly sought-after Northern Soul / Modern Soul collector's item, exchanging hands for large sums of money. DJs Floating Points and Jamie xx have played it. — Source: [Demon Music Group](https://www.demonmusicgroup.co.uk/catalogue/releases/i-always-wanted-to-be-in-the-band-140g-black-vinyl/)
- The album sits at catalog number BL-754211 in the Brunswick discography, sandwiched between Tony Valor Sounds Orchestra's *Gotta Get It* (BL-754210) and Jackie Wilson's *Nobody But You* (BL-754212). — Source: [BSN Pubs Brunswick Discography](https://www.bsnpubs.com/decca/brunswick/brunswick.html)
- Johnnie Gee claims that Frank Sinatra's personal string section from the Chicago Symphony performed all string arrangements on the album. Background knowledge from search results referencing Gee's biographical materials, but no independent source found to verify this claim. — Source: Search results referencing [GECOM Corp / The Untold Story of Johnnie Gee](https://www.gecomgroup.net/) and [Landon Buford / Medium](https://landon-buford.medium.com/find-out-how-step-by-step-band-member-johnnie-gee-has-been-able-to-adapt-in-the-music-industry-for-8f7a20600f7c) (both pages blocked from fetching; claim sourced from search result snippets only)
- The album was reissued on CD in 2008 (papersleeve edition) and on vinyl in 2022 by Demon Records on 140g eco-mix colored vinyl with reproduced original artwork. — Source: [Demon Music Group](https://www.demonmusicgroup.co.uk/catalogue/releases/i-always-wanted-to-be-in-the-band-140g-black-vinyl/)

#### Artist
- Step By Step was a 12-piece soul/funk ensemble from Milwaukee, Wisconsin, formed circa 1968-1969 by brothers Johnnie, Jerome, and Dewitt Gee along with neighborhood friends. Johnnie "Lil' Johnny" Gee was born September 29, 1959, and began drumming at age 6 before becoming the group's lead vocalist and frontman. — Source: [Landon Buford / Medium](https://landon-buford.medium.com/find-out-how-step-by-step-band-member-johnnie-gee-has-been-able-to-adapt-in-the-music-industry-for-8f7a20600f7c) (search snippet)
- In 1972, the group recorded the single "Time After Time" for Cuca Records, a regional label based in Sauk City, Wisconsin. The single featured a soaring falsetto from 13-year-old Johnnie Gee and is among the rarest singles Cuca ever produced, barely circulating beyond Wisconsin. — Source: [Numero Group](https://numerogroup.com/products/step-by-step-time-after-time-bw-now-shes-gone)
- The Cuca single caught the ear of Brunswick Records' Chicago office. In 1975, after touring with the Chi-Lites, tour manager Tim Butler and Chi-Lites member Marshall Thompson introduced the group to Brunswick CEO Nat Tarnopol, who signed them. — Source: [Landon Buford / Medium](https://landon-buford.medium.com/find-out-how-step-by-step-band-member-johnnie-gee-has-been-able-to-adapt-in-the-music-industry-for-8f7a20600f7c) (search snippet)
- During their early years, the group performed alongside Jackie Wilson, James Brown, the Chi-Lites, the Stylistics, and Ike & Tina Turner. — Source: [Landon Buford / Medium](https://landon-buford.medium.com/find-out-how-step-by-step-band-member-johnnie-gee-has-been-able-to-adapt-in-the-music-industry-for-8f7a20600f7c) (search snippet)
- The band split with Brunswick after creative disputes. Johnnie Gee enlisted in the U.S. Army in 1979, received an honorable discharge, and in 1982 founded his own label, Sidetraxx Records and Entertainment. — Source: [Landon Buford / Medium](https://landon-buford.medium.com/find-out-how-step-by-step-band-member-johnnie-gee-has-been-able-to-adapt-in-the-music-industry-for-8f7a20600f7c) (search snippet)
- Numero Group reissued the Cuca-era recordings in 2018/2024, including "Time After Time," "She's Gone," "Come This Way," and "Salt and Pepper," as part of their *Eccentric R&B: The Cuca Label* series. — Source: [Numero Group](https://numerogroup.com/products/step-by-step-time-after-time-bw-now-shes-gone)
- Cuca Records was founded by Jim Kirchstein in 1959 in Sauk City, Wisconsin, originally as a tiny recording studio in the corner of a record store, and documented undiscovered musical talent from rural Wisconsin and the Milwaukee/Chicago/Rockford corridor. — Source: [The Perlich Post](https://theperlichpost.blogspot.com/2024/05/numero-group-returns-to-soul-side-of.html)

The BSN Pubs Brunswick discography lists it as 1977. The Spotify phonogram copyright states ℗ 1977. One Discogs release entry (r2684291) lists 1976; two others (r2706749 and r9374247) list 1977. The Brunswick discography (which sequences by catalog number and tends to be accurate on release years) and the ℗ notice both point to 1977. It is possible the album was recorded in 1976 and released in 1977.

**track length:** Set to "5m 50s." Confirmed via Spotify tracklist. The Brunswick Funk compilation lists the track at 5:48, which is within normal rounding/mastering variance.

**region:** Set to "US - Midwest." Step By Step were from Milwaukee, Wisconsin. Milwaukee does not have its own region tag; US - Midwest is the correct umbrella.

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk.** "Cash Money" is a horn-driven, bass-heavy midtempo funk track released in 1977 on a major label (Brunswick), fitting squarely within the book's definition of classic funk: "music that defined the core vocabulary and was released into the commercial pipeline." Brunswick was a nationally distributed label, and the 1968-1977 timeframe matches exactly. The track's primary identity is funk, not a fusion with jazz, rock, or Latin styles.

2. **Deep Funk.** The album saw little commercial success on release and only found its audience decades later through crate-digging and DJ culture. The title track became a sought-after collector's item, and "Cash Money" was rescued from obscurity by the 2022 *Brunswick Funk* compilation. The book's definition of deep funk is "less a sound than a commercial fate," and Step By Step's story fits perfectly: regional act, poorly-selling record, delayed recognition.

3. **Classic Funk** is the strongest fit because the label (Brunswick) and era (1977) are core classic funk territory, and the track has the horns-and-bass sound profile the genre demands. **Deep Funk** is the strong runner-up because the commercial fate of this record is textbook deep funk. The author might consider which frame serves the book better: the sound, or the story.
