### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The blueprint break"** — Frame the entry around how a three-minute instrumental written by Leo Nocentelli as a replacement opener became one of the most-sampled funk records in hip-hop history (78+ documented samples on WhoSampled), foundational to artists from N.W.A. to A Tribe Called Quest. The hook is the absurd ratio of effort to influence.
- **"Naming a thing after the fact"** — Nocentelli told Songfacts the song "was named 'Cissy Strut' long after I wrote it." A writing angle on how funk titles often follow the music rather than precede it — labels for grooves that already existed on the bandstand.
- **"The Crescent City pocket"** — Use Cissy Strut as the entry point to the New Orleans pocket: how Zigaboo Modeliste's second-line-derived drumming, Porter's bass, and Neville's organ produced a four-piece sound that became the city's lingua franca and Allen Toussaint's house style at Sansu/Marsaint.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Count the bars before anything actually changes. Modeliste, Porter, and Nocentelli play almost the same figure for the entire track — what tiny variations keep it from feeling static? When does Art Neville's organ first enter, and what does it add that the trio couldn't?
- Listen specifically to Zigaboo Modeliste's hi-hat. He's playing a syncopated, almost stuttering pattern that producers have been chopping into samples for forty years. Try to clap along — where does the bar actually start?
- The riff sits in a tiny harmonic space (essentially a blues lick). Try to hum it after one listen, then notice how often that two-bar idea repeats. Why isn't this boring?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Leo Nocentelli wrote the riff specifically to replace "Hold It" as the band's standard set opener — he was sick of playing the old number every night. The title came later. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-meters/cissy-strut
- The single reached #4 on the Billboard R&B chart and #23 on the Billboard Hot 100 — making this instrumental a genuine pop crossover. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cissy_Strut
- "Cissy Strut" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2011. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cissy_Strut
- Rolling Stone placed it at #158 on the 2021 revision of "The 500 Greatest Songs of All Time," noted as one of only a handful of pure instrumentals on the list. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cissy_Strut
- According to AllMusic/Wikipedia album notes, the debut single reportedly sold 200,000 copies in two weeks. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters_(album)
- WhoSampled credits the track with 78+ samples in hip-hop alone, including N.W.A.'s "Niggaz 4 Life" (1991), A Tribe Called Quest's "Da Booty" (1998), Casual's "That's How It Is," Del the Funky Homosapien's "Same Ol' Thing" (1991), and Donald D's "Everyone's Gotta Make a Livin'." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Cissy-Strut/
- Featured in the soundtracks of *Jackie Brown*, *Red*, *King Richard*, *Another Round*, and *Legend*, plus HBO's *The Wire*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cissy_Strut
- The single's official credits list four co-writers — Art Neville, George Porter Jr., Leo Nocentelli, and Joseph Modeliste — splitting the royalties of one of the most-sampled funk records ever evenly across the original lineup. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4590165-The-Meters-Cissy-Strut

#### Album

- The Meters' self-titled debut was released May 1969 on Josie Records (catalog JOS-4010), produced by Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn, and recorded at Cosimo Studios in New Orleans. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters_(album)
- The album charted at #108 on the Billboard 200 and #23 on the R&B Albums chart — modest pop numbers that understate its outsize cultural footprint. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters_(album)
- Track 8, "6V6 LA," is named after a vacuum tube used in guitar amplifiers — a small detail that signals how much these were musicians' musicians. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters_(album)
- Discogs classifies the album under the substyle "Bayou Funk." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/109369-The-Meters

#### Artist

- The Meters formed in 1965 as the house band for Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn's Sansu Enterprises (later Marsaint Music), backing dozens of New Orleans sessions before they ever cut a record under their own name. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- The original quartet — Art Neville, Leo Nocentelli, George Porter Jr., and Joseph "Zigaboo" Modeliste — was nominated for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame four times (1996, 2012, 2013, 2017) and never inducted. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- They received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in January 2018. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- Billboard and Record World both named them Best Rhythm and Blues Instrumental Group of 1970 — meaning by year two they were already the consensus winners of the category. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- Art Neville and Cyril Neville left the band in early 1977; the Meters appeared on *Saturday Night Live* on March 19, 1977 (season 2). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (strongest case).** Per `Matter/On Genres.md`, classic funk is funk released into the commercial pipeline of major labels and chart presence, roughly 1969–1977, where the track's primary identity is funk rather than fusion. "Cissy Strut" was released March/early-1969 on a national label (Josie, distributed by Jubilee), hit #4 R&B and #23 Hot 100, and is named in nearly every history of funk as a foundational text. It sits at the exact opening edge of the classic-funk window the book defines.
- **Deep Funk (weaker but defensible).** The Meters' broader catalog became canonical to crate-digger and rare-groove culture; *Cissy Strut* itself, however, was a chart hit on release, which violates the genre's definition of "delayed recognition." I would not file this track under deep funk, but the *artist* fits the broader deep-funk vocabulary.
- **Not Latin Funk or Jazz-funk** despite occasional listicle tags — there's no Latin percussion and no jazz improvisation in the studio cut. New Orleans second-line phrasing is folded into the rhythm, but the book doesn't have a separate "New Orleans funk" genre, so Classic Funk remains the right slot.

**Suggested value: `Classic Funk`.**

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Louisiana (current).** The Meters formed in New Orleans, recorded at Cosimo Studios in New Orleans, and the song is universally identified with the city's musical lineage. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters_(album). This is the clear right call — keep as-is.
- **No serious alternative.** Josie Records was based in New York (a Jubilee subsidiary), which could in principle support a "US - New York" tag, but the book's regions section explicitly notes that the call is per-entry and represents "the track's origins." For this track, origins are unambiguously Louisiana.
