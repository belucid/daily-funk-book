In today’s song, Granny describes a “Funky Worm” who plays the guitar with no hands. The song hit #1 on the *Billboard* R&B chart and #15 on the Hot 100 in May 1973.

That “guitar” is actually an ARP Soloist synthesizer. Walter “Junie” Morrison spotted the newly released synth in a New York music shop and used it to create the riff you hear as the worm’s part. Morrison is also the voice of Granny, and his twisted sense of funky humor was influential when he later joined George Clinton’s Parliament-Funkadelic in 1977 and helped shape the P-Funk sound.

In 2023, paleontologists paid the song a perfect tribute by naming a 220-million-year-old fossil *Funcusvermis gilmorei*, literally “funky worm,” after the track they looped while collecting the fossils.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Funky Worm" reached #1 on the Billboard R&B chart and #15 on the Hot 100 in 1973, selling over one million copies and earning a gold disc from the RIAA in May 1973. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_Worm
- The track features a comedic narrative centered on a character called "Granny" who owns a magical worm that can play guitar without hands. The Granny voice was performed by keyboardist Junie Morrison, who developed the character during nightclub skits where the band would interact with audiences. The character originally had a "very dirty mouth." — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2015/08/funky-worm-by-ohio-players.html
- Morrison played the iconic synth line on an ARP Pro-Soloist, an early analog synthesizer. He reportedly found the instrument in a shop in New York City and immediately heard an "Arabian style riff" that had "worm written all over it." — Source: https://forum.vintagesynth.com/viewtopic.php?t=53096
- The song was featured on the Bounce FM radio station in *Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas* (2004), where it plays during the first cutscene of the "Cleaning The Hood" mission. — Source: https://gta-songs.fandom.com/wiki/Funky_Worm
- In 2017, the track was featured in a Google Doodle celebrating hip-hop's 44th anniversary. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_Worm
- In January 2023, paleontologists named a newly discovered 220-million-year-old fossil amphibian *Funcusvermis gilmorei* (Latin for "funky worm") after this song. The scientists at Petrified Forest National Park in Arizona played "Funky Worm" constantly while excavating the Thunderstorm Ridge bonebed where the fossil was found. — Source: https://www.daytondailynews.com/what-to-know/funky-fossil-new-discovery-named-after-ohio-players-hit/UTLUKVLA6RAWPFZFDOSAPVIM2M/
- "Funky Worm" is one of the most sampled songs in hip-hop history. The ARP synth line became a defining element of West Coast G-funk. Notable samples include N.W.A's "Dopeman" and "Gangsta Gangsta," plus tracks by Dr. Dre, Too $hort, MC Breed, Ice Cube, Kris Kross, De La Soul, Xzibit, and Game. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Ohio-Players/Funky-Worm/

#### Album
- *Pleasure* was the Ohio Players' third studio album and second for Westbound Records, released December 1972. It peaked at #4 on the Billboard Top Soul LPs chart and #63 on the Billboard 200. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure_(Ohio_Players_album)
- Recorded at Artie Fields Studios in Detroit, Michigan. Engineer: Arlen Smith. Cover photography by Joel Brodsky (known for his iconic Doors photos). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure_(Ohio_Players_album)
- AllMusic described the album's era: "their popularity was growing, slowly but surely, and those who were hip to the band recognized them as one of the most cutting-edge acts in the funk field." — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/pleasure-mw0000264093
- A restored and remastered edition was released in 2023 by Westbound Records. — Source: https://jambands.com/news/2023/08/17/listen-westbound-records-delivers-restored-and-remastered-ohio-players-album-pleasure-share-funky-worm/

#### Artist
- The Ohio Players began in 1959 as the Ohio Untouchables, founded by singer-guitarist Robert Ward in Dayton, Ohio. They initially served as a backing band for the Falcons, the vocal group that featured a young Wilson Pickett. — Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/The-Ohio-Players
- Original guitarist Robert Ward was reportedly unreliable, sometimes abandoning performances mid-show. After a physical altercation between Ward and bassist Marshall Jones in 1964, the group disbanded temporarily before regrouping around 1967 with Leroy "Sugarfoot" Bonner replacing Ward. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Players
- The name change from "Ohio Untouchables" to "Ohio Players" reflected the members' self-perception "as musicians and as ladies' men." — Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/The-Ohio-Players
- At Westbound Records, the Ohio Players met George Clinton, who admired their music. Junie Morrison later joined Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic in 1977 as musical director, contributing to landmark albums including *One Nation Under a Groove* (platinum). Clinton called Morrison "the most phenomenal musician on the planet." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_%22Junie%22_Morrison
- Morrison was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 as part of the Parliament-Funkadelic collective. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_%22Junie%22_Morrison
- Westbound Records was founded by Armen Boladian in Detroit in 1968. He named it after the direction he was driving on 8 Mile Road when the idea struck him. The label also launched Funkadelic. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westbound_Records
- Five core members of the "Funky Worm" era Ohio Players have died: Clarence "Satch" Satchell (brain aneurysm, December 30, 1995), Ralph "Pee Wee" Middlebrooks (cancer, November 15, 1997), Leroy "Sugarfoot" Bonner (cancer, January 26, 2013), Marshall "Rock" Jones (cancer, May 27, 2016), and Junie Morrison (January 21, 2017, in London). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Players

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current value): Strong fit. "Funky Worm" is a major-label single from 1973 that hit #1 on R&B and #15 on pop, squarely within the classic funk era (1968-1977). It was released through Westbound (distributed by Janus), had wide radio play, and sold over a million copies. This is funk at its commercial peak.

2. **Psychedelic Funk**: A secondary case could be made. The track's absurdist concept (a magical worm that plays guitar), Junie Morrison's falsetto Granny character, and the weird, squealing ARP synthesizer all have psychedelic funk DNA. However, the Ohio Players are not part of the Parliament-Funkadelic family tree, and the track's humor is more vaudeville than cosmic. Classic Funk is the stronger label.

3. **Deep Funk**: Does not apply. This was a chart-topping hit on release, not a delayed discovery through crate-digging.

**Recommendation**: Keep `Classic Funk`. It's the right call.
