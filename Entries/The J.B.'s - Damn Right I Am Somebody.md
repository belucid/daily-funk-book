### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"James Brown's ghost producer"** — JB never put his name on the cover, but he produced, co-wrote most of the tracks, and recorded the album from leftover *Payback* session material. The album is the cleanest case study of Brown's "shadow" production work where Fred Wesley fronts and Brown drives from the wings.
- **"From Jesse Jackson's pulpit to the dancefloor"** — Trace the "I Am Somebody" chant from Rev. William Holmes Borders, Sr.'s 1940s Atlanta poem, through Jackson's Operation PUSH Saturday radio broadcasts, onto Sesame Street in 1972, into the JB's 1973 single "Same Beat," and finally into the 1974 album title track. The funk song is the final, most secular link in a 30-year Black-affirmation chain.
- **"The breakbeat factory"** — *Damn Right I Am Somebody* is, by raw sample count, one of the most strip-mined funk LPs in hip-hop history. The album as a vending machine for the next generation: Public Enemy, A Tribe Called Quest, EPMD, De La Soul, Run-DMC, Ice-T, Digable Planets, 2 Live Crew.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Listen for the moment the band locks into the chant — when "I am / Somebody" lands, who's speaking, who's responding, and how much of the energy comes from the horn stabs versus the rhythm pocket? Compare it to "Same Beat" from a year earlier; what did Wesley keep and what did he change?
- The track is built on a single repeated phrase. Count how many times you hear it before you start anticipating the next one. At what point does repetition stop being repetition and become the song itself?
- Fred Wesley is the band leader here, not James Brown, but Brown wrote and produced. Listen for two voices: where does Wesley's trombone arrangement assert itself, and where can you hear JB's fingerprints — the grunt cues, the breakdown structure, the false-stop dramatics?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- "Damn Right I'm Somebody" runs 6:00 on the LP version and was also issued as a 7" single (People PE 638) split into "Part I" (3:20) and "Last Part That Went Over The Fence" (3:00) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/52786-Fred-Wesley-The-JBs-Damn-Right-I-Am-Somebody
- The chant lyric and album concept are a direct response to Rev. Jesse Jackson's "I Am Somebody" recitations, which the JB's had already sampled on their 1973 single "Same Beat" — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1026086/Fred-Wesley-The-J.B.'s-Same-Beat-Jesse-Jackson-I-Am-Somebody/
- The "I Am Somebody" poem itself predates Jackson — it was written in the early 1940s by Rev. William Holmes Borders, Sr. of Atlanta's Greater Wheat Street Baptist Church — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_%E2%80%93_Somebody
- The song has been sampled by A Tribe Called Quest feat. Busta Rhymes on "Oh My God" and 2 Live Crew on "Hangin' Out," among others — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Fred-Wesley/Damn-Right-I'm-Somebody/

### Album

- Recorded at A&R Studios, New York, in November 1973, during the same sessions that produced James Brown's double album *The Payback* — Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/1679972 (release note "Recorded during the same session as [The Payback]")
- Released on People Records (catalog PE 6602), James Brown's vanity imprint distributed by Polydor — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/52778-Fred-Wesley-And-The-JBs-Damn-Right-I-Am-Somebody
- Officially credited to "Fred Wesley & The J.B.'s" — Brown produced (uncredited) and co-wrote six of the eight tracks but kept his name off the cover — Source: https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/damn-right-somebody
- Side One has been compared to Marvin Gaye's *What's Going On* for political content while being far funkier; topics include self-worth, the burden of taxation ("I'm Payin' Taxes, What Am I Buyin'?"), and Black American identity — Source: https://recordcollectormag.com/reviews/album/damn-right-somebody
- Track B5 "You Sure Love To Ball" is a Marvin Gaye composition (from his 1973 LP *Let's Get It On*) — the only non-Brown/Wesley/Ballard song on the record — Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/1679972
- "Blow Your Head" (track A2) became the rhythmic and sonic backbone of Public Enemy's 1987 "Public Enemy No. 1" and has since been sampled by EPMD, Run-DMC, De La Soul, Digable Planets, and Ice-T — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/damn-right-i-am-somebody-mw0000460454 (via search snippet)

### Artist

- The J.B.'s are James Brown's backing band; the lineup that recorded this album was led by trombonist/musical director Fred Wesley after the Collins brothers (Bootsy and Catfish) had decamped for Parliament-Funkadelic — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s
- Fred Wesley was born July 4, 1943 in Columbus, Georgia and raised in Mobile, Alabama; he rejoined Brown's organization in December 1970 to lead the J.B.'s and stayed in that role through 1975 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley
- Maceo Parker (alto sax on the record) was born February 14, 1943 in Kinston, North Carolina — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maceo_Parker
- 1974 was a Wesley-as-leader peak: he released TWO albums fronting JB's lineups that year, *Damn Right I Am Somebody* and *Breakin' Bread* (the latter credited to "Fred & the New J.B.'s") — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_J.B.%27s

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk** — Strongest case. Album was released on People Records (a major-distributed label via Polydor) in 1974, squarely inside the 1969-77 classic-funk era defined in *On Genres.md*. Track's primary identity is funk, not a fusion. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/52778-Fred-Wesley-And-The-JBs-Damn-Right-I-Am-Somebody
- **Deep Funk** — Weaker case. While the broader album was commercially modest and has been heavily crate-dug for samples, the album track in question is the title cut from a charting JB's-family LP on James Brown's label, not an obscure regional pressing. Closer to "rediscovered classic" than "deep funk."
- **Psychedelic Funk** — Not applicable. No P-Funk affiliation or psychedelic content; this is straight JB's-band classic funk.

Recommendation: **Classic Funk**.
