### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The album cut about cash, hiding in plain sight on a party record."** *Party Down* is otherwise a "sun-kissed hedonism" record about parties, women and the good life; "Money Vibrations" is the one moment the record gets serious about something else (Conor Herbert calls it "the album's single thematic departure"). The angle: why does an LP built for the dance floor stop, in the middle of side A, to give us a sermon about the pros and cons of money? https://www.conorherbert.com/music/on-the-party-life-with-little-beaver
- **"The Miami sound, when nobody was watching."** Frame the entry around the TK/Cat machine: Willie Clarke producing, Betty Wright and Latimore (Benny) on the session, Jaco Pastorius hiding under a pseudonym (Nelson "Jocko" Padron), Timmy Thomas on keys — all in an 18,000-square-foot Hialeah warehouse. "Money Vibrations" as a snapshot of what TK could do on a Tuesday afternoon. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TK_Records
- **"The grand master of Miami soul guitarists you've never heard of."** Lead with the fact that Willie Hale played the guitar on Betty Wright's "Clean Up Woman" (and most of the TK catalog), but never had a solo identity bigger than "Party Down." "Money Vibrations" is what his solo voice sounds like when he doesn't have to serve someone else's hit. https://longplaymiami.wordpress.com/tag/tk-records/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- "Lock in on Willie Hale's bass line — Beaver played it himself. It's the elastic floor under everything. Where does the bass do something the lyric can't?"
- "Listen for the gear shift. The album's other tracks invite you to the party; this one steps out for a smoke and gives you a piece of its mind about money. At what moment does Beaver's voice tell you he means it?"
- "Two drummers in the room could have made this groove tight. Robert Ferguson made it 'fatback' — laid behind the beat. Try to feel the drag, and then notice how it makes the bass sound forward."

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- "Money Vibrations" was written by Willie Hale alone (credited on streaming as "W. Hale") — Source: https://www.deezer.com/us/track/682768
- The track is described in retailer copy as a "heavy hardcore funk track with elastic, bumpin' bass lines (played by Beaver himself) and fatback drumming courtesy of Robert Ferguson," about "the pro's and cons of currency." — Source: https://www.vinyljunkies.net/products/little-beaver-party-down-lp
- It's the lone "serious" lyric on an otherwise hedonistic party LP — described as the album's "single thematic departure, maintaining serious lyrical content while drawing groovy influence from Sly Stone." — Source: https://www.conorherbert.com/music/on-the-party-life-with-little-beaver
- Background vocals on the session were by Betty Wright, with Timmy Thomas and Latimore on keys, Willie Clarke producing. — Source: https://www.shazam.com/song/80004964/money-vibrations
- The track is 3:45 long, 110 BPM, and labeled R&B/Soul on Shazam's audio fingerprint. — Source: https://www.shazam.com/song/80004964/money-vibrations

### Album

- *Party Down* was Little Beaver's third LP, released in 1974 on the Cat imprint of TK Records, catalog Cat LP-2604, recorded at TK Productions in Hialeah, FL. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Hale
- The title track "Party Down" hit #2 on the US Billboard R&B chart in 1974, his biggest commercial moment. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Hale
- The album is notorious for featuring a young Jaco Pastorius on bass, credited under the pseudonym "Nelson 'Jocko' Padron" on at least one track. (Chocolate Perry / George Perry handled bass on the others, and Beaver himself played bass on "Money Vibrations.") — Source: https://realgonemusic.com/products/little-beaver-party-down-lp
- The 2020 Real Gone Music reissue was pressed on metallic gold vinyl, limited to 1,000 copies, "to honor the gold record Little Beaver has hanging on his wall on the front cover." — Source: https://elusivedisc.com/little-beaver-party-down-lp/
- The album's "Get into the Party Life" was sampled on Jay-Z's "Party Life" (American Gangster, 2007); People Under The Stairs sampled multiple *Party Down* tracks for "Suite for Beaver, Part 1 & 2" on their 2002 album. — Source: https://arktimes.com/entertainment/2020/10/27/party-down-a-nod-to-little-beaver-and-a-playlist
- Producer Willie Clarke had co-founded Deep City Records, the first African-American-owned independent label in Florida, before joining TK; he later won a 1975 Grammy for Betty Wright's "Where Is the Love." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Clarke_(songwriter)

### Artist

- Willie George Hale was born August 15, 1945, in Forrest City, Arkansas. He got the nickname "Little Beaver" as a child because of his prominent front teeth. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Hale
- He moved to Florida in the early 1960s and in 1964 joined Frank Williams & the Rocketeers, a Miami nightclub act known for "classy, crowd-pleasing routines" and costume changes. — Source: https://www.conorherbert.com/music/on-the-party-life-with-little-beaver
- He is widely regarded as "the grand master of Miami Soul guitarists" — most famously the guitarist on Betty Wright's 1971 "Clean Up Woman." — Source: https://longplaymiami.wordpress.com/tag/tk-records/
- He nicknamed his guitar "Katie Pearl" — later the title of his 2006 album. — Source: https://longplaymiami.wordpress.com/2014/02/28/photo-shoot-willie-little-beaver-hale/
- After TK Records' bankruptcy in 1981, his recording career effectively ended, but Betty Wright recruited him in 2003 to play on Joss Stone's *The Soul Sessions* and *Mind Body & Soul*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Hale
- He still lives in Opa-locka, FL; long play miami's 2014 photo shoot describes him as press-shy and reluctant about "all that fuss." — Source: https://longplaymiami.wordpress.com/2014/02/28/photo-shoot-willie-little-beaver-hale/

### Genre Suggestions

The existing value is **Deep Funk**. The case for keeping it, and two alternatives:

- **Deep Funk (current).** Strong case. *Party Down* the title track was a #2 R&B hit, but the album's deep cuts — including "Money Vibrations" — are textbook crate-digger material: heavy, breakbeat-friendly, championed by hip-hop producers and rare-groove DJs in the decades after release. The Real Gone copy and Vinyl Junkies copy describe it as "heavy hardcore funk" with a "drum break" tag on Last.fm. People Under The Stairs and Jay-Z mining the LP for samples is the textbook deep-funk fate. https://www.last.fm/music/Little+Beaver/_/Money+Vibrations
- **Classic Funk.** Defensible case. The album charted, was distributed nationally on a TK imprint, and lives squarely in the 1969–1977 classic-funk era. If the book wants to emphasize *Party Down*'s commercial reach over Beaver's later obscurity, this is the better label. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Hale
- **(Soul over funk?)** I'd argue against any "soul" label here — the bass-and-drums pocket on "Money Vibrations" is funk-forward, even if Beaver's vocal delivery is smoky-soul. The book's genre list doesn't include a "soul" category anyway.

Recommendation: keep **Deep Funk**; it captures the song's actual life better than "Classic Funk" does.
