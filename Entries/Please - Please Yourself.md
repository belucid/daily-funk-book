### Open Research Questions

The author left no explicit `open:` questions in this entry. Two research questions surfaced during the work:

1. **Artist vs. album name.** The `artist` field reads `Manila Thriller` and `track` reads `Please Yourself`. According to every source found, this is inverted/mislabeled: the **band is named "Please"**, and **"Manila Thriller" is the title of their 1976 album**. "Please Yourself" is the correct opening track (A1) of that album. I cannot edit `artist` or `track`, so I am flagging this for the author. If the intent was a track-per-entry, the accurate metadata is Artist = Please, Album = Manila Thriller, Track = Please Yourself. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/347310-Please-Manila-Thriller ; https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/please/manila-thriller/
2. **Original label — London Records vs. Telefunken.** The Discogs main release (US pressing, PS 672) is on London Records. Multiple write-ups state the band released "two albums and several 45s on the German Telefunken label." Unresolved whether the true original pressing was German Telefunken with London handling the US/UK release. See Field Verification. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/637683-Please-Manila-Thriller

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The Thrilla in Manila, pressed to vinyl."** The album title nods to the Ali–Frazier heavyweight bout at the Araneta Coliseum, Manila, on 1 Oct 1975. A frame around Filipino musicians abroad naming their record after the fight that put Manila on the world's front page. Source: https://www.funkmysoul.gr/albums-no-review/please-1976-manila-thriller/
- **"Filipino funk with a British passport."** A band of Filipino players, based in West Germany, recording funk in an Oxfordshire studio (Chipping Norton) with British blues producer Mike Vernon. A story about diaspora and how a "Manila" record was actually made in England. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/637683-Please-Manila-Thriller
- **"When the metadata lies."** The entry itself embodies a crate-digger hazard: an album title mistaken for an artist. A meditation on how obscure records get miscataloged and rediscovered. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/347310-Please-Manila-Thriller

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is the album's 3-minute opener. Listen for how quickly it commits to the dancefloor: where does the disco pulse (four-on-the-floor, strings) sit against the raw funk-rock guitar? Is this funk wearing disco clothes, or disco with a funk band underneath?
- The players are Filipino, the producer British, the studio English. Listen for anything that sounds "not American" — vocal phrasing, horn writing, the guitar tone — and ask whether you'd ever guess its origin blind.
- Compare "Please Yourself" (3:19, radio-tight) with the album's 7-minute "You Got The Power." What does the band do with a short leash versus a long one?

### Track

### Album

- The title "Manila Thriller" is widely read as a play on the "Thrilla in Manila," the Muhammad Ali vs. Joe Frazier world heavyweight title fight held at the Araneta Coliseum, Quezon City, on 1 October 1975 — months before the January 1976 recording. Source: https://www.funkmysoul.gr/albums-no-review/please-1976-manila-thriller/
- The whole album was cut in a two-week window: "Recorded between January 12th and January 25th, 1976" at Chipping Norton Recording Studios, with overdubs at Lansdowne Studios (London). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/637683-Please-Manila-Thriller
- Produced and arranged by Mike Vernon — the British producer best known for founding Blue Horizon and producing Fleetwood Mac, John Mayall, and Chicken Shack — an unexpected pairing with a Filipino funk band. Orchestral arrangements by Colin Frechter. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/637683-Please-Manila-Thriller
- It was the band's second album; it has become a sought-after crate-digger item (Discogs shows ~1,286 users "want" vs. ~425 "have") and was reissued on CD as a Korean mini-LP papersleeve. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/347310-Please-Manila-Thriller ; https://www.lpcdreissues.com/item/manila-thriller

### Artist

- Please were a Filipino funk-rock band who were mainly active in West Germany during the 1970s, reportedly releasing two albums and several 45s on the German Telefunken label before fading into obscurity. Source: https://www.funkmysoul.gr/albums-no-review/please-1976-manila-thriller/
- The lineup (per Discogs release credits) included Bobby Villegas (bass, vocals), Carlos David (guitar, sitar, 12-string, percussion, vocals — also an arranger), Efren Lozanes (keys, sax, percussion, vocals), Lito Cruz (drums, percussion, trumpet, vocals), Manuel "Manny" Sta. Maria (guitar, trombone, vocals), Mike Sta. Maria (drums), and Roy David (keys, synth, trumpet, vocals). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/637683-Please-Manila-Thriller
