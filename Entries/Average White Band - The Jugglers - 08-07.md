Today’s song, “The Jugglers,” went missing for 41 years. It opened the Average White Band’s 1973 debut _Show Your Hand_. The album sold poorly and their label, MCA, dropped them.

Atlantic Records' Jerry Wexler heard some of the band's unreleased material at a party in Los Angeles, later saying, "Their funk hit me where I lived, their tape was great, and I wanted to sign them on the spot." Wexler's bet paid off fast when their first single on Atlantic, “Pick Up the Pieces,” hit No. 1 on the *Billboard* Hot 100, and the album *AWB* went to No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200.

MCA scrambled to rerelease the debut album with a more commercial cover, the midsection of a woman, and with a suggestive title, *Put It Where You Want It*. The only musical difference between the 1973 original and the 1975 rerelease is that the lead track, “The Jugglers,” was replaced by “How Can You Go Home.”

“The Jugglers” stayed off the reissued debut for decades, which is why it can feel like a lost cut even though it was once the album’s opener. The band finally brought it back in a 2014 box set and recorded it live on *AWB R&B* in 2016 with the two original members still in the lineup: bassist Alan Gorrie, who wrote the song, and guitarist Onnie McIntyre.

**Resolved.** Here's the full commercial timeline:

**AWB (aka "The White Album"), released August 23, 1974, Atlantic Records:**
- Produced by Arif Mardin at Atlantic Recording Studios (NYC) and Criteria Studios (Miami)
- Hit **#1 on the Billboard 200** and **#1 on the R&B Albums chart**
- Hit **#6 in the UK**
- The single "Pick Up the Pieces" reached **#1 on the Billboard Hot 100** in February 1975, knocking Linda Ronstadt's "You're No Good" off the top spot. It was certified Gold (1 million+ copies sold).
- The album was also certified RIAA Gold (500,000+ units shipped)
- This was Robbie McIntosh's final album; he died one month after its release.
— Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWB_(album), https://theafterword.co.uk/fifty-years-of-awb-aka-white-album/

**Cut the Cake, released June 10, 1975, Atlantic Records:**
- Produced by Arif Mardin. Dedicated to "our friend and brother Robbie McIntosh."
- Hit **#4 on the Billboard 200** and **#1 on the R&B Albums chart**
- The title track single "Cut the Cake" reached **#10 on the Hot 100** and **#7 R&B**
- Other singles: "If I Ever Lose This Heaven" (#39 pop, #25 R&B), "School Boy Crush" (#33 pop, #22 R&B)
- Recording was difficult: band members walked out of sessions on three occasions due to "creative and artistic differences"
— Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_the_Cake_(album)

**Put It Where You Want It (MCA reissue of Show Your Hand), 1975:**
- Exact month not confirmed in any source I found, but it was released in 1975, the same year as *Cut the Cake*.
- Peaked at **#39 on the Billboard 200** and **#69 in Canada**
- MCA was clearly capitalizing on AWB's newfound fame on Atlantic. The reissue replaced "The Jugglers" with "How Can You Go Home" and used a more commercial cover (the midsection of a woman).
- Timeline: *AWB* was released August 1974, "Pick Up the Pieces" hit #1 in February 1975, *Cut the Cake* followed in June 1975. The *Put It Where You Want It* reissue came somewhere in that 1975 window. The exact sequencing relative to *Cut the Cake* is not confirmed, but both were released in 1975 while AWB was at peak commercial visibility.
— Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_Your_Hand_(album)

**The AWB logo and cover art:**
- The iconic logo was conceived by **Alan Gorrie** as a doodle: a woman's naked body viewed from behind, nestled into the curves of the "W" in AWB.
- The concept was turned into finished artwork by **Tim Bruckner**, a sculptor and illustrator who also created covers for Ringo Starr's *Ringo* (1973), Ray Charles' *Renaissance*, and Cat Stevens' *Buddha and the Chocolate Box*.
- **Barry Feinstein** handled the overall design and photography for the album.
- The image became the band's permanent logo and has appeared on merchandise, compilations, and promotional materials ever since.

Open: Find out the relation between 2016's The Jugglers and 1973's.

**Resolved.** The 2016 "The Jugglers" on *AWB R&B* is a **live recording** of the original 1973 composition. Here are the key details:

- The original "The Jugglers" was written by Alan Gorrie and appeared as the opening track on AWB's 1973 debut *Show Your Hand* (MCA Records). It runs 4:55.
- The 2016 version on *AWB R&B* (AWB Records) runs 5:13, roughly 18 seconds longer. It was recorded **live** at **One World Theatre**, an intimate 300-seat venue in Austin, Texas, in **September 2015**, with the later-era touring lineup.
- *AWB R&B* is a **live album** of AWB classics. The tracklist includes "Pick Up the Pieces," "Cut the Cake," "Let's Go Round Again," "Put It Where You Want It," and other signature tracks, all performed live. "The Jugglers" is the only track from *Show Your Hand* included, and one of the deeper cuts on the setlist.
- The 2016 lineup featured only two original members: **Alan Gorrie** (bass, vocals) and **Onnie McIntyre** (guitar, vocals), alongside Cliff Lyons (alto sax, keyboards, vocals), Rob Aries (keyboards, bass), Brent Carter (lead vocals, ex-Tower of Power), Rocky Bryant (drums), and Fred "Freddy V" Vigdor (tenor sax, keyboards, vocals). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_White_Band
- There also exists a "First Version" of "The Jugglers" from 1971 (4:06), predating the official band formation, released on the 2014 box set *All the Pieces*. So three distinct recordings exist: 1971, 1973, and 2016.
- The fact that AWB chose to re-record "The Jugglers" for *AWB R&B* is notable: MCA cut it from the 1975 reissue, but 43 years later the band reclaimed it as part of their essential live repertoire.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The track the label threw away"**: When MCA reissued *Show Your Hand* in 1975 as *Put It Where You Want It*, "The Jugglers" was the only track cut from the album, replaced by "How Can You Go Home." The very song that opened the original record was deemed expendable for the commercial repackaging. Yet decades later it caught the ear of Ice Cube's producers. This angle frames the entry around a track whose value was missed by the label but found by crate-diggers and samplers.

2. **"Before the tragedy, before the fame"**: This track is a snapshot of AWB with Robbie McIntosh still alive and drumming, before the September 1974 overdose that killed him. Every note on *Show Your Hand* captures the original six-piece at full strength in a London studio, still unknown, still hungry. The writing hook centers on what this recording preserves.

3. **"Scotland to the groove"**: Six Scottish musicians recording funk in a Wimbledon studio in 1972-73, before relocating to America. The angle opens up the transatlantic story: soul music was actually more popular in Scotland than in London at the time, and AWB's name itself came from a joke about being "average white men" trying to play Black American music.

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk**: The strongest fit. AWB's debut was released in 1973, squarely within the classic funk era (1968-1977). The track is an instrumental funk groove from a band that went on to chart-topping funk success on Atlantic. It fits the book's definition: "music that defined the core vocabulary" with "funk as its primary identity." The band's commercial pipeline (MCA label, singles, touring) aligns with the classic funk profile even though this particular album flopped initially.

2. **Deep Funk**: A strong secondary case. *Show Your Hand* sold poorly on release, MCA dropped the band, and "The Jugglers" was literally cut from the 1975 reissue. The track only found recognition decades later through crate-digging, compilations, and sampling (Ice Cube, 1990). This fits the deep funk definition perfectly: "tracks that were overlooked on release and only found their audience later through crate-digging, DJs, and dance floors."

3. **Jazz-Funk**: A weaker but plausible case. AWB blended funk with jazz-inflected horn arrangements and extended instrumental passages. AllMusic classifies them under funk and smooth soul. "The Jugglers" at 4:55 is an instrumental with room for interplay, but it lacks the extended improvisation and jazz musician pedigree that the book's jazz-funk definition requires.

The UK tag from the region enumeration is the most accurate available option. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_White_Band
