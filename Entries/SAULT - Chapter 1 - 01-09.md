The album *Chapter 1* dropped without warning on January 9, 2026. Mystery is SAULT's hallmark. Despite the title, *Chapter 1* is not their debut. It's their thirteenth album in roughly seven years. Their first album in 2019 was actually called *5*. *7* followed *5*. *9* was their fifth album. *11* arrived in 2022, and *10* in 2025. Confused yet?

The mystery is not limited to release dates and album titles. SAULT is a London-based collective that rarely shows its face and doesn't do interviews. At their first live show, a masked performance in December 2023, they revealed that SAULT stands for “Start A Universal Love Trend.”

SAULT’s membership shifts with each album. Producer Inflo is the most constant presence, with Cleo Sol the singer most closely tied to the group’s sound. *Chapter 1* also features Jack Peñate and contributions from Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, because even a mystery collective has room for Minneapolis sound royalty.

Recent SAULT albums, including *Chapter 1*, rely on spiritual language and gospel feeling. But today’s track, "Chapter 1," is not a sermon. It's built around a killer bassline, infectious chanted vocals, and drumming that deserves a hat tip to whoever is behind the drum kit.

#### Artist

- "SAULT" is an acronym for "Start A Universal Love Trend," revealed for the first time at the band's debut live show at Drumsheds, London, in December 2023, when dancers arranged lettered blocks on stage. Source: https://thebluesproject.co/2023/12/sault-about-the-mystery-indie-rnb-soul-collective/
- The collective is led by producer Inflo (Dean Josiah Cover, born June 4, 1988), who in February 2022 became the first Black recipient of the BRIT Producer of the Year award since the category was introduced in 1977. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflo ; https://brits.co.uk/news/2022/inflo-wins-brit-award-for-producer-of-the-year/
- Lead vocalist Cleo Sol, born Cleopatra Zvezdana Nikolic on March 24, 1990 in Ladbroke Grove, London, is the daughter of two jazz musicians, with a Serbian-Spanish mother and a Jamaican father. She is married to Inflo. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleo_Sol
- SAULT have released five albums simultaneously (November 2022, as a free WeTransfer download), pulled their album *Nine* from streaming after exactly 99 days, and released *Acts of Faith* (2024) as a single 32-minute file. Source: https://thebluesproject.co/2023/12/sault-about-the-mystery-indie-rnb-soul-collective/
- The band has won the Ivor Novello Award for Best Album (2023, for *11*), but has never given a press interview, played a non-masked live show, or officially confirmed a single member's identity. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sault_(band)

### Genre Suggestions

This is a genuinely tough fit. SAULT are most often described in mainstream press as "soul," "R&B," "neo-soul," or "gospel/soul," all of which the book either excludes (soul) or doesn't enumerate. But the album *Chapter 1* is one of their funkier records, and the press coverage of this album in particular leaned hard on funk vocabulary. Three plausible book-genre tags:

- **Nu-Funk.** The book defines this as "a modern funk revival scene that emerged in the mid-1990s, shaped by DJs and bands capturing the feel of rare funk 45s while making new records in that spirit. It is a small but international scene, with standout artists across the US, Europe, and Australia." SAULT are international (UK), modern (2019–2026), and explicitly drawing from 70s/80s funk on this album. They aren't crate-digger 45-revival in the strict Daptone/Truth & Soul mold, but they are arguably the most prominent contemporary act for whom funk-as-source-material is fundamental. Source: https://www.clashmusic.com/news/listen-to-saults-new-album-chapter-1/
- **Psychedelic Funk.** Multiple reviewers reached for psychedelic-soul / Rotary Connection / Sly Stone comparisons specifically for this record. The book's definition stretches comfortably to "fuses funk's groove with psychedelic music's surreal lyrics, colors and sounds," which fits the cinematic, layered, mantra-driven approach of *Chapter 1*. Source: https://soulbounce.com/2026/01/sault-chapter-1/
- **Gospel Funk.** The album opens with "God, Protect Me From My Enemies" and includes "Lord Have Mercy" and "Fulfil Your Spirit." Religious motifs run through SAULT's entire catalog. The title track itself is less explicitly gospel than the bookends, but if the author wants to use this entry to introduce SAULT's gospel-funk dimension, the album supports it. Source: https://www.live4ever.uk.com/sault-chapter-1-album-review/

My strongest single recommendation is **Nu-Funk**, with **Psychedelic Funk** as a close second if the author wants to lean into the Sly/Rotary Connection lineage.
