### Open Research Questions

- **OPEN: What day did Nixon resign?** Richard Nixon announced his resignation on the evening of **August 8, 1974**, and the resignation took effect at noon on **August 9, 1974**, making him the first U.S. president to resign. — Source: https://www.history.com/articles/watergate and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Watergate_scandal
  - Note: The existing entry `James Brown - Funky President (People It's Bad) - 08-08.md` already occupies August 8, so August 9 (the day the resignation took effect) is the cleaner anchor for this entry if a Nixon-resignation tie-in is wanted. August 8 is also already used.

- **OPEN: What was going on at the time of this recording that would have led to calls for impeachment?** The single was released in 1973 (Wikipedia gives June 1, 1973; Deezer lists "01/06/1973" matching the same date in DD/MM/YYYY form) — so it was written and recorded in early 1973, well *before* the impeachment hearings, the Saturday Night Massacre (Oct 20, 1973), or Nixon's resignation. The Watergate break-in occurred June 17, 1972; by early 1973 the Senate Watergate Committee (Ervin Committee) had been established (Feb 7, 1973) and its televised hearings began May 17, 1973. The story was front-page news for over a year before Nixon stepped down, so the recording absolutely fits the moment — the author's premise that there wasn't time for music to respond is too restrictive. The song is an *early* protest, not a late one. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Watergate_scandal, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President, https://hiddentrack.ie/features/songs-and-album-covers/from-protest-to-breakbeat-the-long-life-of-impeach-the-president/

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The accidental breakbeat / when the weakest link becomes immortal.** Roy C told Wax Poetics he "worked hard with the drummer because he wasn't as good a drummer as I would have liked," drilling him over and over in a basement in Jamaica, Queens. That kid — never named publicly — laid down what Marley Marl later called the kick and snare that "changed the world." A protest song that flopped commercially produced the most-sampled drum break in hip-hop. — Source: https://www.tracklib.com/blog/roy-c-hammond-honey-drippers-sample-break

- **A protest song no one was supposed to hear.** Mercury Records turned the track down for fear of alienating white listeners; Roy C pressed it himself on Alaga in a run reported as only a few hundred (some accounts say 100). The political risk and tiny pressing are exactly what kept it in the crates long enough for Bronx and Queens DJs to find it. — Sources: https://hiddentrack.ie/features/songs-and-album-covers/from-protest-to-breakbeat-the-long-life-of-impeach-the-president/, https://www.tracklib.com/blog/roy-c-hammond-honey-drippers-sample-break

- **The longest royalty wait in funk.** Roy C heard his own drums on Janet Jackson's "That's the Way Love Goes" on the radio in 1993 and recognized them. Despite eventual co-writing credits on songs as huge as Mary J. Blige's "Real Love" and Kanye West's "Otis," he reportedly struggled for decades to be paid for the sample. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_C, https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-honey-drippers/impeach-the-president

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The track is famous for the first eight bars, not the song. Try the opposite: listen to the *whole* 3:17 and ignore the intro. What is the protest actually saying once Roy C starts talking the kids into chanting, and what does it tell you about how the song was meant to function?

- Marley Marl said the snare changed the world. Loop the first eight seconds in your head, then play "The Bridge," "Top Billin'," and "Eric B. Is President" back-to-back. What did each producer subtract from those eight seconds, and what did they add?

- This is a record made by teenagers led by a man who thought the drummer wasn't good enough. Listen for what isn't tight — the kid's slight rush, a stick clip, the gang vocal drifting off mic — and decide whether the "weakness" is what makes it sample so well.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The drum intro is widely considered the most-sampled break in hip-hop, with somewhere around 800 officially licensed samples and thousands more unlicensed. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President
- Marley Marl's 1986 production of MC Shan's "The Bridge" is generally cited as the first hip-hop record built on a sampled (rather than re-played) drum break, and that break came from this song. — Source: https://www.tracklib.com/blog/roy-c-hammond-honey-drippers-sample-break
- Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987) reportedly used a two-second slice of the drums that became iconic in part because of sampler memory limits that forced the chop. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President
- The track was rejected by Mercury Records — Roy C said Mercury "could lose all that support" if they offended white listeners — and so he released it on his own Alaga label, with a very small initial pressing. — Source: https://hiddentrack.ie/features/songs-and-album-covers/from-protest-to-breakbeat-the-long-life-of-impeach-the-president/
- When the U.S. House opened a formal impeachment inquiry into Donald Trump in September 2019, Spotify streams of "Impeach the President" jumped more than 1,000 percent. — Source: https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/-71340/the-honey-drippers-impeach-the-president-71423/
- Rolling Stone placed the song at #18 on its list of the 100 Best Protest Songs of All Time. — Source: https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/-71340/the-honey-drippers-impeach-the-president-71423/
- Recorded length is 3:17 per Discogs; Apple Music lists 3:58 for a "Mixed" version. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/403354-The-Honey-Drippers-Impeach-The-President-Roy-Cs-Theme, https://music.apple.com/us/album/impeach-the-president-single/1641992603

#### Album

- The song was not part of an album — it was issued only as a 7" single, Alaga AL-1017, with "Roy C's Theme Song" on the B-side. Roy C himself was the publisher (Johnson & Hammond Music, BMI). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/797856-The-Honey-Drippers-Impeach-The-President-Roy-Cs-Theme
- Tuff City Records re-released the track digitally to iTunes in 2017 and has continued to license and reissue it since. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President

#### Artist

- "The Honey Drippers" here is essentially a stage name. The band was a group of African American high school students from Jamaica High School in Jamaica, Queens, rehearsed in a basement under Roy C's direction. None of the teenagers have ever been named in widely available sources, including the drummer who created the famous break. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President, https://www.tracklib.com/blog/roy-c-hammond-honey-drippers-sample-break
- Roy C is Roy Charles Hammond, born August 3, 1939, in Newington, Georgia; he died September 16, 2020, in Allendale County, South Carolina. His earlier hit "Shotgun Wedding" (1965) hit #14 on the U.S. R&B chart and #6 on the UK Singles Chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_C
- Roy C founded multiple independent labels (Hammond, Black Hawk, Shout, Alaga, and Three Gems) and reportedly released over 125 records, writing most of them himself. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_C
- Because the Honey Drippers' break sits inside so many later hits, Roy C is credited (often via legal settlement) on songs as far afield as Mary J. Blige's "Real Love" and Kanye West's "Otis." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_C
- The Honey Drippers name was reused in the 1980s by Robert Plant for an unrelated project — a frequent point of confusion. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeach_the_President

### Genre Suggestions

Genre is currently empty. Per the book's genre definitions in `Matter/On Genres.md`:

1. **Classic Funk** — The track sits squarely in the 1969–1977 classic-funk window and its identity is funk first (Discogs classifies it as Funk / Soul, style Funk). It is one of the canonical funk 45s of the era, even if it never charted. **Strongest suggestion.** — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/403354-The-Honey-Drippers-Impeach-The-President-Roy-Cs-Theme, Matter/On Genres.md

2. **Deep Funk** — The "defining trait of deep funk is delayed recognition." This track is the archetype: a tiny Alaga pressing, no chart, then resurrected by crate-digging hip-hop DJs in the 1980s and turned into a foundational break. If any single song in the book makes the case for the Deep Funk label, it is this one. — Sources: Matter/On Genres.md, https://www.tracklib.com/blog/roy-c-hammond-honey-drippers-sample-break

3. **Gospel Funk** (weak alternate) — There's a call-and-response, gang-vocal quality to the chant that has a churchy feel, but the lyric is political not religious, so this is a stretch. Not recommended.

Author's call between Classic Funk (it's a 1973 funk single) and Deep Funk (it's *the* deep-funk-to-hip-hop pipeline story). Either is defensible.
