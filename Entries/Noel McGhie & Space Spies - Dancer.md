## Research Notes

### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were left by the author in the entry. One discrepancy that arose during research: several streaming services (Spotify, Apple Music, AllMusic) catalog the album under the title **"Trapeze"** (after its lead track), while Discogs and the 2022 Comet Records reissue use the self-titled **"Noel Mc Ghie & Space Spies."** The current YAML value of `album: Noel McGhie & Space Spies` aligns with the canonical Discogs master title (the LP sleeve carries the artist name, not "Trapeze") and is left unchanged. Sources: [Discogs master 436110](https://www.discogs.com/master/436110-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies), [Apple Music album](https://music.apple.com/us/album/trapeze/1292896244), [Spotify album](https://open.spotify.com/album/6bTBf5gbPE8poOxtIOYWxQ).

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A Jamaican drummer's Paris album, recorded by no Frenchmen"** — The Space Spies are an entirely expatriate ensemble inside one of France's most insular music scenes: a Jamaican-born/UK-raised leader, a Japanese trumpeter, two Martinican Caribbean musicians, and a Brazilian saxophonist. The "French jazz-funk classic" is, on closer inspection, a portrait of postcolonial 1970s Paris. Source: [cometrec.fr](https://cometrec.fr/portfolio/noel-mcghie/).
- **"The free jazz drummer who wanted to dance"** — McGhie's day job was supporting Steve Lacy, Archie Shepp and Anthony Braxton inside Paris's free-jazz underground. *Dancer* is what happens when that same musician sets the avant-garde aside long enough to write something with a pocket. Source: [Point of Departure interview](https://www.pointofdeparture.org/PoD64/PoD64McGhie.html).
- **"Private press, public rediscovery"** — Disques Espérance pressed the LP in tiny numbers in 1975, the record vanished, and crate-diggers slowly turned originals into $200-400 collectibles before Superfly (2015) and Comet (2022) made it generally available. A textbook deep-funk fate. Source: [beatcaffeine.com](https://beatcaffeine.com/french-label-comet-reissues-noel-mcghies-1975-rare-jazz-funk-classic/).

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The track opens with a long instrumental stretch before anyone really "dances." Time the moment McGhie's drums finally commit to a groove versus when Itaru Oki's trumpet enters — who is leading whom?
- This is a record with no Americans on it, but it is unmistakably indebted to Black American jazz-funk of 1973-1974. Listen for the moments that feel borrowed (Crusaders? Donald Byrd? early Herbie?) and the moments that don't — where do you hear Caribbean or Japanese rather than American influence?
- Five musicians, no vocals, almost seven minutes. Pick one instrument other than the lead (try Louis Xavier's bass or Georges-Edouard Nouel's electric piano) and follow only that part for the full track length. What story does that line tell on its own?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Dancer" runs 6m 50s — the second-shortest cut on a five-track album where the longest, "Ubet," sprawls past 11 minutes. The compressed length puts it on the funkier, more song-shaped end of an otherwise spiritual-jazz LP. Source: [Discogs release 2812260](https://www.discogs.com/release/2812260-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies).
- McGhie is credited not only as drummer/percussionist but as composer of every track on the album, including "Dancer." Source: [Discogs release 2812260](https://www.discogs.com/release/2812260-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies) ("Noel McGhie — Percussion, Composed By").

#### Album

- The album was the first and only full-length release of McGhie's career as a leader. He returned as a leader only with a Comet/Futura Marge "Afro-Caribbean Project" decades later. Sources: [cometrec.fr](https://cometrec.fr/portfolio/noel-mcghie/), [futuramarge.bandcamp.com](https://futuramarge.bandcamp.com/album/re-masters).
- The lineup is a multi-continental snapshot of expat Paris: Itaru Oki (trumpet, Kobe, Japan), Georges-Edouard Nouel (electric piano, Fort-de-France, Martinique), Louis Xavier (bass, Martinique), Jorge João (alto/soprano sax, Brazil), with Jamaican-born McGhie on drums. Sources: [Discogs release 2812260](https://www.discogs.com/release/2812260-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies), [Itaru Oki on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaru_Oki), [la1ere.franceinfo.fr profile of G-E Nouel](https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/martinique/le-pianiste-martiniquais-georges-edouard-nouel-en-concert-a-fort-de-france-apres-30-ans-d-absence-1401650.html).
- Original 1975 Disques Espérance pressings sell for roughly $200-400 used; the 2015 Superfly limited reissue itself trades around $100, indicating the catalog was scarce even after rediscovery. Source: [beatcaffeine.com](https://beatcaffeine.com/french-label-comet-reissues-noel-mcghies-1975-rare-jazz-funk-classic/).
- Comet Records issued its remastered vinyl reissue in October 2022; Superfly had done a small reissue in 2015. Sources: [cometrec.fr](https://cometrec.fr/portfolio/noel-mcghie/), [beatcaffeine.com](https://beatcaffeine.com/french-label-comet-reissues-noel-mcghies-1975-rare-jazz-funk-classic/).
- The Discogs master assigns the album the genre "Jazz" with styles **Fusion, Jazz-Funk, Soul-Jazz, Modal** — note the inclusion of "Modal," which is consistent with the long-form spiritual jazz feel of "Ubet" rather than "Dancer." Source: [Discogs master 436110](https://api.discogs.com/masters/436110).

#### Artist

- Noel McGhie was born **April 4, 1944, in Ocho Rios, Jamaica** — the same birthplace and year as Bob Marley (Marley was actually born in Nine Mile, Saint Ann, on Feb 6, 1945, so the Point of Departure framing is approximate, but the year/parish overlap is real). Source: [Point of Departure interview](https://www.pointofdeparture.org/PoD64/PoD64McGhie.html).
- He moved to England in 1962 at age 18, then to Paris around 1970-1971, where he studied classical percussion at the Conservatoire Municipal de Clichy and later at the Agostini Drum School (co-founded by Kenny Clarke and Dante Agostini). Sources: [Point of Departure interview](https://www.pointofdeparture.org/PoD64/PoD64McGhie.html), [Superfly Records storyboard](https://blog.superflyrecords.com/storyboard/noel-mcghie-space-talking/).
- Steve Lacy "discovered" McGhie playing at the Chat qui Pêche club in Paris and brought him into his ensemble; they worked together roughly 1971-1973. He also worked extensively with French protest singer Colette Magny. Source: [Point of Departure interview](https://www.pointofdeparture.org/PoD64/PoD64McGhie.html).
- McGhie cites Rashied Ali, Cozy Cole, Kenny Clarke, Philly Joe Jones, Roy Haynes, Max Roach, and Milford Graves as his drumming influences — a list that spans bebop to free jazz and explains why a "jazz-funk" record from him still leans spiritual and exploratory. Source: [cometrec.fr](https://cometrec.fr/portfolio/noel-mcghie/).
- Trumpeter Itaru Oki, who provides most of the album's melodic identity, was a key figure of late-1960s Tokyo free jazz and a co-founder of the ESSG collective with Masahiko Sato. He had only just relocated to Paris in 1974, so *Dancer* was recorded within months of his arrival. He died Aug 25, 2020. Source: [Itaru Oki on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaru_Oki), [The Wire obituary](https://www.thewire.co.uk/news/59125/itaru-oki-1941-2020).

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (current value, retain)** — This is the right primary label. McGhie was a free-jazz drummer playing funk; the track has extended improvisation, no vocal chorus, jazz-trained soloists, and 1970s timing. Discogs's own styles list leads with Fusion/Jazz-Funk. Matches the book's "jazz-funk is funk played by jazz musicians ... in the 1970s ... built to support soloists" definition almost word-for-word. Source: [On Genres.md] internal; [Discogs master 436110](https://www.discogs.com/master/436110-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies).
- **Deep Funk (alternative)** — Fits the book's definition exactly: a private-press LP, overlooked on 1975 release, rediscovered by crate-diggers and reissued twice for the rare-groove market. If the editorial angle is the record's commercial fate rather than its sound, Deep Funk is the more honest label. Source: [beatcaffeine.com](https://beatcaffeine.com/french-label-comet-reissues-noel-mcghies-1975-rare-jazz-funk-classic/).
- **Soul Jazz (alternative)** — Discogs also lists Soul-Jazz as a style. For a track this groove-forward with an electric piano carrying much of the harmony, Soul Jazz is plausible, though the book treats Soul Jazz as the "earlier simmer" before the 1970s full boil, which would push this LP toward Jazz-Funk anyway.

Recommendation: keep Jazz-Funk; consider Deep Funk if the writing leans into the rediscovery story.

### Region Suggestions

- **France (current value, retain)** — Recorded in Paris, released on a French label (Disques Espérance), and embedded in the Parisian expatriate jazz scene of the early-to-mid 1970s. Per "On Regions," recording/scene location is a defensible criterion, and "France" is on the project's allowed region list. Source: [Field Values.md], [Discogs release 2812260](https://www.discogs.com/release/2812260-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies-Noel-Mc-Ghie-Space-Spies).
- **(Alternative not recommended)** — None of the musicians is French. A case could be made for Jamaica (McGhie's birthplace and the leader) or for a "Caribbean/diaspora" region, but the album is not a Jamaican or Caribbean musical project; it is a Paris jazz scene project. None of the leader's birth-region options (Jamaica, Japan, Brazil, Martinique) is on the allowed regions list except indirectly, and the music does not sound rooted in any of them.
- **Note on missing regions** — The project's region list is geographically thin around expatriate scenes. "France" is correct here, but if other entries surface other Paris-based African or Caribbean-led recordings, the list may want a "Paris diaspora" tag. Not a change to push for on this entry.
