Joe Sample, composer of today’s “Double Bubble,” was born February 1, 1939. Sample was the Crusaders’ keyboardist and primary writer, and this track is a compact example of his impeccable sense of groove.

You will hear a couple more tracks from the Crusaders' *Southern Comfort* album this year, and for good reason. It's a perfect example of jazz-funk at its 1974 commercial peak, before disco and smoother jazz fusion took over later in the decade. The Jazz Crusaders changed both their sound and their name to meet the demand for something funkier.

What sets “Double Bubble” apart from the rest of the album is its short length. At 2m 44s, it's the shortest cut on a 63-minute, 12-track double LP, where tunes like “Whispering Pines” and “Lilies of the Nile” stretch past eight minutes. The limited room for soloing or extended improvisation in a three-minute groove is exactly what bothered some jazz purists. The Crusaders didn't seem to care. I hear “Double Bubble” as their statement that the groove is the end, not the means.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The 2:44 mission statement."** On a sprawling 63-minute double LP full of stretched-out grooves, "Double Bubble" is a pocket-sized anomaly — a compressed Joe Sample composition that says everything the band is about in under three minutes. Angle: why economy can hit as hard as sprawl.
- **"The Carlton effect."** Southern Comfort is the album where 26-year-old Larry Carlton arrives as a full member, and his lyrical electric guitar changes the band's center of gravity. "Double Bubble" is a compact showcase for how Carlton's playing talks to Joe Sample's keys. Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- **"Jazz Crusaders to Crusaders — the funk pivot, complete."** By 1974 the band had dropped "Jazz" from the name and gone fully electric. Southern Comfort is where the jazz-funk identity is locked in, and "Double Bubble" is the clearest miniature of that mature sound. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Written by Joe Sample, the Crusaders' keyboardist and principal composer, born in Houston on February 1, 1939. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Sample
- Clocks in at 2:44 — one of the shortest cuts on a 63-minute, 12-track double LP. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Sequenced alongside other Sample compositions like "A Ballad for Joe (Louis)" (7:29), giving the album a dramatic contrast in compositional scale. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)

#### Album

- Southern Comfort is a double LP recorded at Wally Heider Recording in Hollywood, produced by Stewart Levine. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- Entered the Billboard 200 on October 26, 1974; topped the Jazz LPs chart at #1, hit #3 R&B, #31 Pop. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- The album introduced 26-year-old Larry Carlton as a fully-fledged Crusader — his lyrical guitar "injects a whole new vibe." — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- Released in the UK on ABC Records in April 1975, six months after its October 1974 US release. — Source: https://markattheflicks.blogspot.com/2020/03/southern-comfort-by-crusaders.html
- Earned 3.5 stars in The Penguin Guide to Jazz Recordings. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)

#### Artist

- The Crusaders started in Houston in the mid-1950s as schoolmates at Phyllis Wheatley High School and Texas Southern University, first calling themselves the Swingsters, then the Modern Jazz Sextet. — Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/crusaders
- They became the Jazz Crusaders in 1961 on Pacific Jazz (reportedly at the suggestion of drummer Stix Hooper's wife Ramona), then dropped "Jazz" in 1971 when they went electric and leaned fully into funk. — Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/crusaders ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Joe Sample died in Houston on September 12, 2014, at 75. — Source: https://variety.com/2014/music/news/joe-sample-dead-1201305148/

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (recommended).** Southern Comfort is the canonical mid-'70s Crusaders jazz-funk statement. Sources consistently tag it as jazz-funk / jazz fusion. ### Region Suggestions

- **US - Texas (recommended).** The band formed in Houston and the Houston identity (the so-called "Gulf Coast sound") is central to their story. Sample, Felder, Hooper, and Henderson all came out of Phyllis Wheatley High School. Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/crusaders
- **US - California.** Defensible because the band had long since relocated to LA and Southern Comfort was recorded at Wally Heider Recording in Hollywood with an LA session orbit (Larry Carlton, Stewart Levine). Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
