### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"When the church gets a backbeat."** Gospel Funk is, per the book's own definition, a label coined after the fact by collectors. "Can't Change Myself" is a perfect specimen: a COGIC church singer from Kansas City cutting a tight, secular-sounding groove for a major-distributed gospel label. The angle: how a sanctified message rides a body-moving rhythm without apology.
- **"The B-side that became the A-side."** On the LP the track is buried at B1, but on the 45 it was promoted to the A-side over the title track "Joined Together." Explore why collectors and the label both gravitated to this cut.
- **"A family business of praise."** Mildred wrote much of her own material and recorded with her three daughters (Barbara, Saundra, and Doll). The entry could frame the Melody-Aires as a Midwest matriarchal gospel enterprise spanning 1958 to 1990.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The 7" single pairs "Can't Change Myself" (A) with "Joined Together" (B), but on the parent LP the order is reversed: "Joined Together" opens Side A and "Can't Change Myself" sits at B1. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1151836-Mildred-Clark-And-The-Kansas-City-Melody-Aires-Joined-Together and https://www.discogs.com/release/8309830-Mildred-Clark-The-Melody-Aires-Cant-Change-Myself
- The single was produced by Lee Young and carries publishing by Lion Publishing Co. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8309830-Mildred-Clark-The-Melody-Aires-Cant-Change-Myself
- The track was rediscovered by collectors and anthologized on gospel-funk crate-digger compilations, including "Bless The People Everywhere (Gospel Funk Of Peacock & Songbird)" (Play Back, Poland, 2016) and "A Deeper Dig: Gospel Funk Of ABC Peacock & Songbird 1969-1975." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1328583-Various-Bless-The-People-Everywhere-Gospel-Funk-Of-Peacock-Songbird

#### Album

- "Joined Together" (1974, ABC Peacock) was credited to "Mildred Clark And The Kansas City Melody-Aires," explicitly naming the Kansas City home base that the 45 sleeve dropped. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1151836-Mildred-Clark-And-The-Kansas-City-Melody-Aires-Joined-Together
- The LP is now a scarce collector item; Discogs lists copies starting around $124.95. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1151836-Mildred-Clark-And-The-Kansas-City-Melody-Aires-Joined-Together

#### Artist

- Mildred Clark (born Mildred Seaberry, c. 1933/1934, Little Rock, Arkansas) organized the group in 1958 in Kansas City, Kansas; she was an active member of Madden Temple COGIC. — Source: https://justgospel.weebly.com/mildred-clark-and-the-melody-aires.html
- She wrote much of her own material and recorded with her three daughters — Barbara, Saundra, and Maryetta ("Doll") — across ABC/Peacock, Savoy, and Wajji, producing at least 14 albums between 1964 and 1990. — Source: https://justgospel.weebly.com/mildred-clark-and-the-melody-aires.html
- She died of cancer on April 21, 2007, in Kansas City, Kansas, and is buried at Highland Park Cemetery. — Source: https://journalofgospelmusic.com/gospel/another-loss-rip-mildred-clark/ and https://justgospel.weebly.com/mildred-clark-and-the-melody-aires.html

### Genre Suggestions

- **Gospel Funk (current).** Best fit and well supported. The book defines Gospel Funk as a collector-coined label for gospel that absorbed funk vocabulary; this track is literally anthologized on compilations titled "Gospel Funk Of Peacock & Songbird." Discogs tags it Funk/Soul + Gospel. Keep it.
- **Deep Funk (secondary case).** The book defines deep funk as "delayed recognition" — a track overlooked on release and rediscovered by crate-diggers. This 45 fits that commercial fate precisely (reissued on rare-groove gospel-funk comps decades later). Gospel Funk is the more specific and apt label, but Deep Funk captures the rediscovery story.
- **Classic Funk (weak case).** Released 1974, squarely inside the 1969-1977 classic era, but it came via a regional gospel act on a gospel imprint with limited distribution, so it lacks the mainstream commercial identity the book asks of Classic Funk. Not recommended.
