### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The mystery group with one perfect record."** Raw Image left behind essentially a single 45 and no biography — a chance to write about the thousands of regional funk acts who pressed one promo and vanished, and how crate-diggers reconstruct lives from a catalog number.
- **"Funk's class commentary."** A funk band titling its A-side after E. Franklin Frazier's incendiary 1957 book *Black Bourgeoisie* (a critique of the Black middle class) is a striking choice. The angle: dance music carrying a pointed sociological title.
- **"Promo-only and proud."** Nearly all surviving copies are white-label DJ promos ("Not For Sale"). A short meditation on records that were never really sold, only serviced to radio, yet outlived their commercial moment.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The B-side is "Go For Broke" — flip back and forth: which side sounds like the intended hit, and does the A-side earn its bold title, or is the groove the real statement?
- Listen for the arrangement around the 1-minute mark: this is a tight, horn-and-rhythm regional funk cut, not a polished major-label production. What tells you this came from a small Philadelphia studio (Virtue) rather than a national hit factory?
- At nearly 5 minutes, the A-side is long for a 1975 funk 45. Track where it would have faded for radio versus where the band actually keeps the pocket going.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Black Bourgeoise" runs 4:55, unusually long for a funk 45 of its era, and was issued as the A-side plug track of a promo-only single. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4968066-Raw-Image-Black-Bourgeoise-Go-For-Broke
- Both sides were written and produced by Clifford "Pete" Rudd. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6988961-Raw-Image-Black-Bourgeoise-Go-For-Broke
- The record is a sought-after deep-funk rarity, with collector sale prices reported roughly in the $38–$95 range (median ~$70). Source: https://www.discogs.com/sell/release/4968066
- The track was reissued on the unofficial compilation *The Funky Soul Survivors* (2000, Soul Patrol Records, France) — where its title was "corrected" to "Black Bourgeoisie." Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/589775-Various-The-Funky-Soul-Survivors

#### Album

- No Raw Image studio album exists for this track; it lived only as a 7" single, which is why the `album` field is intentionally empty. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/1237301
- Its only album-format home is a bootleg/unofficial compilation, *The Funky Soul Survivors*. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/589775-Various-The-Funky-Soul-Survivors

#### Artist

- Raw Image is essentially undocumented: the Discogs artist profile is empty and flagged "Needs Major Changes," making this 45 their defining (possibly only) artifact under this name. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/1237301
- The single was mastered at Virtue Studios (Philadelphia), published by Dandelion Music Co., and distributed by Jamie/Guyden Dist. Corp. — situating Raw Image squarely in the Philadelphia soul/funk ecosystem. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6988961-Raw-Image-Black-Bourgeoise-Go-For-Broke
