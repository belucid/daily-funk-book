### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. One question surfaced during research and is documented for the author:

- **Was George Benson actually on "Finger In It"?** Benson is widely credited as the album's guitarist, and the record is frequently reissued/marketed as "George Benson with The Harlem Underground Band." However, the Discogs main release (468942) restricts the named guitar credit (George Benson) to tracks A1, A2 and B3 — NOT to B1 "Finger In It." Ann Winley's vocal credit does cover A1–B1. So per Discogs, Benson may not play on this specific track. This is worth flagging because the Benson connection is the most marketable hook and could be overclaimed for this cut. Approach taken: cross-checked Discogs release-level per-track credits against the master and against secondary write-ups (Melting Pot, music.fandom); the secondary sources speak about the album generally and do not confirm Benson on B1 specifically. Could not conclusively resolve whether Benson plays on "Finger In It."

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The house band with a secret superstar."** The Harlem Underground Band was essentially Paul Winley's studio house band, yet it quietly contained a young George Benson (in the same year as his crossover smash *Breezin'*) plus veterans like Dave "Baby" Cortez and Willis "Gator" Jackson. The hook: a throwaway "underground" record stacked with real talent hiding behind a generic band name.
- **"Funk made by a doo-wop man chasing the next thing."** Paul Winley was a 1950s doo-wop songwriter (he wrote for The Clovers, Ruth Brown, Big Joe Turner) who pivoted into funk in 1976 and then into the earliest hip-hop (first to record Afrika Bambaataa). "Finger In It" sits at the hinge of those eras.
- **"A family business in groove."** The track is written by Paul AND Ann Winley, with Ann singing lead — a husband-and-wife operation whose daughters (Tanya and Paulette) would later cut early rap records for the same label. Funk as a literal family enterprise.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The album's other cut, "Smokin' Cheeba Cheeba," became a sampled rare-groove staple. Play "Finger In It" right after it: what makes "Cheeba" the one diggers chased and this one the deep cut? Listen for the difference in hook and momentum.
- Ann Winley wrote and sings this. Listen to how the vocal is used — as a lead line or more as another rhythmic instrument riding the pocket? Decide whether the title phrase is sung or vamped.
- Discogs questions whether George Benson actually plays guitar on this track. Listen to the guitar: does it sound like a jazz great slumming on a funk session, or like a workmanlike studio player? Trust your ears before the liner notes.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Finger In It" runs 5:42 and was written by Ann Winley and Paul Winley; Ann Winley sings lead. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/468942 (release of master https://www.discogs.com/master/139515-Harlem-Underground-Band-Harlem-Underground)
- The track was reissued in 1979 as the B-side of a Paul Winley 12" single paired with Creative Source's "Who Is He And What Is He To You," at the same 5:42 length — a sign Winley kept mining the 1976 sessions. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/260123-Creative-Source-Harlem-Underground-Band-Who-Is-He-And-What-Is-He-To-You-Finger-In-It
- Per Discogs' per-track credits, the named guitar (George Benson), organ (Dave "Baby" Cortez) and Willis Jackson sax credits are tied to A1/A2/B3 — leaving open whether the album's marquee players are even on B1 "Finger In It." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/468942

#### Album

- *Harlem Underground* (Paul Winley Records, LP127 / PWLP 127, US, 1976) is a prized street-funk LP whose runouts are hand-etched, with the catalog number differing between cover and labels — hallmarks of a small private-label pressing. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/468942
- The album's "Smokin' Cheeba Cheeba" became a rare-groove/hip-hop touchstone; Tone Lōc sampled it for "Cheeba Cheeba" (1989). — Source: https://music.fandom.com/wiki/Harlem_Underground_Band (via search) and https://www.discogs.com/release/2491872-Harlem-Underground-Band-Smokin-Cheeba-Cheeba
- The lineup reads like a hidden all-star session: George Benson (guitar), Dave "Baby" Cortez (the "Happy Organ" / "Rinky Dink" hitmaker, organ), Willis "Gator" Jackson (tenor sax), Sterling Magee/"McGee" (later half of blues duo Satan and Adam, bass), Earl Williams (drums) and Buddy Lucas (harmonica). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/468942

#### Artist

- "Harlem Underground Band" was effectively Paul Winley's studio house band; the same group later backed Winley's pioneering early hip-hop releases, and Winley was the first label to record Afrika Bambaataa. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winley_Records
- Paul Winley was a Washington, D.C.-rooted doo-wop songwriter (his brother was a Clover; he wrote for Ruth Brown and Big Joe Turner at Atlantic) before founding Winley Records in 1956 on 125th Street in Harlem. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winley_Records
- George Benson cut this in 1976, the same year as his blockbuster crossover album *Breezin'* — making his presence on an obscure Harlem private-press funk LP a notable curio. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk** — Best fit. This is a small private-label (Paul Winley) album that sold poorly on release and found its audience later through crate-diggers and samplers; "Smokin' Cheeba Cheeba" from the same LP is a canonical rare-groove/sample record. Per the book's definition, "the defining trait of deep funk is delayed recognition," which describes this LP exactly. — Source: https://www.meltingpotblog.com (Dig Deep feature, via search) and book genre definitions /Planning/Field Values.md.
- **Classic Funk** — Defensible by era (1976 sits inside the book's 1969–1977 classic window) and because the track's primary identity is funk, not a fusion. Weaker than Deep Funk because the record had no mainstream distribution, radio play, or chart presence — the very things the book ties to "classic."
- **Soul Jazz / Jazz Funk** — Discogs tags the album styles as "Soul-Jazz, Funk." With jazz players (Benson, Willis Jackson) and organ-led grooves, a jazz-leaning label is arguable. But "Finger In It" is a vocal funk track, so Deep Funk remains the stronger primary.
