Odetta is folk royalty. Martin Luther King Jr. referred to her as the "Queen of American Folk Music," and Bob Dylan said she "turned me on to folk singing." *Odetta Sings* is primarily covers of songs by McCartney, Jagger/Richards, Randy Newman, Elton John, and James Taylor. But the two strongest tracks are the two Odetta wrote herself. Today’s "Hit or Miss" and "Movin' It On" were recorded at Muscle Shoals Sound Studio in 1970.

The album flopped. Polydor was unable to market a folk icon as a soul crossover act. This did not stop the later rediscovery of "Hit or Miss," the funkiest three minutes of Odetta’s career. The opening drum break has been sampled more than thirty times.

Odetta Gordon received the National Medal of Arts from President Clinton on September 29, 1999, and was named a Library of Congress "Living Legend" in 2003. She performed her final concert at San Francisco's Hardly Strictly Bluegrass Festival in October 2008, and died of heart disease a couple of months later.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The folk queen goes to Muscle Shoals."** Odetta — Martin Luther King Jr.'s "Queen of American Folk Music" and the woman Bob Dylan said "turned me on to folk singing" — walks into Muscle Shoals Sound Studios in 1970, cuts a swampy original with David Hood and Roger Hawkins, and produces the funkiest two minutes and fifty-two seconds of her career. The hook is the unlikely geography of a Birmingham-born civil-rights folk icon making a deep-funk break with the Swampers.
- **"Typecast and ignored, then resurrected by the crate."** *Odetta Sings* flopped on release because Polydor could not sell a "folkie" as a soul/rock crossover act. The break from "Hit or Miss" then became one of the most-sampled funk drum intros in hip-hop history — DJ Shadow & Cut Chemist's *Brainfreeze*, DJ Premier on Jeru the Damaja, Air, Latyrx, Too $hort. A textbook deep-funk arc: commercial failure to crate-dig canon.
- **"Two songs of her own."** On an album of covers from McCartney, Jagger/Richards, Newman, Elton John, James Taylor, the two strongest tracks are the two Odetta wrote herself — "Hit or Miss" and "Movin' It On". A meditation on what an interpreter is hiding when she finally writes her own.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Listen to the first eight seconds: drums alone, then a clipped horn stab. That break is what DJ Shadow, Cut Chemist, and DJ Premier built records on. Try to hear it as a sampler would — where would you loop it, and where would you cut?
- Odetta's voice on this is not the operatic folk contralto of *Odetta Sings Ballads and Blues*. Listen for the rasp she leans into on the choruses — what has she let into her voice that was not there ten years earlier?
- Count the lyric — it is essentially two lines repeated. Listen for what the band does to keep two minutes and fifty-two seconds from feeling repetitive: where do the horns enter, where does the piano (Carole King) sit in the mix, where does Eddie Hinton's guitar punctuate?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- Written by Odetta herself — one of only two originals on an album otherwise composed entirely of covers (the other is "Movin' It On"). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta_Sings
- The opening drum break has been sampled more than thirty times, including by DJ Shadow & Cut Chemist on *Brainfreeze*, DJ Premier (producing Jeru the Damaja's "The Frustrated Nigga"), Air ("Brakes On"), Latyrx, and Too $hort ("Ain't Nothin' but a Word to Me"). — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/Odetta/Hit-or-Miss/sampled/ ; https://jivetimerecords.com/2024/01/odetta-odetta-sings-polydor-1970/
- Bo Diddley covered "Hit or Miss" four years later on his 1974 album *Big Bad Bo*, produced by Esmond Edwards. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/cover/80225/Bo-Diddley-Hit-or-Miss-Odetta-Hit-or-Miss/
- The track was used in a Southern Comfort advertising campaign in 2012, which sparked a wave of streaming-era rediscovery. — Source: http://zeegrooves.blogspot.com/2012/08/polydor-odetta-and-hit-or-miss.html
- On streaming, "Hit or Miss" has accumulated more than 17 million plays — far more than any other track on *Odetta Sings*. — Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2024/01/odetta-odetta-sings-polydor-1970/

### Album
- *Odetta Sings* was her only album for Polydor and was framed as a deliberate crossover bid — recorded half at Muscle Shoals Sound Studios in Sheffield, Alabama with the Swampers (David Hood, Roger Hawkins, Eddie Hinton, Barry Beckett, Jimmy Johnson) and half at Larrabee Sound in Los Angeles with Carole King on piano, Russ Kunkel on drums, Bernie Leadon on guitar, and Merry Clayton / Clydie King / Venetta Fields / Sherlie Matthews on backgrounds. — Sources: https://sessiondays.com/2024/04/10/1970-odetta-odetta-sings/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta_Sings
- Produced by John Boylan; Herb Cohen (Zappa's manager) was executive producer; Jimmie Haskell arranged the horns and strings. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta_Sings
- Track sources span the contemporary rock canon of late 1970: Elton John/Bernie Taupin, Randy Newman, Paul McCartney, James Taylor, Mick Jagger/Keith Richards, plus Bob Dorough/Stuart Scharf and Don Cooper. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta_Sings
- Commercially the album was a failure — Polydor had Odetta "typecast as a folkie" and could not reach a soul/rock audience. The album was released in December 1970 and the 7" single ("Take Me to the Pilot" / "Hit or Miss", Polydor PD 2-14045) did not chart. — Sources: http://zeegrooves.blogspot.com/2012/08/polydor-odetta-and-hit-or-miss.html ; https://floriankeller.net/odetta-hit-or-miss/
- The LP was reissued on limited-edition gold vinyl by Music on Vinyl on October 6, 2017 — its first vinyl reissue. — Source: https://thevinylfactory.com/news/odetta-1970-album-odetta-sings-reissued/

### Artist
- Born Odetta Holmes on December 31, 1930 in Birmingham, Alabama; took the surname Felious from her stepfather Zadock Felious; later known as Odetta Gordon (Gordon being a married name used in the writing credits on *Odetta Sings*). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- Classically trained as an operatic contralto from age 13; appeared in the Turnabout Puppet Theatre and the national tour of *Finian's Rainbow* before pivoting to folk in 1950. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- Martin Luther King Jr. called her "The Queen of American Folk Music" in 1961; she sang "O Freedom" at the 1963 March on Washington. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- Bob Dylan: "The first thing that turned me on to folk singing was Odetta." Joan Baez and Janis Joplin both named her as a direct influence. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- Awarded the National Medal of Arts by President Clinton on September 29, 1999; named a Library of Congress "Living Legend" in 2005. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- Died of heart disease in New York City on December 2, 2008, age 77, weeks after her final concert in Toronto on October 25, 2008. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta

### Genre Suggestions

The On Genres definitions distinguish Classic Funk (mainstream 1969–77 funk that registered commercially), Deep Funk ("delayed recognition" tracks resurrected by crate-diggers), and Country Funk (the 2012-named genre defined by *Country Funk Vol. I–III*).

- **Deep Funk** — Strongest case. The On Genres definition is almost a description of "Hit or Miss": released into commercial obscurity in 1970, ignored on its first run, then resurrected decades later by DJ Shadow, Cut Chemist, DJ Premier, Air, and Latyrx as a sample source. The "fate" of this track is the textbook deep-funk arc. — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/Odetta/Hit-or-Miss/sampled/ ; http://zeegrooves.blogspot.com/2012/08/polydor-odetta-and-hit-or-miss.html
- **Classic Funk** — Reasonable secondary case. The track was a Polydor release in 1970 on a major-label album with John Boylan producing and the Swampers in the rhythm section — the "commercial pipeline of major labels" criterion is met. Its primary identity is funk, not funk-fused-with-folk. The reason this is the weaker case is that the chart failure means the "central sound in mainstream American music" test fails on the *reception* side even though it passes on the *production* side.
- **Country Funk** — Worth considering only because Muscle Shoals + Southern-rooted session players + a folk artist crossing over fits the *Country Funk* compilation profile, but the song itself is not country-flavored — it is straight funk-soul. I would not pick this.

My recommendation: **Deep Funk**, primary.

### Region Suggestions

The On Regions guidance says the tag can represent "where the artist is from, where the band formed, where the label operated, where the recording happened, or where the scene lived" — the author chooses. The Field Values list includes `US - California`, `US - Southeast`, `US - Northeast`, but no `US - Alabama` and no `US - New York`.

- **US - Southeast** — Strongest case if the tag follows the recording. Half the album, and almost certainly the funkier half including "Hit or Miss," was cut at Muscle Shoals Sound Studios in Sheffield, Alabama with David Hood, Roger Hawkins, Eddie Hinton, Barry Beckett, and Jimmy Johnson — the Swampers. The "swampy Muscle Shoals funk" descriptor in the secondary literature is unanimous. — Sources: https://sessiondays.com/2024/04/10/1970-odetta-odetta-sings/ ; https://jivetimerecords.com/2024/01/odetta-odetta-sings-polydor-1970/
- **US - California** — Reasonable case if the tag follows the artist. Odetta lived in Los Angeles from age 7 and built her early career there; *Odetta Sings* was also partly recorded at Larrabee Sound in LA. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta
- **US - Southeast** also has a secondary "artist origin" case — Odetta was born in Birmingham, Alabama. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odetta

My recommendation: **US - Southeast** — both her birthplace and the Muscle Shoals recording location point to the same region, and the song's funk DNA is Muscle Shoals, not LA.
