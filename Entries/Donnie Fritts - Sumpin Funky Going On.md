### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The Alabama Leaning Man finds the pocket."** Jerry Wexler nicknamed Fritts "the Alabama Leaning Man," and the whole *Prone To Lean* record is a slow-talking, country-soul-meets-Muscle-Shoals showcase. "Sumpin Funky Going On" is the one track where the lean gives way to a flat-out groove. Angle: the side B funk pivot on an otherwise laid-back country record. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- **"When two songwriters' songwriters cut loose."** Fritts and Tony Joe White were both deep-bench songsmiths whose biggest hits were sung by other people — Brook Benton, Tina Turner, UB40, Joe Simon. Here they keep one for themselves and let it get sweaty. Angle: what funk sounds like when the writer is the singer. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Joe_White
- **"How a 2:50 album cut became a 'country funk' anchor 40 years later."** Light in the Attic's 2014 *Country Funk II* compilation elevated this side-B obscurity into a defining example of an after-the-fact genre. The track barely registered in 1974 — its meaning was assigned in 2014. Angle: how crate-digger curators name genres after the fact. Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2014/07/23/sumpin-funky-going-on-country-funk-ii-features-willie-dolly-bobby-jackie-kenny-and-more/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- "Fritts sings like he's three steps behind the beat on purpose. Listen for how the Muscle Shoals rhythm section — David Hood's bass, Roger Hawkins on drums — locks the floor down while he leans the vocal across it. Where would you say the pocket actually lives — in the band, or in the lag of his voice?"
- "This is two of Nashville/Muscle Shoals' best songwriters writing something for themselves to keep. Try to hear what Tony Joe White (writer of 'Polk Salad Annie,' 'Rainy Night in Georgia') brings to the lyric versus what Fritts brings. Whose fingerprints are on which line?"
- "At 2:50, it's a flash. Count how many times the title hook comes around. Does the brevity make the groove feel like a tease, or like it makes its case and gets out?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Co-written by Donnie Fritts and Tony Joe White, the Louisiana-born "Swamp Fox" best known for "Polk Salad Annie" (1969) and "Rainy Night in Georgia" (recorded by Brook Benton, 1970). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Joe_White
- Issued as a 1974 Atlantic promo 7" (catalog 45-3255), with mono on the A-side and stereo on the B-side — a classic radio promo configuration. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2823817-Donnie-Fritts-Sumpin-Funky-Going-On
- Sequenced as the opening cut on side B of *Prone To Lean*, the funkiest track on an otherwise country-soul album. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/626980-Donnie-Fritts-Prone-To-Lean
- Selected as track 14 on Light in the Attic's *Country Funk II: 1967-1974* (LITA 116), released July 15, 2014, which canonized the song within the "country funk" canon. — Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2014/07/23/sumpin-funky-going-on-country-funk-ii-features-willie-dolly-bobby-jackie-kenny-and-more/

#### Album

- *Prone To Lean* was Fritts' debut solo album, co-produced by Jerry Wexler and Kris Kristofferson at Muscle Shoals Sound Studio. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- Wexler nicknamed Fritts "the Alabama Leaning Man," which Fritts adopted as a self-description; the album title is a direct nod to the nickname. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- The backing band reads like a Muscle Shoals all-star roster: David Hood (bass), Roger Hawkins (drums), Barry Beckett (keyboards), Spooner Oldham, Eddie Hinton, Jimmy Johnson, Pete Carr, plus Michael Utley on organ and Mickey Raphael on harp. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1960146-Donnie-Fritts-Prone-To-Lean
- Background vocals included Kris Kristofferson, Rita Coolidge, Dan Penn, Billy Swan, John Prine, and Jerry Wexler himself. — Source: http://rockasteria.blogspot.com/2018/11/donnie-fritts-prone-to-lean-1974-us.html
- It would take Fritts 23 years to release a follow-up solo album (*Everybody's Got A Song*, 1997/1998), reportedly because *Prone To Lean* sold poorly and he stayed busy with session and touring work for Kristofferson. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts

#### Artist

- Donnie Fritts (Donald Ray Fritts) was born November 8, 1942, in Florence, Alabama, and died August 27, 2019, in Birmingham, AL, from complications of heart surgery. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- He was Kris Kristofferson's keyboardist for more than four decades, beginning in 1970, and appeared with him on screen in *Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid* (1973), *A Star Is Born* (1976), and *Convoy* (1978). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- As a songwriter, his biggest commercial hits were sung by others: "Breakfast in Bed" (UB40 with Chrissie Hynde, UK #6 in 1988) and "We Had It All" (recorded by Waylon Jennings, Dolly Parton, Tina Turner, Ray Charles, and Willie Nelson, among many). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts
- Inducted into the Alabama Music Hall of Fame in 2008. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts

### Field Verification

- `album`: *Prone To Lean* — confirmed by Discogs master (https://www.discogs.com/master/626980-Donnie-Fritts-Prone-To-Lean), Wikipedia, AllMusic, Apple Music, Spotify.
- `label`: Atlantic Records — confirmed by Discogs (catalog SD 18117 for the LP, 45-3255 for the promo 7"). Publisher on the single is Combine Music.
- `year`: **1974** with a caveat. The 1974 single (Atlantic 45-3255) is unambiguous on Discogs. The album is more contested: Wikipedia, AllMusic-derived secondary sources, blog reviews, and The Second Disc all say 1974; the Discogs main release page lists "February 1975" for the pressing. The most likely explanation is the album was completed and the single released in 1974 with the LP pressed and distributed at the start of 1975 — a common pattern. I retained 1974 to match the published-song chronology and the dominant secondary literature; if the author wants strict pressing-date accuracy, 1975 is defensible.
- `track length`: **2m 50s** per the Discogs master tracklist (https://www.discogs.com/master/626980-Donnie-Fritts-Prone-To-Lean). Last.fm displays 2:52 (https://www.last.fm/music/Donnie+Fritts/_/Sumpin+Funky+Going+On). The Rockasteria post reports 3:54, which appears to be an error — every Discogs pressing and the original LP label list 2:50. I went with the canonical Discogs figure.
- `region`: **US - Alabama** — Fritts is from Florence, AL, and the album was recorded at Muscle Shoals Sound Studio (Sheffield, AL). See Region Suggestions for the field-list caveat.

### Genre Suggestions

- **Country Funk (current; recommended).** This is the defining example. The song was hand-picked by Light in the Attic in 2014 for *Country Funk II* — the compilation series that effectively named the genre — and it's the cleanest country-funk crossover on a country-soul record. Sources: https://theseconddisc.com/2014/07/23/sumpin-funky-going-on-country-funk-ii-features-willie-dolly-bobby-jackie-kenny-and-more/ ; https://countryfunk.bandcamp.com/track/donnie-fritts-sumpin-funky-going-on
- **Deep Funk.** Defensible secondary read: the original 1974 single sold poorly, the album sold poorly, and the song found its audience 40 years later through crate-digger compilation. That arc — overlooked on release, rediscovered later — fits the book's deep-funk definition. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts (album commercial failure context)
- **Classic Funk.** Weakest of the three: it's 1974, on a major label (Atlantic), made by the Muscle Shoals A-team — but the song never had real chart presence and the album's identity is country-soul, not funk. Likely doesn't qualify under the book's "primary identity is funk" test.

- **US - Alabama (recommended, but see note).** Florence, AL was Fritts' lifelong home base, and Muscle Shoals Sound Studio (Sheffield, AL) is where the album was cut. The track is a Muscle Shoals record in every sense — David Hood, Roger Hawkins, Barry Beckett, Jimmy Johnson, Eddie Hinton are the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section / Swampers. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Fritts ; https://www.discogs.com/release/1960146-Donnie-Fritts-Prone-To-Lean
