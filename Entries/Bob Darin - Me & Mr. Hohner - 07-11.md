You've probably heard of Bobby Darin, but maybe not Bob Darin. Same person, different era. Before 1968, Darin was a tuxedo-wearing Vegas crooner with a clean-cut, all-American image. Then everything shattered. He was present at Robert F. Kennedy’s assassination in 1968. He learned that the woman he thought was his sister was actually his mother. And his long-running heart problems, rooted in childhood rheumatic fever, were now impossible to ignore.

Darin retreated to a 14-foot trailer in Big Sur to regroup. He emerged with a new look, a new voice, and a denim-clad protest-singer persona. *Commitment* documents that reset. The songs leaned into a talk-sung, singer-songwriter style rather than crooning, and they rode on soulful, funky rhythms that were crossing over from Black radio in 1969. Darin wrote, arranged, and produced the record himself. Atlantic was not impressed, so he released it on his own label on July 11, 1969, under the name Bob Darin. The label’s instincts about his audience proved right; the album failed to chart.

Nothing here will shock a modern listener, but today’s track, “Me & Mr. Hohner,” was considered too hot for its time. It was banned from his performance on *This Is Tom Jones* and kept off mainstream radio for two taboo words: “pot” and “hash.”

Darin died a few years later, at 37, after open-heart surgery.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"When Bobby became Bob"** — The track is the lens on Darin's 1968-69 reinvention from tuxedoed Vegas crooner to denim-clad protest folkie. RFK's assassination, the revelation that his "sister" was his mother, and his weakened heart drove him to a 14-foot trailer in Big Sur. *Commitment* is the document of that exile, and the album cover credits him as "Bob Darin" — the only LP he ever issued under that name. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin and https://www.discogs.com/master/671982-Bob-Darin-Commitment
2. **"Country funk as a category was invented around this track"** — Light in the Attic explicitly chose "Me & Mr. Hohner" for *Country Funk II: 1967-1974* (2014, LITA 116), one of the compilation series that retroactively named the genre. The book defines country funk by exactly this kind of after-the-fact canonization. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-ii-1967-1974
3. **"The Hohner you thought it wasn't"** — Many listeners (including some critics) hear "Mr. Hohner" and assume a police nightstick. The truth — that Hohner is the German harmonica brand and "Mr. Hohner" is the narrator's instrument that keeps getting him stopped — flips the song from a violent encounter into a musician-as-suspect narrative. The Discogs release credits Darin specifically with "Blues Harp" alongside vocals and keyboards. Sources: https://thisisbobbydarin.wordpress.com/tag/me-and-mr-hohner/ and https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Darin almost talks more than he sings here. Listen for the moments where his phrasing drops out of melody entirely and becomes proto-rap — and ask yourself how a Vegas crooner from 1959 ended up here in 1969.
2. The "Mr. Hohner" of the title is not a person but Darin's harmonica. Listen for when the harmonica enters the conversation, and notice how it answers back to the police voices in the lyric. Who's defending whom?
3. This track was banned from *This Is Tom Jones* and kept off mainstream radio for the words "pot" and "hash." Listen for the specific moments that would have triggered 1969 broadcast standards — and notice what they had to step around to do it.

### Track

- "Mr. Hohner" is the narrator's harmonica — Hohner being the famous German harmonica manufacturer — not a police baton as many listeners assume. — Source: https://thisisbobbydarin.wordpress.com/tag/me-and-mr-hohner/
- Darin is explicitly credited with "Blues Harp" alongside vocals and keyboards on the Discogs release; the harmonica part is his own performance. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment
- The song specifically targets Philadelphia Police Commissioner Frank Rizzo via repeated "South Philly" references; Rizzo became infamous in June 1969 for arriving at a riot scene in a tuxedo with a nightstick tucked into his cummerbund. — Source: https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia-frank-rizzo-police-violence-legacy-shootings-20200603.html
- Darin was forbidden from performing the song on *This Is Tom Jones*; the lyrics' references to "pot" and "hash" kept it off mainstream radio. — Source: https://thisisbobbydarin.wordpress.com/tag/me-and-mr-hohner/
- The track was released as a 7" single (Direction 351) with "Song For A Dollar" on the B-side, pressed May 1969 per the delta number marking. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4741559-Bobby-Darin-Me-Mr-Hohner
- Album version runs 3:10; the 45 A-side runs 3:15 (a 5-second edit) per Discogs master listings. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/671982-Bob-Darin-Commitment and https://www.discogs.com/master/592404-Bobby-Darin-Me-Mr-Hohner
- Billboard praised it as "another strong message lyric set to an infectious beat [with a] top arrangement and vocal workout." — Source: https://thisisbobbydarin.wordpress.com/tag/me-and-mr-hohner/
- Darin's near-spoken vocal delivery has been called "considerably ahead of its time" — a proto-rap performance from a 1959 Best New Artist Grammy winner. — Source: https://thisisbobbydarin.wordpress.com/tag/me-and-mr-hohner/
- Light in the Attic selected the track for *Country Funk Volume II: 1967-1974* (2014, LITA 116) — one of the compilations that retroactively defined "country funk" as a genre. — Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-ii-1967-1974
- Turnpike Troubadours covered the song on August 26, 2024 as an Amazon Music Original — their first release after *A Cat in the Rain* — recorded at 115 Recording in Norman, Oklahoma. — Source: https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2024/08/23/turnpike-troubadours-to-release-cover-of-bobby-darins-me-mr-hohner/

### Album

- *Commitment* was released on Darin's own Direction Records label (catalog 1937) in July 1969 and credited to "Bob Darin" rather than Bobby — the only LP he ever issued under that name. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- Distribution was handled by Amy-Mala-Bell Records Inc., publishing by T. M. Music, Inc., and pressing by Monarch Record Mfg. Co. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment
- Every song was composed, arranged, and produced by Darin himself. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album) and https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment
- The album did not chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- Discogs classifies the album with Genres: Rock, Funk/Soul, Folk/World/Country and Styles: Country Rock, Funk. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/671982-Bob-Darin-Commitment
- Personnel: Bill Aikins (piano); Tommy Amato and Larry Devers (drums); Barry Chapman and Quitman Dennis (bass); Joey Lemon and Bubba Poythress (guitar); Brent Maher (engineer); Beverly Weinstein (art direction); Martin Singer (photography). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4509949-Bob-Darin-Commitment
- The album was written largely in a 14-foot trailer in an orchard on Pfeiffer Beach, Big Sur. — Source: https://www.avclub.com/on-commitment-bobby-darin-became-a-folkie-radical-1798287583
- Critic Richie Unterberger called the backing "only functional and perfunctory in a generic late-'60s folk-country-rock fashion," treating the album as "a curiosity" in Darin's catalog. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- Darin stated Direction Records' mission was to "seek out statement-makers." — Source: https://www.bobbydarin.com/direction-records
- AV Club describes *Commitment* as blending "country rhythms and soul syncopation" with "choogling boogie" reminiscent of Creedence Clearwater Revival, the Grateful Dead, and late-period Beatles. — Source: https://www.avclub.com/on-commitment-bobby-darin-became-a-folkie-radical-1798287583
- A 2023 reissue on blue vinyl (Direction DR1937) brought the album back into print 54 years later. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/29149930-Bobby-Darin-Commitment

### Artist

- Darin (born Walden Robert Cassotto, May 14, 1936, East Harlem) was the inaugural winner of the Grammy for Best New Artist (1959). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- He was present at the Ambassador Hotel on June 4, 1968 during Robert F. Kennedy's assassination, having traveled with RFK's presidential campaign. The trauma triggered his Big Sur retreat. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- In the same period, Darin learned the woman he believed was his sister was actually his mother, and his "mother" was his grandmother — a family revelation that compounded the post-RFK collapse. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- Darin's *Commitment*-era industry backlash included CBS refusing to air "Long Line Rider" (about Arkansas prison abuse) and Dick Clark publicly calling his old friend "a latter-day hippie." — Source: https://www.avclub.com/on-commitment-bobby-darin-became-a-folkie-radical-1798287583
- He died December 20, 1973, age 37, in Los Angeles after open-heart surgery — the cumulative damage of childhood rheumatic fever. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990; Paul Anka announced the honor. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Country Funk** (current value, retained) — The single strongest case. The track was selected for Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume II: 1967-1974* (2014), one of the compilations the book identifies as having defined the genre after the fact. Discogs independently tags the album with Styles "Country Rock, Funk" — a near-exact match for the country funk fusion the book defines. Sources: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-ii-1967-1974 and https://www.discogs.com/master/671982-Bob-Darin-Commitment
2. **Deep Funk** — Also defensible: *Commitment* never charted, sold poorly, and Darin's "outsider period" was forgotten until crate-diggers rediscovered it via Light in the Attic. The book defines deep funk as "delayed recognition" — *Commitment* fits exactly. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album), https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-ii-1967-1974
3. **Funk Rock** — A weaker third option, given the Creedence/Grateful Dead/Beatles influences AV Club hears on the album as a whole. Less specific to this track. Source: https://www.avclub.com/on-commitment-bobby-darin-became-a-folkie-radical-1798287583

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - California** (currently set — retained) — Strongest case.
