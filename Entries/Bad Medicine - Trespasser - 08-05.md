Fans of deep funk no longer need to take cross-country road trips to find lost 45s and master tapes. Thankfully, some people still love the thrill of the hunt, so the rest of us can get our funk fix from the compilations curated by the labels that specialize in locating, licensing, and reviving forgotten gems.

Ask any deep funk aficionado when the genre took off, and they'll likely point to one record: the August 5, 2001 release of Stones Throw Records' double LP and CD, *The Funky 16 Corners*. Today’s song, “Trespasser,” was included on that record. It's a forgotten 1974 instrumental by Bad Medicine, the house band of Enyx, a short-lived Syracuse label created by the owners of Record Kingdom to release records by local bands.

*The Funky 16 Corners* was born from a ten-day collecting and licensing road trip from Indiana to California by deep-funk legend Eothen Alapatt, better known as Egon. At the time, he was the general manager of Stones Throw Records, and the following year he founded the reissue specialist Now-Again Records.

After appearing on the compilation, the original 7-inch "Trespasser" received its own reissue in 2012, complete with a reproduction of the original label. So, if you decide you have to own the single, you can skip the road trip and the expense. Just remember to thank Egon.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Blue-eyed funk in Syracuse."** Bad Medicine was an all-white band playing straight funk/soul/R&B in the early 1970s, which reportedly cost them bookings at some venues. An angle on race, authenticity, and who gets to play funk. — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/artist/bad-medicine/41655492
- **"The second life of a regional 45."** A minor local hit in 1974 that became a cult classic only after Stones Throw's 2001 *Funky 16 Corners* comp — the classic deep-funk rediscovery arc, and a strong case for what "deep funk" means.
- **"From a trespasser's rage to a sampler's gold."** Producer Arthur Lane literally asked drummer Richard Clarke to channel the fury of chasing an intruder off his property; decades later that intensity was chopped up by Limp Bizkit and Cut Chemist. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- The track is an instrumental, split across a 7" as "Trespasser Pt. 1" (2:39) and "Pt. 2" (3:27). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1024070-Bad-Medicine-Trespasser-
- Producer Arthur Lane conceived it by telling drummer Richard Clarke to "transfer the intensity and rage you would have chasing this guy" off your property into the song. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
- George Day (the studio owner/engineer) played Moog synthesizer on the recording, cut at his Dayson Recording Studio in Syracuse. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
- Sampled by Limp Bizkit ("The Key"), Cut Chemist ("Bunky's Pick"), and Yesterdays New Quintet ("Thinking of You"). — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/184791/Limp-Bizkit-The-Key-Bad-Medicine-Trespasser/ ; https://www.whosampled.com/sample/369615/Cut-Chemist-Bunky's-Pick-Bad-Medicine-Trespasser/ ; https://www.whosampled.com/sample/198146/Yesterdays-New-Quintet-Thinking-of-You-Bad-Medicine-Trespasser/

### Album
- "Trespasser" was never on a studio album; it was a standalone Enyx 45. Its most important later home is Stones Throw's compilation *The Funky 16 Corners* (2001), where it is track 4. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Funky_16_Corners ; https://www.discogs.com/master/153065-Various-The-Funky-16-Corners
- The compilation spotlighted 16 obscure late-60s/early-70s funk 45s from across the US and became an influential crate-digger/DJ reference; Cut Chemist's "Bunky's Pick" (which samples Trespasser) appears as a bonus track on the same comp. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Funky_16_Corners

### Artist
- Bad Medicine formed in Syracuse, NY on Halloween night (Oct 31), 1968. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
- An all-white ensemble playing funk/soul/R&B, which reportedly caused booking problems at some venues; they built a following around Syracuse University and opened for Muddy Waters, Lightnin' Hopkins, John Lee Hooker, and Mitch Ryder. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
- They cut rhythm tracks behind soul vocalist Michelle Sobers on Enyx, including a Dr. John–penned track, "When The Battle Is Over." — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
- The band broke up abruptly in 1975 after guitarist Tom Corradino was seriously injured in a car crash; Corradino, Rado, and Clarke later reunited in the D.C.-based Little Red & the Renegades. — Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2017/06/old-school-gold-trespasser-by-bad.html
