Bobby Darin was known for hits like 1958’s “Splish Splash” and “Mack the Knife.” Ten years later, he was a Vegas act, singing standards and coasting on past notoriety. How did he get from there to a down-tempo song about depression?

Part of what changed for Darin is that on June 5, 1968, after campaigning for presidential hopeful Robert F. Kennedy, he was in the Ambassador Hotel pantry when Kennedy was assassinated by Sirhan Sirhan.

Today’s song, “Light Blue,” is a direct response to that night in the pantry. Darin pairs his questioning lyrics with a funky organ-driven groove, and the result is more mournful and resigned than the rest of *Commitment*.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"When Splish Splash went to Big Sur."** The hook is the chasm between the Bobby Darin who recorded "Mack the Knife" and the bearded "Bob Darin" living in a 14-foot trailer in an orchard, writing dark country-funk dirges. "Light Blue" is the sound of a tuxedoed entertainer who has stopped pretending. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
2. **"Grief in a major-label tuxedo."** Darin was in the Ambassador Hotel pantry when RFK was shot in June 1968. *Commitment* — and "Light Blue" in particular — is one of the most direct artifacts of that night in American popular music. The angle: how a personal political trauma rewrote a pop career. — Source: https://www.lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-1969-1975 (Hundley liner notes summary)
3. **"The accidental country-funk record."** Darin did not set out to make a country-funk album; the genre name didn't exist yet. *Country Funk 1969-1975* (2012) is what posthumously placed "Light Blue" inside the country-funk canon. A meditation on how genres get named decades after the fact. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Funk_1969%E2%80%931975

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Bobby Darin sang "Mack the Knife" in a tuxedo at the Copa. Now listen to "Light Blue" — same voice, same lungs, same year as several of his lounge-act TV appearances. What did he keep, and what did he throw away?
2. Listen for the gap between Darin's vocal and the band. The musicians (Bill Aikins on piano, Bubba Poythress and Joey Lemon on guitars) sound loose, almost detached. Is that a flaw, or is that the feeling? — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
3. The lyric repeats "light blue getting darker every day." Time the moment when his voice itself starts to darken alongside the words. Does the arrangement follow him there, or does it stay polite?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- The song was written, produced, and arranged entirely by Bobby Darin himself. — Source: http://www.bobbydarin.net/lightblue.html
- "Light Blue" was placed on Light in the Attic's 2012 compilation *Country Funk 1969-1975* (track 5 of 16), which is essentially the document that named the country-funk genre. — Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-1969-1975
- Reviewers of the compilation called it "the most surprising song" on the set — "a fantastic song about depression and oncoming gloom." The liner notes connect it directly to the RFK assassination and Darin's Big Sur retreat. — Source: https://defendingaxlrose.com/2012/09/26/country-funk-1969-1975/
- The lyric centers on color imagery — light blue "getting darker every day," black roads with no visible end, flashes of bright red — and the repeated refrain "what I wanna know." — Source: http://www.bobbydarin.net/lightblue.html

### Album
- *Commitment* was credited to "Bob Darin," not "Bobby Darin" — a deliberate distancing from his lounge-singer persona. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- Total runtime is only 31:04 across 10 tracks; the album failed to chart. AllMusic gave it 2/5; the Encyclopedia of Popular Music gave 3/5. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- Engineer Brent Maher (later a major Nashville producer for The Judds, among others) recorded the album. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_(Bobby_Darin_album)
- The album addresses "civil rights, poverty, the Vietnam War, and the death of RFK." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- It was the second of two albums Darin released on his own Direction Records label, after *Born Walden Robert Cassotto* (1968). — Source: https://www.bobbydarin.com/direction-records

### Artist
- Darin was diagnosed with rheumatic-fever-related heart damage at age 8 and was told he would not live past 16. He died at 37 following heart surgery in 1973. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- He was the inaugural winner of the Grammy for Best New Artist (1959). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- In 1968 Darin learned that the woman he believed was his mother was actually his grandmother, and his "sister" was his biological mother — a discovery that hit during the same months as the RFK assassination and triggered his Big Sur retreat. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- He campaigned actively for Robert F. Kennedy and was present at the Ambassador Hotel the night Kennedy was shot, June 5, 1968. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Darin
- He founded Direction Records in 1968 with the stated mission to "seek out statement-makers." — Source: https://www.bobbydarin.com/direction-records

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is **Country Funk**, which is well-supported by external curation. Alternatives the author might weigh:

1. **Country Funk** (current, recommended) — The track was selected for Light in the Attic's *Country Funk 1969-1975*, the compilation that effectively defined the genre. Per the book's own definition of country funk ("a crate-digger compilation effectively named the genre"), "Light Blue" is a textbook fit. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Funk_1969%E2%80%931975 ; /Users/sean/Obsidian/BeLucid Vault/Daily Funk Project/Matter/On Genres.md
2. **Deep Funk** — Also defensible. The book defines deep funk as "less a sound than a commercial fate... tracks that were overlooked on release and only found their audience later through crate-digging." *Commitment* was a commercial flop in 1969 and "Light Blue" was rediscovered four decades later by a crate-digger compilation. The defining trait of deep funk — delayed recognition — fits perfectly. The main argument against is sonic: "Light Blue" doesn't have the heavy break-beat feel of most deep funk entries; its country-folk arrangement is more central to its identity than its delayed recognition. — Source: /Users/sean/Obsidian/BeLucid Vault/Daily Funk Project/Matter/On Genres.md
3. **Funk Rock** is NOT recommended — the track leans country-folk, not rock — and **Gospel Funk** could be considered only weakly (a touch of gospel-rich phrasing per the Drift Records review, but no choir, no praise content). — Source: https://driftrecords.com/en-us/blogs/drift/drift-sunday-classic-bobby-darin-commitment
