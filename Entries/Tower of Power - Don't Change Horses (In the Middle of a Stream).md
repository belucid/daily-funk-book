### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The outside writer"** — Every other song on *Back to Oakland* was written by someone in the band. This one was written by Lenny Williams with Johnny "Guitar" Watson, the only non-member credited anywhere on the record. That makes the album's biggest hit single the one song the band did not write, which opens a nice angle on what an outsider's pen can do for a tight, self-contained unit.

2. **"Two funks meeting"** — In 1974 Watson was mid-transformation from Texas blues guitarist into the pimp-hatted funk showman of *Ain't That a Bitch* (1976), while Tower of Power was a horn-section machine at its commercial peak. The song is a handshake between those two trajectories, one arriving and one departing, and neither sounds like the other's records.

3. **"Memory versus the chart"** — Lenny Williams, decades later, described this as a "top 10" hit and "So Very Hard to Go" as a "#1 record." Billboard says #26 and #17. That gap between how a hit feels from the stage and what the trade papers logged is a warm, human hook, and it is not a gotcha: the song has outlived its chart number.

4. **"The Oakland horn wall"** — This is a great entry point for the Tower of Power horn section as an instrument in its own right: five players, arranged as one voice, punching in unison rather than harmonizing behind the vocal.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Count how many bars pass before the horns play anything at all. When the section finally lands, is it backing Lenny Williams or answering him?"

2. "This is the only song on the album Tower of Power didn't write, and the co-writer was bluesman Johnny 'Guitar' Watson. Listen for what a blues singer's phrasing does to a funk band's pocket. Can you hear the seam?"

3. "Follow Rocco Prestia's bass instead of the vocal for one full pass. He almost never lets a note ring. Does that muted, sixteenth-note chatter make the track feel faster or tighter than it actually is?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Co-written by Johnny "Guitar" Watson and Tower of Power's lead singer Lenny Williams. It is the only song on *Back to Oakland* credited to a writer from outside the band; every other track is by Castillo, Kupka, Garibaldi, Williams, Conte or Chester Thompson. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- Peaked at No. 26 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 22 on the Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs chart in 1974. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The 7" single is a substantially shortened edit: 3:02 on the 45 versus 4:28 on the LP, roughly a minute and a half cut for radio. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3637045-Tower-Of-Power-Dont-Change-Horses-In-The-Middle-Of-A-Stream-I-Got-The-Chop
- The B-side, "I Got the Chop," is also from *Back to Oakland*, written by Emilio Castillo and Stephen Kupka, and arranged by the band. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/151537-Tower-Of-Power-Back-To-Oakland
- Backing vocals on the track are by Marilyn Scott and Pepper Watkins, and the arrangement is credited to Tower of Power collectively rather than to Greg Adams, who arranged most of the album. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/151537-Tower-Of-Power-Back-To-Oakland
- Looking back on his Tower of Power years, Lenny Williams named this song among his proudest accomplishments with the band, saying he was honored to write it with Johnny "Guitar" Watson. — Source: https://urbanbridgez.com/2024/05/18/ub-anniversary-revisit-lenny-williams-talks-legendary-career/
- The band still plays it: a live version appears on *50 Years of Funk & Soul: Live at the Fox Theater, Oakland, CA, June 2018*, running 6:28, more than two minutes longer than the studio cut. — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/dont-change-horses-live/1886607297?i=1886607610

#### Album

- *Back to Oakland* is Tower of Power's fourth album, released on Warner Bros. in February 1974. It reached No. 26 on the Billboard 200 and No. 13 on the Top Soul LPs chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- Recorded across two studios on opposite ends of the West Coast: Record Plant in Sausalito, California and Kaye/Smith in Seattle, Washington. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The album is bookended by "Oakland Stroke..." (0:53) and "...Oakland Stroke" (1:08), the same figure opening and closing the record. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- *Modern Drummer* voted it one of the most important recordings for drummers to listen to. AllMusic gave it 4.5 out of 5; Robert Christgau gave it a C+. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland
- The record's string charts pulled in Los Angeles session heavyweights, including Bud Shank on flute and saxophones and Frank Rosolino on trombone, with Harry Betts conducting on "Below Us, All the City Lights." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/151537-Tower-Of-Power-Back-To-Oakland
- The second single, "Time Will Tell," reached No. 69 R&B and No. 27 on the Dance chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland

#### Artist

- Tower of Power began as a teenage soul band in Fremont, California called the Motowns, led by Detroit-born Emilio Castillo. Castillo and Stephen "Doc" Kupka met over the July 4 weekend in 1968 at the Alameda County Fair. — Source: https://thepier.org/tower-of-power-oakland-east-bay-grease/
- Bill Graham signed the band to his Fillmore/San Francisco Records label for their 1970 debut *East Bay Grease*. Castillo: "Bill Graham had always been a fan of horns and rhythm and he liked the band and signed us to our first record deal." — Source: http://www.classicbands.com/TowerOfPowerInterview.html
- Lenny Williams was born February 6, 1945 in Little Rock, Arkansas, and raised in Oakland. He put a solo career on hold to audition for Tower of Power in 1972 and left in 1975 to resume it. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Williams and https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/lenny-williams-7540/
- Castillo confirms Williams sang on the band's highest-charting material: "Lenny Williams was the singer on most of the songs that charted the highest." — Source: http://www.classicbands.com/TowerOfPowerInterview.html
- Johnny "Guitar" Watson was born February 3, 1935 in Houston, Texas. He died May 17, 1996 in Yokohama, Japan, collapsing onstage at the Ocean Boulevard Blues Cafe mid-performance of "Superman Lover." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_%22Guitar%22_Watson
- Bassist Francis "Rocco" Prestia, whose muted sixteenth-note fingerstyle is a signature of this era of the band, was born March 7, 1951 in Sonora, California and died September 29, 2020. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Prestia
- Trumpeter Mic Gillette was born May 7, 1951 in Oakland and died January 17, 2016 in Concord, California. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mic_Gillette
- Note for fact-checkers: the Chester Thompson in Tower of Power (keyboards) is a different musician from the Chester Thompson who drummed for Genesis and Frank Zappa. Discogs disambiguates him as "Chester Thompson (2)." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/151537-Tower-Of-Power-Back-To-Oakland

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty. It is not agent-editable, so this is a recommendation only. Note that `Planning/Field Values.md` spells the jazz option **Jazz Funk** (no hyphen), while other project docs use "Jazz-Funk"; use the Field Values spelling verbatim.

1. **Classic Funk (recommended).** This is squarely the book's definition: a 1974 major-label release, well distributed, a charting single with radio play, from funk's central commercial period, and the track's primary identity is funk rather than funk fused into another genre. It also matches the sibling entry `Tower of Power - Squib Cakes - 11-04.md`, which is tagged Classic Funk. — Source: `Matter/On Subgenres.md`; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland

2. **Jazz Funk (alternative).** Discogs tags the *Back to Oakland* master with the styles Jazz-Funk, Soul and Funk, and files it under the Jazz genre alongside Funk / Soul. There is a real case here: a five-piece horn section, charted arrangements, and players with jazz training. But the book's definition of jazz-funk asks for longer tracks, room for extended improvisation, and less reliance on vocal choruses, and this song is a tight 4:28 vocal single with no solo section. "Squib Cakes" on the same album would fit jazz-funk far better than this does. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/151537-Tower-Of-Power-Back-To-Oakland; `Matter/On Subgenres.md`

3. **Funk Rock (weak, listed for completeness).** Bruce Conte's guitar tone and the backbeat give the track a rock edge, but nothing here reads as a funk/rock fusion in the book's sense. Not recommended.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - California (recommended, and what I wrote).** Tower of Power formed in Oakland in 1968, the band is inseparable from the East Bay in name and identity, the album is called *Back to Oakland*, and it was partly recorded at Record Plant in Sausalito. This also matches the sibling entry `Tower of Power - Squib Cakes - 11-04.md`. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Power; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Oakland

2. **US - West (alternative).** *Back to Oakland* was recorded in two places, and the second was Kaye/Smith Studios in Seattle, Washington. There is no Washington value in the enumeration, and `US - West` would cover both sessions. I do not recommend it: the band's origin and identity are unambiguously Oakland, and the broader tag would lose the thing that matters most about this record.

3. **No case for extending the enumeration.** Washington State appears here only as a rented room. `US - California` is both accurate and defensible, and no new region value is warranted.
