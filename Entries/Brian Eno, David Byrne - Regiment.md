### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Funk built from found voices."** Regiment has no funk vocalist in the traditional sense — its lead "singer" is a sampled Lebanese mountain singer dropped onto a hard, danceable groove. The track is funk assembled out of tape, not performed live, which makes it a clean test of how far the genre's boundaries can stretch.
- **"The sample before sampling."** This 1981 album is routinely cited as a foundational predecessor to hip-hop's sample culture, predating the technology that would make it routine. The hook is funk as a studio collage practice years ahead of its moment.
- **"Consent, culture, and the long tail of a sample."** Dunya Younes did not learn her voice was on the record until 2017, 36 years later. The entry can use Regiment to open the ethical question of appropriating voices from "Music in the World of Islam" for a Western art-funk record.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The "lead vocal" is a Lebanese singer recorded for an ethnographic album and pasted onto a funk groove she never heard. Listen for the seam: can you tell the voice and the band were never in the same room?
- Strip away the wailing vocal sample for a moment and focus only on the bassline and guitar. Is this a funk track underneath, or only funk-adjacent? What would make you call it one or the other?
- This was built in 1979–80 from tape loops, before sampling was a standard tool. Listen for the repetition and try to hear where the loop turns over.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Regiment's lead vocal is a sample of Lebanese mountain singer Dunya Younes (credited "Dunya Yusin"), taken from the track "Abu Zeluf" on the ethnographic album *Music in the World of Islam, Volume One: The Human Voice* — not an original recording made for the session. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- Younes did not know her voice appeared on the record until 2017; after her family contacted Eno and Byrne, Regiment and "The Carrier" were briefly pulled from streaming services, then reinstated after an amicable out-of-court settlement. Younes later said she was flattered and credited it with exposing Lebanese culture to a wider audience. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- The track's composition is credited to Brian Eno, David Byrne and bassist Michael "Busta Cherry" Jones. — Source: https://open.spotify.com/track/70ZJqNYyi9euVNGwjqTWXS

#### Album

- *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts* is widely regarded as a pioneering use of sampling, predating its routine use in hip-hop and electronic music. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- The album triggered multiple clearance/controversy issues: the Islamic Council of Great Britain objected to the Qur'anic recitation on the track "Qu'ran," which was removed and replaced on later pressings, and an evangelist's estate refused clearance for another intended sample. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- It was recorded across 1979–1980 in five studios (R.P.M. and Blue Rock in NYC, Eldorado in Burbank, Different Fur in San Francisco, Sigma Sound in NYC) and charted at #44 on the Billboard 200, #29 UK, and #8 in New Zealand. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- The album marked Byrne's first studio work released outside of Talking Heads. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)

#### Artist

- Brian Eno (b. 15 May 1948, Suffolk, England) is a self-described "non-musician" who joined Roxy Music as synth player in 1971 and coined the term "ambient music." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno
- David Byrne (b. 14 May 1952, Dumbarton, Scotland; raised in the US) was the principal songwriter and frontman of Talking Heads, whom Eno was producing around the same period. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Byrne
- Eno and Byrne's two birthdays fall one day apart (May 14 and May 15), and they did not collaborate on a full album again until *Everything That Happens Will Happen Today* in 2008. — Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Brian-Eno

### Genre Suggestions

- **Avant-Funk (current value — keep).** The book defines avant-funk as the experimental spirit of art rock and post-punk pushed toward rhythm and the dance floor, rooted in the mid-1970s–mid-1980s. Regiment (1981), built from tape loops and a sampled voice over a funk groove by two art-rock figures, is close to a textbook fit. Discogs styles for the album include "Funk," "Avantgarde," and "Experimental." Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/6392-Brian-Eno-David-Byrne-My-Life-In-The-Bush-Of-Ghosts
- **Electro Funk (secondary).** The book limits electro funk to late-1970s–mid-1980s funk defined by electronic texture and experimental studio technique. The loop-based, studio-collage construction supports this, though Regiment leans more art/experimental than synth-and-drum-machine, so avant-funk remains the better primary.
- Recommendation: keep **Avant-Funk** as primary; note **Electro Funk** as a defensible alternate.

### Region Suggestions

- **UK (chosen).** Brian Eno is English and E.G. Records was the UK label; the closest enumerated international option that captures the project's European art-rock identity is UK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno
- **US - New York (alternate).** David Byrne was US-based (Talking Heads, NYC), the album was largely recorded in New York (R.P.M., Blue Rock, Sigma Sound) and released in the US on Sire, and Byrne's scene was downtown NYC. A strong case exists for US - New York. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life_in_the_Bush_of_Ghosts_(album)
- This is a genuinely transatlantic record (English Eno + US-based Byrne, recorded in NYC/CA/SF, dual US/UK labels). Both UK and US - New York are defensible; the author may prefer US - New York given the recording locations and Byrne's scene. Both values appear verbatim in `Planning/Field Values.md`, so either is enumeration-legal. No new region needs adding.
