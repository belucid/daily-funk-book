### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The sample outlived the song."** Alpha sank on release and Stax collapsed months later, yet "Life Is Funky" was resurrected by LL Cool J, Ice-T, Public Enemy, and The Chemical Brothers. The entry can hinge on how a commercial failure became hip-hop and big-beat DNA.
- **"Funk on a sinking ship."** The record came out on Truth, a Stax subsidiary, in the very year Stax was bleeding out toward its December 1975 bankruptcy. A track titled "Life Is Funky" arriving as the label died is a sharp irony to mine.
- **"From the Slauson to the Monopoly."** Robin Lloyd's arc — '60s LA dance-craze hitmaker ("Do The Slauson," "Kick That Little Foot Sally Ann") reinventing himself as a '70s funk bandleader — frames the entry as one performer chasing the sound of each decade.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This four-bar groove has been looped by LL Cool J, Ice-T, Public Enemy, and The Chemical Brothers. Listen for the exact drum-and-bass pocket they all wanted, and decide what makes it so loopable.
- The track is buried on an album that opens and closes with 18- and 47-second instrumental fragments ("Alpha" and "Omega"). Listen to how "Life Is Funky" carries a full-band horn arrangement yet still feels like a stripped-down jam — where does the arrangement add and where does it get out of the groove's way?
- Compare the 3:46 single edit with how The Chemical Brothers chopped it for "Come With Us." What did they keep, and what did they throw away?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Life Is Funky" has been sampled by LL Cool J ("It Gets No Rougher," 1989), Ice-T ("It's On," 1993), Public Enemy ("Whole Lotta Love Goin' On In The Middle Of Hell," 1994), and The Chemical Brothers ("Come With Us," 2002) — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha and https://www.whosampled.com/sample/447156/The-Chemical-Brothers-Come-With-Us-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Life-Is-Funky/
- It was issued as a 7" single (Truth Records TRA-3209) backed with "I'd Rather Loan You Out" — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/652986-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Life-Is-Funky-Id-Rather-Loan-You-Out
- The single A-side runs 3:46 but the same track on the Alpha LP is listed at 3:45 — a minor source discrepancy — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/634880-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Alpha

#### Album

- Alpha (1974) was the group's only full-length, released on Stax's short-lived Truth imprint (TRS-4201) — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- The album bookends itself with a 0:18 intro ("Alpha") and a 0:47 outro ("Omega"), with a 1:11 "Prayer Of The Prisoner" near the end — a concept-album framing rare for an obscure funk LP — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/634880-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Alpha
- It was remixed at the Record Plant in Los Angeles (engineers including Gary Olazabal and Michael Verdick) and mastered in part at Stax by Larry Nix — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/634880-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Alpha
- Alpha was reissued on vinyl for the first time in 50 years on June 28, 2024, via Craft Recordings' Jazz Dispensary Top Shelf series, cut AAA from the original analog tapes by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha

#### Artist

- The group was led by Los Angeles singer/keyboardist "Round" Robin Lloyd, who had '60s regional hits including "Do The Slauson" and the UK-mod favorite "Kick That Little Foot Sally Ann" — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha and http://pancocojams.blogspot.com/2017/05/round-robin-kick-that-little-foot-sally.html
- The band included conguero Jimmy Lee Thompson, formerly of The Isaac Hayes Movement — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- Production was handled by Stax in-house drummer Willie Hall (Booker T. & the MG's / The Blues Brothers) alongside Randall "Randy" Stewart — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/634880-The-Round-Robin-Monopoly-Alpha

### Date Suggestions

The exact 1974 release dates for the single and album are not documented anywhere I could find (Discogs lists year only). I therefore lean on related dated events:

- **December 19, 1975 — Stax seized.** Federal marshals padlocked Stax and seized its master tapes; the label had $304.59 in the bank. This ties directly to the "funk on a sinking ship" angle for a record born on a Stax subsidiary. *Not currently used by any entry.* — Source: https://www.collegehiphop.com/2015/12/howstaxwentbroke/ and https://staxmuseum.org/1969-1975/
- **June 28, 2024 — Alpha reissue.** The album's first vinyl pressing in 50 years; a strong "rediscovery" date if the author wants to foreground the crate-digger afterlife. *Not currently used.* — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- **2002 (Chemical Brothers' "Come With Us") / 1989 (LL Cool J) / 1994 (Public Enemy).** If the author prefers a sampling-anniversary hook, any of these sample release dates could anchor the entry. — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- **Avoid January 12** — Stax/Volt's formal closure date (Jan 12, 1976) is already occupied by `Crusaders - Georgia Cottonfield - 01-12.md`.

My primary suggestion is **December 19** (Stax seizure), as it is thematically richest and currently free.

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk** — The strongest fit by definition. Released 1974 (within the book's 1969-1977 classic window) on a major-label subsidiary (Stax/Truth), the track's primary identity is straight-ahead funk, not funk fused into another genre. — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- **Deep Funk** — Equally defensible. Alpha flopped on release and only found its audience through crate-digging DJs and hip-hop samplers decades later; "delayed recognition" is precisely the book's deep-funk definition. The book may prefer this for the sampling-afterlife angle. — Source: https://craftrecordings.com/blogs/news/the-round-robin-monopoly-alpha
- **Psychedelic Funk** — Craft and several reviewers explicitly call Alpha a "psychedelic-funk rarity," citing the album's psych-pop blend and its Alpha/Omega concept framing. A reasonable third option if the author wants to honor the label's own marketing. — Source: https://glidemagazine.com/302510/the-round-robin-monopolys-rare-psychedelic-funk-nugget-alpha-gets-vinyl-reissue-album-review/

My lead recommendation is **Classic Funk** for the track itself, with **Deep Funk** as a close alternative given the rediscovery story.
