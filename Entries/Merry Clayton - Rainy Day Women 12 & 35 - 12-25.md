What do you name your daughter when she’s born on Christmas Day? “Merry,” of course. Merry Clayton was born on Christmas in 1948 and became one of the great backing singers. You know her voice, even if you don't know her name.

Clayton’s 1975 album *Keep Your Eye On the Sparrow* featured an A-list of funky session players, including Steve Gadd, Idris Muhammad, Bob James, and Ralph MacDonald. She earned that support the hard way from years of key background work for artists like Ray Charles, Neil Young, Mick Jagger, and Joe Cocker. Her vocal work was featured in the Grammy- and Oscar-winning documentary *20 Feet From Stardom*.

In 2014, a car accident left her with both legs amputated below the knee. When she was told, her first question was whether anything had happened to her voice. She recovered and released a new album in 2021, *Beautiful Scars*.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

 Key findings:
  - The track is a tight, sub-3-minute funk reworking of Bob Dylan's famously shambolic 1966 original, arranged by session guitar legend Hugh McCracken
  - The album features a staggering session lineup: Steve Gadd, Idris Muhammad, Bob James, David Spinozza, Ralph MacDonald, Joe Farrell, Tom Scott, with a young Jimmy Iovine engineering
  - Producer Eugene McDaniels was himself a radical artist whose Headless Heroes of the Apocalypse was allegedly suppressed by Spiro Agnew
  - Clayton's life story is extraordinary: Christmas baby named "Merry," dragged from bed in curlers to record "Gimme Shelter" while pregnant (miscarriage followed), brother Sam plays in Little Feat, double leg amputation after 2014 car crash, and a 2021 comeback album
  - The track was recently included on Ace Records' 2026 "Highway of Diamonds: Black America Sings Bob Dylan" compilation
  
1. **"The gospel singer who funkified Dylan"**: Merry Clayton took Bob Dylan's famously shambolic, Salvation Army brass band singalong and turned it into a tight, punchy funk workout. The angle is the transformation itself: what happens when a New Orleans-raised, church-trained voice meets a song that was already controversial on arrival. Clayton's version strips away Dylan's ironic shamble and replaces it with conviction and groove.

2. **"From curlers to legend"**: Clayton's career is defined by being summoned. Dragged out of bed in curlers for "Gimme Shelter," she delivered one of rock's most iconic vocal moments while pregnant, then suffered a miscarriage. This track exists on the album that was her last for Ode Records, the label run by Lou Adler, who would later help coax her back to music after a devastating 2014 car accident that cost her both legs. The theme is resilience and the repeated pattern of showing up when called.

3. **"The New York session that sounded like LA"**: The album was recorded at both A&M Studios in LA and Record Plant in NYC, with a murderer's row of session players (Steve Gadd, Idris Muhammad, Bob James, David Spinozza, Hugh McCracken, Ralph MacDonald). The angle is the collision of New York's elite session world with a New Orleans-born, LA-based gospel-funk singer, all orchestrated by Eugene McDaniels, himself a radical artist whose own album was allegedly suppressed by Spiro Agnew.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- Clayton's version of "Rainy Day Women #12 & 35" runs just 2 minutes 50 seconds, roughly half the length of Dylan's album version, stripping away the original's loose marching band feel and replacing it with a tight funk arrangement. — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/keep-your-eye-on-the-sparrow/362304383
- The track was arranged by Hugh McCracken, legendary NYC session guitarist who played on records by John Lennon, Paul McCartney, Paul Simon, Steely Dan, and many more. — Source: https://sessiondays.com/2020/10/14/1975-merry-clayton-keep-your-eye-on-the-sparrow/
- The track was included on Ace Records' 2026 compilation "Highway of Diamonds: Black America Sings Bob Dylan," which describes Clayton's version as "a cheeky, tight and punchy take on a famously shambolic song." — Source: https://www.soulandjazzandfunk.com/reviews/various-black-america-sings-bob-dylan-ii-ace/

#### Album
- *Keep Your Eye on the Sparrow* was Clayton's fourth and final album for Ode Records, released August 1975. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1267979-Merry-Clayton-Keep-Your-Eye-On-The-Sparrow
- The title track, written by Morgan Ames and Dave Grusin, became the theme song for the TV cop series *Baretta* and reached #45 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #42 R&B. — Source: https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/merry-clayton-born-december-25-1948-soul-singer
- The album was produced by Eugene McDaniels (credited as "Gene McDaniels" on the cover, "Eugene McDaniels" on the label), the radical singer-songwriter whose own 1971 album *Headless Heroes of the Apocalypse* was allegedly pulled from stores at the request of Vice President Spiro Agnew. McDaniels wrote "Compared to What" (a hit for Les McCann & Eddie Harris) and "Feel Like Makin' Love" (a #1 hit for Roberta Flack). — Source: http://eugenemcdaniels.com/biography/
- The session band is staggering: Steve Gadd and Idris Muhammad on drums, Bob James on keyboards, David Spinozza and Hugh McCracken on guitars, Gary King on bass, Ralph MacDonald on percussion, Joe Farrell on sax, Tom Scott on horns and synthesizer. Engineer Jimmy Iovine, who would later co-found Interscope Records and Beats Electronics, also worked on the album. — Source: https://sessiondays.com/2020/10/14/1975-merry-clayton-keep-your-eye-on-the-sparrow/
- Recording took place at A&M Studios in Los Angeles and Record Plant in New York City. — Source: https://sessiondays.com/2020/10/14/1975-merry-clayton-keep-your-eye-on-the-sparrow/

#### Artist
- Clayton was born December 25, 1948, in Gert Town, New Orleans, and named "Merry" because she was a Christmas baby. Her father, Rev. Dr. Alvin G. Williams Sr., was the preacher at New Zion Baptist Church, where she received her earliest gospel training. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- She relocated to Los Angeles at age 8 and began her recording career at 13 or 14, recording a duet with Bobby Darin ("Who Can I Count On?") for his 1963 album. — Source: https://rocksoff.org/merry-bio.htm
- At 15, Clayton joined Ray Charles's backing group, The Raelettes, and credited Ray Charles with teaching her complex four-part harmony. — Source: https://rocksoff.org/merry-bio.htm
- Her most famous session: in 1969, she was dragged out of bed at midnight by producer Jack Nitzsche, showed up to the studio in curlers, and recorded her searing vocals on the Rolling Stones' "Gimme Shelter" in just a few takes. She was pregnant at the time and suffered a miscarriage shortly after. She could not listen to the song for years afterward. — Source: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/rolling-stones-gimme-shelter-merry-clayton-story-miscarriage/
- Her brother is Sam Clayton, percussionist and backing vocalist for Little Feat since 1972. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Clayton
- She married jazz saxophonist Curtis Amy in 1970; he passed away June 5, 2002. They had two sons. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- Clayton sang backing vocals on Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Sweet Home Alabama" alongside Clydie King. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- She contributed to Ringo Starr's "Oh My My" (1973), which reached the Billboard Top 10. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- She performed as the original Acid Queen in the London production of The Who's *Tommy* in 1972. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- She sang backing vocals on Carole King's landmark *Tapestry* album (1971), also on Ode Records. — Source: https://www.legacyrecordings.com/2013/06/03/merry-clayton-one-of-the-stars-of-the-new-20-feet-from-stardom-documentary-and-the-vocalist-on-the-rolling-stones-gimme-shelter-celebrated-on-the-best-of-merry-clayton/
- On June 16, 2014, she was critically injured in a car accident in Los Angeles, resulting in the amputation of both legs below the knee. When informed of the amputations, she asked: "Well, did anything happen to my throat?" — Source: https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/merry-claytons-comeback-after-devastating-accident-ive-been-in-the-battle-of-my-life-051805342.html
- She was prominently featured in the Oscar-winning 2013 documentary *20 Feet from Stardom*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry_Clayton
- Chris Martin convinced her to return to the studio about a year after her double amputation, singing on Coldplay's "Adventure of a Lifetime" and "Up&Up." She released her solo album *Beautiful Scars* in 2021, her first solo record in over 25 years. — Source: https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/merry-claytons-comeback-after-devastating-accident-ive-been-in-the-battle-of-my-life-051805342.html

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Deep Funk** (current value): This is a reasonable fit. *Keep Your Eye on the Sparrow* was not a major commercial success outside the title track's moderate chart performance, and "Rainy Day Women #12 & 35" was a deep album cut, never released as a single. The album has been rediscovered by crate-diggers, and its inclusion on the 2026 Ace Records "Highway of Diamonds" compilation confirms its delayed-recognition status. The deep funk label fits the track's commercial fate.
   - Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/highway-of-diamonds-black-america-sings-bob-dylan

2. **Classic Funk**: The session band (Gadd, Muhammad, Bob James, Spinozza, McCracken, Ralph MacDonald) places this squarely in the 1975 professional funk/soul studio world. The production by Eugene McDaniels and the Ode Records/A&M distribution pipeline means this was a commercially intended release on a well-distributed label. It falls within the 1968-1977 classic funk era. The argument against: the album didn't have major chart impact beyond the title track.
   - Source: https://sessiondays.com/2020/10/14/1975-merry-clayton-keep-your-eye-on-the-sparrow/

3. **Soul Jazz**: The session personnel (Bob James, Joe Farrell, Tom Scott, Steve Gadd, Idris Muhammad) are primarily jazz musicians moonlighting in funk/soul sessions. The album has a sophisticated, jazz-inflected sound. However, the track itself leans more toward a funk groove than a jazz structure.
