### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The nearly-men of soul."** Jimmie & Vella had every advantage — discovered by Nichelle Nichols (Star Trek's Uhura), championed by James Baldwin, produced by Bobby Womack, managed by Jimi Hendrix's own manager — and still it never happened. The entry can frame "Chica Boom" as a glimpse of the funk record they could have been famous for. (Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980)
- **"The funk hiding on the folk record."** The self-titled 1972 Atlantic LP is largely a folky, hymnal soul album (it even closes on "Lord Abide With Me"), yet buried on side B is this percussive groove. A case for the one funk track that a crate-digger's needle stops on.
- **"Cursed by a plane crash."** Manager Michael Jeffery (who also managed Hendrix) died in a plane crash shortly after the album's release, and the record sank. A meditation on how much of funk's "deep funk" canon exists because of bad luck, not bad music. (Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Most of this album is gentle folk-soul; "Chica Boom" is the outlier. Listen for the percussion that gives the track its name — where is the "boom," and what makes this cut so different from the hymn ("Lord Abide With Me") sitting a few tracks away on the same LP?
- The Camerons play guitar and sing; the only other credited musician on the core sessions is drummer Dave Palmer. Listen for how few hands are actually making this groove, then decide whether it sounds sparse or full to you.
- This is a sibling duo. Track Vella's and Jimmie's voices separately — who is carrying the lead, and where do they lock together?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- "Chica Boom" was written by both siblings (credited "Jimmie & Vella Cameron") and issued both as side B1 of the album and as the A-side of an Atlantic 45 (catalog 45-2877), backed with "Old Men." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/915882-Jimmie-Vella-Chica-Boom-Old-Men
- The track was cut at Jimi Hendrix's Electric Lady Studios in New York, with strings arranged by Larry Fallon (who arranged Van Morrison's *Astral Weeks* and Nico's *Chelsea Girl*). — Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- Album and single durations disagree: the album lists "Chica Boom" at 3:16, the 45 master at 3:14. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/251234-Jimmie-Vella-Jimmie-Vella and https://www.discogs.com/master/915882-Jimmie-Vella-Chica-Boom-Old-Men

### Album
- Every track on the 1972 self-titled Atlantic LP (SD 8301) is an original Cameron composition, and Vella wrote or co-wrote the majority — an unusual songwriting split for a Black female artist on a major label in 1972. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/251234-Jimmie-Vella-Jimmie-Vella
- James Baldwin admired them enough to associate his name with their work, praising their "singing of the healing power of the human spirit." — Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- The album's engineer of record is Dave Palmer, who also drums on the sessions; other credits include lacquer cut by George Piros and design by Tom Wilkes. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/25551145

### Artist
- Jimmie & Vella are a brother-and-sister duo from Chattanooga, Tennessee who moved to Los Angeles as teenagers (~1966) and were signed to Reprise while still teens. — Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- Actress Nichelle Nichols (Lt. Uhura on *Star Trek*) discovered them and steered Jimmie toward Frank Silvera's American Theatre of Being, where he performed in Baldwin's play *The Amen Corner*. — Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- Their manager was Michael Jeffery, who managed Jimi Hendrix; Jeffery died in a plane crash soon after this album, and the duo never broke through, later resurfacing with *Song Painters* (1981). — Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980 and https://www.funkmysoul.gr/albums-no-review/jimmie-vella-cameron-1981-song-painters/

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk (strongest).** "Chica Boom" fits the book's Deep Funk definition almost exactly: a track that came from a well-distributed major (Atlantic) but from a poorly selling, low-visibility record, overlooked on release and later prized by crate-diggers (the 45 trades among soul collectors; the album is a Dusty Groove/rare-soul item). The defining trait — delayed recognition — is precisely this record's story. Source: Matter/On Subgenres.md; https://www.dustygroove.com/item/14640/Jimmie-Vella-Cameron:Jimmie-Vella
- **Classic Funk (secondary).** By era (1972) and pipeline (major label single with a B-side) it sits inside the classic-funk window; the objection is that the parent album's primary identity is folk-soul, not funk.
- **Latin Funk (tertiary, worth a listen-check).** The title and percussive character ("Chica Boom," conga-forward) invite a Latin-funk reading if the groove leans on Latin percussion textures. Recommend the author confirm by ear before choosing this. Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980

### Region Suggestions

This is a genuinely ambiguous case; all three candidates below are verbatim-valid enumerated values from `Planning/Field Values.md`.

- **US - California (written to field).** The duo relocated to LA as teenagers, were signed there (Reprise), and operated as an LA-based act through this era — the "scene where the artist lived" reading. Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- **US - Southeast (strong alternate — origins).** Both Camerons are natively from Chattanooga, Tennessee. If the author weights birthplace/roots (as several entries do), Southeast is the more defensible tag. Tennessee maps to `US - Southeast` in the enumeration. Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980
- **US - New York (alternate — recording/label).** The album was recorded at Electric Lady Studios in NYC and released on Atlantic, a New York label. Source: https://www.patreon.com/NeedleMythology/posts/lieutenant-uhura-143631980 ; https://www.discogs.com/release/25551145
