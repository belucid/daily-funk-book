### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in the entry. Open questions surfaced by research:

- **Label name spelling.** Discogs lists the label as "Microtronics Sound" (catalog M1001), and Bull City Soul calls it "Microtronics." A popsike auction listing renders it "Microfonics Sound." The label was owned by E. W. McCuller, who also released the Communicators, Black Experience Band, and Shadows of Soul. I used the Discogs spelling "Microtronics Sound." Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html ; https://www.popsike.com/Funk-45RPM-Duralcha-on-Microfonics-Sound-RARE/270087101153.html
- **Band name spelling.** The band is filed on Discogs and the entry as "Duralcha," but Bull City Soul, Spotify, and Shazam spell it "Duracha." Bull City Soul explains the name is a contraction of Durham, Raleigh, and Chapel Hill, which favors "Duracha"; the vinyl label itself reads "Duralcha." Worth a footnote. Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html
- **Exact release date.** No source gives a day or month, only the year 1974. Mastering was done at Nashville Record Productions (matrix NR5268). I could not locate a precise pressing date. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4199382-Duralcha-Jody-Is-Gone
- **Track length.** Discogs lists the B-side at 3:09. Some digital/streaming uploads may differ slightly; I used the Discogs figure from the original 45.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A name made of three cities."** Duralcha/Duracha is a contraction of Durham, Raleigh, and Chapel Hill, the North Carolina "Triangle." High-school kids from Hillside High named their band after their whole region. A clean hook about local pride pressed onto a one-and-done 45.
- **"The B-side that beat the A-side."** "Jody Is Gone" was the plug side and tells a story; the instrumental flip "Ghet-to Funk," with its crisp open drum break, is the side collectors chase and producers sample. A meditation on how the throwaway side becomes the prize.
- **"Crosstown funk rivals."** McCuller's Microtronics roster pitted Duralcha against the Black Experience Band as competing instrumental funk units in the same scene. Pairs naturally with the book's Communicators & Black Experience Band entry.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Focus on the opening seconds: that exposed, unaccompanied drum break is exactly what crate-diggers and hip-hop producers came for. Count how many bars the drums run naked before the band crashes in.
- This is a teenage band on a private label with one shot at vinyl. Listen for the rough edges, a horn that sits slightly off, a fill that runs hot, and decide whether the imperfection adds to or subtracts from the groove.
- The A-side "Jody Is Gone" has a story and a vocal; "Ghet-to Funk" throws all that out for pure rhythm. Play both back to back and decide which one you would have made the single.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Ghet-to Funk" is the instrumental B-side of Duralcha's only vinyl release; collectors prize it for its "crisp open drum break." Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html
- The B-side was written by Willis Norman; the A-side "Jody Is Gone" was written by Jimmy Liggins (the jump-blues bandleader's name appears as writer on the label). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4199382-Duralcha-Jody-Is-Gone
- It was sampled by Pusha T (feat. Rick Ross) on "Hold On" from the 2013 album "My Name Is My Name." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/229405/Pusha-T-Rick-Ross-Hold-On-Duralcha-Ghet-to-Funk/
- The track was rescued from obscurity onto the Numero Group compilation "Eccentric Soul: Omnibus," which is where most streaming listeners now find it. Source: https://open.spotify.com/track/3vUy84MjtIeUQAPLJkwSOk
- A bootleg/breaks 45 pairs "Ghet-To Funk" with Tom Jones' "Looking Out My Window," a sign of its DJ/breakbeat status. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/706937-Duralcha-Tom-Jones-Ghet-To-Funk-Looking-Out-My-Window

#### Album
- There was no album. "Jody Is Gone / Ghet-to Funk" (Microtronics M1001, 1974) was the band's only vinyl release. Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html
- An original copy is genuinely rare: Discogs shows one listing at roughly $1,320, with 765 collectors wanting it against 66 who own it. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4199382-Duralcha-Jody-Is-Gone
- The single was mastered at Nashville Record Productions (matrix NR5268-1/-2), published by Nawdarn, BMI. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4199382-Duralcha-Jody-Is-Gone

#### Artist
- The band's name is a contraction of Durham, Raleigh, and Chapel Hill; members were students at Hillside High School in Durham, NC. Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html
- The lineup ran ten deep: Michael Meeks (vocals), Terry Bullock and Drew Marsh (trumpets), Bobby Moody (sax), Stanley Saunders (trombone), Craig Beaumont (keys), Darrell Kelly (lead guitar), Jonathan Eubanks (bass), Alfred Burton (drums), and Tim Alston. Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html
- Label owner E. W. McCuller also produced the Communicators, the Black Experience Band, and Shadows of Soul, making Microtronics a small hub of Durham-area funk. Source: http://www.bullcitysoul.org/duracha.html

### Genre Suggestions

- **Deep Funk (current, recommended).** Textbook case: an overlooked 1974 private-press regional 45 that found its audience decades later through crate-diggers and sampling, now worth four figures. The book defines deep funk as "delayed recognition," which fits exactly. Source: per On Subgenres.md definition; https://www.discogs.com/release/4199382-Duralcha-Jody-Is-Gone
- **Classic Funk (secondary).** It sits squarely in the 1969-1977 classic window and is straight-ahead instrumental funk with no fusion. But it never entered the commercial pipeline (no chart, no distribution, one tiny pressing), which is the disqualifier the book draws against classic funk. Deep funk captures the commercial-fate reality better. Source: On Subgenres.md
- Not recommended: any fusion label (jazz-funk, funk-rock, latin). The track is unadorned horn-band funk built around a drum break.
