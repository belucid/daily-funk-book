### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The Encyclopedia of Soul, abbreviated."** Stuff was originally called The Encyclopedia of Soul — a fitting name for a unit of Cornell Dupree, Eric Gale, Richard Tee, Gordon Edwards, Chris Parker, and Steve Gadd. The shortening to "Stuff" is itself a thesis: the most recorded sidemen in New York deciding that their own band only deserves a four-letter shrug. "Foots" is the opening statement of a group that already knew it had nothing left to prove. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikell%27s
- **"A band of bands."** Every member of Stuff was simultaneously the rhythm section on someone else's hits — Aretha, Paul Simon, Van McCoy's "The Hustle," John Lennon. "Foots" is what happens when the session players write the chart for themselves. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band)
- **"Slow funk, professional grade."** Where classic funk in 1976 was tightening into disco or expanding into P-Funk theatre, "Foots" is a relaxed, almost conversational pocket — a duet between Eric Gale and Cornell Dupree on top of Gadd and Edwards. It makes a case for the value of restraint at funk's most maximalist moment. Source: https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/04/stuff-stuff-1976.html

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- After the opening vamp settles, listen for the two-guitar conversation between Cornell Dupree and Eric Gale. They never solo on top of each other. Can you tell which one is in your left ear and which is in your right, and which one tends to answer the other?
- Steve Gadd and Chris Parker both play drums on the studio "Foots" — that's two of the most in-demand drummers of the era on a single jazz-funk track. Listen for the second kit (hi-hat work, percussion fills). When can you hear two drummers, and when does it sound like one?
- "Foots" was the lead track Stuff chose to play on Saturday Night Live, October 2, 1976. Imagine you're in the SNL studio audience hearing this for the first time. What about the song is built for a TV audience that has never heard the band before?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- "Foots" opens side one of Stuff's self-titled debut and is credited collectively to all six members (Edwards, Tee, Dupree, Gale, Parker, Gadd) — Source: https://www.shazam.com/en-us/song/363675209/foots and https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/04/stuff-stuff-1976.html
- Stuff performed "Foots" on Saturday Night Live in Season 2, Episode 3 (October 2, 1976), the Eric Idle/Joe Cocker episode, just before or alongside the album's release — Source: https://www.onesnladay.com/2018/08/03/october-2-1976-eric-idle-joe-cocker-stuff-s2-e3/ and https://snl.fandom.com/wiki/October_2,_1976
- The track is built around a guitar duet between Cornell Dupree and Eric Gale, locked over the Gordon Edwards/Steve Gadd pocket — a deliberate showcase for two of the most recorded session guitarists of the era — Source: https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/04/stuff-stuff-1976.html
- Runs 3:58 on the LP; reissued as a 12" promo single backed with "(Do You) Want Some Of This" in 1976 — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1455207-Stuff-Foots-Do-You-Want-Some-Of-This

### Album

- Recorded at Long View Farm Studios in North Brookfield, Massachusetts (the same rural studio later used by the Rolling Stones to rehearse for their 1981 US tour) during 1975-1976 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(Stuff_album)
- Produced by Herb Lovelle and Tommy LiPuma; engineers included Al Schmitt and Gil Markle; mastered by George Marino — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(Stuff_album) and https://labibledelawestcoast.blogspot.com/2014/04/stuff-stuff-1976.html
- The album peaked at #163 on the US Billboard 200; commercial impact was modest in the US but the LP was eventually certified platinum in Japan — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/warner/warner/warner2900.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band)
- Catalog number BS 2968, Warner Bros. Records — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/warner/warner/warner2900.html

### Artist

- Stuff evolved out of a band called Encyclopedia of Soul; the name was shortened because it was too long for marquees — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikell%27s
- The band held a multi-night residency at Mikell's jazz club on the corner of 97th Street and Columbus Avenue in Manhattan, where they hosted jam sessions with Stevie Wonder, Joe Cocker, and other visiting stars — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikell%27s
- They were discovered as a working band by Woodstock organizer Michael Lang, who brought them to Warner Bros. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band)
- Stuff backed Joe Cocker on his Stingray world tour and recorded on session dates for Aretha Franklin, Paul Simon, Bill Withers, Brook Benton, Herbie Mann, John Lennon, and Van McCoy's "The Hustle" — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Gale
- More Stuff (1977) was Grammy-nominated — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band)

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (current value):** Best fit. Stuff is the canonical example of the genre's "by the players" definition in the project's own framework — six veteran jazz/R&B session musicians playing funk with extended jazz-style improvisation, longer-than-radio track lengths (here exactly under 4 minutes, but the band typically stretched live), and a rhythm section built to support soloists. Wikipedia tags the album as "jazz, funk, jazz fusion." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(Stuff_album)
- **Soul Jazz:** A defensible alternative, especially given Richard Tee's prominent organ work and the band's roots in late-1960s R&B session work as "Encyclopedia of Soul." But the project's own genre note positions soul jazz as the "earlier, smaller-ensemble side" and jazz-funk as the "bigger band" successor — and Stuff is the latter. Source: project's `Matter/On Genres.md`
- **Classic Funk:** Not recommended. While 1976 sits inside the project's 1969-1977 classic funk window, Stuff's primary identity is funk played by jazz musicians, not funk as the dominant commercial American pop form.

Recommendation: Keep `Jazz-Funk`.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - New York (set as current):** The right answer. Stuff was a New York City band end to end: formed in NYC, residency at Mikell's in Manhattan, recorded at Long View Farm but mastered at Sterling Sound (NYC), members all worked the NYC session circuit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikell%27s
- **US - Northeast:** A defensible broader region if the project ever wants to dilute the NY tag, given Long View Farm was in Massachusetts. But the band itself is unambiguously NYC. Not recommended.

Recommendation: `US - New York`.
