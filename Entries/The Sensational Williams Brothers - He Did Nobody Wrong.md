### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. One question surfaced during research and is resolved below:

- **Where does "He Did Nobody Wrong" actually appear?** The track is not on the group's better-known 1974 ABC/Songbird LP *What's Wrong With The People Today*, nor on either gospel-funk reissue comp (*Bless The People Everywhere*, *A Deeper Dig*), nor in the Discogs artist discography list, nor on any catalogued 7" single. It was finally located as **track B2 (side B, track 6) of the 1978/79 Savoy LP *I've Got A Home* (Savoy SL/MG 14493)**. Confirmed via the Discogs release tracklist, the Spotify album, and the iHeart album page. This was the single hardest fact to pin down and is the reason the YAML fields were initially empty.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Gospel that grooves five minutes past the sermon."** At 5:20, this is the longest-burning cut on the B-side — gospel built for the pocket, not the radio edit. A hook about a praise group stretching out like a funk band.
- **"The other Williams Brothers album track."** The book already has an entry for the title track *I've Got A Home* (dated 09-06). This theme frames *He Did Nobody Wrong* as the deeper, funkier B-side companion from the same 1978 Savoy session — two entries, one record, two different faces of the group.
- **"Quartet tradition meets the rhythm section."** Doug and Melvin Williams trade leads over Leonard's bass and Maurice Surrell's drums, with a real horn section (Darryl Dixon et al.). A theme about a family gospel quartet absorbing the secular funk vocabulary of 1978.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This runs 5:20, far longer than a gospel radio cut. Mark the moment the song stops "preaching" and just rides the groove — where does the band decide the pocket is the message?
- Listen for the horn section (Darryl Dixon and company) against the family vocals. Is this a gospel quartet with a funk band bolted on, or a funk band that happens to be singing scripture?
- Melvin Williams plays the lead guitar AND sings lead here. Try to hear where the same musician is steering both the vocal phrasing and the guitar line.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "He Did Nobody Wrong" is the longest track on side B at 5:20, an unusually extended runtime for a gospel cut of the era — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-Ive-Got-A-Home
- The track is essentially invisible to the rare-funk reissue economy: despite the *I've Got A Home* LP coming from the same gospel-funk world that fed the *Bless The People Everywhere* and *A Deeper Dig* comps, this specific song was never anthologized and barely registers in databases — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1328583-Various-Bless-The-People-Everywhere-Gospel-Funk-Of-Peacock-Songbird

#### Album

- *I've Got A Home* was produced by John Daniels with Fred Mendelsohn as executive producer — Mendelsohn was a long-time Savoy gospel/R&B figure, tying this family-quartet record to Savoy's broader gospel machine — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-Ive-Got-A-Home
- The album credits show a full secular-style rhythm-and-horn lineup: Steven Ford on keys, a five-piece horn section (Darryl Dixon, Dave Watson, Rick Freistat, Peter Baerga, Doug Rothauser), with the Williams family playing the rhythm section themselves — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-Ive-Got-A-Home
- Sources disagree on the year: Spotify and iHeart digital metadata list June 1978, while Discogs and the jazzdisco Savoy catalog index both date the vinyl to 1979 — Source: https://www.jazzdisco.org/savoy-records/catalog-14400-series/album-index/

#### Artist

- The group was founded in 1960 in Smithdale, Mississippi (and is associated with Jackson, MS) by Leon "Pop" Williams; they are credited as among the first to push contemporary/funk currents into the traditional gospel quartet form — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Williams_Brothers_(gospel_group)
- Over their career the group released roughly 42 albums and charted 23 on Billboard's Gospel Albums chart — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Williams_Brothers_(gospel_group)
- The book already contains a separate entry for the album's title track, "I've Got A Home" (dated 09-06), making this a rare case of two entries drawn from one LP — Source: local vault file `The Sensational Williams Brothers - I've Got a Home - 09-06.md`

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Gospel Funk**. This is well-supported and should stand.

- **Gospel Funk (KEEP):** The book defines this as "gospel music that absorbed the funk vocabulary of its era." That is exactly this record — a traditional gospel quartet (Doug/Melvin Williams leads) backed by a funk rhythm section and a five-piece horn line, on a 1978 Savoy gospel LP. Discogs tags the release Funk/Soul with styles Gospel, Rhythm & Blues, Funk — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3695236-The-Williams-Brothers-Ive-Got-A-Home
- **Classic Funk (weak alternative):** Only if the author wants to stress the secular-band textures; but the era (1978) is at the tail of the defined 1969–1977 classic window and the primary identity is gospel, so Gospel Funk is the better fit.
- **Deep Funk (weak alternative):** Defensible on the "delayed/limited recognition" criterion — the track is essentially uncatalogued and unanthologized — but it never had the crate-digger rediscovery arc that defines deep funk, so this is a stretch.

Recommendation: keep **Gospel Funk**.
