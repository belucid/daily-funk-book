### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items on this entry. Two questions I raised and chased myself:

**Who actually delivered Browne to GRP — George Benson or Earl Klugh?** Unresolved; sources conflict.
- Wax Poetics says **George Benson** championed him, arguing he'd get lost at Columbia among Miles Davis and Freddie Hubbard, and steered him to Grusin and Rosen's Arista subsidiary. Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/
- Browne's own official bio says he was signed to GRP "after introduction by **Earl Klugh** to Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen." Source: https://www.tombrowne.org/bio
- Wikipedia follows the Klugh version (this is also what the sibling *Funkin' for Jamaica* entry states). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- A fourth variant appears in writeups of Bernard Wright, describing Browne as "a trumpeter discovered by Blackman" — almost certainly a garbled reference and not corroborated anywhere else. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/bernard-wright-mn0000057427
- Approach taken: cross-checked Wax Poetics, tombrowne.org, Wikipedia, All About Jazz and WDET. My read for print: both can be true (Benson advocating, Klugh making the physical introduction), but if the author needs one name, **Earl Klugh** is the version Browne himself puts out.

**Has "Throw Down" been sampled?** No documented samples found.
- WhoSampled returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so I could not read its "Throw Down" page directly; searches surfaced only samples of *other* Browne tracks ("Funkin' for Jamaica," "Thighs High"). Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Tom-Browne/

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The Jamaica, Queens farm team."** This one 3:58 track puts Marcus Miller (20), Bernard Wright (15), Buddy Williams and Browne himself in a room together — a group of neighborhood kids from the same square mile of Queens who would go on to define 1980s session music. The debut record is the class photo. Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/
- **"The record before the hit."** Everyone knows *Love Approach* and "Funkin' for Jamaica." Almost nobody knows that a year earlier Browne opened his debut with a leaner, jazzier, hornier version of the same idea — and it went to #6 on the Jazz chart. A theme about what an artist sounds like the moment before the formula locks in. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar
- **"Funk's afterlife as radio-station furniture."** In 2013 "Throw Down" ended up on Worldwide FM in *Grand Theft Auto V*, curated by Gilles Peterson — meaning millions of people have heard this 1979 jazz-funk deep cut while stealing cars, without ever knowing its name. A theme about the strange modern channels through which old funk finds new ears. Source: https://www.gtabase.com/grand-theft-auto-v/radio-stations/worldwide-fm
- **"The bandleader who yields."** Browne's name is on the sleeve, but Michael Brecker's tenor takes the biggest solo real estate. A theme about jazz-funk leaders who hire people who can out-blow them.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Two different players are stacked on Oberheim synthesizers here — Dave Grusin and Rob Mounsey — while Bernard Wright, then fifteen years old, plays the electric piano underneath. Try to pull the keyboard layers apart. Which one is actually carrying the harmony, and which is just gloss?
- The leader is a trumpet player, but the longest, loudest solo belongs to Michael Brecker's tenor sax. Time both solos. Ask yourself whether Browne is being generous, outgunned, or simply smart about what the tune needs.
- Marcus Miller was twenty when he cut this, a year before the "Funkin' for Jamaica" line that made him famous. Follow only the bass for one pass. Can you already hear the player who would later turn the bass into a lead melodic voice?
- Buddy Williams's drum part is tight enough that it has been transcribed as a study piece for student drummers. Listen for what he *doesn't* play — count how few fills there are across four minutes.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Throw Down" is the opening track of Browne's debut album and one of only two originals he wrote for it (with "Promises For Spring"). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/300069-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- The session personnel on this single track is a jazz-funk all-star list: Tom Browne (trumpet), Marcus Miller (bass), Buddy Williams (drums), Ron Dean Miller (electric guitar), Bernard Wright (electric piano), Dave Grusin and Rob Mounsey (both on Oberheim synthesizer), and Michael Brecker (tenor sax). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1127805-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- Bernard Wright was **fifteen** when he played electric piano on this record; *Browne Sugar* is among his very first session credits. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Wright ; https://www.allmusic.com/artist/bernard-wright-mn0000057427
- In the UK the track was promoted harder than in the US: Arista issued it as the **A-side** of a 7" (ARIST 297, b/w "What's Going On") and as half of a 12" double-A-side (ARIST 12297). In the US it was relegated to the **B-side** of the "Brother, Brother" 7" (GS 2501). — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/4582011-Tom-Browne-Throw-Down ; https://www.discogs.com/release/1104394-Tom-Browne-Whats-Going-On-Throw-Down ; https://www.discogs.com/release/4430603-Tom-Browne-Brother-Brother
- The track was chosen by Gilles Peterson for **Worldwide FM in *Grand Theft Auto V*** (2013), the game's jazz/world station — arguably its largest audience ever. — Sources: https://www.gtabase.com/grand-theft-auto-v/radio-stations/worldwide-fm ; https://gillespeterson.net/projects/gtav
- It has had a second life as a teaching piece: Songsterr hosts a drum transcription and the Columbia College jazz fusion ensemble has recorded it. — Sources: https://www.songsterr.com/a/wsa/tom-browne-throw-down-drum-tab-s1160938 ; https://soundcloud.com/columbiacollegemusic/throw-down-tom-browne
- Shazam lists the track at 87 BPM in D minor. — Source: https://www.shazam.com/en-us/song/1290563058/throw-down

#### Album
- *Browne Sugar* (1979) was Browne's debut and carried catalog number **GRP 5003** — an extremely early number in the Arista/GRP series, released the year after Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen founded the label. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1127805-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRP_Records
- It charted three ways: **#6 Jazz Albums**, **#50 R&B/Hip-Hop Albums**, **#147 Billboard 200**. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar
- Recorded at **A&R Studios**, New York City; mastered by Ted Jensen at Sterling Sound; produced, arranged and conducted by Dave Grusin with Larry Rosen. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1127805-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- Grusin brought a full orchestral apparatus to a funk debut: a twenty-piece string section led by concertmaster David Nadien, plus a four-man reed/horn section (Eddie Daniels, Phil Bodner, Dave Tofani, Wally Kane). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1127805-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- Three of the eight tracks are covers, including Marvin Gaye's "What's Going On" and Mtume/Lucas's "The Closer I Get To You," with Patti Austin on vocals (appearing courtesy of CTI). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/300069-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- AllMusic rates the album four stars out of five. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar (citing https://www.allmusic.com/album/browne-sugar-mw0000187773)

#### Artist
- Tom Browne was born **October 30, 1954** in Queens, New York, and grew up in Jamaica, Queens alongside Marcus Miller, Bernard Wright, Omar Hakim, Lenny White and Lesette Wilson. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter) ; https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/
- Weldon Irvine — Nina Simone's former musical director — was the mentor figure of that Queens scene, organizing rehearsals and critiquing young players in his car at 3 a.m. — Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/
- Browne attended Manhattan's High School of Music & Art on scholarship, studying classical trumpet under Murray Karpilovsky, a principal trumpeter from Toscanini's NBC Symphony. — Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- Music was the side project: Browne told Wax Poetics he was "gearing towards an aviation career" and that "music was kinda something I did as my cool-out." He later left music for years to fly as a charter and airline captain, most recently on ATR72 and Fokker F-27 aircraft for a FedEx Feeder operator. — Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://www.allaboutjazz.com/musicians/tom-browne
- Before his own records, Browne broke in as a sideman with Sonny Fortune, Dr. Lonnie Smith, Weldon Irvine, Woody Shaw, Freddie Hubbard and the Fatback Band. — Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/
- As of 2023 Browne releases through his own imprint, Bee Horn Records. — Source: https://www.tombrowne.org/bio

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz Funk — recommended.** Fits the book's definition on all three axes. *By the players*: Browne is a classically trained trumpeter who came up as a sideman with Sonny Fortune, Woody Shaw and Freddie Hubbard, and the track is stacked with jazz professionals (Michael Brecker, Marcus Miller, Dave Grusin). *By the music*: instrumental, no vocal chorus, built to hand solo space to a horn player over a funk rhythm section. *By the era*: 1979, the tail of the decade the book assigns to jazz-funk. Discogs independently styles both the album and the 12" as **Jazz-Funk**, and Wikipedia's genre line for the album reads jazz-funk / funk / soul / jazz. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/300069-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar
- **Classic Funk — secondary case.** The record went into the full commercial pipeline: a major-label-distributed release with radio play and three separate Billboard chart placements. But 1979 sits just outside the book's ~1969-1977 classic window, and the track's primary identity is funk *fused into jazz* rather than funk plain. Weaker than Jazz Funk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar
- **Deep Funk — argued against, deliberately.** The Gilles Peterson / *GTA V* afterlife and the track's status as a crate-digger's cut make this tempting, but the book defines deep funk as *delayed recognition* for records overlooked on release. *Browne Sugar* was not overlooked: it hit #6 Jazz and #50 R&B in its own year. Recommend the author resist this one. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar

Recommendation: **Jazz Funk**. (Note: the sibling entry uses the value `Jazz-Funk` with a hyphen, while `Planning/Field Values.md` enumerates `Jazz Funk` without one — worth normalizing across entries.)

### Region Suggestions

- **US - New York — recommended, and written to the field.** Every vector points here. Browne was born in Queens and raised in Jamaica, Queens; the album was recorded at A&R Studios in Manhattan; the rhythm section (Marcus Miller, Bernard Wright, Buddy Williams) is a New York session crew, most of it from the same Queens neighborhood; and Arista/GRP was a New York operation. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browne_Sugar ; https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://www.discogs.com/release/1127805-Tom-Browne-Browne-Sugar
- **US - Northeast — not recommended.** Only worth considering if the author wants to avoid stacking too many entries on New York. It loses the whole point: the Jamaica, Queens specificity is the story of this record, and `US - New York` is an enumerated value, so there is no reason to blur it.
- **UK — not recommended, but noted.** "Throw Down" was pushed as an A-side single in Britain while the US buried it on a B-side, and its later rare-groove/Gilles Peterson standing is a British phenomenon. That is a *reception* story, not an origin story, and the book's region field is meant to represent origins. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/4582011-Tom-Browne-Throw-Down ; https://gillespeterson.net/projects/gtav
