### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The doctor will see you now.** Frame the entry around Dr. Funkenstein as a character: Clinton's self-coronation as a "cool ghoul with the bump transplant," an intergalactic pharmacist whose prescription is the Funk. The track is his theme music, a villain-entrance strut for a benevolent cosmic mad scientist.
- **Clone wars.** The album's conceit is that Starchild (from the previous year's Mothership Connection) is secretly working for Dr. Funkenstein, cloning funk carriers to seed Earth. The entry can use the track as a doorway into Clinton's unbroken mythology, where each album is a chapter in an unfolding sci-fi scripture.
- **The Brown horns go P-Funk.** Fred Wesley's horn charts, with Maceo Parker on sax, make this feel like James Brown's horn section stepping onto a UFO. A good angle for showing how the JB's migrated to Clinton's camp and reshaped 70s funk.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Listen for Clinton's first entrance as Dr. Funkenstein, when he drops the "Kiss me on my ego" line over the horn stabs. Is he preaching, hustling, or both? The swagger is the point.
- Track Bootsy Collins's bass underneath the horn fanfare. It's doing two jobs at once: locking the groove and editorializing on top of it. Where does the bass stop supporting the vocal and start talking back?
- This is a theme song for a character. If you didn't know the lyrics, what would you guess Dr. Funkenstein looks like, and what does he do? Listen once, then check your answer against the album cover.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Dr. Funkenstein" runs 5:46 on the album and was the second single from The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein, peaking at #46 on the Billboard Hot Soul Singles chart in early 1977. A modest chart showing for a track that would later become one of P-Funk's most quoted. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Funkenstein
- Written by George Clinton, Bootsy Collins, and Bernie Worrell; produced by Clinton. The single's B-side was "Children of Production" (Casablanca NB 875). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Funkenstein
- Heavily sampled: Del the Funky Homosapien ("Soopa Feen"), Ice Cube ("Dr. Frankenstein"), De La Soul feat. Snoop Dogg ("Pain"), Too Short ("It's Your Life"), Jaz feat. Jay-Z ("It's That Simple"), and Parliament's own "The Landing (Of the Holy Mothership)" all pull from it. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Parliament/Dr.-Funkenstein/
- The character reappears well beyond funk: the Twelfth Doctor uses "Dr. Funkenstein" as an alias in the 2015 Doctor Who episode "The Zygon Invasion." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Funkenstein
- Red Hot Chili Peppers recorded a live cover that appeared on the 1998 compilation Under the Covers: Essential Red Hot Chili Peppers. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Funkenstein

#### Album

- The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein was Parliament's fifth studio album and their second gold record, reaching #3 on the Billboard R&B Albums chart and #20 on the pop chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- Recorded at United Sound Systems in Detroit and Hollywood Sound in Hollywood, CA, produced by George Clinton. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- The album deepens the mythology introduced on Mothership Connection (1975): Starchild is revealed to be a secret agent for Dr. Funkenstein, whose mission is to seed Earth with clones carrying the Funk. The opening "Prelude" narrates the scheme in scripture-like prose. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/parliament-the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein/
- The lineup is one of P-Funk's most celebrated: Bootsy Collins (bass), Bernie Worrell (keyboards), Fred Wesley (trombone, horn arrangements), Maceo Parker (sax), plus guest appearances including the Brecker Brothers. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- Rolling Stone awarded it 5 stars; AllMusic gave 4.5 stars; Robert Christgau gave it a B+ in his October 25, 1976 Consumer Guide column. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- Outkast sampled "Children of Production" on "Southernplayalisticadillacmuzik," one of many hip-hop lineages traceable to this record. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/parliament-the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein/

#### Artist

- George Edward Clinton was born July 22, 1941 in Kannapolis, North Carolina. He began as leader of a 1950s Plainfield, NJ doo-wop group called The Parliaments before recasting the unit as the Parliament/Funkadelic twin-band collective. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(funk_musician)
- Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, with 16 members honored together. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(funk_musician)
- The Mothership stage prop, piloted by Dr. Funkenstein in concert, became one of the most iconic set pieces in rock history. A 1990s rebuild of the original now lives in the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-Funk_Mothership

### Genre Suggestions

- **Psychedelic Funk (current, strongly supported).** The definition in "On Genres & Regions" explicitly names George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic universe as the center of this genre, and "Dr. Funkenstein" is an archetypal entry: surreal mythology, alter-ego storytelling, hallucinatory worldbuilding. Keep this.
- **Classic Funk (secondary possibility).** 1976 is squarely in the 1968–1977 classic-funk window, the track was released on a major label (Casablanca), charted on Billboard, and its primary musical identity is funk. A case could be made for classic funk, but Psychedelic Funk is the more specific and distinctive label.
- **No change recommended.** Stick with Psychedelic Funk.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Michigan (current).** Defensible: recorded at United Sound Systems in Detroit, and the Parliament/Funkadelic operation was centered in Detroit during its Westbound/Casablanca peak. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- **US - Northeast.** The Parliaments formed in Plainfield, New Jersey in the 1950s, and P-Funk's origin story has strong Plainfield roots. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(funk_musician)
- **Recommendation:** keep **US - Michigan**. The track itself was recorded there, the mid-70s P-Funk enterprise was Detroit-based, and the horn section (Wesley/Parker) connects it to Detroit sessions rather than NJ origins. No change.
