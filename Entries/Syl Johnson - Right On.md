### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were in the entry. Questions that came up and could not be settled:

- **Track length, 7:10 or 7:14?** The original Twinight LP lists 7:10, per Discogs' transcription of LPS 1002 (https://www.discogs.com/release/2091517-Syl-Johnson-Is-It-Because-Im-Black). The audio on Apple Music, Deezer and the Discogs-linked YouTube upload all runs about 7:14. The field uses the audio length, 7m 14s. Change it to `7m 10s` if the book should follow the printed label.
- **Album year, 1969 or 1970?** Discogs, Wikipedia and Numero's essay say 1970, and Numero gives "April 1970" (https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology). Apple Music's metadata shows 1969, which is probably a copy of the title single's date (Sept 1969). Numero's "50th anniversary" reissue (2019) also points back to 1969. The field uses 1970.
- **Was "Right On" a single at the time?** No contemporary Twinight 45 was found. Searching Discogs for Twinight singles (cat. 105–155, 1967–1971) turns up no "Right On". The earliest 7" is "Right On (Pt. 1)/(Pt. 2)" in Numero's 2011 *Syl Johnson Single Box* (ESBOX-003, https://www.discogs.com/release/2912002-Syl-Johnson-Syl-Johnson-Single-Box), also listed as Eccentric ES-O24 (https://www.discogs.com/release/17912503-Syl-Johnson-Right-On-). The two-part edit may have been prepared in 1970 and never issued, but no source confirms that.
- **Who plays on it?** The LP credits arrangers Byron Bowie, Jerry Wilson and Larry Nestor, and producers James L. Jones and Pieces of Peace. It gives no per-track players. Numero names the Pieces of Peace line-up as Bernard Reed (bass), John Bishop (guitar), Hal "Heavy" Nesbitt (drums), Michael Davis (trumpet) and Jerry Wilson (alto sax), but credits them only on the title single. It is likely, but unconfirmed, that they play on "Right On".
- **Album title punctuation.** Discogs and the original label show no question mark. Wikipedia's discography writes *Is It Because I'm Black?*. The field follows the original label.
- **Sampling history of "Right On".** Searches turned up no samples (they all lead to "Different Strokes" and "Is It Because I'm Black"). WhoSampled was not checked directly.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The party after the protest"**: The first Black concept album ends not with a lament but with a seven-minute party. Numero says the closer "devolves into a full-on party track," with Syl riffing "as if to answer his critics." That opens a question about joy as a reply to oppression.
- **"Thirteen months before *What's Going On*"**: Numero says the album came out in April 1970, a full 13 months before Marvin Gaye's record, and "can rightly be called the first black concept album." This is the story of a pioneer outsold and forgotten, with "Right On" as its last word.
- **"The single nobody pressed"**: A seven-minute album closer that waited 41 years to be cut into Parts 1 and 2 on a 45 (Numero, 2011). The theme is how reissue labels rewrite what a "single" was.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The album opens with a slow, aching question ("Is It Because I'm Black") and ends with this. Listen to how the mood turns in the first minute of "Right On". Is the band answering the title track?
- Over seven minutes the groove barely changes. Track what Syl's voice does to keep it moving, and catch the moment it stops being a song and becomes a party.
- Numero later split this track into Part 1 and Part 2 for a 45. Where would you make the cut, and what would a radio listener in 1970 have missed?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Right On" closes the album (B5) at a printed 7:10. Only the title track (7:35) is longer. It is credited to James L. Jones and Syl Johnson — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/221532-Syl-Johnson-Is-It-Because-Im-Black
- Numero: "The album's closer burns the brightest… 'Right On' devolves into a full-on party track," ending with Syl riffing on a lyric line "as if to answer his critics." — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology
- It was first released as a 7" in 2011, split into Parts 1 and 2, in Numero's limited *Syl Johnson Single Box* (1,500 copies; the first 300 came with a Syl Johnson coin) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2912002-Syl-Johnson-Syl-Johnson-Single-Box
- WEFUNK Radio played it on Show 239 — Source: https://www.wefunkradio.com/song/239_bf/syl+johnson/right+on

#### Album

- Twinight LPS 1002, US, 1970. Producers are James L. Jones and Pieces of Peace. Jerry Griffith did the cover design and photography. Mastering was at RCA's Mid-America Recording Center, Chicago — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2091517-Syl-Johnson-Is-It-Because-Im-Black
- It came out in April 1970, "a full 13 months before Marvin Gaye's *What's Going On*." Numero calls it "the first black concept album, a distinction few give it credit for." — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology
- The album sold poorly. Johnson blamed its weak appeal to white audiences, calling it "a college record." — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_Because_I%27m_Black
- The back cover was shot at a burned-out building on 43rd Street, and the title was finger-painted over a stock photo of a brick wall — Source: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology
- The album includes covers of "Come Together" (Lennon–McCartney), "Black Balloons" (Oscar Brown Jr.) and "Walk a Mile in My Shoes" (Joe South) — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/221532-Syl-Johnson-Is-It-Because-Im-Black
- The title single (Twinight 125, Sept 1969) reached No. 11 R&B and No. 68 Hot 100. Johnson wrote it after Martin Luther King's assassination as a "sympathy song" — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_It_Because_I%27m_Black

#### Artist

- Born Sylvester Thompson, July 1, 1936, near Holly Springs, Mississippi. Died February 6, 2022, in Mableton, Georgia, aged 85 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syl_Johnson ; https://stereogum.com/2175154/syl-johnson-dead-at-85/news
- His brother, the Chicago blues guitarist Jimmy Johnson, died January 31, 2022, six days earlier — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Johnson_(blues_guitarist) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syl_Johnson
- Johnson was Twinight's star. When Willie Mitchell lured him to Hi Records in 1971, the label folded within months (early 1972) — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinight_Records
- He says he started the label (as "Twilight") to put out "Come On Sock It to Me", and that Pieces of Peace were a band only because "I had the gigs!" — Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/syl-johnson-father-of-the-style
- He is among the most-sampled artists in hip-hop, mainly through "Different Strokes" (Wu-Tang, Public Enemy, Jay-Z, Tupac) — Source: https://nbcdfw.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/syl-johnson-chicago-soul-and-blues-artist-dies-at-85/2881461
- He threatened to sue Numero Group in their first conversation. Numero then made *Complete Mythology* (2010), which earned two 2012 Grammy nominations (Best Historical Album, Best Album Notes) — Source: https://stereogum.com/2175154/syl-johnson-dead-at-85/news ; https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2012/02/12/146751073/one-grammy-award-you-wont-see-on-tv
- His daughter is the Grammy-nominated singer Syleena Johnson — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syl_Johnson

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is empty. Definitions are from `Matter/On Subgenres.md`. Note that `Matter/On Genres & Regions.md` does not exist; that file appears to be the current one.

- **Deep Funk (primary)**: By the book's definition, deep funk is a "commercial fate": overlooked on release and found later. If it was on a known label, it came from a poorly selling, thinly distributed record. That fits closely. It is a non-single closer on a small Chicago label's LP that "did not sell nearly as well" (Numero), and the label folded in 1972. It surfaced through Numero's 2010–2011 reissues. Sources: https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinight_Records
- **Classic Funk (secondary)**: 1970 falls inside the book's 1969–1977 classic era, and the track's main identity is a funk party groove. But Twinight was neither a major nor well distributed, and the track was never on radio as a single. That weakens the case.
- Not recommended: Gospel Funk. Despite the call-and-response feel, there is no religious framing.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Illinois (written to field)**: Twinight was founded in Chicago in 1967, and the LP was mastered at RCA's Mid-America Recording Center, Chicago. Numero says the album was rehearsed at Twinight's storefront studio at 2131 S. Michigan, Chicago's Record Row. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twinight_Records ; https://www.discogs.com/release/2091517-Syl-Johnson-Is-It-Because-Im-Black ; https://numerogroup.com/blogs/stories/syl-johnson-complete-mythology
- **US - Midwest (alternative)**: Use this only if the author wants to group Chicago with a wider Midwest story. Illinois is more specific and is in the enumeration.
- Johnson's Mississippi birth points to US - Southeast, but the recording is entirely a Chicago product.
