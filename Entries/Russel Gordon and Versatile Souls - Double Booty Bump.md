### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A record with no biography."** The defining fact about this track is the absence of facts: as the funk-o-logy thread puts it, there is "no findable information" about Mr. Gordon or his "versatile souls." The entry could be framed around how a record can become collectible precisely because almost nothing is known about who made it.
- **"Funk out of Cajun country."** This is heavy ghetto funk pressed in Lafayette, Louisiana — Acadiana, a region far better known for Cajun and zydeco than for breakbeat funk. The producer, J.J. Caillier, later became a zydeco-revival figure. A theme about Black funk emerging from the same soil as accordion music.
- **"Dug up by a DJ, not a label."** The track owes its second life to UK Deep Funk DJ Ian Wright spinning it at Madame JoJo's in the mid-1990s and its inclusion on a French bootleg-ish compilation — a story about how 1990s rare-groove crate culture, not the original industry, canonized this record.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The B-side, "Don't Cry Baby," is a "blues cajun" piece with patois dialogue and a baby crying throughout. Listen to the A-side first, then the B-side back to back: what does the same band's range tell you about where in the country this was made?
- This is prized as a heavy "booty" funk drum record. Cue the break and ask: is the appeal the drummer's pocket, or the rawness of the pressing itself? Name the exact bar where the groove locks in.
- Knowing nothing was professionally promoted about this single, listen for what sounds homemade versus what sounds studio-polished. Where do you hear the budget?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The original 7" is genuinely rare and expensive: a near-mint copy sold for $393 on eBay in July 2014, and Discogs has shown a single copy listed at ~$338. — Source: https://www.popsike.com/RARE-HEAVY-FUNK-45-RUSSELL-GORDON-DOUBLE-BOOTY-BUMP/111403931952.html
- The track was revived for the deep-funk scene by English DJ Ian Wright (co-founder of the Deep Funk nights at London's Madame JoJo's) in the mid-1990s. — Source: http://funk-o-logy.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1999
- It was reissued/compiled on the French "The Funky Soul Survivors" compilation (Soul Patrol Records), one of a long line of rare-funk comps from that label. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/589775-Various-The-Funky-Soul-Survivors
- The B-side "Don't Cry Baby" is a Cajun-blues number sung partly in patois with a baby crying through the record — a striking tonal opposite to the funk A-side. — Source: http://funk-o-logy.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1999

#### Album

- There is no studio album. The song was issued only as a 7" single (Jay-Cee JC-1002); all album-format appearances are later various-artists compilations. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3563570-Russell-Gordon-And-The-Versatile-Souls-Double-Booty-Bump

#### Artist

- Virtually no biographical information exists on Russell Gordon or the Versatile Souls; the funk-o-logy researcher states outright that nothing about them is findable beyond what the label copy says. — Source: http://funk-o-logy.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1999
- The record was produced by J.J. (Jay Jay) Caillier under "Acadiana Music" (ASCAP), on his own Jay-Cee label out of Lafayette, Louisiana. Caillier was an R&B singer turned producer/DJ who later became a force in the 1980s zydeco revival, DJing at Lafayette's KJCB and promoting his son's band the Zydeco Knockouts. — Source: https://www.sirshambling.com/artists_2012/C/jay_jay_caillier/index.php
- The label's flagship record was JC-1001, J.J. Caillier's own anti-drug funk track "Pusherman" — placing "Double Booty Bump" as JC-1002, the label's second release. — Source: https://www.popsike.com/SOUL-FUNK-RAP-45-JJ-CAILLIER-PUSHERMAN-LOUISIANA-RAPPER-JAYCEE-1001-HEAR/181775729976.html
