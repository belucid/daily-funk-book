Here’s a hot take: the slower the funk, the funkier it gets.

Today’s song is Billy Swan’s four-minute country-funk crawl through Elvis Presley’s 85 bpm “Don’t Be Cruel.” Any bar band can cover a tune, but it took real imagination to rework Presley’s pop shuffle at half the tempo and ten times the funk.

Born on May 12, 1942, Swan was seven years younger than Elvis. He went to Memphis in 1963 to pursue songwriting and made a pilgrimage to Graceland. There, he struck up a conversation with the gate guard, Elvis's uncle, who offered him a room at his house. Swan lived with the family for a while, running around Memphis with Elvis's younger cousins.

In 1974, Swan included "Don't Be Cruel" on his debut album, *I Can Help*. It's easy to hear the track as a love letter to Elvis and his music. Elvis repaid the compliment by cutting Swan’s “I Can Help” for his *Today* album in 1975.

Let today’s listen slow your heart rate while it puts a smile on your face.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Slowing the King down."** The hook is what Billy Swan does to Elvis's "Don't Be Cruel": he halves the tempo, doubles the length, and turns a finger-snapping pop shuffle into a four-minute country-funk crawl. This frames the entry around the act of re-interpretation, not just the cover. It also lets you talk about Otis Blackwell's song surviving every decade by sounding completely different in each one.
- **"The Graceland kid who wasn't."** Swan's whole career sits in Elvis's orbit (he hung around Travis Smith and the Presley cousins as a teenager, Elvis later cut Swan's "I Can Help" for the 1975 *Today* LP), but Wikipedia is careful to debunk the "Swan worked as a guard at Graceland" rumor. The entry could use the slow "Don't Be Cruel" as a fan's love letter back to Elvis — the closest Swan ever got to handing him a song in return.
- **"How country funk got named."** "Don't Be Cruel (Slow Version)" is the *opening track* of Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume II 1967–1974* (2014). That comp, along with Vols. I and III, is the reason this sub-genre has a name at all. The entry could be an origin-story for the label "country funk" itself, with Swan as exhibit A.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is Elvis's "Don't Be Cruel" cut roughly in half tempo. After you've listened once, go back to Presley's 1956 original and time how long it takes him to get through one verse versus Swan. Where does Swan put the *space* that Elvis filled with snap?
- Listen to the organ — it's Bobby Emmons's electric piano/organ work plus Swan's own keys, the same instrumental palette that drove the "I Can Help" hit. At what moments does the organ stop accompanying the vocal and start answering it?
- This track opens Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume II*. Try to hear why the compilers chose it as the *first* thing you hear on a record arguing that country funk is a real genre. What in the first 30 seconds tells you "country" and what tells you "funk"?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- "Don't Be Cruel" was written by Otis Blackwell in 1956 with Elvis Presley credited as co-writer; Swan's cover is therefore a country-funk reinterpretation of a Brill Building / R&B copyright that already had a strange dual-author story — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Be_Cruel
- Swan roughly doubles the length of Presley's original, stretching it past four minutes and "letting the groove guide the way" — described by Aquarium Drunkard as taking "the fuzzed-out swing of Elvis' original" through "a lounged and syrupy lens" — Source: https://aquariumdrunkard.com/2019/01/22/billy-swan-%E2%80%8E-dont-be-cruel-slow-version/
- The Slow Version is the opening track of Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume II 1967–1974* (released July 15, 2014), which is one of the three compilations that effectively named the genre — Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-ii-1967-1974
- The track was released as a single in the UK on Monument (MNT 3244) with B-side "P.M.S. (Post Mortem Sickness)", entered the Official UK Singles Chart on May 24, 1975, peaked at No. 42, and ran 4 weeks — Source: https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/billy-swan-dont-be-cruel/
- The single also charted internationally, reaching No. 4 on the Swiss Music Charts in 1975 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Be_Cruel
- According to Swan's own interview, the *I Can Help* sessions producing the slow "Don't Be Cruel" went so well that he later cut a full Elvis tribute album, *Like Elvis Used to Do*, at Sun Studio in Memphis — Source: https://www.elvis.com.au/presley/interview-billy-swan.shtml

### Album

- *I Can Help* was Swan's debut LP for Monument Records, US catalog KZ 33279, recorded at Young'un Sound in Murfreesboro, Tennessee, co-produced by Chip Young and Billy Swan, on a budget of roughly $19,000 — Source: https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-billy-swan-can-help and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Can_Help
- Personnel on the album included a deep bench of Memphis/Nashville studio players: Reggie Young (electric guitar), Bobby Emmons (organ/electric piano), Bobby Wood (piano), Mike Leech (bass), Hayward Bishop (drums), Charlie McCoy (harmonica), Lloyd Green (steel guitar), Dennis Linde (acoustic guitar/backing vox), with arrangements by Bill Justis — Source: https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-billy-swan-can-help
- A Monument exec hired to evaluate the record before release declared there were "no hits" on it; the title cut then went to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in November 1974 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Can_Help
- Tracklist (per Wikipedia / Discogs aggregations): "Lover Please", "I Can Help", "I'm Her Fool", "I'd Like To Work For You", "Shake, Rattle And Roll", "Queen Of My Heart", "Don't Be Cruel (Slow Version)", "Wedding Bells", "Ways Of A Woman In Love", "P.M.S. (Post Mortem Sickness)" — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Can_Help

### Artist

- Billy Swan (William Lance Swan) was born May 12, 1942 in Cape Girardeau, Missouri — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Swan
- Swan wrote "Lover Please" as a teenager; Clyde McPhatter's 1962 cover became a No. 7 Billboard pop hit, giving Swan his first songwriting income — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Swan
- Swan swept floors at Columbia Recording Studios in Nashville and then *handed that exact job off to his friend Kris Kristofferson* when he left; he later toured as Kristofferson's bass player starting in 1971 — Source: https://www.elvis.com.au/presley/interview-billy-swan.shtml
- The RMI organ Swan used to write "I Can Help" was a wedding gift from Kris Kristofferson and Rita Coolidge — Source: https://www.elvis.com.au/presley/interview-billy-swan.shtml
- Elvis Presley recorded Swan's "I Can Help" on March 10, 1975 for the *Today* album; Felton Jarvis later gave Swan the pair of black socks Elvis had worn at the session — Source: https://www.elvis.com.au/presley/interview-billy-swan.shtml
- A persistent rumor that Swan worked as a security guard at Graceland is explicitly denied on Wikipedia: he was friends with one of the guards but never worked there — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Swan
- Swan's daughters Planet Swan and Sierra Swan are also recording artists — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Swan

### Genre Suggestions

- **Country Funk (current value)** — Strongest fit. This is the genre's defining compilation's opening track, and the slow-tempo organ-and-shuffle treatment of an Elvis tune is squarely in the "country plus funk vocabulary" pocket described in `On Genres.md`. Keep as-is.
- **Deep Funk (alternate)** — Defensible on grounds of *commercial fate*: the slow "Don't Be Cruel" was an album cut and a B-list European single in 1975 that only found its true audience through Light in the Attic's 2014 reissue. That arc (overlooked on release, rediscovered by crate-diggers/compilers decades later) is exactly the "deep funk" definition in `On Genres.md`. Worth considering if you want to highlight the rediscovery story over the country side.
- **Country Funk + nothing else** — Recommended. The Country Funk label is more distinctive than adding Funk Rock or Soul to it; the track really is the cleanest country funk specimen on the list.
