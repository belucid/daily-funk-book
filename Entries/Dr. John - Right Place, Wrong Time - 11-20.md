Born Malcolm John Rebennack on November 20, 1941, in the Third Ward of New Orleans, Dr. John (not a real doctor) became one of the musicians most synonymous with the city’s rich musical culture. Through the 1960s, Dr. John was a first-call session player for anyone recording there, and a cult figure onstage as a psychedelic voodoo shaman.

Today’s song, “Right Place, Wrong Time,” gave him his only Top 10 pop single on the *Billboard* Hot 100. The rhythm section is The Meters, so the track has that greasy groove you can only get from New Orleans.

Dr. John wrote most of the twisted lyrics, but he also collected lines from friends. Bob Dylan contributed "I been in the right trip, but I must have used the wrong car," Bette Midler gave him "My head’s in a bad place, but I don’t know what it’s there for," and Doug Sahm pitched in “I been in the right set, but it must have been the wrong sign.” But the strangest line is pure Dr. John: “just need a little brain salad surgery.” It was Ninth Ward slang for oral sex, obscure enough to slide past radio censors.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The accidental all-star session."** The lyrics were partly crowd-sourced from Bob Dylan, Bette Midler, and Doug Sahm; the guitar solo was a one-take hallway grab from David Spinozza after Leo Nocentelli's original part went missing; the whole thing was held together by Allen Toussaint with the Meters laying down the groove. The track is a masterpiece of serendipity. Sources: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/dr-john-in-the-right-place/ ; https://americansongwriter.com/how-a-1973-one-hit-wonder-found-success-that-he-could-be-proud-of-with-the-help-of-bob-dylan-bette-midler-and-others/

- **"The one-hit wonder who never needed a hit."** Dr. John was a session musician and cult figure for 15 years before this track gave him his only Top 10 pop single. The entry can explore what it means when a career musician gets one mainstream moment, and whether it changed anything about who he was. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/dr-john/right-place-wrong-time

- **"Brain salad surgery and Ninth Ward slang."** The lyric "just need a little brain salad surgery" is old New Orleans street slang for oral sex. Emerson, Lake & Palmer, sharing a record promoter with Dr. John, heard the phrase and used it as the title for their 1973 prog-rock album. An entry angle about how New Orleans vernacular slipped into the cultural water supply. Sources: https://americansongwriter.com/how-a-1973-one-hit-wonder-found-success-that-he-could-be-proud-of-with-the-help-of-bob-dylan-bette-midler-and-others/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Place,_Wrong_Time_(song)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- "The Meters are the engine room here, but the guitar solo isn't Leo Nocentelli. It's session player David Spinozza, called in from the hallway for one take. Can you hear the seam where one New York guitarist replaces a New Orleans one?"

- "Dr. John growls the whole vocal, but listen to the bridge where he slips into stride piano. That's the oldest New Orleans piano tradition showing up inside a funk groove. Does it feel like a detour, or does it anchor the whole track?"

- "Allen Toussaint produced this, and the Meters are locked in tight underneath. Compare the groove to the Meters' own 'Cissy Strut.' How much of what you're hearing is Dr. John, and how much is the Meters?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Written by Mac Rebennack (Dr. John), but lyrics were partly contributed by Bob Dylan ("I been in the right trip, but I must have used the wrong car"), Bette Midler ("My head's in a bad place, but I don't know what it's there for"), and Doug Sahm ("I been in the right set, but it must have been the wrong sign"). — Source: https://americansongwriter.com/how-a-1973-one-hit-wonder-found-success-that-he-could-be-proud-of-with-the-help-of-bob-dylan-bette-midler-and-others/
- The guitar solo was supposed to be played by Leo Nocentelli of the Meters, but the original tape of his part went missing. David Spinozza happened to be walking past Atlantic's New York studios and was grabbed for a replacement. He nailed it in one take. — Source: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/dr-john-in-the-right-place/
- Dr. John had written the song roughly 10-15 years before recording it and likely wouldn't have recorded it without Allen Toussaint's encouragement. — Source: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/dr-john-in-the-right-place/
- The lyric "just need a little brain salad surgery" is old Ninth Ward New Orleans slang for oral sex. Emerson, Lake & Palmer heard the phrase (through a shared record promoter) and used it as their 1973 album title. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Place,_Wrong_Time_(song) ; https://americansongwriter.com/how-a-1973-one-hit-wonder-found-success-that-he-could-be-proud-of-with-the-help-of-bob-dylan-bette-midler-and-others/
- The intro was sampled by Dr. Dre for the 1992 film Deep Cover. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Place,_Wrong_Time_(song)
- Covered by Maceo Parker, Ray J., and The Jon Spencer Blues Explosion, among others. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Dr.-John/Right-Place,-Wrong-Time/
- Featured on the soundtrack to Richard Linklater's 1993 film Dazed and Confused. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/dr-john/right-place-wrong-time
- Peaked at #9 on the Billboard Hot 100 (summer 1973), #6 in Canada, #11 on the Cash Box Top 100. Ranked as the 24th biggest hit of 1973. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Place,_Wrong_Time_(song)

#### Album

- In the Right Place was recorded at Criteria Studios in Miami, Florida in 1972. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place
- Produced by Allen Toussaint, who also played keyboards, acoustic guitars, percussion, background vocals, and arrangements. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place
- The album reached #24 on the Billboard 200, spent 33 weeks on the chart, and became the biggest-selling album of Dr. John's career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place
- Released February 25, 1973 on Atco Records. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place
- "Such a Night" from the album was performed at The Band's Last Waltz concert (1976), filmed by Martin Scorsese. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place
- Additional musicians: Gary Brown (saxophone), Ralph MacDonald (percussion), David Spinozza (guitar solo). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Right_Place

#### Artist

- Born Malcolm John Rebennack on November 20, 1941, in the Third Ward of New Orleans, Louisiana. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._John
- Died June 6, 2019, in New Orleans, at age 77. — Source: https://www.npr.org/2019/06/06/730481477/dr-john-legend-of-new-orleans-dead-at-77
- Active as a New Orleans session musician from the late 1950s; created the Dr. John persona (a voodoo-inspired theatrical character) for his 1968 debut album Gris-Gris. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._John
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2011. Won six Grammy Awards from 15 nominations over his career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._John
- His father ran an appliance shop in New Orleans that also sold records, an early musical influence. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._John
- Dr. John described the Meters as "the fonkiest rhythm section anywhere." — Source: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/dr-john-in-the-right-place/

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (recommended).** "Right Place Wrong Time" is a 1973 Top 10 hit built on a tight funk groove played by the Meters, produced by Allen Toussaint, and released on a major label (Atco/Atlantic). It fits the book's definition of classic funk perfectly: core vocabulary, commercial pipeline, chart presence, primary identity as funk rather than a fusion. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Place,_Wrong_Time_(song) ; https://www.songfacts.com/facts/dr-john/right-place-wrong-time ; Rate Your Music tags the single as "Funk": https://rateyourmusic.com/release/single/dr-john/right-place-wrong-time-i-been-hoodood/

- **Jazz-Funk (defensible alternative).** Dr. John's piano playing draws on jazz traditions (stride piano in the bridge), the album as a whole straddles jazz and funk, and AllMusic tags the song under both Funk and Album Rock styles. However, "Right Place Wrong Time" is more a funk single than a jazz-funk exploration: it's short (2:50), vocal-driven, and built for radio. Classic Funk is the stronger fit. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/song/right-place-wrong-time-mt0000839057
