### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The first Japanese artist on Blue Note."** Kuroda was the first Japanese musician signed to the legendary jazz label. "Piri Piri" — an instrumental he wrote — sits at the center of that milestone debut, a good hook for a story about a Kobe/Ashiya-raised trumpeter reaching the American jazz mecca. (Sources below.)
- **"A name from another continent."** The title is a Swahili/African term ("pepper pepper"), the peri-peri chili sauce common across Africa — a Japanese trumpeter in Brooklyn naming his funkiest cut after an African spice. It opens an angle on jazz-funk's borderless, cross-cultural DNA. Source: https://www.bluenote.com/spotlight/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-ascends-with-rising-s/
- **"The José James pipeline."** The track (and album) is a product of Kuroda's New School friendship with vocalist/producer José James, who produced Rising Son. A theme about how modern jazz careers are built on peer networks, not just A&R. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Kuroda called this one of his funkiest solos on the record. Track the moment the horns drop out and the rhythm section (Nate Smith's drums, Solomon Dorsey's bass) takes the pocket — how long do they hold the groove before the trumpet returns?
- This is jazz musicians playing funk. Listen for what stays "jazz" — the extended 7-minute runtime, the room left for improvisation — versus what makes it funk. Where's the line?
- Two horns (Kuroda's trumpet, Corey King's trombone) trade over the same groove. Notice how the arrangement stacks them: are they harmonizing the head, or answering each other?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track

- "Piri Piri" takes its name from a Swahili phrase meaning "pepper pepper," referring to the peri-peri chili sauce common in African cooking — an African culinary reference on an album by a Japanese trumpeter. Source: https://www.bluenote.com/spotlight/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-ascends-with-rising-s/
- Kuroda wrote "Piri Piri" himself (credited Written-By); Blue Note's review singles it out as home to "one of his funkiest solos on the disc," driven by a "gripping groove" from the rhythm section. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/714619-Takuya-Kuroda-Rising-Son , https://www.bluenote.com/spotlight/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-ascends-with-rising-s/
- The track runs about 7:04 — a length that signals jazz-fusion habits (room to stretch out) rather than a radio-length funk single. Source: https://takuyakuroda.bandcamp.com/album/rising-son-3

### Album

- Rising Son was Kuroda's Blue Note debut, released February 18, 2014, and produced by his New School friend, vocalist José James. Source: https://www.bluenote.com/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-to-make-blue-note-deb/
- The album leans hard on '70s soul-jazz and hip-hop, including a cover of Roy Ayers' "Everybody Loves the Sunshine" (with José James on vocals) — connecting Kuroda directly to the Ayers lineage also present elsewhere in this project. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/takuya-kuroda-mn0002411813/biography
- It reportedly hit No. 1 on Japan's jazz charts and was reissued on vinyl by First Word Records in January 2024, with a Joe Armon-Jones remix of "Everybody Loves the Sunshine" added. Sources: https://news.theurbanmusicscene.com/2024/01/first-word-records-to-reissue-takuya-kurodas-debut-lp-rising-son/ , https://takuyakuroda.bandcamp.com/album/rising-son-3
- Personnel: Kuroda (trumpet), Corey King (trombone), Kris Bowers (keys), Solomon Dorsey (bass), Nate Smith (drums); José James produced, Brian Bender engineered. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/714619-Takuya-Kuroda-Rising-Son

### Artist

- Takuya Kuroda was born February 21, 1980, in Ashiya, Hyōgo (near Kobe), Japan. He followed his older trombonist brother into big-band playing before moving to the US in 2003. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
- He studied at Berklee and then earned a degree from The New School (College of Performing Arts) in 2006, settling in Brooklyn, NY. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
- He was the first Japanese artist to sign with Blue Note Records. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/takuya-kuroda-mn0002411813/biography
