"Spider" closes side one of Herbie Hancock’s 1976 album *Secrets*. The following year, Columbia released it as a 7-inch single with "Maiden Voyage" on the B-side, but this single was not the album version. Instead, it used a 10-minute live take from *V.S.O.P. (Live)*, recorded at the Newport Jazz Festival on June 29, 1976.

Hancock created V.S.O.P. in 1976 as a challenge to himself and his audience. V.S.O.P. stood for Very Special One-time Performance, and the concept was to feature the major eras of Hancock's career on one bill.

In these concerts, Hancock played acoustic piano on Miles Davis material with the V.S.O.P. Quintet, essentially the mid-1960s Miles Davis Quintet with Freddie Hubbard on trumpet. He played electric synths on avant-garde jazz with his Mwandishi sextet, and jazz-funk tunes like "Spider" with the Headhunters.

For extra credit, also listen to the version of "Spider" on *V.S.O.P. (Live)*. What does Hancock do with the extra three minutes, and which version do you prefer?

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **Two Spiders, two records.** The studio "Spider" closes side one of Secrets in 1976. The next year, Columbia released a "Spider" 7" single (catalog 3-10563) backed with "Maiden Voyage," but the A-side wasn't the album version: it was the 10:12 live take from V.S.O.P., recorded at the Newport Jazz Festival in New York on June 29, 1976. So if a fan in 1977 bought the single after hearing Secrets, they got a different performance than the one that hooked them. A frame for how Hancock kept this band moving across studio and stage in the same calendar year. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/9422111-Herbie-Hancock-Spider ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSOP_(album) ; https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/v-s-o-p/
- **The blaxploitation theme that never was.** Spider has the urban, cinematic feel of mid-70s soundtrack funk, the kind of Shaft-or-Coffy moodpiece Hancock had already proven he could write (he scored *Death Wish* in 1974). A frame for Spider as a film-cue groove without a film, and for how much of 70s funk lived in adjacent dialogue with blaxploitation soundtracks. Sources: https://nicksvinylpicks.wordpress.com/2022/08/23/herbie-hancock-secrets/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson
- **The Funk Brother who took over Secrets.** Wah Wah Watson, fresh from Motown's Funk Brothers, co-wrote five of seven tracks on Secrets, including Spider (Ragin / Hancock / Jackson). A frame for how a Detroit session guitarist quietly redirected Hancock's San Francisco fusion lab toward tighter, song-shaped funk in the album's Headhunters-era moment. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The drum / bass / guitar bed on Spider feels like it could score a 1976 detective scene. If you were music supervisor for a film, where would you drop this seven-minute cue: opening titles, foot chase, after-hours stakeout? Source: https://nicksvinylpicks.wordpress.com/2022/08/23/herbie-hancock-secrets/
- Bennie Maupin owns a small armory of reeds on Secrets: tenor and soprano sax, saxello, bass clarinet, and the electronic Lyricon. On Spider, listen for the timbre of his solo voice. Can you tell which horn he's holding? Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- Two Detroit-trained guitarists are in the room: Wah Wah Watson (the wah-pedal master, Funk Brother) and Ray Parker Jr. (rhythm specialist, future "Ghostbusters" hitmaker). Try to track which one is doing what. When does the wah-wah lead step out, and when does Parker lock in the rhythm? Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Spider" is co-credited to Melvin "Wah Wah" Ragin, Herbie Hancock, and Paul Jackson, the same Hancock-Jackson-Ragin team that wrote "Swamp Rat" on the same album. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- The studio version on Secrets runs 7:21; a live 10:12 version, recorded by The Headhunters at the Newport Jazz Festival in New York City Center on Tuesday, June 29, 1976, appeared a year later on the V.S.O.P. double LP. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSOP_(album) ; https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/v-s-o-p/
- Columbia released "Spider" as a 7" single in 1977 (catalog 3-10563), but the A-side was the live V.S.O.P. take, not the studio recording, backed with "Maiden Voyage." Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9422111-Herbie-Hancock-Spider
- The Secrets sessions were tracked at Wally Heider Recording in San Francisco; Spider was engineered by David Rubinson and Fred Catero. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/secrets/
- One reviewer described the cut as "dramatic like a film soundtrack song (think Shaft), harkening back to the Fat Albert Rotunda album in its urban soundtrack feel." Source: https://nicksvinylpicks.wordpress.com/2022/08/23/herbie-hancock-secrets/
- Sampled by Polish hip-hop group Nagły Atak Spawacza featuring Slums Attack on "Anty." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/879411/Nag%C5%82y-Atak-Spawacza-Slums-Attack-Anty-Herbie-Hancock-Spider/

#### Album

- Secrets is Herbie Hancock's seventeenth album, released August 15, 1976 on Columbia Records (catalog PC-34280). Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/secrets/
- It hit #1 on Billboard's Jazz Albums chart, #8 on R&B, and #49 on the Pop 200, where it stayed seventeen weeks. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/secrets/
- Secrets unofficially closed the classic Headhunters lineup. Bennie Maupin and Paul Jackson carry over from the Head Hunters band, but drummer Mike Clark and percussionist Bill Summers are gone, replaced by James Levi and Kenneth Nash; James Gadson drums on "Doin' It." Source: https://www.progarchives.com/album.asp?id=19962 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- The album cover photo was shot by San Francisco photographer Herb Greene, best known as the Grateful Dead's house photographer. Source: https://nicksvinylpicks.wordpress.com/2022/08/23/herbie-hancock-secrets/
- "Cantelope Island," another Secrets cut, is a re-recording of Hancock's 1964 Blue Note classic "Cantaloupe Island," with the spelling changed. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)

#### Artist

- Herbert Jeffrey Hancock was a child prodigy who performed Mozart's Piano Concerto No. 26 with the Chicago Symphony Orchestra at age eleven, on February 5, 1952. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- He graduated from Grinnell College in 1960 with degrees in both electrical engineering and music, training that fed his later embrace of synths and wind controllers. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- Wah Wah Watson (Melvin Ragin, December 8, 1950 - October 24, 2018) was a member of Motown's Funk Brothers; his guitar work on The Temptations' "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone" is foundational. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson
- Hancock formed the V.S.O.P. project in 1976: a touring concept where the same evening could include the acoustic V.S.O.P. Quintet (Hancock with the mid-60s Miles Davis quintet alumni and Freddie Hubbard), the electric Mwandishi sextet, and the Headhunters jazz-funk band, all on a single bill. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSOP_(album)
- Secrets sessions overlapped with Watson's solo debut Elementary, which was also released on Columbia in 1976 and co-produced with Hancock's longtime producer David Rubinson. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (best fit, recommended).** Hancock is a jazz musician building a funk groove with extended instrumental space. Spider runs over seven minutes, leans on Maupin's reed solo and stacked Hancock keyboards, and uses the Headhunters rhythm section to support soloists rather than chasing a vocal hook. This is the textbook of the book's jazz-funk definition. The companion Doin' It entry (same album) was also slotted as Jazz-Funk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- **Classic Funk (defensible alternate).** Released by a major artist on a major label inside the 1969-1977 window, with a deep pocket and a memorable head; "Spider" sits comfortably alongside other classic-era funk instrumentals. The longer runtime and improvisational structure pull it back toward jazz-funk, though.
- **Psychedelic Funk (weak).** The synth pads and cinematic mood give it a hallucinatory edge, but Spider lacks the surreal storytelling, alter egos, or cosmology the book uses to define psychedelic funk.

**Recommendation:** Jazz-Funk.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - California (set, recommended).** Secrets was recorded entirely at Wally Heider Recording in San Francisco, and the Hancock / Headhunters operation was Bay Area-based in 1976. Bassist Paul Jackson, a Spider co-writer, was from Oakland. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/music/discography/album/secrets/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Jackson_(bassist)
- **US - Illinois (alternate).** Hancock was born and raised in Chicago. Defensible if the book ever pivots to artist-origin tagging, but the Doin' It and Chameleon entries both went with US - California for the same album / band, so consistency favors California here too.
- **US - Michigan (weak).** Co-writer Wah Wah Watson came up through the Detroit / Motown session pipeline, and Ray Parker Jr. is also a Detroit guitarist. Real long shot, but a reminder of how much Detroit DNA is on this cut.

**Recommendation:** US - California.
