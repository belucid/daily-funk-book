Patrice Rushen, the piano prodigy behind today's track, drew more than her share of sellout accusations when she transitioned from straight jazz into jazz-funk. Some of the criticism originated from the hopes jazz purists placed in her as a gifted young player who might rejuvenate the genre as the older generation faded. Some of it no doubt reflected a familiar double standard: as a woman, she was an easier target.

It was unfair to expect Rushen to stay pure while many of her elders were already chasing bigger audiences and paydays. Thankfully, she ignored her critics and made some of the funkiest jazz-funk records of the era. She later scored disco hits, including 1982’s “Forget Me Nots,” a strong candidate for disco's last big hit.

Born on September 30, 1954, Rushen was 22 when today’s track, “The Hump,” was released. She began playing piano at the age of three and performed at the Monterey Jazz Festival at seventeen. She signed with the jazz label Prestige at nineteen and released three albums there before moving to Elektra to pursue a more commercial direction. She went on to have a wide-ranging career as a music director, a composer for film, television, and the concert hall, and a forceful advocate for music education.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "The Hump" was co-written by Patrice Rushen and Darryl Cox, a drummer who also co-wrote "Let There Be Funk" on the same album. Rushen wrote five of the eight songs on Shout It Out herself. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- The track runs 6:11 on the LP, unusually long for a funk cut. That length is the jazz-funk bargain: radio-friendly groove with jazz-sized room to stretch. — Source: https://patricerushen.bandcamp.com/album/shout-it-out
- Maxwell's "Sumthin' Sumthin'" from his 1996 debut Urban Hang Suite is built on a sample of "The Hump," as are Shabba Ranks's "Respect" and a track by The Eighth. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Patrice-Rushen/The-Hump/
- James Gadson's pocket on the track is a textbook 1970s fusion/funk groove: displaced backbeat, open hi-hat on 4, 16th-note fills laid right on the pulse at even volume. A drum-transcription blog called it a lesson in how to play "so people like playing with you." — Source: https://cruiseshipdrummer.com/2018/03/31/transcription-james-gadson-shout-it-out/
- A 12" of "The Hump" b/w "Roll With the Punches" was issued later on Prestige (cat. D-157), keeping the track alive as a DJ cut after the album had come and gone. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/950724-Patrice-Rushen-The-Hump-Roll-With-The-Punches

#### Album

- Shout It Out was released February 21, 1977 on Prestige (catalog P 10101). It was recorded in 1976, which is why AllMusic lists a 1976 date even though Wikipedia, Discogs, Rate Your Music, and Rushen's own Bandcamp all cite 1977. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album), https://www.allmusic.com/album/shout-it-out-mw0001879018, https://patricerushen.bandcamp.com/album/shout-it-out
- It was Rushen's third and final Prestige album. She moved to Elektra in 1978 and would write "Forget Me Nots" four years later. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Rushen
- Executive producer was Orrin Keepnews, the legendary jazz producer who co-founded Riverside Records and ran much of Prestige's jazz output for Fantasy. The record was mastered by Bernie Grundman at A&M Studios in Hollywood. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)
- The session cast reads like a 1970s LA studio dream team: James Gadson (drums), Al McKay of Earth, Wind & Fire (guitar), Bill Summers of the Headhunters (percussion), Tom Scott (tenor sax and lyricon), bassist Charles Meeks, and horn arranger Jerry Hey. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_McKay, https://www.sessiondays.com/2023/11/1977-patrice-rushen-shout-it-out/
- Rushen co-produced the album (with Reggie Andrews and Tommy Vicari) at 22, a rare producer credit for a young woman at a major jazz label in 1976. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_It_Out_(Patrice_Rushen_album)

#### Artist

- Patrice Louise Rushen was born September 30, 1954 in Los Angeles. Her parents enrolled her at USC's Thornton School of Music at age four, and she was giving classical recitals by six. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Rushen
- She signed to Prestige at 19, after her high-school combo took first prize at a student band competition at the Monterey Jazz Festival. — Source: https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/patrice-rushen-1954/
- Her publishing company is called Baby Fingers Inc., a reference to a childhood nickname for her small hands. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Rushen
- Rushen was the first woman to serve as music director of the Grammy Awards (46th, 47th, and 48th ceremonies) and the first in 43 years to direct the Emmy Awards. She has also been music director for Janet Jackson's world tour, the NAACP Image Awards, the People's Choice Awards, and HBO's Comic Relief. — Source: https://patricerushen.com/full-length-bio
- She chaired USC Thornton's popular music program from 2014 to 2024 and is a three-time Grammy nominee. — Source: https://patricerushen.com/full-length-bio

### Genre Suggestions

`Jazz-Funk` is the right call. Per the book's definition, jazz-funk is "funk played by jazz musicians whose earlier careers were in more traditional jazz styles," with longer track lengths and room for extended improvisation. The Hump fits cleanly: Rushen came up through Prestige as a straight jazz player, the track runs 6:11 with extended soloing, and the rhythm section is built to support soloists.

Alternatives considered:

- **Classic Funk.** The groove, hooks, and vocals are as funky as many classic-funk entries from 1977. But the longer run time, improvisation focus, and Rushen's jazz lineage push it out of the classic-funk lane by the book's definition.
- **Soul Jazz.** The book reserves soul jazz for "the earlier, smaller-ensemble side of the story" and frames jazz-funk as "the full boil." Shout It Out is boil, not simmer: big band, dense arrangement, funk-forward rhythm section.

Recommendation: keep `Jazz-Funk`.
