### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were present in the entry. Questions surfaced during research that the author may want to pursue:

- **Who wrote it, Whitaker or Ayers?** The original 1972 LP credits Harry Whitaker as sole writer (publisher: I Ain't In It Music, ASCAP) and as arranger of B2 (https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming). A 2002 Village Voice piece also says Whitaker composed it (https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239), as does Peace & Rhythm (https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/harry-whitaker-sept-19-1942-nov-17-2010). **Counter-claim:** T.J. English's 2022 Village Voice article calls it "Ayers's piece" and credits Whitaker only as arranger and performer (https://villagevoice.com/smoking-harry-whitaker-a-village-jazz-story). The label credit is the most authoritative source; treat Whitaker as the writer.
- **Was it a separate session?** On the LP, B2 alone has different personnel and production: Ron Carter on bass (John Williams plays everywhere else), Bob Fusco on guitar (Sam Brown elsewhere), and Ed Kollis as co-producer or producer (album producer: Myrnaleah Williams). Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming and the 2009 Verve CD credits at https://www.discogs.com/release/3011661-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming. That pattern points to a separate session, possibly a Whitaker-led one, but I found no source that says so. Liner notes or session logs (e.g., the Van Gelder logs, or Bill Pratt's original liner notes) would settle it.
- **Why Brooklyn?** No source explains the title. Whitaker lived in the West Village in 2002 (https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239), and Ayers was an LA native. Approaches tried: Village Voice (2002, 2022), Peace & Rhythm, Radio Milwaukee, retailer notes. Not resolved.
- **Kendrick Lamar sample, unverified.** Radio Milwaukee (https://radiomilwaukee.org/2014-04-01/brooklyn-sampled) and Elemental Music (https://www.elemental-music.com/releases/4703-roy-ayers-ubiquity-he-s-coming-limited-gatefold-edition.html) say Kendrick Lamar sampled the track, and a web search summary tied it to "good kid." **Counter-evidence:** the Wikipedia sample credits for *good kid, m.A.A.d city* list no sample on "good kid" and no Roy Ayers sample anywhere (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Kid,_M.A.A.D_City). Do not print this without confirmation; WhoSampled (403 to the fetcher) or the official album credits should be checked by hand.
- **DJ Shadow sample, likely wrong.** Radio Milwaukee lists DJ Shadow as a sampler of this track, but other sources tie DJ Shadow to "He's a Superstar" from the same album (https://guerssen.com/product/roy-ayers-ubiquity-hes-coming-gatefold/). Unconfirmed.
- **Was there a 7" single?** No contemporary 1972 single turned up on Discogs. The track's only 7" issue is Dynamite Cuts' 2019 *He's Coming: 45's Collection* (https://www.discogs.com/release/13077744-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming-45s-Collection), and there is an unofficial 2007 UK promo 12" paired with The Jacksons (https://www.discogs.com/release/1130300-Roy-Ayers-The-Jacksons-We-Live-In-Brooklyn-Baby-This-Place-Hotel-Heartbreak-Hotel).
- **No exact release date.** Discogs, Wikipedia and Apple Music only give 1972. No Billboard chart entry was found (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers_discography).

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The sideman's song.** The best-known track on a Roy Ayers album was written and arranged by his keyboardist, Harry Whitaker, who is credited as the writer and was still collecting hip-hop royalties from it decades later while playing a restaurant gig on Houston Street (https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239). This theme is about who really authors a "Roy Ayers" record.
- **A borough in a loop.** Digable Planets ("Borough Check," 1994) and Mos Def ("Brooklyn," 1999) both reached for this record when they wanted to say "Brooklyn" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowout_Comb, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_on_Both_Sides). The track became a place-name in sound, a 1972 jazz-funk record that later stood for a borough's hip-hop identity.
- **Haunted funk.** AllMusic's Jason Ankeny calls it "a haunting hybrid of jazz, funk, and soul" (quoted at https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/hes-coming-roy-ayers-ubiquity/0060252702252). It is a moody, slow-burning, vocal-incantation piece on an album otherwise focused on spiritual and political messages. This theme is a case for funk as atmosphere instead of dance-floor drive.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The vocals here are more incantation than lyric. Do they work as words or as another instrument in the arrangement?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Harry Whitaker, not Roy Ayers, is credited as writer and arranger. The publisher is "I Ain't In It Music" (ASCAP), a different publishing house from Ayers' own Roy Ayers Ubiquity publishing on the other originals. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming
- It is the only track on the album with Ron Carter on bass and Bob Fusco on guitar, and the only one with Ed Kollis credited as (co-)producer. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming ; https://www.discogs.com/release/26180456-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming
- Digable Planets sampled it on "Borough Check" (feat. Guru) from *Blowout Comb*, released October 18, 1994. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowout_Comb
- Mos Def sampled it in the second movement of "Brooklyn" from *Black on Both Sides*, released October 12, 1999. The three-movement song also samples Milt Jackson and the Notorious B.I.G.'s "Who Shot Ya?" — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_on_Both_Sides ; https://radiomilwaukee.org/2013-06-04/roy-ayers-and-mos-def-live-in-brooklyn-baby
- In 2002, the Village Voice called it a "seminal (and much sampled) rare groove tune." It also reported that Whitaker was receiving hip-hop royalties from Digable Planets, A Tribe Called Quest and Mos Def. — Source: https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239
- AllMusic (Jason Ankeny): "an absolute masterpiece, a haunting hybrid of jazz, funk, and soul that exemplifies the Ayers aesthetic at its most far-reaching and inventive." — Source: https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/hes-coming-roy-ayers-ubiquity/0060252702252 (AllMusic review reproduced; allmusic.com returned 403)
- Its demand as a DJ record led to an unofficial 2007 UK 12" pairing it with The Jacksons' "(This Place Hotel) Heartbreak Hotel." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1130300-Roy-Ayers-The-Jacksons-We-Live-In-Brooklyn-Baby-This-Place-Hotel-Heartbreak-Hotel

#### Album

- *He's Coming* was recorded at Rudy Van Gelder's studio in Englewood Cliffs, NJ, with Van Gelder engineering. It was mastered by Bob Ludwig at Sterling Sound, and Billy Cobham plays drums "by courtesy of Columbia Records." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming
- The album opens with Jesus Christ Superstar material ("He's a Superstar," plus Lloyd Webber/Rice's "I Don't Know How to Love Him") and a Hollies cover ("He Ain't Heavy"). It was framed as a spiritual statement about the life and rebirth of Jesus. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming ; https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/hes-coming-roy-ayers-ubiquity/0060252702252
- Liner notes were by Bill Pratt of WRTI 90.1 FM Philadelphia. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming
- No US or R&B chart entry. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers_discography
- It is reissued often: Verve CD 2009, Elemental 180g LP 2022, Dynamite Cuts 2x7" 2019, and a Verve Vault LP dated July 10, 2026. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/3011661-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming ; https://www.discogs.com/release/26180456-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming ; https://www.piccadillyrecords.com/167841/Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-He's-Coming-(Verve-Vault)-Verve

#### Artist

- Roy Edward Ayers Jr. was born September 10, 1940, in Los Angeles. Lionel Hampton gave him his first pair of vibraphone mallets at age five. He died March 4, 2025, in New York City at 84. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers
- He named the band Ubiquity because the word means being everywhere at once. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers
- Harry Whitaker (born September 19, 1942, Pensacola, FL; died November 17, 2010) joined Ayers in 1970. He later served as Roberta Flack's musical director, co-wrote the *Coffy* soundtrack with Ayers, and recorded the cult album *Black Renaissance: Body, Mind and Spirit* on MLK Day 1976. — Sources: https://villagevoice.com/smoking-harry-whitaker-a-village-jazz-story ; https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/harry-whitaker-sept-19-1942-nov-17-2010
- **Discrepancy on Whitaker's cause of death:** "heart attack" (https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/harry-whitaker-sept-19-1942-nov-17-2010) vs. "congestive heart failure brought on by hypertension" (https://villagevoice.com/smoking-harry-whitaker-a-village-jazz-story).
- **Discrepancy on Whitaker's time with Roberta Flack:** "1974 to 1981" (https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239) vs. "five years" (https://villagevoice.com/smoking-harry-whitaker-a-village-jazz-story) vs. "nine years" (search summary of allaboutjazz.com, page 403).

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz Funk (primary).** Ayers was a bebop vibraphonist turned groove bandleader. The players are jazz musicians (Ron Carter, Sonny Fortune, Billy Cobham), and it was recorded at Van Gelder's studio in 1972. Discogs tags the album "Jazz-Funk" (https://www.discogs.com/master/118094-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming), which matches the book's player and era definitions.
- **Deep Funk.** The album didn't chart (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers_discography). The track found its audience later through rare groove DJs (Village Voice called it a "rare groove tune": https://www.villagevoice.com/?p=27239) and hip-hop samplers (Digable Planets, Mos Def). This is exactly the book's "delayed recognition" definition, though it was on a major label.
- **Soul Jazz.** Discogs also tags "Soul-Jazz." It is a weaker fit, because the book reserves soul jazz for earlier, smaller ensembles, and this is a dense, layered arrangement.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - New York (chosen).** Ubiquity was a New York-based band in this period. Whitaker settled in NYC in the 1960s and joined Ayers in 1970 (https://villagevoice.com/smoking-harry-whitaker-a-village-jazz-story). Polydor Inc. operated from 1700 Broadway, New York. The title names Brooklyn, and its sampling legacy is Brooklyn hip-hop (Digable Planets' "Borough Check," Mos Def's "Brooklyn"). The studio was across the river in Englewood Cliffs, NJ (https://www.discogs.com/release/499479-Roy-Ayers-Ubiquity-Hes-Coming), which is part of the NYC jazz ecosystem.
- **US - Northeast.** This option is defensible if the author prefers to weight the New Jersey recording location.
- **US - California.** This option only works on Ayers' birthplace and upbringing in Los Angeles (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Ayers). It is weak for this track.
