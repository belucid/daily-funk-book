Most of Dr. Strut’s debut album, released June 2, 1979, was cut in just one or two takes with minimal overdubbing, rare for an LA jazz-fusion session in 1979. “Blowtop” captures what the band was chasing: a quick punch out of the gate, then restless energy as the groove shifts around.
 
Dr. Strut originally formed as the Legendary Dynamic Groovadelics, but thankfully Motown head Berry Gordy rechristened them Dr. Strut when he signed them to the label’s new jazz division. Gordy shut that division down two years later, leaving the band to find a Japanese label for their third record. They were big in Japan.

“Blowtop” was composed by saxophonist David Woodford. You already know his work: he is the tenor saxophonist who later screamed through Glenn Frey’s “The Heat Is On” in *Beverly Hills Cop*.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The flagship that almost wasn't"** — Dr. Strut was hand-picked as the face of Motown's brand-new jazz division in 1979, and Berry Gordy killed that division roughly two years later, leaving the band to scramble for a Japanese label to release their third record. The debut's confident, live-takes energy reads differently once you know how short the runway turned out to be. Source: http://overdoseoffingalcocoa.blogspot.com/2014/03/dr-strut.html

- **"Studio rats with one take to prove it"** — Most of the debut was cut in first or second takes with minimal overdubbing, which is rare for an LA fusion session in 1979. "Blowtop," at a tight 3:19, captures that quick, punch-it-and-move energy. Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Dr.+Strut/+wiki

- **"The sax player you've already heard"** — "Blowtop" is a David Woodford composition, and Woodford is the tenor that later screamed through Glenn Frey's "The Heat Is On" in Beverly Hills Cop. Framing the entry around "you've heard this guy before, you just didn't know it" is a welcoming hook for newcomers. Source: https://www.mmone.org/david-woodford/

#### Album

- The debut was cut with minimal overdubbing, mostly first or second takes — a jazz-session ethic applied to an LA fusion record — Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Dr.+Strut/+wiki
- The album contains a rare Donald Fagen / Walter Becker instrumental, "Canadian Star," that Steely Dan themselves never released — reportedly written during the Gaucho sessions — Source: http://overdoseoffingalcocoa.blogspot.com/2014/03/dr-strut.html
- Guitarist Tim Weston was an assistant engineer on Steely Dan's Countdown to Ecstasy and is credited on the liner notes for "Weekend Knob Job," which is almost certainly how the Becker/Fagen cut ended up on a Motown jazz-funk debut — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2690585-Steely-Dan-Countdown-To-Ecstasy
- Producer Lee Young Sr. was a veteran of the pre-bebop jazz world: he had drummed behind Fats Waller, Duke Ellington, and Count Basie, and spent nine years as Nat "King" Cole's musical director before building Motown's jazz division in 1979 — Source: https://www.adampwhite.com/westgrandblog/father-and-son
- The album total runs a lean 35:58 across ten tracks, with a 1:01 closing tag ("No! You Came Here For An Argument") — a very un-fusion runtime — Source: https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/dr-strut-dr-strut/0060255789751
- Both the debut and its 1980 follow-up Struttin' placed high on the Billboard Jazz Albums chart; specific peak positions could not be verified from sources reached during research — Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Dr.+Strut/+wiki

#### Artist

- Dr. Strut was originally called "The Legendary Groovadelics" (some sources add "Dynamic"); Berry Gordy personally insisted on the new name when he signed them and publicly dubbed them "the flagship of the new jazz division" — Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Dr.+Strut/+wiki
- Sax/flute player David Woodford went on to play the tenor solo on Glenn Frey's "The Heat Is On" (the Beverly Hills Cop theme) and recorded with Aerosmith, Rod Stewart, Bonnie Raitt, and Stevie Ray Vaughan — Source: https://www.mmone.org/david-woodford/
- Drummer Claude Pepper — whose real name was John Mack — co-founded Jack Mack and the Heart Attack in 1980. The band was on stage at Centennial Olympic Park in Atlanta on July 27, 1996, minutes before the pipe bombing that killed one person and injured more than a hundred — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Mack_and_the_Heart_Attack
- Keyboardist Kevin Bassinson left music for film and is best known today as the composer of the Cyborg (1989) score — Source: https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0060311/
- The third Dr. Strut album, Soul Surgery, was recorded the day after Berry Gordy shut down Motown's jazz division; engineer Barney Perkins pushed them to finish it, no US label would pick it up, and it eventually surfaced in 1982 on the Japanese label Agharta/Canyon — Source: http://overdoseoffingalcocoa.blogspot.com/2014/03/dr-strut.html
- The album was mixed by Roger Nicholls (not to be confused with the songwriter of the same name), whose credits include Steely Dan work — a second tie back to the Becker/Fagen universe — Source: https://recordselectorlv.com/products/dr-strut (context)

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (current, keep it).** This is the correct primary tag. Every reference body (Discogs, AllMusic, Qobuz, Record Selector, fan blogs) places Dr. Strut in jazz-funk/jazz fusion. The players' backgrounds are jazz, the format is instrumental, the grooves are funk-based, and the era (1979 LA, Motown jazz division, post-Brecker Brothers, post-Tom Scott LA Express) is textbook jazz-funk. Source: https://recordselectorlv.com/products/dr-strut

- **Soul Jazz — weak case.** The organ-trio/small-group feel of soul jazz doesn't really fit. Dr. Strut is a six-piece with a full horn-and-rhythm section, extended chord voicings, and fusion-era production. Mention only if you want to deliberately surface soul jazz as a parent genre.

- **Deep Funk — consider as a secondary tag.** The project's own definition of deep funk is "tracks overlooked on release and only found their audience later through crate-digging, DJs, and dance floors." Dr. Strut's debut charted at release, so it isn't deep funk in that sense — but the band and this track specifically have lived an entirely second life as crate-digger favorites over the last two decades, which is exactly the cultural fate the genre is named for. If the entry leans into the "the flagship that disappeared" theme, deep funk is defensible. Source for the band's obscurity today: http://overdoseoffingalcocoa.blogspot.com/2014/03/dr-strut.html

### Region Suggestions

- **US - California (suggested, now set).** Dr. Strut formed in Los Angeles in 1977, recorded at Motown's LA-based studios, and all five founding members were LA-based sessioners. This is the cleanest region tag. Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Dr.+Strut/+wiki

- **US - Michigan — rejected.** Motown's Detroit heritage is not where this record lives. By 1979 the label had relocated to Los Angeles (Hitsville USA in LA), and Dr. Strut were an LA group through and through. Using Michigan would be misleading.

- **US - West — defensible backup.** If the author wants to fold Dr. Strut into a broader West Coast fusion narrative alongside Tom Scott's LA Express, the Brecker-adjacent LA session scene, and the smooth jazz pre-history, the US - West tag would work. US - California is more specific and preferred.
