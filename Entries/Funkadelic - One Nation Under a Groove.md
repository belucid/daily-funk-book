### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The hit that wasn't supposed to be a hit."** Clinton openly admitted to Blues & Soul that they "really made it more commercial" this time, and the band cheekily sang "corny or not, here we come." This is the rare P-Funk track where the band consciously chased a radio hit, and it became their first million-seller. The tension between Funkadelic's far-out reputation and a deliberate commercial play is a rich angle. Sources: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/funkadelic/one-nation-under-a-groove
2. **"The Ohio kid who walked in and rewrote P-Funk."** Junie Morrison, the teenage musical brain behind Ohio Players' "Funky Worm," joined P-Funk as musical director in 1977 and immediately co-wrote and arranged Funkadelic's biggest single. According to Morrison, Clinton wasn't even in the room when the track started: it was Shider who shepherded him through the band's chemistry. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_%22Junie%22_Morrison ; https://www.songfacts.com/facts/funkadelic/one-nation-under-a-groove
3. **"Hymn to anthem."** The opening melody is lifted from the gospel chestnut "Jesus' Love Is Very Wonderful." Clinton transposed church-camp sing-along into secular funk utopia, swapping Jesus's love for the groove itself. A clean line to draw from gospel roots to P-Funk's universe-building. Sources: https://www.edgeoftheline.co/articles/onenation

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. The first ten seconds are sung to the melody of the children's gospel song "Jesus' Love Is Very Wonderful." Once you hear it, you can't unhear it. Does it change how the rest of the chant lands for you?
2. Around the 4:28 mark, Bernie Worrell stops anchoring the One and starts wandering. Track what the synth bass does from there. Is the groove still locked, or is the floor finally giving way?
3. Listen to the contrast between Junie Morrison's strained, high-strung lead vocal and George Clinton's loose, conversational interjections. Which voice is actually selling you the utopia?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The single was Funkadelic's first million-seller and the longest-running #1 of any single on the Billboard Soul chart in 1978, holding the top spot for six consecutive weeks. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove_(song)
- George Clinton sat on the title phrase for two years before recording, and wrote no lyrics until the band was in the studio. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/funkadelic/one-nation-under-a-groove
- Two competing origin stories for the title: (a) Clinton saw UN workers raising flags during a filmed Mothership stunt outside UN headquarters and a friend riffed "one nation under a groove"; (b) in a 2018 Guardian interview, Clinton credited two fans named LaTanya and Darlene who told him after a DC show that the night felt like "one nation under a groove." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove_(song) ; https://www.songfacts.com/facts/funkadelic/one-nation-under-a-groove
- Junie Morrison: "George was not present at the inception of the track." Garry Shider was the one who eased Morrison into the band's working chemistry. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/funkadelic/one-nation-under-a-groove
- Bootsy Collins played drums on the track, not bass. Bernie Worrell handled synth bass. — Source: https://www.edgeoftheline.co/articles/onenation
- The chant melody is borrowed from the gospel/Sunday-school song "Jesus' Love Is Very Wonderful." — Source: https://www.edgeoftheline.co/articles/onenation
- The track has been sampled in 90+ songs, including Ice Cube ft. George Clinton's "Bop Gun (One Nation)" (1993), EPMD's "So Wat Cha Sayin'" (1989), YG's "Twist My Fingaz" (2015), and En Vogue's "Strange." Aaliyah's "Young Nation" interpolates the chorus. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Funkadelic/One-Nation-Under-a-Groove/ (search snippet) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove_(song)
- The track exists in three commercially released lengths: 4:12 single edit, 7:33 album version, 11:26 12-inch version. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove_(song)
- 7" single catalog: Warner Bros. WBS 8618 (US); B-side was "One Nation Under a Groove (Part II)." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1647694-Funkadelic-One-Nation-Under-A-Groove-Part-I (per search snippet)
- Recognized by the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's "500 Songs That Shaped Rock and Roll," ranked #210 on Rolling Stone's 2021 "500 Greatest Songs of All Time," and #178 on Rolling Stone's 2022 "200 Greatest Dance Songs." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove_(song)

#### Album

- Recorded primarily at United Sound Studio in Detroit; one track (a live take of "Maggot Brain") was cut on April 15, 1978 at Monroe Civic Center in Monroe, Louisiana. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove
- Album released September 22, 1978; certified platinum by RIAA on December 19, 1978, less than three months later. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove
- First Funkadelic album with Walter "Junie" Morrison; he co-wrote five tracks including the title cut. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove ; https://www.edgeoftheline.co/articles/onenation
- US pressings included a bonus 7-inch EP; European pressings replaced it with a bonus 12-inch containing the EP tracks on one side and an extended "One Nation Under a Groove" on the other. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/54666-Funkadelic-One-Nation-Under-A-Groove (per search snippet)
- Hit #1 on the Billboard Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums chart and #16 on the Billboard 200, Funkadelic's commercial peak. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove
- Ranked #177 on Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time (2003 and 2012 editions). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove

#### Artist

- Funkadelic formed in Plainfield, New Jersey in 1968 as a rebranded touring vehicle for George Clinton's Parliaments, a doo-wop group from his Plainfield barbershop. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkadelic
- The band moved from Westbound Records to Warner Bros. in 1976, starting with Hardcore Jollies. One Nation was their third Warner album. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkadelic
- George Clinton was born July 22, 1941 in Kannapolis, North Carolina. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(funk_musician)
- Garry Shider was born July 24, 1953; died June 16, 2010. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Shider
- Walter "Junie" Morrison was born June 28, 1954 in Dayton, Ohio; died January 21, 2017 in London. He wrote and arranged Ohio Players' "Funky Worm" before joining P-Funk in 1977. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_%22Junie%22_Morrison

1. **09-22 — Album release date (September 22, 1978).** The most defensible anchor: this is the day Funkadelic's commercial peak hit shelves. No existing dated entry uses 09-22. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove
2. **09-30 — First week at #1 on the Billboard R&B chart.** The track began its six-week reign at #1 on the soul chart on September 30, 1978. No conflict in the dated entries. Source: search snippet citing Wikipedia and Billboard chart archives

5. **06-28 — Junie Morrison's birthday (June 28, 1954).** Fits the "Ohio kid who rewrote P-Funk" angle. No conflict. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_%22Junie%22_Morrison

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Psychedelic Funk (current value).** Strong defensible call. The book defines psychedelic funk as the broad P-Funk lane, and this is the title track of the album that crystallized that universe. Funkadelic is literally the canonical psychedelic funk band. Source: Daily Funk Project/Matter/On Genres & Regions.md
2. **Classic Funk.** Also reasonable. The track sits firmly inside the 1969-1977 classic-funk window (just one year past it), was a major-label release with chart presence, a million-selling single, and radio play, which exactly matches the book's definition of classic funk: funk as a central sound in mainstream American music. Worth noting if you want to avoid stacking too many entries under Psychedelic Funk. Source: Daily Funk Project/Matter/On Genres & Regions.md
3. **Funk Rock.** A defensible third. Funkadelic is widely categorized as "funk rock band" in the encyclopedic sources, and the album includes "Who Says a Funk Band Can't Play Rock?!" The rock-leaning psychedelic guitar lineage (Hampton, Shider) is part of the track's DNA. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkadelic ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove

Recommendation: keep Psychedelic Funk. It is the cleanest fit and the entry is the perfect anchor for that genre in the book.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - Michigan (current suggestion, filled).** Recording took place at United Sound Studio in Detroit. By the late 1970s, P-Funk's operational base was Detroit, and the album was made there. The book's region note says the call should "represent the track's origins and be defensible," and Detroit is where this specific track was tracked. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Nation_Under_a_Groove
2. **US - Northeast.** Defensible alternative. Funkadelic formed in Plainfield, New Jersey, and Clinton built the Parliaments scene there. If you'd rather tag by band origin than recording location, Northeast is the correct umbrella (NJ isn't broken out in the region list). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkadelic
3. **US - Ohio.** Weaker but worth noting. Junie Morrison was from Dayton, the album's musical director was Ohio-born, and the P-Funk Ohio Players cross-pollination is part of the story. I would not lead with this, but it's defensible if you ever want to emphasize the Ohio angle in the writing theme.

Recommendation: stay with US - Michigan. The Detroit recording site is the most concrete tie.
