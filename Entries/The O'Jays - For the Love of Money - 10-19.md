When you listen to today's track in its entirety, the lyrics offer a damning list of what people will do for money: lie, cheat, and steal from their own mothers. Yet the chorus is so infectious that it became the unironic opening theme for NBC's *The Apprentice*, a show celebrating wealth and greed.

Anthony Jackson was just 21 years old when he showed up for his session gig as a bassist at Sigma Sound Studios in Philadelphia. He brought a pick and a wah-wah pedal, an unusual choice for a bassist at the time which earned him a writing credit on the track. The studio had recently purchased a new Eventide phaser, and eager to try it out, engineer Joe Tarsia recorded the bass with and without the effect. Producer Kenny Gamble preferred the phased version and added an echo to the mix. The end result opens the song and became one of the most sampled basslines in history.

Jackson died on this day in 2025, but not before leaving his mark on this song and the over 100 tracks that sampled it.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The bassist who rewrote the opening"** — Anthony Jackson was a 21-year-old session bassist who showed up with a wah-wah pedal on his Fender Precision Bass. Engineer Joe Tarsia ran it through a brand-new Eventide phaser, producer Kenny Gamble reached for the echo button during the mix, and the result became one of the most sampled bass lines in history. Jackson received a co-writing credit, an almost unheard-of honor for a session musician. He died in October 2025, making this a timely tribute.

2. **"A warning disguised as a groove"** — The song's title quotes 1 Timothy 6:10, and the lyrics are an unsparing catalog of what people will do for money: lie, cheat, steal from their own mothers. Yet the groove is so infectious that it became the theme song for The Apprentice, a show celebrating wealth and deal-making. The irony writes itself. Gamble had recently converted to Islam and was wrestling with spiritual questions about his own financial success.

3. **"Studio alchemy at Sigma Sound"** — The Sigma Sound Studios story is rich: Tarsia had just installed the Eventide phaser, the Kepex noise gate shaped the vibraphone, and the ghostly background vocal effect came from a reverse-echo technique. The track was built in layers across days or weeks, a production method that Gamble and Huff used to create their Philadelphia Sound.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The opening bass line was played by Anthony Jackson on a Fender Precision Bass with a pick and a wah-wah pedal, run through a brand-new Eventide phaser that engineer Joe Tarsia had just installed at Sigma Sound Studios. During the final mix, producer Kenny Gamble impulsively reached over and hit the echo button, adding the cascading delay that made the riff iconic. — Source: https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-tracks-o-jays-love-money-373580
- Jackson received co-writer credit alongside Gamble and Huff, a rare honor for a session musician and a testament to how central the bass line was to the song's identity. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_Money
- The ghostly background vocal sound was achieved through a reverse-echo technique: vocals were recorded in reverse so that the echo preceded the sung words, creating an eerie floating effect. — Source: https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-tracks-o-jays-love-money-373580
- The Kepex noise gate, triggered by the snare drum, was used to shape the vibraphone tone played by Vince Montana. — Source: https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-tracks-o-jays-love-money-373580
- The track was recorded on October 3, 1973, and the sessions involved 54 to 60 musicians, almost a symphony orchestra. — Source: https://www.mixonline.com/recording/classic-tracks/classic-tracks-o-jays-love-money-373580
- The single version (3:42) was edited down from the album version (7:14) but both received substantial radio play, unusual for such a long track. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_Money
- Jackson's pick-playing bass style was inspired by James Brown's guitarists Jimmy Nolen and Catfish Collins. As a teenager, Jackson would jam with his friend Reggie Lucas, doubling Nolen-style guitar parts an octave lower on bass. — Source: https://www.guitarworld.com/features/anthony-jackson-on-for-the-love-of-money
- The song was nominated for the 1975 Grammy Award for Best R&B Vocal Performance (Duo, Group or Chorus), losing to "Tell Me Something Good" by Rufus featuring Chaka Khan. In 2016, over 40 years later, it was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_Money
- The song has been sampled in over 100 tracks, making it one of the most sampled recordings in music history. Notable samples include Bone Thugs-N-Harmony's "Foe tha Love of $" (1995, featuring Eazy-E), Grandmaster Melle Mel's "Step Off" (1985), and Dr. Dre's "For the Love of Money" from the album Compton (2015). — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-O%27Jays/For-the-Love-of-Money/sampled/
- The song served as the opening theme for NBC's The Apprentice (2004-2017) and as Donald Trump's WWE entrance music, an ironic fate for a track that warns against greed. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_Money

#### Album
- Ship Ahoy (1973) was a concept album centered around slavery. The nearly ten-minute title track depicted the Middle Passage of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic. Gamble and Huff had originally written it for the Shaft in Africa soundtrack before giving it to the O'Jays. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The album hit #1 on Billboard's Black Albums chart and #11 on the Pop Albums chart, and was certified Gold by the RIAA on January 21, 1974, and Platinum on August 11, 1992. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- Ship Ahoy produced two major singles: "For the Love of Money" (#3 R&B / #9 Pop) and "Put Your Hands Together" (#2 R&B / #10 Pop). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)
- The New York Times, in a 1974 review, called it "Gamble and Huff at their creative best." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_Ahoy_(album)

#### Artist
- The O'Jays formed in Canton, Ohio, in 1958 while members attended Canton McKinley High School. They went through several names: The Triumphs, then The Mascots, before naming themselves after Cleveland DJ Eddie O'Jay, who championed their early career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- Original member William Powell died of cancer on May 26, 1977, at age 35, while still an active member of the group. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- The O'Jays were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2005 and the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2004. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O%27Jays
- Anthony Jackson, who co-wrote "For the Love of Money" at age 21, went on to invent the modern six-string bass guitar (which he called the "contrabass guitar"), commissioning luthier Carl Thompson to build the first one in 1975. He played on over 3,000 recording sessions across his career. Jackson died on October 19, 2025, at age 73 from complications of Parkinson's disease. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Jackson_(musician); https://downbeat.com/news/detail/in-memoriam-anthony-jackson-1952-2025
- Kenny Gamble had recently converted to Islam around the time of writing this song, and was wrestling with the tension between his spiritual beliefs and his financial success, which informed the song's warning about greed. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_and_Huff

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current value) — Strong case. The track was a major commercial hit from the core funk era (1973), featured on a major label (Philadelphia International), received significant radio play and chart success (#9 Pop, #3 R&B). The bass-driven groove, horn arrangements, and rhythmic pocket are quintessentially funk. It fits squarely within the book's definition: "music that defined the core vocabulary and was released into the commercial pipeline of major labels." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_Money

2. **Deep Funk** — Weak case. This track was NOT overlooked on release. It was a top-10 pop hit and top-3 R&B hit. It does not fit the "delayed recognition" criterion that defines deep funk in this book.

3. **Soul Jazz** — No case. While the production is sophisticated, this is not a jazz-oriented track. It's a vocal group performance built on a funk groove with orchestral sweetening. The Philadelphia International sound borrows from soul and R&B traditions, not jazz.

**Recommendation**: "Classic Funk" is correct. This is textbook classic-era funk from a major label with significant commercial success.
