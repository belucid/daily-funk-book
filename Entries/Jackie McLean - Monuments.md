### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. Items I could not resolve on my own initiative are recorded below.

- **Exact release date of the *Monuments* LP.** Discogs, jazzdisco.org and AllMusic all give only the year 1979. Steps taken: Discogs master 368801 and all five catalogued versions (US LP 2388286 and 9562468, UK LP 3099676, UK cassette 16197227, 2016 Japanese CD 9073518) — every one lists `released: 1979` with no month or day; jazzdisco.org's McLean discography lists only "November 1978–January 1979" as the recording window; AllMusic and Dusty Groove both block automated fetching (HTTP 403) and their search snippets give only the year. The nearest hard date I could anchor is the UK singles chart debut of the album's single, 7 July 1979 (Official Charts). Suggested next approach for the author: *Billboard* new-release listings for spring/summer 1979, or the RCA promo 12" JD-11570 (https://www.discogs.com/release/1167168-Jackie-McLean-Doctor-Jackyll-And-Mister-Funk), which may carry a dated promo stamp.
- **Was the title track "Monuments" ever issued on a 45?** I could find no evidence for it. Discogs shows only one single from the album, "Doctor Jackyll And Mister Funk" b/w "On The Slick Side" (7", 12" and promo variants, US and UK). Searched Discogs by artist + release title and by artist + track across `type=release`; no 45 with "Monuments" as an A- or B-side appears. Treat "Monuments" as an album-only track.
- **What "Monuments" is about.** The track is an instrumental — the vocal credits on the LP jacket cover only A3, A4, B1 and B2, and A1 carries no vocalist. It was written by producer Mitch Farber, not McLean, so there is no McLean statement of intent to find. No interview, liner note or review discussing the title's meaning surfaced in searching.
- **Mitch Farber.** Beyond his role here (composer of six of seven tracks, producer, arranger, working for Absolute Music, Ltd., publishing through Distant Land Music, BMI) I found no biography, interview or obituary. He appears to be an otherwise thinly documented figure, which is itself striking given he wrote nearly the whole record.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The album where the composer wasn't the leader."** Jackie McLean was one of hard bop's genuine composers — Miles Davis liked his tunes enough to record "Dig" and "Little Melonae" (https://www.milesdavis.com/track/little-melonae/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dig_(composition)). On *Monuments*, McLean writes nothing. Every track except "Dr. Jackyll And Mister Funk" (music by Farber, lyrics by Neil Sheppard) is credited "Written-By Mitch Farber," and Farber also produced and arranged (https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments). A whole entry can hang on what it means when a great composer is hired to play someone else's record under his own name.
- **"The last record before the silence."** *Monuments* was McLean's last studio album as a leader for nine years; he did not go back into a studio as a leader until *Dynasty* for Triloka in 1988 (https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/mclean-jackie). The funk record is the door closing. He spent the interval teaching at the Hartt School and running the Artists Collective in Hartford (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean).
- **"When the critics call it a fiasco and the diggers call it a find."** AllMusic's Scott Yanow writes the record off as "electronic rhythms, dumb vocals... and unadventurous playing that is instantly forgettable," an "artistic (and commercial) fiasco" (https://www.allmusic.com/album/monuments-mw0000868107); Dusty Groove sells it as "a rare electric outing from legendary altoist Jackie McLean — and one that comes off surprisingly well" (https://www.dustygroove.com/item/5163/Jackie-McLean:Monuments). Same record, two verdicts forty years apart. Good vehicle for the book's running argument about what jazz fans meant by "sellout."
- **"The session cartel."** The rhythm section on *Monuments* — Hiram Bullock, Will Lee, Steve Jordan, Clifford Carter, plus Mark Egan — is the 24th Street Band, formed in New York in 1978, who cut their own debut at Sound Ideas on 13–15 December 1978, right in the middle of the *Monuments* sessions (https://sessiondays.com/2022/06/17/1979-the-24th-street-band-the-24th-street-band/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Bullock). The same four men became the core of Paul Shaffer's World's Most Dangerous Band on *Late Night with David Letterman* (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Shaffer_and_the_World%27s_Most_Dangerous_Band). An entry about the anonymous New York players whose groove you have heard ten thousand times without knowing their names.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Harry Lookofsky's violin is on only two tracks on this album, and "Monuments" is one of them. Find it in the arrangement. Does the string line make this sound like a jazz record trying to be a disco record, or a disco record with a jazz player standing in front of it?
- McLean spent the 1950s and 60s with the sharpest, most acidic alto tone in hard bop. Play four minutes of "Let Freedom Ring" and then play "Monuments." Is that the same horn player? What survived the move to a funk band, and what got smoothed off?
- This is the title track of an album that most critics dismissed and most crate diggers now hunt. Listen once through and cast your vote before you read anything about it.
- Count how long McLean waits before he plays. Then ask who this record is actually built around — the leader, or the rhythm section behind him.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Monuments" is the album's opening track, A1, running 4:30 — the second-shortest cut on the record. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments
- It is one of only two tracks on the LP (with "Gotta Get A Piece Of Your Soul") to carry Harry Lookofsky's violin. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments
- It was written by producer Mitch Farber, not by McLean. Farber wrote six of the seven tracks outright and co-wrote the seventh. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments
- Harry Lookofsky (1 October 1913 – 8 June 1998), the violinist on this track, was a veteran New York session player and the father of Michael Brown of The Left Banke; he co-produced "Walk Away Renée." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Lookofsky
- It appears never to have been issued as a single; the only 45/12" pulled from the album was "Doctor Jackyll And Mister Funk" b/w "On The Slick Side." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1968345-Jackie-McLean-Doctor-Jackyll-And-Mister-Funk

#### Album

- Recorded and mixed at RCA Studios, New York, November 1978 – January 1979. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments and https://www.jazzdisco.org/jackie-mclean/discography/
- The album's single "Doctor Jackyll And Mister Funk" entered the UK singles chart on 7 July 1979, peaked at No. 53 and stayed four weeks — the only UK singles chart entry of Jackie McLean's entire career. — Source: https://www.officialcharts.com/artist/17830/jackie-mclean/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean
- AllMusic (Scott Yanow) calls the record "a set of electronic rhythms, dumb vocals... and unadventurous playing that is instantly forgettable," an "artistic (and commercial) fiasco." — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/monuments-mw0000868107
- Dusty Groove, by contrast, describes it as "a rare electric outing from legendary altoist Jackie McLean — and one that comes off surprisingly well." — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/5163/Jackie-McLean:Monuments
- Discogs files the album under Jazz and Funk / Soul, with styles Jazz-Funk and Disco. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/368801-Jackie-McLean-Monuments
- The horn section is a who's-who of New York studio brass: Urbie Green and Sam Burtis (trombone), Marvin Stamm and Marky Markowitz (trumpet/flugelhorn), Phil Bodner (flute), and "Blue Lou" Marini — an original Blues Brothers horn — on second alto. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2388286-Jackie-Mclean-Monuments and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Marini
- The rhythm section (Hiram Bullock, Will Lee, Steve Jordan, Clifford Carter, Mark Egan) is effectively the 24th Street Band, formed in New York in 1978; Bullock, Lee and Jordan went on to Paul Shaffer's World's Most Dangerous Band on *Late Night with David Letterman*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Bullock and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Shaffer_and_the_World%27s_Most_Dangerous_Band
- The album has had a long afterlife on the dance floor via its single: Carl Cox's "Dr. Funk" samples "Dr. Jackyll and Mister Funk." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/452227/Carl-Cox-Dr.-Funk-Jackie-Mclean-Dr.-Jackyll-and-Mister-Funk/
- The record has been reissued on CD only once, and only in Japan — Sony's 2016 "Crossover & Fusion Collection 1000" limited edition, SICJ 158. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/368801-Jackie-McLean-Monuments
- Discogs collectors hold 559 copies against 298 wants, with 46 copies for sale from $0.67 — a cheap record that is nonetheless actively wanted, which is the standard shape of a rediscovered "wrong turn" album. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/368801-Jackie-McLean-Monuments (figures as of August 2026)

#### Artist

- John Lenwood "Jackie" McLean was born 17 May 1931 in Harlem, New York City, and died 31 March 2006 in Hartford, Connecticut, aged 74. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean and https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jackie-McLean
- His father John played guitar in Tiny Bradshaw's orchestra and died in 1939, when Jackie was eight. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/122384-Jackie-McLean and https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jackie-McLean
- He grew up receiving informal tutoring from neighbours Thelonious Monk, Bud Powell and Charlie Parker. — Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jackie-McLean
- He made his recording debut at 20 on Miles Davis's *Dig* (1951), playing his own composition of the same name — a tune he had written at 17 or 18 and played for Davis at Birdland. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dig_(composition) and https://ethaniverson.com/interviews/interview-with-jackie-mclean-by-steve-lehman/
- Miles Davis "recorded almost everything he wrote," including "Little Melonae," which McLean dedicated to his daughter. — Source: https://www.milesdavis.com/track/little-melonae/
- He acted as well as played, appearing in Jack Gelber's play *The Connection* at the Living Theatre and in Shirley Clarke's 1961 film version. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/122384-Jackie-McLean
- He began teaching at the Hartt School, University of Hartford, in 1968, and co-founded the Artists Collective in Hartford with his wife Dollie McLean in 1970; he later founded the university's African American Music Department, now the Jackie McLean Institute of Jazz. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean
- *Monuments* was his last leader date for nine years. He did not record as a leader again until *Dynasty* (Triloka, 1988), with his son René McLean. — Source: https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/mclean-jackie
- He received an NEA Jazz Masters fellowship in 2001 and was elected to the *DownBeat* Hall of Fame in 2006, the year he died — one of very few musicians so honoured in their year of death. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean and https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/jackie-mclean
- He is buried at Woodlawn Cemetery in the Bronx, under black granite etched with a saxophone. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: empty. Against the book's subgenre definitions in `Matter/On Subgenres.md`:

1. **Jazz Funk — recommended.** This is the textbook case on all three of the book's tests. *By the players*: McLean is a Charlie Parker–lineage bebop and hard bop alto player with a Blue Note contract from 1959 to 1967, moving to a funk rhythm section for a major label — the book's own line about "jazz listeners hearing this shift as a sellout when their favorite artists from the 1950s and 1960s chased a bigger audience with a funky beat" describes the reception of this exact record. *By the music*: a jazz soloist out front of a locked funk rhythm section, mostly instrumental (the title track has no vocal at all), with horn and string arrangements rather than vocal choruses. *By the era*: 1979, the tail end of the decade the book assigns to jazz-funk. Discogs styles the release Jazz-Funk. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/368801-Jackie-McLean-Monuments, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_McLean
2. **Deep Funk — a defensible second.** The book defines deep funk as a commercial fate rather than a sound: overlooked on release, found later by crate diggers, and when on a well-known label, "from a poorly selling record with limited distribution." *Monuments* qualifies on the fate: written off by AllMusic as a commercial fiasco, never given a US CD reissue, reissued only once and only in Japan in 2016, and now carried by Dusty Groove and Sounds of the Universe as a rediscovery with 298 Discogs wantlists. The argument against is that the record is not obscure in the crate-digger sense — it came out on RCA with a charting UK single — so Jazz Funk is the cleaner call. Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/monuments-mw0000868107, https://www.dustygroove.com/item/5163/Jackie-McLean:Monuments, https://www.discogs.com/master/368801-Jackie-McLean-Monuments
3. **Soul Jazz — worth naming but not recommending.** JazzMusicArchives files *Monuments* as a soul jazz record, and McLean's own 1960s Blue Note work sits near that tradition. But the book reserves soul jazz for "the earlier, smaller-ensemble side of the story" — organ trios and quartets — and *Monuments* is a fourteen-piece studio production with a horn section, a string line and backing vocalists. That is the "full boil," not the "early simmer." Source: https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/album/jackie-mclean/monuments

Not recommended: **Electro Funk** (the book limits it to funk defined by its electronic texture; the Yanow review's "electronic rhythms" jab notwithstanding, this is a live studio band with real drums, bass, guitar, brass and violin) and **Disco** (not one of the book's subgenres, though Discogs tags the release that way).
