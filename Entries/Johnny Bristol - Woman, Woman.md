### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is empty.

1. **"A Motown man's women's-lib manifesto."** The Joint of the Day Substack reads "Woman, Woman" as a women's liberation anthem in which Bristol declares himself on the side of women's lib. That is a striking stance for a man who spent a decade writing Motown love songs. The angle is the gap between the album's sweet title single and its opening statement.
2. **"The producer steps out front."** Bristol was a behind-the-glass hitmaker. He co-wrote "Ain't No Mountain High Enough"-era Gaye/Terrell sides and "Twenty-Five Miles", and produced "Someday We'll Be Together". For his own debut he opened with a five-minute funk track instead of the hit. It's a story about what a producer does when he finally gets the mic.
3. **"The other side of the hit."** "Hang On In There Baby" went #8 pop, #2 R&B and #3 UK, but the album opens with "Woman, Woman", which a UK dealer calls an "underground monster". The angle is how hit albums hide their funk on side A, track 1.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

The `listening mission` field is empty.

1. "The album's big hit is a silky love song, but Bristol chose to open with this. Listen to the first minute: what does the groove tell you he wanted you to know about him before you heard the single?"
2. "H.B. Barnum's strings and horns sit on top of a hard-funk rhythm section. Listen for the moments when the orchestra gets out of the way. Where does the track feel most like a funk record and least like a Motown production?"
3. "The album closes with a 45-second 'Woman, Woman (Reprise)'. Listen to both. Why bookend a love-song album with this message?"

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Woman, Woman" opens the album (A1, 5:08). A short "Woman, Woman (Reprise)" closes it (B5), which frames the whole LP. Both are written solely by Bristol. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/89134-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby
- It has been described as an "ultra-funky", lyrically pointed women's liberation anthem, with horns, strings and synthesizers. — Source: https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/johnny-bristol-february-3-1939-march
- A UK record dealer singles it out as an "underground monster", alongside the title track, as the LP's highlights. — Source: https://ifmusic.co.uk/product/johnny-bristol-hang-on-in-there-baby
- Music is published by Bushka Music and Jobete Music Co. Jobete is Motown's publishing arm, so Bristol's songs still carried a Motown tie even on MGM. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/570919-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby

#### Album

- Producer: Johnny Bristol. Arranger: H.B. Barnum. Engineer: Greg Venable. Lacquer cut at Artisan Sound Recorders. US catalog number MGM M3G 4959. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/570919-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby
- Charted #7 R&B and #82 pop (album). The Hip-o Select reissue notes date it to July 1974. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8867889-Johnny-Bristol-The-MGM-Collection
- Album peaks per Wikipedia: UK #12, Australia #74, US #82, US R&B #7. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol
- The title single went US #8, R&B #2 and UK #3 (BPI Silver). It was covered by Bette Midler, Curiosity Killed the Cat (UK #3, 1992), Gary Barlow and Blue. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_On_in_There_Baby
- Bristol's original of "Love Me For A Reason" (B4) came before the Osmonds' hit version. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol
- Ed Greene is the only confirmed session musician (drums). — Source: https://sessiondays.com/2022/11/03/1974-johnny-bristol-hang-on-in-there-baby/
- The album earned Bristol a Grammy nomination for Best New Artist (he lost to Marvin Hamlisch). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol
- Japan reissued the album in the "Free Soul 20th Anniversary" series in 2014, a sign of its cult standing with crate-diggers. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/89134-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby (release 6910600)

#### Artist

- John William Bristol was born February 3, 1939, in Morganton, NC, and died March 21, 2004, in Brighton Township, MI. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/85762-Johnny-Bristol
- He met Jackey Beavers in the Air Force. As Johnny & Jackey they cut "Someday We'll Be Together" (1961, Tri-Phi). Bristol later produced it for Diana Ross & the Supremes as their final #1 (1969) and sings the male response on that record himself. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol ; https://encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/bristol-johnny
- He could not read or write music. — Source: https://encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/bristol-johnny
- He married Iris Gordy, Berry Gordy's niece. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol (the "niece" relationship is background knowledge; the page states only the marriage to Iris Gordy).
- He left Motown in 1973 for CBS, where he produced Boz Scaggs (*Slow Dancer*), Johnny Mathis, Jerry Butler and Tom Jones. He signed to MGM as an artist because CBS was lukewarm on his own records. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol ; https://northcarolinamusichalloffame.org/inductee-item/johnny-bristol/
- He was inducted into the North Carolina Music Hall of Fame. — Source: https://northcarolinamusichalloffame.org/inductee-item/johnny-bristol/

### Region Suggestions

The `region` field was empty and is now set to `US - California`.

1. **US - California (chosen).** After leaving Motown in 1973, Bristol worked out of Los Angeles for CBS and MGM. The album is described as "prime '70s L.A. R&B/pop". Confirmed drummer Ed Greene and the (unconfirmed) guitarist David T. Walker were L.A. session mainstays, and the lacquer was cut at Artisan Sound Recorders in Hollywood. — Sources: https://encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/bristol-johnny ; https://3g.y.qq.com/n/qqmusic/album/001BDYdw2TJXuv.html ; https://sessiondays.com/2022/11/03/1974-johnny-bristol-hang-on-in-there-baby/ ; https://www.discogs.com/release/570919-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby (the Hollywood location of Artisan is background knowledge; no source found in this pass)
2. **US - Michigan (alternative).** Bristol's identity as a writer and producer was forged at Motown in Detroit (1963–1973), the songs are Jobete-published, and he lived in Michigan in later life. Choose this if the author wants to frame him as a Motown export. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Bristol ; https://www.discogs.com/release/570919-Johnny-Bristol-Hang-On-In-There-Baby
3. **US - Southeast (weak).** He was born in Morganton, NC. That is his birthplace only and is not connected to this recording.
