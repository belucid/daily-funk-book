### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The cover that outgrew the original (for a new generation)."** This is the book's second visit to "Everybody Loves the Sunshine" (the Roy Ayers 1976 original is already an entry). Kuroda's 9-minute version has become an algorithm-era phenomenon and is, for millions of younger listeners, their first contact with the song. A theme about how a cover can become the "front door" to a classic. Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview
- **"First Japanese artist on Blue Note."** Kuroda made history as the first Japanese artist to cut a record for Blue Note, arriving via Kobe big bands, Berklee, The New School, and Brooklyn's Afrobeat circuit. A theme about the long geographic road into an American institution. Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview / https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
- **"Producer as bandleader."** Kuroda is the leader, but singer José James conceived and produced the record, put his own touring band behind the trumpet, and stepped up to the mic only for this Ayers cover. A theme about who really shapes a "debut." Source: https://www.bluenote.com/spotlight/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-ascends-with-rising-s/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The book also has Roy Ayers' 1976 original. Play them back to back: Ayers floats for four minutes, Kuroda stretches past nine. Where does Kuroda's version stop being a cover and start being a jam, and does the extra length earn itself?
- The leader here is the trumpeter, but the voice you hear is producer José James. Track how Kuroda's muted-and-open trumpet answers James' vocal line: who is really leading this track?
- This was cut by a trumpeter who spent six years in a New York Afrobeat band. Listen past the mellow surface for the Afrobeat pulse in Nate Smith's drums and the guitar. Can you hear West Africa inside a California summer song?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Kuroda's version runs 9:26 on vinyl, more than double the length of Roy Ayers' roughly 4-minute 1976 original. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5957688-Takuya-Kuroda-Rising-Son
- The lead vocal is by producer José James, not Kuroda; James otherwise stayed behind the board and sang only on this Ayers cover. — Source: https://www.bluenote.com/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-to-make-blue-note-deb/
- The track became a streaming phenomenon, cited in 2024 at roughly 23 million Spotify plays and 15 million YouTube views, and serves as most listeners' entry point to Kuroda. — Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview
- Rising Son contains two Roy Ayers compositions back to back: "Everybody Loves The Sunshine" (track 5) and "Green And Gold" (track 6). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5763051-Takuya-Kuroda-Rising-Son

#### Album

- Rising Son was Kuroda's Blue Note debut and made him the first-ever Japanese artist to record for the label. — Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview
- It was conceived and produced by vocalist José James, who put his own touring band behind Kuroda: Kris Bowers (keys), Solomon Dorsey (bass), Nate Smith (drums), Corey King (trombone), with Lionel Loueke on guitar. — Source: https://www.bluenote.com/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-to-make-blue-note-deb/
- The album reportedly hit #1 on Japan's jazz charts and was reissued on vinyl in 2024 for its 10th anniversary (with a Joe Armon-Jones remix), after Blue Note's exclusivity window closed. — Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview
- Blue Note describes Afrobeat rhythms as central to the record, reflecting Kuroda's six years in the NY Afrobeat band Akoya. — Source: https://www.bluenote.com/trumpeter-takuya-kuroda-to-make-blue-note-deb/

#### Artist

- Takuya Kuroda was born February 21, 1980, in Ashiya, Hyōgo (Kobe metro area), Japan, and followed his trombonist older brother into the local big-band scene. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
- He moved to the US in 2003, studied at Berklee, then earned his degree from The New School's jazz program in 2006 before settling in Brooklyn. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
- His early NY resume includes Junior Mance, Valery Ponomarev's big band, and Akoya Afrobeat, feeding the soul-jazz-meets-Afrobeat blend of Rising Son. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (recommended).** The track is a modern jazz-funk cut: a jazz trumpeter and a jazz band working a slow funk groove, and it directly covers the definitive 1970s jazz-funk record (the Ayers original is tagged Jazz-Funk in this book). Best continuity with the source material and the groove. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/714619-Takuya-Kuroda-Rising-Son
- **Nu-Funk.** A 2014 recording that revives and updates classic funk/soul-jazz with hip-hop-informed production and contemporary players. If the book wants to mark this as present-day funk rather than a 70s artifact, Nu-Funk is the strongest contemporary tag. — Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview
- **Soul Jazz.** Kuroda is frequently described as mixing post-bop with adventurous soul-jazz, and the mellow, groove-forward feel fits. A defensible secondary tag. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda

Recommendation: **Jazz-Funk** primary, with **Nu-Funk** as the strong "this is contemporary" alternative.

### Region Suggestions

- **Japan (set by author).** Kuroda's defining hook is being the first Japanese artist to record for Blue Note; he was born in Ashiya/Kobe. A national-origin framing that foregrounds his identity. — Source: https://hypebeast.com/2024/1/takuya-kuroda-rising-son-everybody-loves-the-sunshine-interview / https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuya_Kuroda
