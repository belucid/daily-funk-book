### Writing Theme Suggestions

The author's existing theme is: *"Most sellout backlash was directed at Jazz musicians, but blues musicians also crossed over and made funk in search of more commercial success."* Strong angle — Collins is a perfect case, since the Iceman's Telecaster here is bending toward a Meters-style pocket rather than a Texas blues shuffle.

Alternatives:

1. **"When Canned Heat handed the Iceman to Imperial."** The whole LP exists because Canned Heat discovered Collins in a Houston club in June 1968 and personally brokered the Imperial deal, with Bob Hite writing the liner notes. The album title itself is lifted from a Canned Heat lyric. That patronage story frames why a Texas blues guitarist suddenly sounds like he's sitting in at Cosimo's.
2. **"The Sissy was funk's first dance."** "Do the Sissy" belongs to a brief but deep lineage of funk records built around the Sissy dance (Rufus Thomas's "Sophisticated Sissy," the Meters' "Sophisticated Cissy" and "Cissy Strut"). The dance originated in Black gay communities before crossing over, and nearly every record named for it was funk.
3. **"Master of the Telecaster, apprentice of the pocket."** Collins's signature icy tone, altered F-minor tuning and capo trick, applied to a Meters-derived groove instead of slow blues. The entry could turn on how his guitar sounds "cold" even inside a funk arrangement.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Do the Sissy" opens Side One of *Love Can Be Found Anywhere (Even In A Guitar)* at 4:04. — Source: https://www.alligator.com/albums/Albert-Collins---Love-Can-Be-Found-Anywhere-Even-In-A-Guitar-Vinyl-LP/
- Also issued as a 45 backed with "Turnin' On" on Imperial 66391. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
- The track is explicitly modeled on the Meters, giving it a distinct New Orleans feel despite coming from a Texas bluesman recording in California. The broader "Sissy" dance family of records (Bob & Earl 1964, Rufus Thomas, the Meters' "Sophisticated Cissy" and "Cissy Strut") was one of funk's earliest dance crazes, and nearly all of its records were funk. — Source: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2011/06/sissy-variations-or-queen-of-funky.html
- Produced by Bill Hall. — Source: https://www.alligator.com/albums/Albert-Collins---Love-Can-Be-Found-Anywhere-Even-In-A-Guitar-Vinyl-LP/
- The Sissy dance reportedly originated in Black gay communities before crossing over into mainstream Black dance culture; the name was "wryly ironic." — Source: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2011/06/sissy-variations-or-queen-of-funky.html

#### Album

- The LP's unusual title was lifted from a lyric in Canned Heat's "Fried Hockey Boogie," chosen in honor of Canned Heat and their lead singer Bob Hite, who wrote the album's liner notes. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
- Alligator describes it as "one of those hard-edged gems from the early days that's a lot more funk than his later blues." — Source: https://www.alligator.com/albums/Albert-Collins---Love-Can-Be-Found-Anywhere-Even-In-A-Guitar-Vinyl-LP/
- Imperial catalog number LP-12428, released 1968. First of three albums Collins cut for Imperial (followed by *Trash Talkin'*, 1969, and *The Compleat Albert Collins*, 1970). — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/3285133-Albert-Collins-Love-Can-Be-Found-Anywhere-Even-In-A-Guitar and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins

#### Artist

- Albert Gene Collins, "the Master of the Telecaster" and "the Iceman," was born October 1, 1932 in Leona, Texas, and died November 24, 1993 in Las Vegas of metastasized lung cancer, aged 61. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
- His cousin was Lightnin' Hopkins, who played family gatherings when Albert was a child. Another cousin, Willow Young, taught him the open F-minor tuning (FCFA♭CF) he used with a capo for his entire career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
- In June 1968, Canned Heat caught Collins playing in Houston, got him an agent, and introduced him to Imperial Records in Los Angeles. He moved to California and recorded *Love Can Be Found Anywhere (Even In A Guitar)* as his Imperial debut that year. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
- Collins later won a Grammy in 1986 for *Showdown!* with Robert Cray and Johnny Copeland, and was a direct influence on Stevie Ray Vaughan and Jimmie Vaughan. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Collins
