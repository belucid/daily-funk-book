Lenny Kravitz and Saul Hudson, better known as Slash from Guns N' Roses, attended Beverly Hills High School together in the early 1980s. Friends in school, they built separate careers in the music industry and lost touch until a chance encounter at the 1990 American Music Awards. A couple months later, the old friends were on a train from New York to a studio in Hoboken, New Jersey. They brought two guitars, a gallon of vodka, and a plan to make a record together.

The resulting album, *Mama Said*, was released in 1991 and sold over 3 million copies worldwide. The intro to the lead single, "Always on the Run," is built around an existing guitar riff by Slash that hadn't found a place in any Guns N' Roses songs. Kravitz brought the funk to match Slash's rock, playing the drums, bass, and keyboards on the song. The horns were by Earth, Wind & Fire's horn section.

You probably know the mother behind all the 'my mama said...' lines in the song. The lyrics recount the maternal wisdom of Kravitz's mother, Roxie Roker. She was an actress who played Helen Willis on *The Jeffersons*. Roker died of breast cancer on December 2, 1995, four years after the song's release.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Two high school kids walk into a studio"** — The central origin story is irresistible: Kravitz and Slash attended Beverly Hills High School together (alongside Nicolas Cage), lost touch, locked eyes at the 1990 American Music Awards, and within months were on a train to Hoboken with a gallon of vodka and two guitars. The song exists because of that reunion. This theme frames the entry around fate, friendship, and the spark of creative chemistry.

2. **"Mama said, and he listened"** — The lyrics are a direct channel of Roxie Roker's maternal wisdom, making this track a love letter from a son to his mother. Roxie was Helen Willis on *The Jeffersons*, making Kravitz a kind of TV-era funk royalty before he ever picked up a guitar. She died of breast cancer in 1995, four years after the song's release. This theme opens a personal, emotional angle.

3. **"The riff Guns N' Roses couldn't play"** — Slash wrote the central riff intending it for GN'R, but drummer Steven Adler couldn't handle it. So Slash gave it away to Kravitz. There's something delicious about a funk-rock classic existing because a hard rock drummer couldn't keep up. This theme gives the entry a sharp narrative hook about the riff's unlikely journey.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- Slash originally wrote the riff for Guns N' Roses, but drummer Steven Adler had difficulty playing it. Slash gave it to Kravitz instead. By 2019, Slash gave a different version of the story, telling *The Guardian* he never actually presented it to GN'R because "it didn't seem appropriate," and that Kravitz simply asked to use the riff. — Source: [Ultimate Classic Rock](https://ultimateclassicrock.com/lenny-kravitz-slash-always-on-the-run/)
- Slash flew from London to New York on the Concorde after his last European tour date. They took a train to Hoboken, NJ, with a gallon of vodka and a bag of ice that was "slowly melting," and cut the track in a single day at Waterfront Studios. — Source: [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/news/lenny-kravitz-slash-collaboration)
- The lyrics are autobiographical: Kravitz said the song is about his mother's wisdom. "She would want me to slow down sometimes to think and take things in." — Source: [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/lenny-kravitz/always-on-the-run)
- The track peaked at #8 on Billboard's Modern Rock Tracks chart and #5 in the Netherlands (Dutch Top 40). — Source: [Wikipedia - Always on the Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Always_on_the_Run_(Lenny_Kravitz_song))
- On June 6, 1992, Kravitz joined Guns N' Roses onstage in Paris during the Use Your Illusion Tour to perform this song live. — Source: [Wikipedia - Always on the Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Always_on_the_Run_(Lenny_Kravitz_song))
- "Always On the Run" is Kravitz's third-most-played live song in his entire career. — Source: [Ultimate Classic Rock](https://ultimateclassicrock.com/lenny-kravitz-slash-always-on-the-run/)
- The track has been sampled by hip-hop artists including NATAS ("Get My Dick Out Yo Mouth") and DMN ("Aformaoriginalmental"). — Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Lenny-Kravitz/Always-on-the-Run/)

#### Album
- *Mama Said* was written and recorded during the collapse of Kravitz's marriage to Lisa Bonet (of *The Cosby Show*). He experienced depression and "a brief period of hibernation" before making the album. — Source: [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/tribute-celebrating-30-years-of-lenny-kravitz-mama-said)
- Kravitz produced the entire album himself and played most instruments: vocals, guitar, keyboards, bass, drums, and electric sitar. — Source: [Wikipedia - Mama Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Said_(album))
- The album also features Sean Ono Lennon (John Lennon's son) on piano on "All I Ever Wanted." — Source: [Wikipedia - Mama Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Said_(album))
- "It Ain't Over 'til It's Over," another single from the album, reached #2 on the Billboard Hot 100 and featured the Phenix Horns from Earth, Wind & Fire. — Source: [Wikipedia - Mama Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Said_(album))
- The album went platinum in the US (1.88 million units) and sold over 3 million copies worldwide. — Source: [Wikipedia - Mama Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Said_(album))

#### Artist
- Kravitz was born May 26, 1964, in New York City. His mother, Roxie Roker, played Helen Willis on *The Jeffersons* (1975-85). His father, Sy Kravitz, was an NBC news producer who knew Duke Ellington, Miles Davis, and other jazz legends. — Source: [Wikipedia - Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz)
- He is named after his uncle, Leonard M. Kravitz, a Korean War soldier who was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor in 2014 by President Obama after a decades-long campaign to correct racial and religious bias in military honors. — Source: [US Army](https://www.army.mil/article/122071/lenny_kravitz_honors_uncle_medal_of_honor_recipient)
- Kravitz attended Beverly Hills High School alongside Slash, Nicolas Cage, and Maria McKee. — Source: [Wikipedia - Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz)
- Saxophonist Karl Denson, who played on *Mama Said*, later co-founded the Greyboy Allstars, one of the key acid jazz/funk bands of the 1990s. Denson has said that when Kravitz "bid adieu to horns," Denson left rock and started his jazz career. — Source: [Wikipedia - Karl Denson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Denson)
- Kravitz won four consecutive Grammy Awards for Best Male Rock Vocal Performance (1999-2002), a record at the time. He has sold over 40 million albums worldwide. — Source: [Wikipedia - Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz)

### Genre Suggestions

The current genre is **Funk Rock**, which is a strong fit. Here are considerations:

1. **Funk Rock (current, recommended)** — This is the right primary label. The track is built on a heavy guitar riff from Slash, driven by a funk pocket rhythm section, with horn accents. It channels Led Zeppelin and Hendrix through a funk groove. Multiple sources describe it as "Led Zeppelin-esque" funk rock. The *On Genres* document doesn't yet have a full definition for Funk Rock, but the track exemplifies the fusion of rock guitar with funk rhythmic foundations. — Sources: [Albumism](https://albumism.com/features/tribute-celebrating-30-years-of-lenny-kravitz-mama-said), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Always_on_the_Run_(Lenny_Kravitz_song))

2. **Psychedelic Funk** — A secondary case could be made. Kravitz's self-production style, multi-instrumentalism, and the track's retro sonic palette (vintage gear, analog feel) connect to the psychedelic funk tradition. His influences include Hendrix and Sly Stone, both pillars of psychedelic funk. However, this track lacks the surreal/hallucinatory storytelling and invented-world qualities the book associates with psychedelic funk. — Source: [Wikipedia - Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz)

3. **Classic Funk** — A weak case. While the track has funk bones, it was released in 1991, well outside the 1968-1977 classic era, and its rock guitar dominance places it more comfortably in funk rock territory. — Source: Genre definitions in *On Genres & Regions*

**Recommendation:** Keep **Funk Rock** as the genre.
