### Writing Theme Suggestions

- "Answering the Crusaders": The album was explicitly conceived as Soul Media's response to the American group The Crusaders, whom Inagaki admired. "That's How I Feel" is itself a cover of a Crusaders tune (written by Crusaders bassist Wilton Felder), and the same LP also covers the Crusaders' "Put It Where You Want It." The entry could frame the track as a Japanese band absorbing and answering an American sound. (Source: https://www.hhv-mag.com/review/jiro-inagaki-soul-media-in-the-groove/?lang=en ; composer per https://www.shazam.com/song/1508946516/thats-how-i-feel)
- "Japan's own funk language": Inagaki and Soul Media built a Japanese jazz-funk that crate-diggers later prized. The hook: how a Tokyo saxophone veteran (formerly of the Crazy Cats) ended up making some of the most sampled, sought-after Japanese funk.
- "The cover that outshines the listing": A track filed plainly under "Jazz" on Discogs that functions as taut, drum-forward funk — an argument about how genre tags fail this music.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The existing mission focuses on the bass carrying the groove while the drums roam free. Alternative: "This is a cover of a Crusaders tune by a Japanese band. Listen for what feels distinctly un-American about the phrasing and the horn tone — and decide whether it sounds like imitation or translation."
- "Track the drums as a lead voice, not a timekeeper. Pick the single fill or accent that you'd most expect to hear from a horn instead."
- "At 3:35 this is unusually short for jazz-funk. Notice what gets sacrificed — is there room to solo, or is everything compressed into the groove?"

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- "That's How I Feel" is a cover; the composition is credited to Wilton Felder, bassist/saxophonist of The Crusaders — Source: https://www.shazam.com/song/1508946516/thats-how-i-feel
- It opens side A as track A1 of the LP and runs just 3:35, short for the jazz-funk idiom — Source: https://music.apple.com/co/album/in-the-groove/1508946305
- The track was uploaded to the Internet Archive in 2024 under the slug "thats-how-i-feel," used as the de facto title for the whole album rip — Source: https://archive.org/details/thats-how-i-feel

### Album
- "In The Groove" was conceived as a direct response to The Crusaders, whom Inagaki was closely following; the LP also covers the Crusaders' "Put It Where You Want It" alongside Joe Sample / B.B. King material — Source: https://www.hhv-mag.com/review/jiro-inagaki-soul-media-in-the-groove/?lang=en
- Recorded 1973; described as the step where Soul Media acquired a "sophisticated black feeling" en route to their landmark "Funky Stuff" (1975) — Source: https://www.hhv-mag.com/review/jiro-inagaki-soul-media-in-the-groove/?lang=en
- The album has become a crate-digger favorite, repeatedly reissued including a 2024 Record Store Day pressing and Mr Bongo vinyl editions — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/168250/Jiro-Inagaki-His-Soul-Media:In-The-Groove-white-vinyl-pressing-2024-Record-Store-Day-Release

### Artist
- Jiro Inagaki (born October 3, 1933, Tokyo; died January 18, 2024) was a veteran Japanese saxophonist who played with Frankie Sakai and Hajime Hana and the Crazy Cats before forming Soul Media in 1969 — Source: https://en.everybodywiki.com/Jiro_Inagaki
- He plays tenor and soprano sax and flute, occasionally alto; Soul Media spanned a 1968-1980 golden age of jazz, jazz-funk and jazz-rock — Source: https://wajazz.bandcamp.com/album/wajazz-legends-jiro-inagaki-selected-by-yusuke-ogawa-universounds
- Inagaki also scored films and TV soundtracks in Japan — Source: https://en.everybodywiki.com/Jiro_Inagaki

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz Funk** (strongest): Soul Media is funk played by a jazz bandleader and his sidemen, in the 1970s, with a soloist-supporting rhythm section — squarely the book's jazz-funk definition. Most reviews and Discogs filings call the record "jazz-funk." Source: https://jazznblues.club/viewtopic.php?t=53157 and book definition in Matter/On Subgenres.md
- **Soul Jazz**: A secondary case — the cover-tune basis and organ/sax-forward textures echo the soul-jazz lineage, though the book reserves soul jazz for the earlier, smaller-ensemble side, so jazz-funk is the better fit for a 1973 full-band cut.
- **Classic Funk**: Weak fit. The book's classic funk is the commercial American mainstream; this is a Japanese jazz-band recording, so it does not belong here despite its funky drive.
- Recommendation: `Jazz Funk`.
