### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in the entry. One question surfaced during research and is documented below: the precise meaning/origin of the title phrase "Look-Ka Py Py."

- Meaning of "Look-Ka Py Py": No authoritative source defines the phrase. The title track features wordless/scat vocal chanting rather than lyrics, and the phrase appears to be an onomatopoeic vocalization evoking New Orleans street-parade and second-line rhythm rather than a translatable word. Several writers note the meaning is simply unknown. I searched Wikipedia, AllMusic, Discogs, and general web sources; none provides a definitive etymology. Documented as: meaning unresolved; best interpretation is a nonsense/scat vocal phrase rooted in New Orleans street chant. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py and https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Look-Ka-Py-Py/

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- "When the groove is the lyric" — The title is a wordless scat phrase; the song says everything through the pocket, not through words. Angle: how the Meters made instrumentals that feel like songs.
- "Restraint as a superpower" — Critics single out the band's economy: brief, precise guitar; bass and drums leaving space. A case for what funk achieves by *not* playing.
- "The New Orleans counter-argument to James Brown" — Funk's other origin point. While Brown drove funk hard and tight from the front, the Meters built a looser, syncopated, second-line pocket. What makes New Orleans funk its own dialect?

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Focus on the "loose-tight axis": Zigaboo Modeliste's drums push slightly forward while Leo Nocentelli's guitar pulls back. Can you hear the two of them disagreeing about the beat, and why does that tension feel so good?
- This track has no words, just a chanted "Look-Ka Py Py." Listen for how the vocal functions as another percussion instrument rather than a melody. When does it enter, and what does it add that the drums don't?
- Count how few notes Art Neville's organ actually plays. What is he leaving out, and how does that space change the groove?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The title track reached #11 on the US R&B Singles chart, the Meters' second-biggest hit after "Cissy Strut." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py
- The album cut runs 3:15, but the 1969 7" single (Josie 45-1015) is an edited 2:55. Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/1018288 (single) and https://api.discogs.com/releases/389034 (LP)
- "Look-Ka Py Py" has been heavily sampled in hip-hop, including DJ Shadow's "Entropy" and Cypress Hill's "The Phuncky Feel One." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Look-Ka-Py-Py/sampled/

#### Album

- The album was recorded at LeFevre Sound Studios in Atlanta, Georgia, not New Orleans, and produced by Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py
- It is an all-instrumental record of 12 tracks, totaling about 32:33. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py
- It ranked #218 on Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time (2003), #220 (2012), and #415 (2020); Pitchfork gave it a 10/10. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py

#### Artist

- The Meters were the house band for Allen Toussaint's Sansu Enterprises, backing countless New Orleans sessions even as they cut their own hits. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- They are widely credited as co-originators of funk alongside James Brown, and were sampled by Run-DMC, N.W.A, Ice Cube and many others. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- The band received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2018; "Cissy Strut" entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2011. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Classic Funk**.

- **Classic Funk (keep)** — Released 1969/1970 on a well-distributed label (Josie), charting #11 R&B; it is funk's primary identity here, squarely within the book's 1969–1977 classic era. Strong fit. Source: On Subgenres.md; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look-Ka_Py_Py
- **Deep Funk (consider as secondary)** — Although it charted on release, the track's "delayed recognition" through crate-digging DJs and heavy hip-hop sampling matches the deep-funk arc. However, because it was a genuine R&B hit, Classic Funk is the better primary label. Source: On Subgenres.md; https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Look-Ka-Py-Py/sampled/
