I really wanted at least one Michael Jackson entry in this book, but it was not as easy as it sounds. Throughout his career, Michael ruled the pop and dance genres, but he never released a pure funk track.

I considered a couple songs from 1979's *Off the Wall*, like "Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough" or "Workin' Day and Night," but these are inside a disco-forward album. This leads us to today’s track, “Wanna Be Startin’ Somethin'’,” which was released as a single on May 9, 1983, and stands out as perhaps Michael’s funkiest moment. Jackson reportedly wrote it in 1978 for his sister La Toya Jackson. He then made a demo during the *Off the Wall* era, but it was left off only to be revived in 1982 for *Thriller*, the best-selling album of all time.

The reason I was so eager to include a Michael Jackson track is his vocal style. Outside of James Brown, few singers use staccato attacks, yelps, and rhythmic grunts so effectively as vocal percussion. Think about the first instant you recognize an impersonation of Brown or Jackson. It's not the words. It's the sounds.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current `writing theme` is: "Why Michael never made any pure funk music"

This is a strong theme and directly addressed in the entry body. Here are alternative angles:

1. **"The chant heard around the world"** — The "mama se, mama sa, mama coo sa" hook is borrowed from Manu Dibango's 1972 Cameroonian hit "Soul Makossa," creating a chain of lawsuits that stretched across three decades and three continents. This angle lets you write about how a vocal riff from Douala, Cameroon became the catchiest moment on the best-selling album of all time, and what that says about funk's relationship to African music.

2. **"The bathroom stomp board"** — During the Thriller sessions at Westlake Studios, Jackson, Nelson Hayes, and Steven Ray stomped on a four-foot by three-foot piece of plywood to add percussive texture to the track. Engineer Bruce Swedien recorded the results. This angle lets you write about Jackson's obsessive pursuit of rhythm and the handmade, analog grit hiding inside a pop blockbuster.

3. **"The song that almost belonged to La Toya"** — Jackson wrote the song in 1978 for his sister La Toya about her troubled relationships with her sisters-in-law. A demo was cut during the Off the Wall sessions but didn't make that album. Four years later, he re-recorded it for himself and it became Thriller's opening track. This angle lets you write about the gap between a song's origin and its destiny.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The song was originally written in 1978 for Michael's sister La Toya Jackson, about her troubled relationships with her sisters-in-law. Michael recorded a demo during the Off the Wall sessions but kept the track for himself, re-recording it four years later for Thriller. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanna_Be_Startin%27_Somethin%27
- The closing chant "mama se, mama sa, mama coo sa" is derived from Manu Dibango's 1972 Cameroonian hit "Soul Makossa." Dibango sued Jackson, and the case was settled out of court in 1986 for one million French francs. When Rihanna later sampled the same chant for "Don't Stop the Music" (2007), Dibango sued again in 2009, this time naming both Jackson and Rihanna. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanna_Be_Startin%27_Somethin%27
- The bassline is built on the same pattern as Cerrone's 1977 disco track "Supernature," consisting entirely of the notes D, E, and B. — Source: http://www.soundsjustlike.com/1857/michael-jackson-sounds-like-cerrone/
- The percussion includes a "bathroom stomp board," a four-foot by three-foot piece of plywood stomped on by Michael Jackson, Nelson Hayes, and Steven Ray in the studio. — Source: https://www.thriller40.com/music/wanna-be-startin-somethin/
- The track was nominated for a Grammy for Best R&B Song at the 1984 ceremony but lost to "Billie Jean," another Jackson/Thriller single. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanna_Be_Startin%27_Somethin%27
- "Wanna Be Startin' Somethin'" never had a music video during its original release; an official animated lyric video was not released until November 2022 for the Thriller 40th anniversary. — Source: https://www.michaeljackson.com/en-ca/news/wanna-be-startin-somethin-lyric-video-out-now/
- AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine called it "the freshest funk on the album [but] the most claustrophobic, scariest track Jackson ever recorded." — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/thriller-mw0000056882
- It was the fourth consecutive top-ten single from Thriller, peaking at #5 on the Billboard Hot 100 and reaching #1 in Canada and the Netherlands. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanna_Be_Startin%27_Somethin%27
- The song was scheduled to open the "This Is It" concerts in London in 2009, the concert series that never happened due to Jackson's death. — Source: https://jacksondynasty.net/2022/08/23/wanna-be-startin-somethin/

#### Album

- The album produced seven singles from its nine tracks and remains the best-selling album of all time with approximately 70 million copies sold globally. It is certified 34x Platinum in the US. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_(album)
- Jackson wrote only four of the nine songs: "Wanna Be Startin' Somethin'," "The Girl Is Mine," "Beat It," and "Billie Jean." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thriller_(album)

#### Artist
- Michael Jackson was born August 29, 1958, in Gary, Indiana, and died June 25, 2009, in Los Angeles from acute propofol intoxication. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson
- Louis Johnson of the Brothers Johnson played bass on the track. Johnson was known for his slap bass technique and played a Music Man StingRay bass that Leo Fender personally made for him. Johnson died May 21, 2015, at age 60. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Johnson_(bassist)
- Paulinho da Costa, the Brazilian percussionist on the track, is one of the most recorded percussionists in music history, with credits spanning from Ella Fitzgerald to Daft Punk. — Source: Background knowledge, but no source found.

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current value) — The strongest fit. The entry body makes the case that this is Michael's funkiest output, and AllMusic's Erlewine called it "the freshest funk on the album." The rhythm section (Louis Johnson's bass, David Williams' guitar, Paulinho da Costa's percussion) is rooted in classic funk vocabulary. The track was released in 1982, just past the classic funk era defined in this book (1968-1977), but the sound is closer to classic funk than any other category. The Cerrone bassline and the staccato vocal delivery both sit squarely in the funk tradition. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/thriller-mw0000056882

2. **Psychedelic Funk** — A weaker but defensible case. The track's layered, almost hallucinatory arrangement, its borrowed African chant, and its six-minute runtime give it a density and strangeness that echoes psychedelic funk's surreal, maximalist tendencies. However, there's no connection to the P-Funk universe or its aesthetic, so this feels like a stretch. — Background knowledge.

3. **Afrobeat** — Not recommended, but worth noting: the "Soul Makossa" chant and Paulinho da Costa's percussion give the track African and Latin rhythmic textures. However, the track is not built on an Afrobeat groove structure and has no connection to the West African Afrobeat scene. The African element is borrowed flavor, not foundational structure. — Background knowledge.

**Recommendation:** Keep Classic Funk. It is the most defensible choice and aligns with the entry's editorial angle.
