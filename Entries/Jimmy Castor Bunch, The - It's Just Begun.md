### Open Research Questions

No `open:` / `Open:` / `OPEN:` prompts were present in the entry body. One factual ambiguity worth flagging for the author: sources disagree on whether "It's Just Begun" was ever a charting single in its own right. Wikipedia's album infobox lists the title track's single "released 1970," but no source I found gives a chart position for the *single*; the charting hit from the LP was "Troglodyte (Cave Man)" (#6 Hot 100 / #4 R&B). The album itself charted (#27 Billboard 200 / #11 R&B / #21 Canada). I could not locate a 45cat/Discogs page confirming a US 7" of "It's Just Begun" b/w a specified flip on original 1972 RCA (45cat returned HTTP 403). Best current reading: "It's Just Begun" was primarily an album track that became famous later, not a hit single.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The existing theme** ("Repetition turning words into sound/rhythm") is well supported — the chant-like "It's just begun / it's just begun..." hook and the horn/vocal riffing lend themselves to the words-become-rhythm angle.
- **"The song that hip-hop built its body on"** — a case that this track's cultural life was almost entirely posthumous to its release: it became the definitive b-boy/breaking anthem a decade later, not a 1972 hit. The record's meaning was written by dancers and DJs, not the charts.
- **"The Everything Man's Latin engine"** — frame around Jimmy Castor's Afro-Latin roots (timbales, congas, Lenny Fridie Jr.'s percussion) and how a Harlem doo-wop/Latin-soul journeyman fused those textures into funk.

- Jimmy Castor plays sax and timbales here. Try to pick out the Latin percussion under the funk groove — where does the Afro-Cuban side of the "Everything Man" poke through?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "It's Just Begun" became a foundational b-boy/breaking anthem and was used in the breakdance scene of the film *Flashdance* (1983), cementing its status in hip-hop dance culture — Source: https://amara.org/videos/Ij485sN3BOr4/en/1550628/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Just_Begun
- The songwriting credit trio is Gerry Thomas, Jimmy Castor, and John Pruitt; the track was produced by Castor-Pruitt Productions — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Just_Begun and https://www.discogs.com/release/254129-The-Jimmy-Castor-Bunch-Its-Just-Begun
- Jimmy Castor re-cut the song as a solo 12"/7" in 1983 ("It's Just Begun" / "E-Man Boogie '83"), riding the breakdance revival — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/125674-Jimmy-Castor-Its-Just-Begun-E-Man-Boogie-83

#### Album
- Songs from the album have been sampled more than 100 times, making *It's Just Begun* one of the most sampled LPs in hip-hop — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Just_Begun
- The album charted #27 on the Billboard 200, #11 R&B, and #21 on the Canadian Album Chart — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Just_Begun
- The "Creation" prologue and epilogue that bookend the record used an orchestra of ~30 musicians, a lavish framing device around an otherwise lean funk band — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Just_Begun
- The other hit from the LP, "Troglodyte (Cave Man)," reached #6 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #4 R&B — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troglodyte_(Cave_Man)
- Original US pressings were mastered at RCA Victor Studios, Nashville, and pressed at RCA's Rockaway plant on Dynaflex vinyl — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/254129-The-Jimmy-Castor-Bunch-Its-Just-Begun

#### Artist
- Jimmy Castor was nicknamed "The Everything Man" / "E-Man"; born James Walter Castor, June 23, 1940, in Manhattan (Harlem), died January 16, 2012, in Henderson, Nevada — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Castor
- Sources conflict on his birth date (June 23, 1940 vs. other listed dates); per the NYT obituary his son said Castor deliberately let people assume he was younger, by as much as seven years — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Castor
- As a teenager Castor sang doo-wop and even briefly stood in for Frankie Lymon; "I Promise to Remember," which he co-wrote and which closes this album, was a Frankie Lymon & the Teenagers hit — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Castor
