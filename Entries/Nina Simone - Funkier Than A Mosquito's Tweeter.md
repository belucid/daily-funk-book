Two research questions surfaced and were resolved as far as sources allow:
- **Exact 1974 release month/day:** Not found. Discogs, AllMusic, and retail listings all give only the year 1974. Apple Music shows "January 1, 1974," which is a placeholder default, not a confirmed date. Documented as year-only.
- **Songwriter identity:** Credited on the record to Alline Bullock (spelled "Aillene Bullock" on Discogs), Tina Turner's sister. It is widely held that Ike Turner wrote the song and credited relatives; the current Wikipedia article attributes it plainly to Alline Bullock with no dispute noted. Flagged as a spelling/attribution discrepancy below rather than resolved definitively.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Nina out-funks the funk."** A jazz-and-protest artist takes an Ike & Tina B-side and, with African and Eastern percussion, makes what critics call the definitive version — a case study in a cover eclipsing the original.
- **"The title she changed."** The song was originally "Mosquita's"; Simone's 1974 release is why every later reissue reads "Mosquito's." An entry about how one recording rewrote a song's name.
- **"A last word for RCA."** Recorded live at Philharmonic Hall in 1973, released on Simone's final RCA album just as she was breaking with the US music industry and leaving the country — the funk as a parting shot.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Simone strips the horn-driven Ike & Tina arrangement down to voice, piano, and a bed of hand percussion (sitar, African and Eastern instruments). Listen for what carries the groove when there's no band vamp — where does the "funk" actually live here?
- Compare the opening line's delivery to Tina Turner's original. Simone is nearly conversational, almost menacing. At what moment does she stop singing to the "dirty old man" and start performing for the room?
- This is a live recording. Try to hear the audience and the hall around her — where does the take feel loose or unrepeatable in a way a studio cut wouldn't be?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The song's title was originally spelled "Funkier Than a Mosquita's Tweeter"; the "Mosquito's" spelling became standard only after Nina Simone's 1974 release. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkier_Than_a_Mosquito%27s_Tweeter_(song)
- Simone's version replaces the original band arrangement with "special rhythmic effects on a variety of African and Eastern instruments," and is often called the definitive reading and one of her finest vocal performances. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkier_Than_a_Mosquito%27s_Tweeter_(song)
- The lyric opens with the withering "you're nothin' but a dirty, dirty old man." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkier_Than_a_Mosquito%27s_Tweeter_(song)
- Simone began performing the song in 1971 and recorded this take at Philharmonic Hall, NYC, in July 1973. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkier_Than_a_Mosquito%27s_Tweeter_(song)

#### Album
- *It Is Finished* was Nina Simone's last album for RCA Records. — Source: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/nina-simone-it-is-finished-album-review/
- It is a live album drawn largely from a Philharmonic Hall performance, with sessions dated across 1971–1973 (RCA Studios and Philharmonic Hall, NYC); pressing was done in Indianapolis. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1437674-Nina-Simone-It-Is-Finished-
- Personnel include Harold Wheeler (conductor/arranger/co-producer), Nat Shapiro (co-producer), and Nadi Qamar on African/Eastern percussion, with Sam Waymon (Simone's brother-in-law) on organ and harmony vocals. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1437674-Nina-Simone-It-Is-Finished-

#### Artist
- The original of this song came from the Ike & Tina Turner camp, credited to Tina's sister Alline Bullock; it appeared on *Workin' Together* (1970) and was the B-side of "Proud Mary" in January 1971. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkier_Than_a_Mosquito%27s_Tweeter_(song)
- Nina Simone (born Eunice Kathleen Waymon) was a classically trained pianist from Tryon, North Carolina; *It Is Finished* comes near the end of her US recording career before she left the country. — Source: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/nina-simone-it-is-finished-album-review/
