You have options for how to hear today’s song, James Brown’s “Escape-ism.” You can start with the 3m 19s “(Pt. 1),” the A-side of the 1971 single. You can continue to “(Pt. 2)” for another 4m 10s. But to really hear how Brown drove his band, go for the 19m 10s “complete take.”

Just weeks before the April 8, 1971 session at King Studios in Cincinnati, brothers William “Bootsy” Collins on bass and Phelps “Catfish” Collins on guitar quit Brown’s band to join the more drug-friendly world of George Clinton’s Parliament-Funkadelic. Bootsy later said Brown’s disapproval of their drug use was a major reason for the split.

Brown plugged the gaps in his young band, and used the next studio session to lecture them through a one-chord vamp about “Escape-ism,” his code for drugs. Brown and the band were so talented that the lecture became a top 10 R&B hit.

By comparing the 19-minute track to the three-minute single, you can learn a lot about Brown’s creative process: he would record the band live, call out what he wanted as the groove mached on, then carve the best single from the result. The next best bits landed as the B-side. Getting to hear the whole extended jam is a rare treat.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current theme is "James Brown's relation to the band, and drugs". Strong angle. Three sharper variants worth considering:

- **"Lecturing the new guys."** A few weeks before this session, Bootsy and Phelps "Catfish" Collins quit Brown's band, with Bootsy later admitting LSD was a big reason ("LSD was a big part of why I left James Brown's band"). Brown plugs the holes with a fresh, partly-anonymous lineup, drags them into King Studios on April 8, 1971, and immediately lectures them on "Escape-ism" — code for drugs. The song is, almost literally, the speech he wishes he could have made to Bootsy. Source: https://liveforlivemusic.com/news/bootsy-collins-lsd-james-brown/
- **"Killing time becomes a hit."** Per Robert Christgau, the track was "supposedly cut to kill time until Bobby Byrd arrived" for what would become "I Know You Got Soul" (recorded the same day, April 8, 1971). A throwaway groove-and-talk became a #6 R&B hit, the first release on People Records, and a Bell Biv DeVoe sample twenty years later. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- **"One chord, nineteen minutes."** The aired single is just a slice. The 1992 CD reissue revealed the full 19:09 take: Brown forgetting Jimmy Parker's name on mic, quizzing the new band on their hometowns, riffing about cops and "dust," and never letting the one-chord vamp drift out of pocket. A study in how long a master can hold a single chord. Sources: https://www.kalamu.com/bol/2007/04/08/james-brown-%E2%80%9Cescape-ism%E2%80%9D-complete-take/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Pants_(album)

#### Track

- "Escape-ism" was reportedly cut to kill time at King Studios in Cincinnati on April 8, 1971, while the band waited for Bobby Byrd to arrive for the session that produced "I Know You Got Soul" (also recorded that day). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- The single (People Records 45-2500) was the very first release on Brown's own People Records label. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism
- The full 19:09 unedited take was unreleased until the 1992 CD reissue of Hot Pants, remixed from the original eight-track master by Joseph M. Palmaccio and Harry Weinger at PolyGram Studios that November. — Source: https://www.kalamu.com/bol/2007/04/08/james-brown-%E2%80%9Cescape-ism%E2%80%9D-complete-take/
- The lyric is built around two anti-drug catchphrases: "I ain't got no dust" (a denial of having PCP/angel dust) and the homily "when you forget that grits is groceries and that eggs is poultry, you lose your thing." — Source: https://www.kalamu.com/bol/2007/04/08/james-brown-%E2%80%9Cescape-ism%E2%80%9D-complete-take/
- On the long take, Brown forgets saxophonist Jimmy Parker's name on mic and quizzes the new band members about their hometowns — a reflection of how recently most of them had been hired. — Source: https://www.kalamu.com/bol/2007/04/08/james-brown-%E2%80%9Cescape-ism%E2%80%9D-complete-take/
- The single charted #6 R&B and #35 Pop in 1971. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism
- Bell Biv DeVoe's 1990 #3 pop smash "Poison" sampled "Escape-ism" alongside Brown's "Mother Popcorn." — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1147064/Bell-Biv-DeVoe-Poison-James-Brown-Escape-Ism/
- A live version of the track appears on Brown's 1971 live album Revolution of the Mind: Live at the Apollo, Volume III. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism

#### Album

The "Hot Pants" single itself was tracked at Starday-King Studios in Nashville on May 13, 1971. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Pants_(album), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Pants_(James_Brown_song)

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is **Classic Funk**. Strong fit and recommended to keep.

- **Classic Funk (recommended).** "Escape-ism" sits squarely in the 1969–1977 window, was released by a major commercial artist on a Polydor-distributed label, charted top-10 R&B, and is built on the most fundamental classic-funk move: a one-chord vamp riding "the One." It defines the vocabulary the book uses for the genre. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape-ism
- **Deep Funk (alternative for the long take only).** The 19:09 unedited take was effectively buried until 1992 — the kind of "delayed-recognition" story the book associates with deep funk. But because the *single* was a hit on release, the track as a whole doesn't fit deep funk's "commercial fate" criterion. Source: https://www.kalamu.com/bol/2007/04/08/james-brown-%E2%80%9Cescape-ism%E2%80%9D-complete-take/

No change recommended.

- **US - Southeast (current, defensible).** Brown was born in Barnwell, SC and based his operation out of Augusta, GA throughout this period. The book seems to consistently tag Brown's catalog to his Southeast home base. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown
