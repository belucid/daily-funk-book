### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were in the entry. Questions that came up during research:

- **Where exactly is Cannonball's voice?** Duke's own site says Blue Note let him use Adderley's voice "at the end" of the track. A search-result summary of the All About Jazz review describes it as Adderley introducing Duke at one of their shows. The AAJ page returned 403, so I could not read the exact wording. Both may be true: a stage introduction of Duke placed at the end. Confirm by listening. Sources: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html, https://www.allaboutjazz.com/face-the-music-george-duke-big-piano-music-review-by-aaj-staff
- **Which recording is the voice from?** Not stated anywhere I found. The 1971 *The Black Messiah* LP has an "Introduction" (0:50) and a "Monologue" (2:20) of Cannonball speaking, so those are likely candidates. Blue Note granting permission fits, because Adderley's Capitol catalog sat inside the EMI/Capitol group with Blue Note. That last point is my inference, not a sourced fact. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1262319-Cannonball-Adderley-The-Black-Messiah
- **Is "Part Two" a sequel or a remake?** No source says if Part Two reuses themes from the 1971 "The Black Messiah" or is a new piece. Discogs and Wikipedia credit both to Duke alone. Compare the two by ear.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The student finishes the sermon.** Duke wrote "The Black Messiah" as a 25-year-old sideman in Cannonball's band in 1971. Thirty-one years later, on his own label, he opened his album with "Part Two" and gave his late mentor the last word, using Cannonball's actual voice.
- **Face the music: going independent.** Duke left Warner Bros. in 2000, started BPM, and called this first BPM album "a turning point in my career." The opening track looks back to where he started while he steps out on his own.
- **Jazz-funk after jazz-funk.** A 2002 track by a 1970s jazz-funk architect, with a core band (Christian McBride, Little John Roberts, Jef Lee Johnson) picked for "spiritual continuity." It asks whether the 1970s pocket can be rebuilt in the 2000s.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- When Cannonball's voice shows up near the end, ask why Duke saved it for last instead of opening the album with it.
- Put on the 16-minute 1971 "The Black Messiah" by the Cannonball Adderley Quintet, then play Part Two. What did 31 years and his own label change in how Duke writes for a band?
- Duke said he wanted this album to feature his piano playing. Notice how often you hear acoustic piano against the funk rhythm section, and when the keyboards take over.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Duke called the track his tribute to his mentor Julian "Cannonball" Adderley, and he thanked Blue Note's Bruce Lundvall for permission to use "Cannon's voice at the end." — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- It is the album's opening track, runs 8:22, and is credited to George Duke alone. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/283433-George-Duke-Face-The-Music
- "Part One" is Duke's "The Black Messiah," the 16:12 title track of Cannonball Adderley's 1971 double LP, recorded live at the Troubadour in West Hollywood, August 3 to 9, 1971. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Messiah
- On the 1971 version, Duke "intros the creative vamp as Cannonball rolls in with an explosive sax solo." — Source: https://www.makingascene.org/cannonball-adderley-black-messiah-live-troubador-1972-2-cd
- Players on the track (album-wide credits): George Duke (keyboards), Jef Lee Johnson (guitars), Christian McBride (bass), Little John Roberts (drums), Lenny Castro (percussion), Everette Harp (alto sax), Dan Higgins (tenor sax), Isaac Smith (trombone), Oscar Brashear (trumpet, flugelhorn). Discogs does not give per-track personnel. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3115798-George-Duke-Face-The-Music

#### Album

- *Face the Music* was the first release on Duke's own label, BPM (Big Piano Music). He called it "definitely a turning point in my career." — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- Duke used one core group throughout for "spiritual continuity," centered on Christian McBride, Little John Roberts, and Jef Lee Johnson. — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- It peaked at No. 4 on Billboard's Top Contemporary Jazz Albums chart, No. 7 on Top Jazz Albums, and No. 24 on Top Independent Albums. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Music_(George_Duke_album)
- It includes a cover of James Brown's "Ain't It Funky Now." Duke says it grew out of a jam about James Brown, with Jef Lee Johnson starting the groove. — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- "Ten Mile Jog" was cut down from a jam of about 45 minutes to roughly 11 minutes, with Airto and Sheila E. — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- It was recorded and mixed at Le Gonks West, Duke's studio in Los Angeles, by Erik Zobler, and mastered by Doug Sax and Robert Hadley at The Mastering Lab. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3115798-George-Duke-Face-The-Music
- JazzTimes called it "zesty, free-spirited, funk-fusion jams," and AllMusic's Jonathan Widran gave it 4 of 5 stars. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Music_(George_Duke_album)
- Cross-reference: "George Duke - Let's Roll" is also from this album and is already in the book's entries (status `research-review`).

#### Artist

- George Duke was born January 12, 1946, in San Rafael, California, and died August 5, 2013, in Los Angeles, of chronic lymphocytic leukemia. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- He spent about two years in Cannonball Adderley's band, and moved between Adderley and Frank Zappa's Mothers of Invention. He appears on nine Zappa albums. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- In 2000 he left Warner Bros. and launched BPM (Big Piano Music). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Music_(George_Duke_album)
- He was also a major producer for Anita Baker, Gladys Knight, The Pointer Sisters, Smokey Robinson, and Take 6. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- Cannonball Adderley was born September 15, 1928, in Tampa, Florida, and died August 8, 1975, in Gary, Indiana, after a stroke. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_Adderley

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz Funk (primary):** By players, Duke is a jazz pianist from the Adderley and Zappa bands who became one of jazz-funk's main figures. By the music, an 8-minute instrumental with a horn section and a funk rhythm section fits. Critics described the album as "funk-fusion jams" (JazzTimes, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Music_(George_Duke_album)), and Discogs styles it "Fusion, Jazz-Funk" (https://www.discogs.com/master/283433-George-Duke-Face-The-Music). The only problem is era: the book places jazz-funk in the 1970s, and this is 2002. But this is a 1970s architect deliberately continuing a 1971 piece with players chosen for continuity, so the label is defensible.
- **Nu-Funk (secondary, weak):** The era fits (post-mid-1990s), but nu-funk in the book means retro bands imitating 45s, and Duke is an original player, not a revivalist. Mention mainly to rule it out.
- **Soul Jazz (not recommended):** The roots in Cannonball's soul-jazz band are real, but the large ensemble and electric palette are past the book's small-group soul-jazz definition.

### Region Suggestions
