Patrice Rushen, the piano prodigy behind today's track, drew more than her share of sellout accusations when she moved from straight jazz into jazz-funk. Some of that no doubt reflected a familiar double standard. As a woman, she was an easier target. More generously, some of it may have come from the hopes jazz purists placed on her as a gifted young player, one they imagined might breathe new life and vigor into the genre as an older generation faded.

It was unfair to expect Rushen to stay pure while so many of her elders were already chasing larger financial rewards. Thankfully, she ignored her critics and made some of the funkiest jazz-funk records of the era, then went on to score disco hits as well, including 1982’s “Forget Me Nots,” a strong candidate for the last great disco hit.

Born on September 30, 1954, Rushen was only 23 when today’s track, “[track title],” was released. She began playing piano at three, gave recitals by six, and performed at the Monterey Jazz Festival at seventeen. She signed with the storied jazz label Prestige at twenty and released three albums there before moving to Elektra to continue on in a more commercial direction. Rushen later built a wide-ranging career as a music director, composer for film, television, and the concert hall, and as a forceful advocate for music education.
