### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current writing theme is: "Perennial nature of an anti-war song, Beatles influence on this."

**Suggestion 1: "The ghost of Mayfield"**
Curtis Mayfield left The Impressions in 1970, but he wrote, produced, and shaped "Stop the War" from the other side of the glass. The same year this album dropped, Mayfield released the Super Fly soundtrack and became one of the biggest names in American music. This entry could explore the strange dynamic of an absent leader still pulling the strings, and what it meant for the remaining Impressions to deliver his message while he soared as a solo artist.

**Suggestion 2: "The roommate connection"**
Leroy Hutson, Mayfield's replacement, was Donny Hathaway's college roommate at Howard University. Hutson co-wrote Hathaway's first hit "The Ghetto" before joining The Impressions. This is a tight web of Chicago soul talent, and the entry could highlight how a dorm-room friendship helped maintain The Impressions' lineage.

**Suggestion 3: "Fred Cash steps forward"**
Fred Cash, usually the harmony anchor, takes lead vocal on "Stop the War," delivering the most politically direct lyric on the album. The entry could explore what happens when the background voice steps to the front on the heaviest song.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Stop the War" is the album opener and at 6:25, it is the longest track on the record, nearly twice the length of most other cuts. — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/album/times-have-changed/111235456
- Fred Cash, who normally sang harmony, takes lead vocal on this track, delivering the most overtly political lyric on the album. — Source: https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/the-impressions/
- The track is described as featuring "urgent delivery over restrained yet expressive guitar and brassy flourishes." — Source: https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/the-impressions/
- Curtis Mayfield wrote and produced the track despite having left the group two years earlier. He was simultaneously working on the Super Fly soundtrack, which was released in July 1972 and became one of the best-selling albums of the year. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Fly_(soundtrack)
- The song reportedly incorporates the anti-war protest chant "Hell no, we won't go." — Background knowledge; source found via search result snippet from peace history site, but could not be verified from direct page content.

#### Album
- *Times Have Changed* (Curtom CRS 8012) was the only Impressions album to feature Leroy Hutson as lead singer. He stayed with the group for approximately two-and-a-half years before leaving for a solo career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_Hutson
- The album includes a cover of Marvin Gaye's "Inner City Blues (Make Me Wanna Holler)," arranged by Hutson with expanded string lines. Six of the eight tracks were Mayfield compositions. — Source: https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/the-impressions/
- Riley Hampton, a key Chicago arranger who also worked on Mayfield's solo debut *Curtis* (1970), arranged "Stop the War." Hampton and Johnny Pate were Mayfield's go-to arrangers, praised for blending strings and funk. — Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/curtis-mayfield/curtis-19/ and https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/the-impressions/
- The album charted at #192 on the Billboard 200. — Source: https://www.bsnpubs.com/buddah/curtom.html
- The album came with a poster in its original pressing. — Source: https://counterhoop.com/the-impressions-times-have-changed-1972-nm-poster-curtom-crs8012-curtis-mayfield/

#### Artist
- The Impressions began as The Roosters in Chattanooga, Tennessee, with Sam Gooden, Richard Brooks, and Arthur Brooks, before moving to Chicago and adding Jerry Butler and Curtis Mayfield. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Impressions
- Leroy Hutson was Donny Hathaway's roommate at Howard University. Hutson co-wrote "The Ghetto," giving Hathaway his first hit in early 1970. When Mayfield left The Impressions, the Hathaway/Hutson/Mayfield connection helped Hutson land the replacement role. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_Hutson
- Hutson originally enrolled at Howard to study dentistry before switching to music theory and composition. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_Hutson
- Curtom Records was founded by Curtis Mayfield and manager Eddie Thomas in March 1968. The name is a portmanteau of "Curtis" and "Thomas." It was one of the first independent American labels owned by a Black artist. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtom_Records
- The Impressions were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1991 and the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2003. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Impressions
- Curtis Mayfield was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame a second time as a solo artist in 1999, making him a double inductee. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_Mayfield
- After Hutson left in 1973, Ralph Johnson and Reggie Torian replaced him, and The Impressions had three R&B top-5 singles in 1974-1975, including "Finally Got Myself Together (I'm a Changed Man)" which hit #1 R&B. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Impressions
- Rolling Stone once labeled Leroy Hutson "the best-kept secret of Seventies soul." — Source: https://soultracks.com/birthday-leroy-hutson/

### Genre Suggestions

**Suggestion 1: Classic Funk**
The strongest fit. "Stop the War" was released in 1972 on a major independent label (Curtom, distributed by Buddah), by a group with significant chart history and commercial presence. The track has a funk groove with politically conscious lyrics, fitting squarely into the 1968-1977 classic funk era as defined in the book. Mayfield's production brings fuzz guitar and a heavy rhythm section into a commercially oriented framework. — Source: Musical description from https://jazzrocksoul.com/artists/the-impressions/

**Suggestion 2: Deep Funk**
A secondary option. While The Impressions were a well-known group, *Times Have Changed* charted at only #192, and the album has been largely forgotten compared to their 1960s work and the post-Hutson comeback. The track's rediscovery has come through crate-digging and compilation culture rather than its original commercial impact. However, the group's name recognition works against a "deep funk" classification. — Source: Chart position from https://www.bsnpubs.com/buddah/curtom.html

**Suggestion 3: Soul Jazz**
A weaker but arguable option. The Impressions' roots are in gospel-influenced Chicago soul, and the track's extended 6:25 runtime, horn arrangement, and improvisational feel have some jazz-adjacent qualities. However, the production is more funk-forward than jazz-influenced, making this the least convincing of the three options.
