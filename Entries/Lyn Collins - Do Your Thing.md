### Open Research Questions

No author `open:` items were present in this entry. Residual questions from my research:

- **Which night at the Apollo?** The 1988 CD liner notes say the take was recorded September 14, 1972; the 2016 *Live At The Apollo Volume IV* vinyl notes say the stand was recorded September 13-14, 1972. Whether the Funky People take and the Apollo IV take are the same performance (differently edited: 3:58 vs 3:50) or takes from different nights is not documented in any source I found. SecondHandSongs (https://secondhandsongs.com/performance/90571) lists September 14, 1972 as the recording date but returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching — author can verify in a browser.
- **Is there a studio version?** I found none. The track is not on *Think (About It)* (1972) or any People Records single — Discogs searches for Lyn Collins singles/masters containing "Do Your Thing" return only the live recordings and compilations. Steps taken: Discogs API searches by artist+track (master and 7" release types), full tracklist pulls of the *Think (About It)* album master (https://www.discogs.com/master/52645-Lyn-Collins-Think-About-It) and single master (https://www.discogs.com/master/215092-Lyn-Collins-Think-About-It). Conclusion: live-only, first issued 1988.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **Sixteen years in the vault.** A 1972 Apollo performance nobody heard until February 1988 — the exact moment hip-hop's golden-age samplers were hungriest for JB-camp grooves, and the same year "It Takes Two" turned Lyn Collins into the most sampled woman in music. The performance waited until the world it would feed existed. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/853886-Various-James-Browns-Funky-People-Part-2, https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/
- **Covering the rival.** In September 1972 Isaac Hayes was the newly Oscar-crowned king of soul; here James Brown's revue claims Hayes' hit for its own stage, with Brown's protégée compressing a 19-and-a-half-minute Stax epic into a four-minute funk sermon. A turf war fought by cover version. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaft_(Isaac_Hayes_album), https://getondown.com/products/james-brown-funky-people-part-2-2-lp
- **The understudy's night.** Collins mailed Brown a demo tape and waited years for her slot, finally becoming his top female singer after Vicki Anderson left. This recording catches her at the Apollo, months after "Think (About It)," proving she belonged. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Isaac Hayes stretched "Do Your Thing" past nineteen minutes on *Shaft*; Lyn Collins gets it done in under four. What did she keep, what did she throw overboard, and does the song miss any of it?
- This is a slow, smoldering groove played live, where every band fights the urge to rush. Listen for the moments the J.B.'s lean back instead of pushing forward — who in the band is holding the tempo down?
- Brown called her "The Female Preacher." Track the sermon mechanics: where does she talk, where does she sing, and can you find the exact moment the line between the two disappears?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- It is a cover of Isaac Hayes' "Do Your Thing" from the *Shaft* soundtrack (Enterprise, July 23, 1971), where the album version sprawls to 19:30; Hayes' single edit (January 1972) reached the Billboard Hot 100 Top 40 (#30). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaft_(Isaac_Hayes_album)
- The Lyn Collins version was recorded live at the Apollo Theater, New York, September 13-14, 1972, and sat unreleased for sixteen years until *James Brown's Funky People (Part 2)* (Polydor, February 12, 1988). — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1398854-Various-James-Browns-Funky-People-Part-2, https://www.discogs.com/release/853886-Various-James-Browns-Funky-People-Part-2
- Get On Down's reissue copy calls it a "slow, smoldering cover" and says of the parent compilation that "just about every note heard in this incredible collection has been used on not one, but multiple rap classics." — Source: https://getondown.com/products/james-brown-funky-people-part-2-2-lp
- It has been sampled by Rakim on "The 18th Letter (Always and Forever)" and by Miami bass DJ Uncle Al on "15th Ave," among others. — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/136138/Rakim-The-18th-Letter-(Always-and-Forever)-Lyn-Collins-Do-Your-Thing/, https://www.whosampled.com/sample/188983/DJ-Uncle-Al-15th-Ave-Lyn-Collins-Do-Your-Thing/ (WhoSampled returns 403 to automated fetching; links verified via search listings)
- A second, shorter edit (3:50) of the Apollo performance appeared in 2016 on *Get Down With James Brown: Live At The Apollo Volume IV*, sequenced between Danny Ray's introduction of Collins and her seven-minute live "Think (About It)" — a set where she also covers the Staple Singers' "I'll Take You There." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8393298

#### Album

- *James Brown's Funky People (Part 2)* (Polydor, released February 12, 1988) compiles People Records-era artists — Bobby Byrd, Marva Whitney, Maceo & The Macks, Fred Wesley & The JB's, Myra Barnes — and landed squarely in hip-hop's golden age of sampling. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/853886-Various-James-Browns-Funky-People-Part-2, https://getondown.com/products/james-brown-funky-people-part-2-2-lp
- The compilation opens with Bobby Byrd's "I Know You Got Soul" — the subject of another entry in this book; the two entries are catalog siblings. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/52693-Various-James-Browns-Funky-People-Part-2
- The full Apollo stand that produced this track was shelved for 44 years, finally issued for Record Store Day 2016 as a vinyl release limited to 2,400 copies. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8393298

#### Artist

- Born Gloria Lavern Collins, June 12, 1948, in Texas; she mailed James Brown a demo tape and waited years for her promised spot, becoming his top female vocalist after Vicki Anderson departed. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Collins, https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/
- Brown nicknamed her "The Female Preacher" for her exhortatory vocal style; "Mama Feelgood" (1973) became a second nickname. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/
- She is described as "by far the most sampled female artist of all time, with portions of her recordings used in well over 3,500 songs," most famously the "Think (About It)" breaks behind Rob Base & DJ E-Z Rock's "It Takes Two" (1988). — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Collins, https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/
- "Think (About It)" (1972) reached #66 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #9 R&B. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Collins
- She died March 13, 2005, in Pasadena, California, at 56, from a seizure related to cardiac arrhythmia. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Collins
- Caution for fact-checkers: despite the shared surname (and one search snippet claiming kinship), no reliable source documents any family relation between Lyn Collins and Bootsy/Catfish Collins; uDiscoverMusic's profile makes no such claim. Treat any "related to Bootsy" assertion as unsupported. — Source: absence of claim in https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/lyn-collins-mama-feelgood/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyn_Collins

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** — Strongest suggestion. A 1972 performance by James Brown's revue with the J.B.'s, dead center in the book's 1969-1977 classic window, on the major-label (Polydor) commercial pipeline; the track's primary identity is funk, not a fusion. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/8393298 (Genres: Funk/Soul), Matter/On Subgenres.md
2. **Deep Funk** — Strong alternate. The book defines deep funk by "delayed recognition," and this take was literally unreleased for sixteen years, surfacing in 1988 expressly for the crate-digging era and then feeding samples to Rakim and Miami bass. The case against: it's a James Brown-organization recording, not an obscure regional pressing. — Sources: Matter/On Subgenres.md, https://getondown.com/products/james-brown-funky-people-part-2-2-lp
3. **Gospel Funk** (weak) — Her "Female Preacher" delivery gives the performance a churchy charge, but the lyric is secular self-determination, not praise. Not recommended.
