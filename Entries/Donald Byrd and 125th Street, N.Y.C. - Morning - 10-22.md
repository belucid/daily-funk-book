Grammy-winning jazz musician, composer, and arranger Clare Fischer was born October 22, 1928, in Durand, Michigan. He moved to Hollywood in 1962 and worked in the Wrecking Crew-era LA session ecosystem.

Today’s track, “Morning,” is a Fischer composition that started life as a cha-cha on his 1965 Afro-Cuban album *Manteca!* This version strips away the Latin percussion and drops his Fender Rhodes into a jazz-funk groove. Fischer has a lighter touch than many on the instrument, and he shows his range across the rest of the album too, including synths, acoustic piano, and even some alto saxophone.

Fischer first worked with Donald Byrd as an arranger in the late 1950s. Twenty years later they reunited for *Donald Byrd and 125th Street, N.Y.C.*, which, despite the title, was recorded at The Sound Factory in Hollywood. Fischer later did most of Prince’s arranging from 1985 until Prince’s death in 2016.

**1. "The twenty-two year reunion"**
Clare Fischer and Donald Byrd first worked together in 1957, when Fischer arranged an album of standards with strings for Byrd. That album sat unreleased for twenty-five years. Over two decades later, Fischer composed, played, and arranged on "Morning" for Byrd's Elektra debut. The theme of a creative partnership that kept circling back could frame the whole entry.

**2. "When the bandleader steps aside"**
Byrd built a career as a trumpet virtuoso, but on "Morning" he cedes the floor entirely to Clare Fischer's Fender Rhodes. This fits a broader late-career pattern: on the Elektra albums, Byrd "contented himself with playing as part of a sleek if still funky ensemble." The entry could explore why a star instrumentalist would choose to disappear.

**3. "A Latin standard goes funk"**
Fischer's "Morning" started life as a cha-cha-cha on his 1965 Afro-Cuban album *Manteca!*, built on a standard 32-bar A-A-B-A form. Byrd's version strips the Latin percussion and drops the tune into a jazz-funk pocket, transforming it completely. The theme could explore what happens when a composition changes genre homes.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Morning" is the only track on the album not written or co-written by Donald Byrd. It is the sole Clare Fischer composition on the record. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd_and_125th_Street,_N.Y.C.
- Fischer's original 1965 recording of "Morning" on *Manteca!* contains a hidden Stravinsky quotation: halfway through trombonist Gil Falco's solo, the music morphs into a 16-bar development of the principal motif from "Spring Rounds," the fourth section of *The Rite of Spring*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_(Clare_Fischer_composition)
- The track was sampled by MC Eiht on "Got Cha Humpin'" (1997, from *Last Man Standing*). — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/87795/MC-Eiht-Got-Cha-Humpin'-Donald-Byrd-125th-St,-N.Y.C.-Morning/
- "Morning" has been described as "a Fender-Rhodes e-piano heaven" and "a Jazz-Funk gem and the highlight of this release." — Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/donald-byrd/donald-byrd-and-125th-st-n_y_c/
- Fischer's original "Morning" was his most famous contribution to the cha-cha-cha genre. Notable cover versions include recordings by Sergio Mendes, Cal Tjader, Tito Puente, Poncho Sanchez, Dianne Reeves, and Jeanie Bryson. Jose Jose recorded a Spanish-language version titled "Una Mañana" in 1969 with different lyrics by Joaquin Prieto. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_(Clare_Fischer_composition)

#### Album
- The album cover features a painting by Ernie Barnes, the former NFL offensive lineman turned painter best known for "The Sugar Shack" (featured on the TV show *Good Times*). Barnes also painted covers for Curtis Mayfield's *Something to Believe In* and The Crusaders' *Ghetto Blaster*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Barnes
- The album was produced by Donald Byrd himself, credited as "Blackbyrd Productions Inc." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd_and_125th_Street,_N.Y.C.
- Clare Fischer is credited on an extraordinary range of instruments across the album: organ, Yamaha EX-42, ARP Pro Soloist synthesizer, Fender Rhodes, Dyno My Piano, acoustic piano, and alto saxophone. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd_and_125th_Street,_N.Y.C.
- The album was arranged by Motown veteran Wade Marcus, with Byrd contributing arrangements on some tracks. — Source: https://theseconddisc.com/2017/04/13/love-byrd-big-break-collects-funky-jazz-soul-of-donald-byrds-complete-elektra-years/
- The album received mixed critical reception. AllMusic users rate it 4.2/10. Some reviewers criticized disco influences and mechanical drumming, while others praised the jazz-funk tracks. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/donald-byrd-and-125th-street-nyc-mw0002214265

#### Artist
- Donald Byrd's full birth name was Donaldson Toussaint L'Ouverture Byrd II, named after Haitian revolutionary leader Toussaint L'Ouverture. His father was a Methodist minister. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd
- Byrd mentored a young Herbie Hancock in New York, taking him "under his wing" and giving him crucial business advice: retain your publishing rights rather than surrendering them to Blue Note. That advice paid off enormously when Hancock's "Watermelon Man" became a hit. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd
- Fischer and Byrd's relationship stretches back to 1957, when Fischer arranged an album of standards with strings for Byrd. The session was done for Warner Brothers but went unreleased for 25 years. However, Byrd played his copy for Dizzy Gillespie, who was so impressed he hired Fischer to arrange *A Portrait of Duke Ellington*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Fischer; https://www.dustygroove.com/item/372654/Donald-Byrd:September-Afternoon-Donald-Byrd-With-Clare-Fischer-Strings
- At Howard University in 1973, Byrd co-produced The Blackbyrds from his own students, a fusion group that had hits with "Happy Music" and "Walking in Rhythm." *Black Byrd* (1973) became Blue Note's best-selling album for many years. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd
- At North Carolina Central University, Byrd helped establish the first bachelor's degree program in Jazz Studies in North Carolina in 1977. One of his students, Aaron Mills, went on to play bass in Cameo and later collaborated with OutKast and Cee Lo Green. Another student, keyboardist Chip Crawford, became the accompanist for jazz vocalist Gregory Porter. — Source: https://www.ncarts.org/blog/2019/04/12/when-byrd-flew-north-carolina-central-university
- Byrd earned a J.D. in 1976 and a Ph.D. in music education in 1982, in addition to his earlier B.M. from Wayne State University and M.M. from the Manhattan School of Music. He was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2000. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Byrd
- Clare Fischer won three Grammys, including Best Latin Recording for *2+2* (1981). He provided orchestral arrangements for Prince across multiple albums and soundtracks. Herbie Hancock cited Fischer's Hi-Lo's vocal arrangements as foundational to his own harmonic approach. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Fischer
