*Secrets* was Herbie Hancock's sixth record for Columbia. Released on August 15, 1976, it topped *Billboard's* jazz album chart and was #8 on the R&B chart. It's a banger.

Melvin Ragin, better known as Wah Wah Watson, is all over today’s song, "Doin' It." Watson made a literal name for himself by making a guitar talk through a wah-wah pedal. Here, he also handles the recurring vocal chant, reshaping his voice with a talk box. His work earned him co-writing and co-producing credits on the track.

Watson was a Funk Brother, playing in Motown's house band on classic sessions like "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone." From there, he became one of Hancock’s key funk-era collaborators, co-writing and recording with him from 1974 to 1980.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The Funk Brother in the Headhunters' chair.** "Doin' It" is essentially Wah Wah Watson's job interview as Hancock's new guitar foil and co-writer. Watson, fresh from Motown's Funk Brothers and "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone," reshapes the Headhunters band away from free-wheeling fusion toward a tighter, more song-shaped funk. Watson is even credited with bass and lead vocals on this track, an unusually large footprint for a guest. Sources: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson ; https://www.guitarplayer.com/news/watch-funk-guitar-master-wah-wah-watson-groove-with-herbie-hancock
- **The instrumental that almost wasn't.** Most of the album was tracked at Wally Heider's, but "Doin' It" alone was cut at the Columbia studio across town and is the only piece on the record with anything like a real vocal hook ("just keep on doin' it"). A frame for how much of Hancock's mid-70s identity hinged on whether he was chasing a single or a side-long groove. Sources: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- **Two Detroit guitarists, one San Francisco room.** The track's writing credit reads Hancock / Ragin / Ray Parker Jr., pairing two future Hollywood Walk of Fame guitarists, both Detroit-rooted session players, in Hancock's San Francisco fusion lab. A small story about how funk's session pipeline crossed coasts in the mid-70s. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Parker_Jr.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Listen for the Lyricon, Bennie Maupin's electronic wind instrument that sounds like a saxophone trying to become a synthesizer. It threads through the back half of the track. Can you tell when he switches from soprano sax to Lyricon? Source: https://emeapp.org/2024/12/17/lyricons-evis-and-ewis-the-evolution-of-the-electronic-wind-instrument/
- The "just keep on doin' it" chant is the only vocal on the record. Notice how it functions less like a hook and more like another rhythm instrument folded into the groove. What would be lost if the voices weren't there? Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- Wah Wah Watson built his career making a guitar talk through a wah pedal. Track him for the full eight minutes and try to map what he's doing against what Ray Parker Jr.'s rhythm guitar is doing underneath. When do they swap roles? Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Doin' It" is the only track on Secrets recorded at Columbia Recording Studios in San Francisco; every other track was cut at Wally Heider Studios across town. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- The track is co-credited to Hancock, Melvin "Wah Wah Watson" Ragin, and Ray Parker Jr., with Watson playing guitar, bass, and lead voice on the cut. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- Released as a 7" single (Columbia 3-10408) backed with "People Music" in 1976; the single edit ran 3:21, while the album version is 8:03. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/ and search results from 45cat (https://www.45cat.com/record/310408)
- The single peaked at #104 on the US Billboard Hot 100, #83 on the R&B chart, and #40 on the Dance Club Songs chart. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock_discography
- Bennie Maupin plays the Lyricon, an early electronic wind controller built by Computone in Massachusetts; the first production Lyricon went to Tom Scott in 1974, with Maupin among the next handful of buyers. Source: https://emeapp.org/2024/12/17/lyricons-evis-and-ewis-the-evolution-of-the-electronic-wind-instrument/
- Sampled by Shabba Ranks on "Respect" and reworked in Bomfunk MC's "Freestyler (Skillsters Remix)." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/Herbie-Hancock/Doin'-It/

#### Album

- Secrets is Herbie Hancock's seventeenth album and was released August 15, 1976 on Columbia Records (catalog PC-34280). Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- It hit #1 on Billboard's Jazz albums chart, #8 R&B, and #49 on the Pop 200. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- The cover photograph, by longtime San Francisco photographer Herb Greene (best known for shooting the Grateful Dead), shows Hancock staring straight down the lens. Source: https://nicksvinylpicks.wordpress.com/2022/08/23/herbie-hancock-secrets/
- Secrets marks the unofficial end of the classic Headhunters lineup: only Bennie Maupin (reeds) and Paul Jackson (bass) carry over, with drummer Mike Clark replaced by James Levi and James Gadson. Source: https://www.progarchives.com/album.asp?id=19962
- "Cantelope Island," another Secrets track, is a funk re-recording of Hancock's 1964 Blue Note classic "Cantaloupe Island" (note the spelling change). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)

#### Artist

- Herbert Jeffrey Hancock was a child prodigy who performed Mozart's Coronation Concerto with the Chicago Symphony Orchestra at age eleven in 1952. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- He graduated from Grinnell College in 1960 with degrees in both electrical engineering and music, a background that informed his later embrace of synthesizers and wind controllers. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock
- Wah Wah Watson, a Funk Brother on classic Motown sessions including "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone," played and co-wrote with Hancock from roughly 1974 to 1980. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wah_Wah_Watson
- Ray Parker Jr. had played guitar on Stevie Wonder's 1972 Rolling Stones tour and was a sideman in Barry White's Love Unlimited Orchestra before joining the Secrets sessions; he formed Raydio in 1977. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Parker_Jr.

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz-Funk (best fit).** Hancock is a jazz musician building a funk groove with extended instrumental space; the personnel (Maupin on Lyricon, Hancock on stacked keyboards, the Headhunters rhythm section) fit the book's working definition of jazz-funk almost perfectly. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secrets_(Herbie_Hancock_album)
- **Classic Funk (defensible alternate).** Watson's writing pulls the track toward verse-and-chant funk with a vocal hook; this is the most "song-shaped" cut on a major-label record from a major artist in the heart of the 1969-1977 classic funk window. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- **Psychedelic Funk (weak third).** The Lyricon and stacked synths give the back half a hallucinatory feel, but it's a stretch given the dominant character is groove and pocket, not the surreal storytelling the book uses to define psychedelic funk.

Recommendation: Jazz-Funk.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - California (set).** Secrets was recorded entirely in San Francisco (Wally Heider Studios and Columbia Recording Studios), and the Headhunters operated out of the Bay Area in this period. Source: https://www.herbiehancock.com/album/secrets/
- **US - Illinois (alternate).** Hancock was born and raised in Chicago. If the book is consistently keying region to artist origin, Illinois works.
- **US - Michigan (weak third).** Both Wah Wah Watson and Ray Parker Jr. came up through the Detroit / Motown session pipeline, and they are central to this specific track. A real long shot, but not absurd given how much Detroit DNA is in the cut.

Recommendation: US - California, on the recording-location and band-base argument. Flag for the author if they prefer artist-origin tagging.
