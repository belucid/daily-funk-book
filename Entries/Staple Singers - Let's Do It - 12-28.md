How do you convince a deeply religious 60-year-old man to sing and play on a song about sex, alongside his own daughters? If you're Curtis Mayfield, you tell Pops Staples, "The Lord won't mind. It's just a love song."

Fortunately, that worked. Today's song, "Let's Do It Again," was a massive hit for the Staple Singers. It topped the *Billboard* Hot 100 on December 27, 1975, the day before Pops Staples' 61st birthday.

*Let's Do It Again* was a 1975 blaxploitation buddy comedy starring Bill Cosby and Sidney Poitier. Mayfield produced the soundtrack and enlisted the Staple Singers, who were eager to hear themselves in a movie. The soundtrack became the only Staple Singers album to hit #1 on the R&B album chart. It also peaked at #20 on the *Billboard* 200, a spot they reached only one other time with their 1972 album *Be Altitude: Respect Yourself*.

Like many blaxploitation films, the music outlasted the movie. And for good reason. For my money, there's nothing funkier than a Staple Singers slow-jam.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Gospel's secret: the Lord won't mind."** The most vivid story here is Pops Staples' refusal to sing a sex song, and Curtis Mayfield coaxing him across the line with "The Lord won't mind. It's just a love song." That one sentence captures a quietly radical idea: the distance between ecstatic gospel and earthly love was always shorter than the church wanted to admit. (Sources: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-staple-singers/lets-do-it-again, https://stereogum.com/2054791/the-number-ones-the-staple-singers-lets-do-it-again/columns/the-number-ones)

2. **"The last #1."** "Let's Do It Again" was the final pop number-one of 1975, and the last major crossover hit the Staples ever had. Framing the entry around endings, the close of the Stax era, the end of their hit-making run, the final Hot 100 topper of a year that belonged to disco, gives the reader a sense of funk history tilting on a hinge. (Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/)

3. **"A gift on the eve of a birthday."** The song hit #1 on December 27, 1975, the day before Pops Staples' 61st birthday. There is a warm, family-gift quality to that timing, especially given that Pops had to be talked into recording the song in the first place. A small-scale, human-sized angle. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(song))

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. "Listen for Pops Staples' solo lead, the verse that starts 'Now, I like you, lady.' He nearly refused to sing it. Can you hear any reluctance in his delivery, or did he commit all the way?"

2. "This was the last #1 pop hit of 1975, released as disco was taking over the dancefloor. Listen to the tempo and the pocket: how does this groove resist the four-on-the-floor pulse that was about to dominate the charts?"

3. "Curtis Mayfield wrote this for a gospel family group. Listen for where the gospel DNA peeks through, the call-and-response, the harmonies, the ecstatic delivery, even as the lyrics stay firmly earthbound."

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Pops Staples initially refused to sing the song, saying "I'm a church man, and I'm not singing that." Curtis Mayfield replied, "The Lord won't mind. It's just a love song." Mavis and her sisters persuaded him so they could all hear themselves in a movie. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-staple-singers/lets-do-it-again
- Pops' reluctant solo: "Now, I like you, lady / So fine with your pretty hair / If you don't mind my compliments / Just digging on the clothes you wear." — Source: https://stereogum.com/2054791/the-number-ones-the-staple-singers-lets-do-it-again/columns/the-number-ones
- The single was released October 13, 1975 on Curtom, catalog CMS-0109, with the instrumental "After Sex" as B-side. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(song)
- It hit #1 on the Hot Soul Singles chart on December 6, 1975, and topped the Billboard Hot 100 on December 27, 1975, the day before Pops Staples' 61st birthday. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(song)
- It was the last pop #1 of 1975, dethroning Silver Convention's "Save Me." — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/
- Time magazine lumped the song into a December 1975 "sex rock" feature alongside Donna Summer and KC & The Sunshine Band, with activist groups objecting that such music encouraged unwanted pregnancies. — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-staple-singers/lets-do-it-again
- For the 1994 Curtis Mayfield tribute album _All Men Are Brothers_, Mayfield recorded his vocal take lying down, his first time ever singing the song himself. (He was paralyzed from the neck down after a 1990 stage accident.) — Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-staple-singers/lets-do-it-again
- The groove was sampled for Ice Cube's "It Was a Good Day (Remix)," Bone Thugs-n-Harmony's "Do It Again," Wreckx-N-Effect's "Let's Do It Again," and Prince Vince & The Hip Hop Force's "Changes." Cover versions include George Benson (1988) and BlackGirl (1995). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(song)
- Stereogum describes the production as "skritchety-scratch guitar, soft bass burble, sun-dappled harp flourishes, golden-hour-sunray strings." — Source: https://stereogum.com/2054791/the-number-ones-the-staple-singers-lets-do-it-again/columns/the-number-ones

#### Album

- The eight-track soundtrack was written and produced entirely by Curtis Mayfield (the closing track "Chase" was co-composed with Quinton Joseph, Philip Upchurch, Gary Thompson, Floyd Morris, and Joseph "Lucky" Scott). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(soundtrack)
- Recorded at Curtom Studios, Chicago. Arrangements by Gil Askey and Rich Tufo. — Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/lets-do-it-again-mw0000038581 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(soundtrack)
- Core band: Floyd Morris and Rich Tufo on keyboards; Curtis Mayfield, Gary Thompson, Phil Upchurch on guitars; Quinton Joseph on drums; Henry Gibson on congas and bongos; Lucky Scott on bass. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(soundtrack)
- The album was the Staples' only #1 R&B album, topping that chart on December 6, 1975, and peaked at #20 on the Billboard 200, a mark they had only previously matched with 1972's _Be Altitude: Respect Yourself_. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/
- The soundtrack also yielded a Top 5 R&B hit with "New Orleans," also a Mayfield composition. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/
- The project was Mayfield's first #1 as a writer-producer (he had worked on major soundtracks before, including _Super Fly_, but had not previously topped the Hot 100 with a song he wrote and produced). — Source: https://stereogum.com/2054791/the-number-ones-the-staple-singers-lets-do-it-again/columns/the-number-ones
- Behind-the-scenes twist: Marv Stuart, Mayfield's manager and Curtom partner, said Curtom bid on signing the Staple Singers as an act and lost to Warner Bros., "and ended up producing them" instead. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/
- The film _Let's Do It Again_ (Sidney Poitier's 1975 comedy with Bill Cosby and Jimmie Walker, about two blue-collar friends rigging a boxing match) was released October 11, 1975, two days before the single dropped. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(1975_film)

#### Artist

- The Staple Singers formed in 1948 in Chicago as a family gospel act, led by Roebuck "Pops" Staples on guitar and vocals, with his children Cleotha, Pervis, Yvonne, and Mavis. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Staple_Singers
- Before their pop peak, they were the gospel group most closely associated with Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and the civil rights movement; Pops met King after hearing him preach in 1963 and built much of the group's 1960s repertoire around movement anthems. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Staple_Singers
- After Stax Records went bankrupt in 1975, the Staples signed with Curtis Mayfield's Curtom label just in time to make this record. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/staple-diet-soul/
- It was effectively the end of their commercial run: they never had another major crossover hit. — Source: https://stereogum.com/2054791/the-number-ones-the-staple-singers-lets-do-it-again/columns/the-number-ones
- Mavis Staples is the last surviving member; Pops died December 19, 2000, Cleotha February 21, 2013, Yvonne April 10, 2018, Pervis May 6, 2021. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pops_Staples, https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/105652778/cleotha-staples, https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/188698993/yvonne-staples, https://chicago.suntimes.com/2021/5/12/22432829/pervis-staples-dead-obituary-co-founded-the-staple-singers-dies-at-85-mavis-staples

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Gospel Funk**. This fits the book's definition ("a gospel act getting seriously funky on a track"): the Staple Singers were a four-decade gospel family act, and this is them riding one of Curtis Mayfield's funkiest Chicago grooves. The twist that the lyric is not a joyful noise unto the Lord, but an earthly come-on, is the most interesting thing about the entry and an argument for keeping this tag.

Alternative cases:

1. **Classic Funk.** By the book's definition, classic funk is funk that lived in the commercial pipeline of major labels, well-distributed singles, radio, and charts during 1968–1977. This song was a #1 Hot 100 hit on Curtom in 1975, recorded at a full-scale studio with a professional funk band under Curtis Mayfield. Its primary sonic identity is funk, not gospel. A very defensible primary tag. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Do_It_Again_(song)

2. **Gospel Funk (keep).** The backstory is the story. Pops refusing to sing, Mayfield saying "The Lord won't mind," the whole gospel-to-earthly tension, is the best writing hook, and Gospel Funk announces that hook on the metadata line. Recommended if the author plans to lean into the Pops-reluctance angle.

**Recommendation:** Keep **Gospel Funk** if the writing theme centers on Pops' reluctance or gospel-meets-sensual; consider **Classic Funk** if the theme is the chart peak, Mayfield's production, or the end-of-an-era angle. Either is defensible.
