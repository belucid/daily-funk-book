Today's track, "Cool Ade" by Preston Love is a collaboration between two generations of talented musicians. Older jazz men Johnny Otis (producer, pianist, vibes) and Preston Love provide reputational gravitas to an overtly silly and commercial album, while Otis' teenage son Shuggie Otis, makes his recorded debut on guitar at just 14 years old.

Make sure you listen to this one on headphones and notice the intense stereo separation of the rhythm section and the rest of the band.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The swing man who got funky"** — Preston Love's career arc is extraordinary: from Count Basie's lead alto chair in the 1940s, to Motown's West Coast house bandleader in the 1960s, to cutting a raw, funky instrumental record with a 14-year-old guitar prodigy. This track captures the moment a big band veteran fully embraced the groove.

2. **"Father and son, godfather and guest"** — The album is really a collaboration between three generations of musical authority: Johnny Otis (producer, pianist, vibes), his teenage son Shuggie, and Preston Love. The interplay between Otis family creative control and Love's veteran alto opens up a story about musical mentorship and family bands.

3. **"Omaha to Hollywood and back"** — Everything about this album, right down to its title, is a love letter to Love's Omaha roots, even though it was recorded in LA. The food-themed track titles (Chicken Gumbo, Chili Mac, Neck Bones, Hoe Cakes and Sorghum, Pot Likker) evoke a specific Black Midwestern culture that Love carried with him to the West Coast.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Cool Ade" was released as a 7" single on Kent Records with "Shuggie's Blues" on the B-side (Discogs release 9696761), making it one of the album cuts the label identified as having standalone hit potential. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9696761-Preston-Love-Cool-Ade-Shuggies-Blues
- The track features a call-and-response vocal, making it one of the few non-purely-instrumental cuts on an album that is mostly instrumental. — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/723747/Preston-Love:Preston-Love-s-Omaha-Bar-B-Q-180-gram-pressing
- At 2:02, it's one of the shortest tracks on the album, built in a tight "funky 45" mode. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/omaha-bar-b-q-mw0000015093

#### Album
- Every track on the album is named after food or drink: Chicken Gumbo, Chili Mac, Cream Dream, Neck Bones, Cool Ade, Omaha Bar-B-Que, Hoe Cakes and Sorghum, Shuggie's Chittlin' Blues, and Pot Likker. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/omaha-bar-b-q-mw0000015093
- The full title is "Johnny Otis Presents: Preston Love's Omaha Bar-B-Q," reflecting the dual authorship. Johnny Otis and Shuggie Otis co-wrote most of the material, and Johnny Otis produced. — Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/omaha-bar-b-q
- Shuggie Otis was just 14 years old when the album was recorded in 1969. His bluesy playing already featured a "slightly distorted wah-wah edge." B.B. King called Shuggie his "favorite new guitarist" in a 1970 Guitar Player magazine interview. — Sources: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/omaha-bar-b-q and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuggie_Otis
- Original vinyl copies of the album now sell for significant collector prices, with UK copies fetching over £60. — Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/omaha-bar-b-q
- The album was arranged by Johnny Otis and Maxwell Davis, with engineering by Gordon Calcote and John Cevetello. — Source: search results referencing Discogs credits
- The album's full personnel: Preston Love (alto saxophone, flute), Shuggie Otis (guitar, electric bass), Johnny Otis (piano, vibraphone), and Clifford Solomon (tenor saxophone). — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/omaha-bar-b-q-mw0000015093

#### Artist
- Preston Love joined the Count Basie Orchestra in 1943 after Basie's lead alto player Earle Warren fell ill at Omaha's Dreamland Ballroom. Warren himself recommended Love as his substitute, and Love's sound was so close to Warren's that Basie hired him on the spot. — Source: https://davidbristow.com/articles/swingin-with-preston-love/
- Love played on Count Basie's only #1 hit, "Open the Door Richard" (1947). — Source: https://richielove.com/preston-love-sr-tribute/
- Love became Motown's first West Coast house bandleader/contractor, playing and touring with The Four Tops, The Temptations, Tammi Terrell, Marvin Gaye, Gladys Knight, The Jackson 5, and others. — Source: https://richielove.com/preston-love-sr-tribute/
- He appeared in Clint Eastwood's film "Play Misty for Me." — Source: https://richielove.com/preston-love-sr-tribute/
- Love co-launched Spin Records in 1952 and recorded for Federal and King labels. — Source: https://richielove.com/preston-love-sr-tribute/
- He published his autobiography "A Thousand Honey Creeks Later: My Life in Music from Basie to Motown, and Beyond" in 1997 (Wesleyan University Press), with a foreword by Johnny Otis and an afterword by George Lipsitz. The title references Honey Creek, Iowa, where a 15-year-old Love made his musical debut. — Source: https://www.amazon.com/Thousand-Honey-Creeks-Later-Motown_and/dp/081956320X
- His sons Richie and Norman Love are both accomplished saxophonists, and his daughter Portia Love is a vocalist. — Source: https://richielove.com/preston-love-sr-tribute/
- Love was a civil rights activist in Los Angeles, organizing protests and demonstrations. — Source: https://omahafreedomfestival.com/the-blog/from-omaha-to-hollywood-the-incredible-journey-of-musician-preston-love-sr/
- In the 1930s-40s, Omaha was the central booking hub for jazz in the upper plains region. Love recalled it as "the booking center for the biggest dance territory in the world." — Source: https://davidbristow.com/articles/swingin-with-preston-love/
- Tenor saxophonist Clifford Solomon, who plays on this album, later became Ray Charles's musical director from 1974 to 1987. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Solomon

### Field Verification

- **album:** `Preston Love's Omaha Bar-B-Q` — Verified. Full title is "Johnny Otis Presents: Preston Love's Omaha Bar-B-Q" but the shorter form is standard for the artist's own album listing. Sources: AllMusic, Discogs, Ace Records.
- **label:** `Kent Records` — Verified. Original release was on Kent (catalog KST 540), a subsidiary of the Bihari brothers' Modern Records empire. Later reissued on BGP/Ace Records. "Kent" alone is also acceptable. Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/omaha-bar-b-q
- **year:** `1970` — **Discrepancy noted.** AllMusic lists the album as 1969. Ace Records says "originally released on Kent, 1970." The album was recorded in 1969. The Kent album discography places KST 540 between KST 539 (B.B. King, 1970), consistent with a 1970 release. The current value of 1970 appears correct for the release date (vs. the recording date of 1969). No change made.
- **track length:** Updated from `2m` to `2m 02s`. Source: AllMusic (2:02) and Apple Music (2:02).
- **region:** Set to `US - California`. The album was recorded in Los Angeles by Johnny Otis's LA-based band. Preston Love was living in LA at the time as Motown's West Coast bandleader (early 1960s to 1972). However, **an argument exists for `US - Midwest`**: Love was born and raised in Omaha, Nebraska, the album is literally titled "Omaha Bar-B-Q," and all the food references point to Black Midwestern culture. ### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** — The strongest fit. This is a short, punchy, groove-driven instrumental built on a tight rhythm section with saxophone leads. It sits squarely in the late-1960s funk tradition: horn-driven, rhythm-first, no extended solos. The Dusty Groove description calls the album tracks "funky 45 mode" material. — Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/723747/Preston-Love:Preston-Love-s-Omaha-Bar-B-Q-180-gram-pressing

2. **Deep Funk** — A strong secondary case. The track is obscure, collectible (original 7" singles are rare), and built for the dancefloor in a raw, unpolished way. The album's collector market status and the single release both align with the deep funk 45 crate-digging world. — Source: https://www.acerecords.co.uk/omaha-bar-b-q

3. **Jazz-Funk** — A tertiary option. Preston Love's pedigree is jazz (Count Basie Orchestra), and his alto playing brings jazz phrasing to funk grooves. The presence of vibraphone (Johnny Otis) and the improvisational feel could push it toward jazz-funk. But the track is more groove than jazz, so this is the weaker case.
