### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The chemist who quit polymers for poetry"** — Wilson majored in chemistry, spent a decade (1963–1973) as a fiberglass-manufacturing consultant, and didn't pick up a guitar until age 30. His own line — "Back then I was making money, now I'm making music" — is a ready-made hook. Source: https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/larry-jon-wilson-1940-2010/
- **"Named by a compilation, decades later"** — A useful lens on the Country Funk subgenre itself: this 1977 cut only became 'country funk' when Light in the Attic placed it on *Country Funk Volume III (1975–1982)* in 2021. The genre was coined after the fact. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-3-1975-1982
- **"No bag to be placed in"** — Wilson's own diagnosis of his commercial failure ("My love for different kinds of music has apparently left the music that I write and perform with no bag to be placed in") frames an entry about uncategorizable artists who funk found anyway. Source: https://larry-jon-wilson.com/biography-and-pictures/

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- The track was chosen by Light in the Attic for *Country Funk Volume III (1975–1982)*, appearing as track #10 (the LIATC product page lists it; the curatorial act of placing a 1977 Monument country single on a "country funk" comp is what brings it into this book). Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-3-1975-1982
- On the *Loose Change* LP the song sits on the "Muscle Shoals Side" (Side B), one of two explicitly named sides — Side A is the "Nashville Side." Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/14624435-Larry-Jon-Wilson-Loose-Change
- Mickey Newbury, a celebrated songwriter in his own right, appears on the album credited for "Humming, Whistle" rather than as a writer or instrumentalist. Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/14624435

### Album
- *Loose Change* is split into a "Nashville Side" and a "Muscle Shoals Side," pulling together two of the South's signature studio traditions on one record. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/14624435-Larry-Jon-Wilson-Loose-Change
- The personnel reads like a who's-who of the Memphis/Muscle Shoals session world: Reggie Young, Bobby Wood, Bobby Emmons-adjacent players, Joe Osborn and Tommy Cogbill on bass, Larrie Londin on percussion. Source: https://api.discogs.com/releases/14624435
- It was Wilson's third of four Monument albums (after *New Beginnings* 1975 and *Let Me Sing My Song to You* 1976, before *The Sojourner* 1979), none of which produced a hit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Jon_Wilson

### Artist
- Wilson didn't touch a guitar until age 30 and taught himself, leaving a career as a fiberglass-manufacturing consultant (1963–1973, United Merchants and Manufacturers, Langley SC) to pursue music. Source: https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/larry-jon-wilson-1940-2010/
- He majored in chemistry at the University of Georgia and attended Carlisle Military Academy in Bamberg, SC. Source: https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/larry-jon-wilson-1940-2010/
- A *Saturday Review* critic called his debut "the best thing I have heard in country, rock, pop, or you-name-it for a very long time," yet no hit ever came; his early albums were reissued in Britain in 2000 and named *MOJO*'s "Re-Issue of the Month." Source: https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/larry-jon-wilson-1940-2010/
- He was a fixture at Nashville's Bluebird Cafe, left the industry disillusioned around 1980, and returned later in the decade. Source: https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/larry-jon-wilson-1940-2010/

### Genre Suggestions

The current value, **Country Funk**, is well supported and should stand. The track's inclusion on Light in the Attic's *Country Funk Volume III (1975–1982)* — the very series that, per this book's own definition, named and codified the subgenre — is about as authoritative a country-funk credential as exists. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/products/country-funk-volume-3-1975-1982

Alternative considerations (for completeness, not recommended over Country Funk):
- **Classic Funk** — Does NOT fit. Per the book's definition, classic funk requires funk to be the track's primary identity within mainstream funk distribution; this is fundamentally a country record that absorbed funk feel. Source (definition): Matter/On Subgenres.md
- **Deep Funk** — Tempting on the "delayed recognition" axis (a poorly-selling 1977 Monument LP rediscovered by compilers decades later), but the book reserves deep funk for crate-dug funk obscurities, not country sides. Country Funk already captures the rediscovery story better. Source (definition): Matter/On Subgenres.md

Recommendation: keep **Country Funk**.
