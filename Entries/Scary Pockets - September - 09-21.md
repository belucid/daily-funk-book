Most nu-funk is original material that sounds like it could have been released during the classic funk period of the early 1970s. Scary Pockets flips this formula by reworking pop songs listeners already know, placing them in a 1970s-sounding live band with a tight pocket and a rhythm-first arrangement.

Unlike typical Scary Pockets covers such as "MMMBop" or "Creep," "September" is a stress test of the band’s approach because the original song already belongs to the funk canon. Funking up a pop song is one thing. Reworking an established funk classic is a higher bar. Judge for yourself, but I think they clear it.

Scary Pockets’ keyboardist Jack Conte is also Patreon’s co-founder and CEO. From 2017 to 2023, the band built its audience by releasing a new funk cover on YouTube each week. The band’s Patreon supporters helped fund the project and sustain the weekly cadence. We should all be so lucky to find a way to seamlessly blend our day job and hobby.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The weekly-funk machine."** Scary Pockets was a factory for groove: one funk cover every week, rotating vocalists and session players, for six straight years (2017 to December 2023). "September" is a single snapshot of that engine in motion. The hook is the discipline and the format, not just this track. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets

2. **"Patreon founder moonlights as funk bandleader."** Jack Conte, the co-founder and CEO of Patreon, is also the keyboardist and co-leader of Scary Pockets. The project is both a working band and a living demo of the creator-economy ideas Patreon exists to support. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Conte and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets

3. **"Nu-funk as covers craft."** Most nu-funk is original material that sounds old. Scary Pockets flipped the formula: take songs the listener already knows and drop them into a live, pocket-first arrangement. "September" is the stress test for that approach, since the EW&F original is already peak groove. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Earth, Wind & Fire's "September" is built on a dense horn-and-vocal layer cake. Scary Pockets strips it to a small combo with one voice. Listen for what they choose to keep (the bass line, the "ba-dee-ya"), and what they let go. Does the song still feel like "September" without the firewall of horns?

2. Stacey Ryan is the only lead voice here, where EW&F had Maurice White, Philip Bailey's falsetto, and a full vocal stack. Track how she handles the chorus payoff alone. Does the leaner arrangement make the hook feel smaller, or sharper?

3. Listen to how the bass and drums lock in under the verses. In an EW&F arrangement you hear the groove with the horns. Here you hear the groove because nothing else is in the way. Which version pulls you into dancing faster?

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Nu-Funk (current) — Recommended.** Scary Pockets is a textbook case: a post-2015 band making new funk recordings that consciously evoke vintage 45s, built for the streaming/YouTube era. They fit the book's definition of "a modern funk revival scene... capturing the feel of rare funk 45s while making new records in that spirit." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Pockets

2. **Classic Funk — Not a fit.** Although the source song (EW&F) is classic funk, the book defines Classic Funk by era (roughly 1968 to 1977). A 2022 cover falls outside that window.

3. **Acid Jazz — Weak alternative.** The emphasis on tight groove, hooks, and a curated club-friendly atmosphere over extended improvisation has some kinship with acid jazz, but Scary Pockets do not foreground the sampling/DJ-culture lineage central to that genre. Stick with Nu-Funk.

**Recommendation:** Keep Nu-Funk.
