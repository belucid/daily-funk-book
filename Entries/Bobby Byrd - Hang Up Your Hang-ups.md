**Important title finding (please review):** I could find no official release by Bobby Byrd titled "Hang Up Your Hang-ups." The only place that title appears is track B5 ("Hang Up Your Hang-Ups", 2:55) on *The First Family Of Funk* (I Dentify IDLP 801, UK, 1993), a UK compilation whose back cover credits distribution to "International Brothers" and which looks unofficial. Discogs' own linked YouTube video for that track on the compilation's master page is titled "Bobby Byrd--Hang Ups We Dont Need" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KE_YXu-YQ_Y), and the running time (2:55) is close to the King 7" (2:52). Taken together, this strongly suggests that "Hang Up Your Hang-ups" is a compiler's retitling of Byrd's 1970 King single **"Hang Ups We Don't Need (The Hungry We Got To Feed)"** (King 45-6289, written and produced by James Brown). Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1670577-Various-The-First-Family-Of-Funk, https://www.discogs.com/master/1392155-Various-The-First-Family-Of-Funk, https://www.discogs.com/master/247255-Bobby-Byrd-Hang-Ups-We-Dont-Need-The-Hungry-We-Got-To-Feed.
- Counter-check: "Hang Up Your Hang Ups" is also a well-known Herbie Hancock song (*Man-Child*, 1975) — https://jazz.com/music/herbie-hancock-hang-up-your-hang-ups — and a one-off 45 by Cincinnati's Umoja — https://www.zeroto180.org/umojas-hang-up-free-funk/. Neither is connected to Byrd. Be careful that the entry title doesn't get mixed up with the Hancock song.
- I did not change the `track` field (not editable). The author may want to retitle the entry/track to the official "Hang Ups We Don't Need (The Hungry We Got To Feed)". All YAML values above assume this identification.
- I did not listen to both recordings to confirm they are identical. That would close this out.

**Release month of the 7" not confirmed.** Discogs gives only "1970" for King 45-6289. The neighboring catalog number, King 45-6290, is James Brown's "Funky Drummer" (https://www.discogs.com/release/560161-James-Brown-Funky-Drummer), which Wikipedia dates to March 1970 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_Drummer). So the Byrd single likely came out around February–March 1970. This is an inference from catalog sequence, not a sourced date. 45cat (https://www.45cat.com/record/6289us) returned HTTP 403 and could not be checked; Billboard's early-1970 "New Releases"/R&B listings would settle it.

**When Byrd rejoined Brown is disputed.** The Substack profile says Byrd "quit for two years beginning in 1968" and was back in 1970 (https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/bobby-byrd-august-15-1934-september). Derek's Music Blog says Byrd and Vicki Anderson returned to Brown's revue in 1969 (https://dereksmusicblog.com/2017/10/19/bobby-byrd-help-for-my-brother-the-pre-funk-singles-1963-1868/). Since the single was likely out in early 1970, a 1969 return fits better.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A song with two names"**: The track came back to UK rare-groove crates under a bootlegger's title, "Hang Up Your Hang-Ups," which hides the original's pointed message, "The Hungry We Got To Feed." The entry could be about how a compiler's retitling changes what a record seems to say.
- **"Brown's message, Byrd's voice"**: In 1970 James Brown wrote and produced message songs for his inner circle. This one has Byrd delivering Brown's social sermon (and the same year Brown and Byrd cut a "Fight Against Drug Abuse" PSA promo for King). The angle is Byrd as Brown's mouthpiece and right-hand man in the year of "Sex Machine."
- **"Live on stage (sort of)"**: The album version sits on *I Need Help (Live On Stage)*, an LP that reviewers say carries canned applause. The entry could look at the fake live album as a James Brown-camp marketing trick.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The single is called "Hang Ups We Don't Need (The Hungry We Got To Feed)." Listen for the moment the message comes through. Is this a protest song, a sermon, or a party record with a conscience?
- Play the 7" single version against the "Live On Stage" album version. Does the crowd noise make the groove feel bigger, or does it give away that it was added later?
- Byrd was the hype man on "Sex Machine" that same year. Listen to how he works this rhythm section as the lead voice. What does he do that Brown wouldn't?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- The official title is "Hang Ups We Don't Need (The Hungry We Got To Feed)." It was issued as King 45-6289 (1970), credited "A James Brown Production," with Brown as sole writer. Running time 2:52. B-side: "You Gave My Heart A Brand New Song To Sing" (Byrd/Hobgood/Brown). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/551666-Bobby-Byrd-Hang-Ups-We-Dont-Need-The-Hungry-We-Got-To-Feed
- The two sides have very different matrix numbers (A-side SK-13390, B-side SK-12437), which suggests the B-side was an older recording paired with a fresh A-side. This is an inference from the matrix numbers. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/551666-Bobby-Byrd-Hang-Ups-We-Dont-Need-The-Hungry-We-Got-To-Feed
- The track has been described as a "powerful message song" that Brown wrote and produced for Byrd in 1970. — Source: https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/bobby-byrd-august-15-1934-september
- It resurfaced in the UK rare-groove era as "Hang Up Your Hang-Ups" on the 1993 compilation *The First Family Of Funk* (I Dentify IDLP 801), next to Fred Wesley & The J.B.'s "Use Me" and other James Brown-family obscurities. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1670577-Various-The-First-Family-Of-Funk
- Today it streams as "Hang Ups We Don't Need..." in two versions: a "[Live]" version on *I Need Help (Live On Stage)* (3:05 Apple / 3:06 Deezer) and a "Single Version" on the 1995 Polydor/Chronicles best-of *Bobby Byrd Got Soul* (2:57 on Discogs/Deezer, 3:03 on Apple). Discogs misspells the best-of track as "Hangs Up We Don't Need." — Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/hang-ups-we-dont-need-the-hungry-we-got-to-feed-live/1437583226?i=1437583436, https://www.deezer.com/track/609311082, https://www.discogs.com/master/52842-Bobby-Byrd-Bobby-Byrd-Got-Soul-The-Best-Of-Bobby-Byrd

### Album
- *I Need Help (Live On Stage)*, King KS 1118 (1970), credited "A James Brown Production." It was recorded at King Studios, engineered by Ron Lenhoff, with liner notes by King's Hal Neely. "Hang Ups We Don't Need" is side B, track 5 (3:06). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/685081-Bobby-Byrd-I-Need-Help-Live-On-Stage
- Despite the "Live On Stage" title, a review says the album is "presented as a live set, but the canned applause sounds fake." The review picks out "I Need Help," "You Got to Have a Job," and "Hangups" as the cuts with the new, fierce funk sound. (The page returned 403 to direct fetch; the quotes come from search excerpts.) — Source: https://www.jazzmusicarchives.com/artist/bobby-byrd
- Apple Music gives the album's release date as September 22, 1970, and AllMusic agrees. — Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/hang-ups-we-dont-need-the-hungry-we-got-to-feed-live/1437583226?i=1437583436, https://www.allmusic.com/album/i-need-help-mw0002836872
- The title single "I Need Help (I Can't Do It Alone)" reached #14 R&B and #79 Pop in 1970, Byrd's first chart hit in five years. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd, https://dereksmusicblog.com/2017/10/19/bobby-byrd-help-for-my-brother-the-pre-funk-singles-1963-1868/

### Artist
- Bobby Byrd was born August 15, 1934, in Toccoa, Georgia, and died of cancer on September 12, 2007, in Loganville, Georgia. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd formed the gospel group that became the Famous Flames and is credited with discovering James Brown in juvenile prison. His family helped get Brown paroled. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd hired Bootsy and Catfish Collins for the new J.B.'s in 1970, the same year as this single. — Source: https://jointzoftheday.substack.com/p/bobby-byrd-august-15-1934-september
- Also in 1970, King pressed a promo 7" PSA, "Fight Against Drug Abuse," credited to James Brown & Bobby Byrd. It fits the same message-record moment. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4850393-James-Brown-Bobby-Byrd-Fight-Against-Drug-Abuse
- Jay-Z sampled Byrd's "I'm Not to Blame" (from the same *I Need Help* LP) for "U Don't Know" (2001). He was reportedly paid 65% of the royalties. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd was inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame posthumously in 2012 as a Famous Flame. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (primary).** A 1970 James Brown production on King, right at the start of the book's 1969–1977 classic window. It went out through Brown's main commercial pipeline as a single and LP track, and its identity is straight funk. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/551666-Bobby-Byrd-Hang-Ups-We-Dont-Need-The-Hungry-We-Got-To-Feed, https://www.discogs.com/master/247255-Bobby-Byrd-Hang-Ups-We-Dont-Need-The-Hungry-We-Got-To-Feed
- **Deep Funk (alternative).** The 7" doesn't appear to have charted: Wikipedia's chart list for Byrd names "I Need Help" but not this single. Its later life came through UK rare-groove compilations (*The First Family Of Funk*, 1993), which fits the book's "delayed recognition" definition. The catch is that it came out on a well-known label, so it only qualifies under the "poorly selling record" clause. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd, https://www.discogs.com/release/1670577-Various-The-First-Family-Of-Funk

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Ohio (set).** The LP credits King Studios (Cincinnati) as the recording location, and King Records was based in Cincinnati, where Brown's 1969–70 sessions were cut (e.g. "Funky Drummer," recorded at King Studios, Cincinnati). This also matches the label-and-studio logic used for other King-era James Brown-camp entries. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/685081-Bobby-Byrd-I-Need-Help-Live-On-Stage, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_Drummer
- **US - Southeast (alternative).** Byrd was born in Toccoa, Georgia, and that's where the Famous Flames and the Brown partnership began. Choose this if the book tags by artist origin. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
