### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were present in the entry body when research began.

Outstanding ambiguities surfaced during research:

- **Recording studio discrepancy.** Discogs credits (album personnel) name *Studio Renapec, Kinshasa* (the state-run Régie Nationale des Productions Éducatives et Culturelles) as the recording location, with Elondo Ekoma as recording engineer. KLOF Mag's review of the 2024 *Congo Funk!* compilation instead names *"Simbard Studios"* in Kinshasa, describing it as the studio whose house band (International Sim Sim) populated Orchestre Nzazi. These cannot both be primary venues for the same session, so one source is in error or is conflating two studios. The Discogs credit is taken from the LP label and is treated here as the canonical source for the audit trail; KLOF Mag's claim is preserved in the Interest section. (Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/8823384-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi ; https://klofmag.com/2024/04/congo-funk-sound-madness-from-the-shores-of-the-mighty-congo-river/)
- **Region of origin.** Discogs categorises the release as "Congo, Republic of the," but the recording address (Studio Renapec, Kinshasa) places it in Zaïre / present-day DR Congo. The "Republic of the Congo" tag on Discogs appears to be a database artifact, not a statement about Brazzaville. Verified via the Renapec studio profile (https://www.discogs.com/label/538596-Studio-Renapec-Kinshasa) which is described as Kinshasa-based.
- **Petelo Vicka's pre-Kinshasa biography.** KLOF Mag states Petelo fled Angola during its war of independence (i.e. before 1975) and resettled in Kinshasa. No corroborating source (Wikipedia, AllMusic, MusicBrainz) was found. Background detail flagged for fact-checking.
- **Translation of "Sungu Lubuka."** The track title is in Kikongo (the LP's lyric booklet is reportedly in Kinkongo + French per NoForm). A 19th-century Kongo dictionary (Bentley) defines *sungu* as "violent death" and *lubuka* as a verb related to awareness or escape, but no clean phrase translation is published. Best left to a Kikongo speaker. (Source: https://archive.org/stream/dictionarygramma00bentuoft/dictionarygramma00bentuoft_djvu.txt)

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"An album recorded in a single evening — and shelved for forty-two years."** KLOF Mag reports the *Nzazi* LP was cut in one evening session and "never released in Zaire" at the time. Discogs, however, dates an original 1982 LP pressing (catalog Minica MI/05). The hook is the gap between making the music and meeting its audience: a 1982 Kinshasa session that became "new" in 2024 via Analog Africa and the NoForm/Ketu reissue.
- **"The refugee who hired the studio band."** Per KLOF, Petelo arrived in Kinshasa as an Angolan war refugee and built Orchestre Nzazi out of musicians from International Sim Sim, the studio's in-house band. The entry could trace how funk in Kinshasa was assembled from migrants, session players, and "research music" — the local term Simbard Studios gave its jazz/funk experiments.
- **"The Zaire 74 aftershock, ten years late."** Most Zaire-funk narratives focus on the immediate post-1974 fallout (James Brown's Kinshasa set as catalyst). *Sungu Lubuka* lands at the tail of that wave, in 1982, when the funk impulse had to coexist with the soukous Zaiko Langa Langa generation. Theme: what Congolese funk sounds like once it stops being an event and becomes a habit.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Track the lead guitar of Eloys Simon Sungu against the rhythm guitar of Boleko Rock for the first 90 seconds. Where does the song stop sounding like rumba and start sounding like funk — is it a specific bar, or a shift in the bass?
- Listen for the flute (Nsimba Vuvu Mampoko) and the trumpet/tenor sax break around the midpoint. These are not standard rumba textures; they're the "research music" Simbard reportedly cultivated. Does the horn writing feel closer to Fela's Egypt 80, to a JB's chart, or to neither?
- This is a 7-minute-49 piece that, on its 1982 LP, never had a chance to be heard outside Kinshasa. Listen to it knowing it sat unheard for forty years and ask: what would have changed if Western crate-diggers had caught it in 1983?

#### Album

- The 1982 LP (Minica MI/05) is the only known full-length record by Petelo Vicka; the 2024 reissue by NoForm: Production (Geneva) and Ketu Records (Marseille) is built around restoration work by remixing engineer Andoche Firmin Ntoumi, who also receives credit on the original LP. Source: https://noformprod.bandcamp.com/album/petelo-vicka-son-nzazi
- The reissue includes a 6-page French booklet with biography, context, and lyrics in Kinkongo with French translation — a sign of how much of Petelo's story had to be reconstructed from scratch. Source: https://noformprod.bandcamp.com/album/petelo-vicka-son-nzazi
- As of 2024, Discogs shows 390 collectors wanting an original copy versus 69 who own one, putting the original Minica pressing among the more sought-after Kinshasa private-label LPs of the early '80s. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/3400123-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi

#### Artist

- Per the KLOF Mag review of *Congo Funk!*, Petelo arrived in Kinshasa as a refugee from the Angolan war of independence (which ran into 1975), before founding Orchestre Nzazi. Source: https://klofmag.com/2024/04/congo-funk-sound-madness-from-the-shores-of-the-mighty-congo-river/
- Orchestre Nzazi was effectively built out of the house band of the studio that recorded them, International Sim Sim — meaning Petelo's "band" is a session collective rather than a touring unit. Source: https://klofmag.com/2024/04/congo-funk-sound-madness-from-the-shores-of-the-mighty-congo-river/
- The musical director and lead guitarist Eloys Simon Sungu (credited on Discogs as "Sungu Elvys S.") shares a surname with the track title *Sungu Lubuka*; whether that's coincidence or a personal reference is unresolved. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8823384-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi

### Genre Suggestions

The current value is **Afrobeat**, which the book defines as a broad label for any funk rooted in Africa. That holds.

- **Afrobeat** (currently set). The book's definition explicitly uses Afrobeat as the umbrella for African-rooted funk including West African and Central African scenes; Discogs also tags the LP with "Afrobeat" alongside "African" and "Rumba." Best fit given the project's vocabulary. Source: book's *On Genres.md*; https://www.discogs.com/master/3400123-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi-Petelo-Vicka-Et-Son-Nzazi
- **Deep Funk** (secondary case). The book defines Deep Funk as "less a sound than a commercial fate" — tracks overlooked on release that found their audience later through crate-digging. *Sungu Lubuka* fits the pattern almost perfectly: a 1982 Kinshasa private-label LP that went essentially unheard until two crate-dig reissues forty-two years later. If the author wants to lean the entry into the rediscovery story rather than the African-funk one, Deep Funk is a defensible alternative. Source: book's *On Genres.md*; https://noformprod.bandcamp.com/album/petelo-vicka-son-nzazi
- **Jazz Funk** (tertiary, weaker). The track runs 7:49 with a horn-and-flute-led instrumental stretch and minimal vocal hook, which matches the book's jazz-funk traits (longer track lengths, room for extended improvisation, less vocal-chorus dependence). The "research music" tag KLOF reports from Simbard Studios reinforces that framing. But the rhythm section is rumba-rooted rather than jazz-rooted, so this is the weakest of the three. Source: book's *On Genres.md*; https://klofmag.com/2024/04/congo-funk-sound-madness-from-the-shores-of-the-mighty-congo-river/
