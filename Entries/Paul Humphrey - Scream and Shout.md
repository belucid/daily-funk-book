### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The session drummer steps to the front."** Humphrey spent the 60s-70s as one of L.A.'s most-recorded sidemen (Marvin Gaye's *Let's Get It On*, Wes Montgomery, Charles Mingus, the Lawrence Welk Show). *Me And My Drums* is the rare moment the hired hand makes the record entirely his own — almost a two-man (Humphrey + Tony Drake) bedroom-funk experiment.
- **"A drum album that became a digger's prize."** A private/small-label 1979 LP that flopped commercially but is now a crate-digging staple — a clean illustration of the deep-funk "delayed recognition" arc.
- **"Funk by two people and a pile of synths."** The whole album is essentially Humphrey and Anthony L. Drake overdubbing drums, bass, clavinet, organ and ARP synths — a DIY take on jazz-funk that prefigures one-man-band funk production.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is a drummer's solo album — track exactly when Humphrey lets the kit lead versus when he buries it under clavinet and synth. Where does the drummer choose NOT to show off?
- Two musicians played almost everything here. Listen for the seams: can you hear where the live drums meet the overdubbed bass and ARP synths, or does the groove hide the multitracking?
- Compare the title's promise ("Scream & Shout") with how restrained the instrumental actually is. Where is the "scream" — in the synth, the drums, or nowhere at all?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Scream & Shout" was written by Anthony L. Drake (not Humphrey), who also plays bass, keyboards, organ and percussion across the album — making the record effectively a two-man project. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1167322-Paul-Humphrey-Me-And-My-Drums
- The track exists in three notably different lengths from the same year: ~6:08 (album), 7:38 (12" promo), and 3:25 (7" single edit). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/350204-Paul-Humphrey-Scream-Shout and https://www.discogs.com/release/4912684-Paul-Humphrey-Scream-Shout-Here-To-Stay
- It was issued as both an A-side 7" single (b/w "Here To Stay," Stanson SS-2751) and a standalone 12" promo — unusual promotional attention for a track buried as A4 on a private-press LP. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4912684-Paul-Humphrey-Scream-Shout-Here-To-Stay

#### Album
- *Me And My Drums* was cut at a string of L.A.-area rooms including Buzzard Studio, the Tricentennial Electronic Music Studio in Carson, and Gold Star Studios, with executive production by Sonny Craver and liner notes credited to "King Oliver." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1167322-Paul-Humphrey-Me-And-My-Drums
- Dealers describe it as a "scatter-shot approach to jazz funk" built around drums, bass and "lots of electronic keyboards and Arps," prized today as a rare independent LP. — Source: https://soundsoftheuniverse.com/product/paul-humphrey-me-and-my-drums
- It is one of a long run of Humphrey leader albums (Supermellow 1973, America Wake Up 1974, Cochise 1974, The Drum Session 1975/1977) but the only one on the obscure Stanson label. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Humphrey_(American_musician)

#### Artist
- Paul Nelson Humphrey (b. Oct 12, 1935, Detroit; d. Jan 31, 2014) played drums on Marvin Gaye's *Let's Get It On* (1973). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Humphrey_(American_musician)
- As leader of Paul Humphrey and the Cool Aid Chemists he scored 1971 hits "Cool Aid" (US #29, R&B #14) and "Funky L.A." (R&B #45). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Humphrey_(American_musician)
- He was the featured drummer on television's Lawrence Welk Show from 1976 to 1982 — overlapping the very years he cut this gritty private-press funk LP. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Humphrey_(American_musician)
