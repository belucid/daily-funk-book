 Today’s song, “Your Mama Wants Ya Back,” is by Betty Davis, born Betty Mabry on July 26, 1944, in Durham, North Carolina. The Davis name came from her brief marriage to jazz great Miles Davis, but that connection often obscures the fact that Betty Davis was not just a funky singer. She was the writer, arranger, and producer of her own sound. 
 
Those in the know cherish Davis’ raw, funky vocals, but *They Say I’m Different* is even more remarkable for its credits. Davis wrote, arranged, and produced her albums, an almost unheard-of level of control for a Black woman in funk in the early 1970s.
 
Recorded at the Record Plant in Sausalito, *They Say I'm Different* was released on Just Sunshine Records in 1974. It peaked at #46 on the *Billboard* R&B albums chart, but Davis’ career was boxed in by a familiar double standard. U.S. television networks effectively shut her out for a sexually aggressive stage persona that would have drawn far less panic from a man.

The album didn't sell well, and later became expensive and hard to find for funk collectors. In 2007, Light in the Attic reissued it, bringing it back into wider circulation. It was reissued again for the 50th anniversary in 2023, when it re-entered the German albums chart at #41.

### Open Research Questions

**Open: what caused it to enter the German album charts at #41 in 2023.**

Resolved: the chart re-entry was driven by a Light in the Attic Records reissue campaign in August 2023. On August 25, 2023, LITA released a four-album Betty Davis reissue collection marking the 50th anniversary of her 1973 self-titled debut. *They Say I'm Different* was one of the four titles, alongside *Betty Davis* (1973), *Is It Love or Desire?* (recorded 1976, originally released 2009), and *Crashin' From Passion* (1979 sessions, getting its first-ever vinyl pressing). The campaign was meaningful for several converging reasons:

- **Collaborator's-blessing framing.** The reissues had been started in close collaboration with Betty Davis before her death in February 2022. The campaign was effectively the first major posthumous push for her catalog and got broad coverage in *Rolling Stone*, *Uncut*, *The Quietus*, and SoulTracks. — Sources: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/betty-davis-four-essential-albums-reissued-1234776098/ ; https://www.uncut.co.uk/news/four-betty-davis-albums-to-be-reissued-in-august-142886/ ; https://thequietus.com/articles/33101-light-in-the-attic-lines-up-four-betty-davis-album-reissues
- **Audiophile vinyl edition.** *They Say I'm Different* was issued on 180g clear-orange vinyl, remastered by Dave Cooley at Elysian Masters from the original tapes, pressed at RTI, with a 20-page booklet of new liner notes by Davis's close friend Danielle Maggio plus rare photos and lyrics. Limited audiophile pressings of cult albums routinely chart in Germany, where the vinyl-collector market is unusually large and chart entries weight physical sales heavily. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/28086259-Betty-Davis-They-Say-Im-Different ; https://soultracks.com/story-betty-davis-reissues-2023/
- **No competing 2023 catalog event.** No documentary release, sync placement, or major sample event on the album surfaced in 2023 that could explain the chart move independently. The reissue is the only documented 2023 commercial driver. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Say_I%27m_Different

**Discrepancy flagged for the author (not an open question):** several blog-level write-ups (e.g., eatsdrinksandleaves.com) credit the personnel of "Your Mama Wants Ya Back" to Larry Graham (bass), Gregg Errico (drums), Neal Schon (guitar) and the Pointer Sisters. Those musicians are documented on Betty Davis's 1973 self-titled debut, not on *They Say I'm Different*. Wikipedia's album personnel list and a track-by-track liner-note transcription at historicalalbumsdatabase.wordpress.com both place an entirely different band on the 1974 album: bass by Larry Johnson; drums split between Mike Clark, Nicky Neal, Willy Sparks and Ted Sparks; guest guitar by Buddy Miles; backing vocals by Debbie Burrell, Elaine Clarke, Mary Jones and Trudy Perkins. The historicalalbumsdatabase track-level credits specifically tag "Your Mama Wants Ya Back" with Merl Saunders on electric piano and Jimmy Godwin on rhythm guitar. Treat the Larry Graham / Gregg Errico story as a probable web error and credit the *They Say I'm Different* band instead.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The mama trick."** The lyric uses the singer's mother as a guilt lever to call a wandering lover home, flipping the usual seduction script: instead of "I want you back," it's "your mama wants you back." A natural angle for unpacking how Betty wrote desire and family obligation as the same string. Source: https://sonichits.com/video/Betty_Davis/Your_Mama_Wants_Ya_Back
- **"Self-produced and uncredited."** Betty wrote, arranged, and produced *They Say I'm Different* herself in 1974, an almost unheard-of credit profile for a Black woman in funk at the time, and got no industry traction for it. The track is a tight three-and-a-half-minute showcase of that producer's ear. Source: https://www.popmatters.com/betty-davis-betty-davis-they-say-im-different-2496212994.html
- **"Anybody's funk band."** For album two she ditched the Sly-and-Santana all-star roster of her debut and built a working band of mostly unknowns at Record Plant Sausalito. The groove on "Your Mama" proves the music was hers, not the famous players'. Source: https://beatpatrol.wordpress.com/2009/02/28/betty-davis-betty-davis-1973-and-they-say-im-different-1974-2/

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The bass line locks in immediately and barely leaves the same handful of notes for three and a half minutes. Listen for the small rhythmic tweaks the bassist makes over the course of the track. What does he change, and when?
- Count how many different ways Betty sings the word "mama." Which one lands hardest, and why?
- This song runs 3:27, which is short for 1974 funk. Listen for what gets cut: there is no extended jam, no fade, no breakdown. Decide whether the discipline helps the groove or starves it.

#### Album

- *They Say I'm Different* was recorded at Record Plant, Sausalito, in 1973 and released on Just Sunshine Records in 1974. Betty Davis wrote, arranged and self-produced every track, an exceptionally rare credit profile for a Black woman in funk at the time. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Say_I%27m_Different
- For her second album she dropped the celebrity backing band of her debut (Larry Graham, Gregg Errico, Neal Schon, Pointer Sisters, Tower of Power horns) and assembled a largely unknown working band, with Buddy Miles and Headhunters drummer Mike Clark as the most notable holdovers from the star-studded first record. — Source: https://beatpatrol.wordpress.com/2009/02/28/betty-davis-betty-davis-1973-and-they-say-im-different-1974-2/
- The album peaked at #46 on the Billboard Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums chart in 1974, then re-entered the German Albums chart at #41 in 2023, nearly fifty years after release. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Say_I%27m_Different
- The 2007 reissue on Light in the Attic, with liner notes by Oliver "O-Dub" Wang, is what brought the album back into wide circulation. — Source: https://www.popmatters.com/betty-davis-betty-davis-they-say-im-different-2496212994.html
- Just Sunshine Records was Woodstock co-founder Michael Lang's label, distributed by Gulf+Western's Famous Music Group from 1971-1974. The label's roster also included Karen Dalton, Mississippi Fred McDowell, and Betty Davis. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lang_(producer)
- The lead single from the album was "He Was a Big Freak" b/w "Shoo-B-Doop and Cop Him" (Just Sunshine JSA-516, May 1974). "Your Mama Wants Ya Back" does not appear to have been issued as a single. — Source: https://www.funkmysoul.gr/betty-davis-1974-they-say-im-different/

#### Artist

- Born Betty Gray Mabry on July 26, 1944 in Durham, North Carolina; died February 9, 2022 in Homestead, Pennsylvania at 77. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- Betty married Miles Davis in September 1968; they divorced about a year later. She introduced him to Sly Stone and Jimi Hendrix, and is credited with shaping the cultural and aesthetic environment that produced *Bitches Brew* (the album's title was reportedly her suggestion). — Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis
- She was effectively barred from U.S. television in the 1970s for what venues and networks called a sexually aggressive stage persona; her records sold better in Europe than in the U.S. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- After her father's death in 1980 she withdrew from the music industry entirely, moved to Homestead, Pennsylvania, and lived quietly until the 2017 documentary *Betty: They Say I'm Different* prompted a wider rediscovery. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- A handful of sources (BlackPast.org, NC state arts pages) list her birth year as 1945 rather than 1944; Wikipedia, IMDb, and her major obituaries (Rolling Stone, NPR) all use 1944. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis ; https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/betty-davis-1945-2022/

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty.

- **Classic Funk.** The track sits squarely in the 1969-1977 classic-funk window the book defines, was issued on a major-distributed label (Famous Music handled distribution for Just Sunshine), and its primary identity is funk rather than funk fused with another idiom. AllMusic files the album under R&B / Funk / Soul / Psychedelic Soul. — Sources: https://www.allmusic.com/album/they-say-im-different-mw0000458848 ; ./Matter/On Genres & Regions.md
- **Deep Funk.** Equally defensible. The album sold poorly on release (peak #46 R&B), Betty was effectively shut out of mainstream U.S. radio and TV, and the record's wide audience came decades later through crate-diggers and the 2007 Light in the Attic reissue. That arc fits the book's deep-funk definition (overlooked on release, found later through DJs and re-issues) more cleanly than most "classic funk" hits do. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Say_I%27m_Different ; https://www.popmatters.com/betty-davis-betty-davis-they-say-im-different-2496212994.html
- **Funk Rock.** A weaker third option. The album's overall sound has a blues-rock edge (Buddy Miles on guitar elsewhere on the record, the screamed vocal attack), but "Your Mama Wants Ya Back" specifically is one of the more straight-ahead funk grooves on the LP and not the strongest funk-rock specimen.

Recommendation: Deep Funk best captures the song's commercial fate and the way most modern listeners will encounter it; Classic Funk is the cleanest by-the-book sonic match.
