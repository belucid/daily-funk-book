### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Cover songs as conscious campaign trail."** Wake Up! was conceived as Legend's musical response to the 2008 Obama campaign — soul protest covers re-armed for a new political moment. Compared to What is the album's most pointed political statement, and Black Thought's verse drags the 1969 lyric into the Bush-era present. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Up!_(John_Legend_and_the_Roots_album)

- **"Tryin' to make it real."** McDaniels wrote the line "tryin' to make it real, compared to what?" in 1966 as an indictment of pretense — the Vietnam War, LBJ, consumer culture, racial double-talk. Track the line's portability: how does a 1966 lyric still bite in 2010, and where does it land in 2026? Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compared_to_What

- **"What a Roots cover sounds like."** Questlove explicitly said he wanted to "choose cover songs that were so under the radar, so uniquely interpreted, that it would take you a second to realise that these are cover songs at all." Compared to What is a stress test of that thesis — a song with 270+ recorded versions, including one of the most famous live jazz performances ever pressed. Source: https://albumism.com/features/john-legend-and-the-roots-wake-up-album-anniversary

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The McCann–Harris Swiss Movement original is a smoky, churning piano-and-sax jam captured live at Montreux. The Legend/Roots version is a clean studio cut with a featured Black Thought rap verse. Cue them up back-to-back: where does the Roots version honour the original's swagger, and where does it sand off the rough edges?

- Legend takes McDaniels' lyric at face value and sings it like a Sunday sermon. Listen for the moment where the band drops out and his voice carries the indictment alone. Does the polish dilute the protest, or does it make the words easier to actually hear?

- Black Thought's verse updates McDaniels' 1966 grievances with 2010 ones. Track which lines are McDaniels and which are Trotter — and ask which century the loudest complaint belongs to.

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- The song was written by Gene McDaniels and copyrighted in 1966; he wrote it specifically for his friend Les McCann, who cut the first recorded version that August. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compared_to_What
- The definitive recording is the Les McCann / Eddie Harris live take from June 21, 1969 at the Montreux Jazz Festival — McCann and Harris weren't booked to play together and agreed on the spot. "Compared to What" opened Swiss Movement, sold over a million copies, hit #35 R&B, and effectively put Montreux on the international map. — Source: https://eugenemcdaniels.com/compared-to-what
- By 2011 the song had been recorded by more than 270 artists, including Ray Charles, Roberta Flack (her debut single, 1969), and Brian Auger. It has also been used in Casino (1995), Boogie Nights (1997), and The Man from U.N.C.L.E. (2015). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compared_to_What
- McDaniels' lyric was a direct shot at the Vietnam War and President Lyndon B. Johnson, and also touched second-wave feminist concerns including reproductive rights — unusually pointed material for a 1966 commercial release. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compared_to_What

### Album
- Wake Up! was released September 21, 2010 on John Legend's G.O.O.D. Music imprint through Columbia Records. It is mostly covers of late-1960s and 1970s socially conscious soul, with one original ("Shine"). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Up!_(John_Legend_and_the_Roots_album)
- The album was inspired by the 2008 Obama campaign. Legend: "I was in the middle of campaigning for Barack Obama and feeling inspired by the atmosphere in the country at the time." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Up!_(John_Legend_and_the_Roots_album)
- It debuted at #8 on the Billboard 200 (63,000 first-week copies) and hit #3 on the R&B/Hip-Hop Albums chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Up!_(John_Legend_and_the_Roots_album)
- Wake Up! won three Grammys at the 53rd Annual Grammy Awards on February 13, 2011: Best R&B Album, Best Traditional R&B Vocal Performance ("Hang On In There"), and Best R&B Song ("Shine"). — Source: https://www.grammy.com/videos/53rd-annual-grammy-awards-pre-telecast-best-rb-album
- Questlove's stated curatorial goal: pick cover songs so obscure or so uniquely interpreted that listeners wouldn't immediately register them as covers. Compared to What is the album's most exposed exception — it's a famous song treated with relative reverence. — Source: https://albumism.com/features/john-legend-and-the-roots-wake-up-album-anniversary

### Artist
- The Roots formed in 1987 at the Philadelphia High School for the Creative and Performing Arts when drummer Ahmir "Questlove" Thompson and rapper Tariq "Black Thought" Trotter were 16 and 15 respectively, busking on Philadelphia street corners with Questlove on bucket drums. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roots
- John Legend (born John Roger Stephens, December 28, 1978, Springfield, Ohio) was the first artist signed to Kanye West's GOOD Music. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend
- The Roots became the house band for Late Night with Jimmy Fallon in 2009, which is the world Wake Up! was made inside — Legend would later perform on the show with them many times. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roots
- Gene McDaniels (Eugene Booker McDaniels, b. February 12, 1935, Kansas City, KS; d. July 29, 2011, Kittery Point, ME) had earlier pop hits — "A Hundred Pounds of Clay" (#3, 1961) and "Tower of Strength" (#5, 1961) — before pivoting to a more politically charged, Black-consciousness writing voice; "Compared to What" and Roberta Flack's #1 "Feel Like Makin' Love" (1974) are the products of that pivot. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_McDaniels

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Nu-Funk**.

Per the book's `On Genres.md`, Nu-Funk is "a retro funk revival scene that emerged in the mid-1990s, shaped by DJs and bands capturing the feel of funk 45s while making new records in that spirit." Wake Up! is a 2010 album of soul/funk covers performed by a hip-hop band and a contemporary soul singer aiming at the spirit of 1960s/70s consciousness records. That's adjacent to Nu-Funk's project, but the cultural lineage runs through hip-hop and contemporary soul, not the 45-flipping DJ scene.

Alternative suggestions:

- **Classic Funk.** The book defines classic funk as the 1969–1977 era. The Legend/Roots version is a 2010 recording, so by the book's own era boundary it doesn't qualify. Mention only because the source material (McCann/Harris 1969) is squarely within the classic-funk window. Not a strong fit for the cover.

- **Funk Rock / Jazz-Funk hybrid.** Compared to What has always been a jazz-soul track at heart — piano-led, sax-forward, vocal indictment over a swung-but-funky pocket. The Roots' arrangement keeps that DNA. Of the book's defined genres, **Jazz-Funk** is the closest match to the song's musical bones, even on the 2010 recording.

- **Keep Nu-Funk.** Defensible as the book's only label for "contemporary retro funk revivalism." Wake Up! is broadly part of that retro-conscious-soul current of the late-2000s/2010s (alongside Sharon Jones & the Dap-Kings, Lee Fields, Charles Bradley, etc.). If Nu-Funk is the book's home for "retro-funk made today," this entry belongs there.

**Suggestion ranking:** Nu-Funk (current) is acceptable; Jazz-Funk is musically the most accurate; if the book wants a "post-revival, hip-hop-adjacent contemporary soul cover" bucket, Nu-Funk is the closest existing tag.
