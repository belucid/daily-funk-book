Today’s song, "Dedicated to the Press," finds Betty Davis taunting a system that never understood her. After two albums on a smaller label, *Nasty Gal* was supposed to be her breakthrough: a chance to bring her brand of raw funk to a major label. Island Records’ Chris Blackwell bought out her earlier contract on Robert Palmer’s recommendation, but the new album underperformed commercially, and Island shelved her follow-up. Davis’ commercial recording career was effectively over.

That was a travesty, as *Nasty Gal* may have been the funkiest album released in 1975. Her funk-rock sound wasn't commercially rewarded, but the world eventually caught up with late-1970s era Parliament-Funkadelic and the funk-rock that followed.

Davis’ mix of funk, rock, sex, humor, and confrontation was too much for the industry in the moment, especially from a woman. Her reputation surged in the 2000s as funk lovers rediscovered her catalog. The reissues made it clear that she had not failed the era. The era failed her.

Davis died of cancer on February 9, 2022, just a week after her first diagnosis.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Talking back."** The song is funk's version of a press release: Betty answering her critics in the same medium that pilloried her, using "Extra, extra" as a hook and a taunt. This frames the entry around the rare moment when an artist puts the journalists in the song. Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/rediscovery/betty-davis-nasty-gal/, https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/20269/1/nasty-gal-betty-davis
2. **"Why do they blame me for what I am?"** Center the entry on the song's own rhetorical question, and the gap between Betty's self-image (a Black woman writing her own funk, picking her own band, choreographing her own moves) and the moral panic around her. The NAACP-and-pulpit-driven radio boycott is a fact pattern most readers will not know. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis, https://www.scotsman.com/news/people/obituary-betty-davis-flamboyant-funk-singer-famed-for-raucous-performances-3584543
3. **The major label that didn't get it.** Nasty Gal was Betty's first and only major-label record, made at Island after Chris Blackwell signed her on Robert Palmer's recommendation. The album underperformed, Island shelved her follow-up, and Betty was effectively done. "Dedicated to the Press" is the sound of an artist taunting a system that was about to cancel her. Sources: https://lightintheattic.net/blogs/features/betty-davis-nasty-gal, https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/20269/1/nasty-gal-betty-davis

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Listen for the "Extra, extra, have you read about me?" newspaper-vendor hook. How does Betty use that one phrase to shift the song from gossip column to comeback?
2. Funk House plays this loose and live, almost like a road-tested groove caught in a single take. Pick out Carlos Morales's guitar against Larry Johnson's bass: who is leading the bite, and who is keeping the pocket?
3. Betty was producing herself, in her late twenties, on her first major-label album. Listen for one moment in the vocal where you hear her smile at her critics, and one where you hear her done with them.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The song is co-written by Betty Davis and her bassist Larry Johnson, the only track on Nasty Gal where Johnson gets a co-write. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album)
- The lyric opens by cribbing the old newsboy holler: "Extra, extra, have you read about me? They say I stick out my tongue quite lecherously..." It is one of the few funk records that makes its own bad press the hook of the song. Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/rediscovery/betty-davis-nasty-gal/
- The song's central refrain, "Why do they blame me for what I am?", reads less like a complaint and more like a thesis statement for the album. Source: https://www.last.fm/music/Betty+Davis/_/Dedicated+to+the+Press/+lyrics
- Wax Poetics argues Betty was actually misdirecting her aim: "the press loved her records, Nasty Gal in particular." The real problem was format-bound radio programmers who heard her as "too rock for Black radio" and "too funked up for white radio." Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/rediscovery/betty-davis-nasty-gal/
- The track was released as a 7" single backed with the album closer "The Lone Ranger" in 1975. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/527457-Betty-Davis-Dedicated-To-The-Press

#### Album

- Nasty Gal was recorded in May 1975 at Media Sound in New York City, with Betty producing and a young Bob Clearmountain (later one of the most decorated mix engineers in pop) as engineer. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album)
- The backing band, Funk House, was Carlos Morales (guitar/lead vocals), Larry Johnson (bass), Nicky Neal (drums/backing vocals) and Fred Mills (keyboards/vocals), augmented by Errol "Crusher" Bennett on congas, James Allen Smith on ARP synth and Buddy Williams on percussion. Several of them had been playing together as kids in North Carolina before Betty drafted them. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/blogs/features/betty-davis-nasty-gal
- One track on the LP, the ballad "You and I," is co-written by Miles Davis and arranged/conducted by Gil Evans, an island of tenderness on a record otherwise built for confrontation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album)
- This was Betty's first and only major-label album. Chris Blackwell signed her after Robert Palmer (yes, that Robert Palmer) made the introduction. Island declined to release her 1976 follow-up Is It Love or Desire?, which sat unreleased for 33 years. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/blogs/features/betty-davis-nasty-gal, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- The album peaked at #54 on Billboard's Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums chart in 1976; "Shut Off the Lights" stalled at #97 R&B. There were no radio hits. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album)
- Initial press was rough, Pitchfork later scored the reissue 8.4 and AllMusic gave it 4.5/5. The reissue work was done by Light in the Attic Records and is what put Betty back into the conversation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album)

#### Artist

- Betty was born Betty Gray Mabry on July 26, 1944 in Durham, North Carolina, and was raised partly on her grandmother's farm in Reidsville, NC, where she heard her first blues records. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis
- She wrote her first song, "I'm Going to Bake That Cake of Love," at age 12. Source: https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/betty-davis-1945-2022/
- She was one of the first Black models featured in Seventeen and Glamour, and ran the Cellar nightclub in Manhattan in the mid-1960s, where she befriended Jimi Hendrix and Sly Stone. Source: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis
- She married Miles Davis in September 1968 and was on the cover of Filles de Kilimanjaro that same year, with a track ("Mademoiselle Mabry") named after her. The marriage lasted about a year. Miles credited her with introducing him to Hendrix and Sly, the seedbed for his electric turn on In a Silent Way and Bitches Brew. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- She wrote "Uptown (to Harlem)" for the Chambers Brothers in 1967, before her own recording career took off. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- Betty was barred from network television and saw shows boycotted by religious groups, and her records were condemned by the NAACP, which lobbied stations not to play her. Some of this is explicitly named on Wikipedia and corroborated by her UK obituary. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis, https://www.scotsman.com/news/people/obituary-betty-davis-flamboyant-funk-singer-famed-for-raucous-performances-3584543
- After Island dropped her, she completed one more album of sessions in 1979 (eventually released as bootlegs/Crashin' from Passion), retreated to Pittsburgh after her father's death in the early 1980s, and effectively vanished from public life for 25 years. Source: https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/20269/1/nasty-gal-betty-davis, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
- She died in Homestead, Pennsylvania on February 9, 2022, age 77, of cancer. Source: https://www.npr.org/2022/02/09/1079644021/betty-davis-funk-pioneer-dies, https://www.washingtonpost.com/obituaries/2022/02/10/funk-singer-betty-davis-dead/

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk (suggested primary).** Nasty Gal sits squarely in the 1969–1977 classic funk window the book defines, and unlike Betty's first two records on Just Sunshine, this one was a major-label release on Island with a real shot at "the commercial pipeline." Tight 3:40 runtime, vocal hook, no extended improvisation, no rock guitar dominance. By the book's own working definition, that's classic funk. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album), and book's own definition in `Matter/On Genres & Regions.md`
2. **Funk Rock (strong alternative).** Betty's whole catalog is routinely described as a funk-rock fusion, and the album's guitar tone, attitude and vocal aggression read closer to funk-rock than to Mavis-era classic funk. Wax Poetics specifically frames her as "too rock for Black radio." If the book wants Betty to represent the rock end of the funk spectrum, this is the lane. Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/rediscovery/betty-davis-nasty-gal/, https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/20269/1/nasty-gal-betty-davis
3. **Psychedelic Funk (weak, do not recommend).** Sometimes claimed for Betty by association with the Sly/Hendrix axis, but "Dedicated to the Press" is not surreal, not character-driven, and not building an invented world the way the book defines psychedelic funk. Skip.

Recommendation: **Classic Funk** as a confident default; **Funk Rock** if the author wants to use this entry to widen the book's funk-rock count.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - New York (set).** The album was tracked at Media Sound in New York City, Betty herself spent her formative musical years in Greenwich Village and at the Cellar, and Island's US operation was based in NYC. By the book's own "where the recording happened" criterion, this is defensible. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasty_Gal_(album), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Davis
2. **US - Southeast (alternative).** Betty was born in Durham, NC and the core of Funk House (Larry Johnson, Nicky Neal, Fred Mills) were childhood friends from North Carolina who brought their NC R&B chops to the band. If the book reads Betty as a Southern artist who happened to record in New York, this is the better tag. Source: https://lightintheattic.net/blogs/features/betty-davis-nasty-gal, https://www.britannica.com/biography/Betty-Davis
3. **No new region needed** — both options exist in the enumeration.

Recommendation: leave as **US - New York** unless the writer's theme leans on Betty's Southern roots, in which case switch to **US - Southeast** with an audit line.
