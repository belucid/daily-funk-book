### Writing Theme Suggestions

The `writing theme` field is currently empty. Suggested angles:

1. **"The jazz musician who needed some money"** — The album title was reportedly a sly nod to Harris's actual financial state at the time. According to Jive Time Records, "the album title reflected Harris's genuine financial situation, though the project inadvertently became his best work of the decade." A great hook for an entry that frames the song as documentary autobiography in funk form. (Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/)

2. **"Sly's drum machine, Eddie's Varitone"** — Frame the track as the moment a respected post-bop tenor saxophonist absorbs Sly Stone's lo-fi electronic textures from *There's a Riot Goin' On* / *Fresh* and runs them through his own electrically amplified saxophone. A clean, surprising lineage hook. (Source: Wikipedia describes the track using "the same drum machine that had recently become Sly's obsession.")

3. **"Eddie steps to the mic"** — *I Need Some Money* was Harris's first all-singing album, the start of "a series of humorous hard-luck songs that would be the flagships of his next few LPs." A nice angle on a career pivot at age 40 from a player to a singer-bandleader. (Source: AllMusic via https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/)

4. **"Too weird to play"** — Musician John Dwyer (Thee Oh Sees / Osees) bought his copy for $5 in a used jazz bin and discovered a previous radio DJ had written "too weird to play" next to the track. He embraced it as the perfect epitaph. A great hook for a "deep cut even now" entry. (Source: https://www.ravensingstheblues.com/john-dwyer-on-eddie-harris-i-need-some-money/)

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The track opens with the catchy vocal complaint that gives the album its name, riding on a "chugging Varitone groove" — Harris's tenor sax run through a Selmer Varitone amplification system. — Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/
- The track's claustrophobic, drum-machine-driven feel was modeled directly on Sly Stone's *There's a Riot Goin' On* and *Fresh*: "Harris makes full use of the drum machine that had recently become Sly's obsession, using his voice to inhabit the same claustrophobic, narcotized space that was Sly's stomping ground." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Need_Some_Money
- John Dwyer of Thee Oh Sees / Osees calls "I Don't Want Nobody" (the album's other showpiece) one of only four songs that makes him "well up on a night-time drive on tour"; he discovered the album via TV on the Radio producer Dave Sitek and bought his copy for $5 with a previous DJ's note "too weird to play" scribbled next to the track. — Source: https://www.ravensingstheblues.com/john-dwyer-on-eddie-harris-i-need-some-money/
- The track was issued as a 7" single on Atlantic 45-3245 in 1975, backed with "I Don't Want Nobody." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/332596-Eddie-Harris-I-Need-Some-Money-I-Dont-Want-Nobody (single master, verified via Discogs API 2026-05-16) and https://www.discogs.com/release/1104949-Eddie-Harris-I-Need-Some-Money-I-Dont-Want-Nobody (1975 US 7" pressing).

#### Album

- *I Need Some Money* was Harris's first all-singing album, kicking off "a series of humorous hard-luck songs that would be the flagships of his next few LPs." (AllMusic, quoted via Jive Time Records.) — Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/
- The album was recorded at Paragon Studios in Chicago on two dates: July 15 and December 2, 1974, and released on Atlantic in 1975 (catalog SD 1669), produced by Geoffrey Haslam. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Need_Some_Money
- The closing track, "That's It," is described as "an alternate-world version of Quincy Jones' Sanford and Son theme — slower, but just as inspirational and grimy." — Source: https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/
- Eddie Harris plays not only tenor saxophone and Varitone on the record but also piano, organ, electric piano, reed trumpet, and vocals. The reed trumpet was one of his own hybrid inventions. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Need_Some_Money and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- AllMusic's review awarded 2.5 stars out of 5; the broader cult around the record has built since. — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/i-need-some-money-mw0000944796 (rating per Wikipedia summary; review text not accessible from page fetch)

#### Artist

- Eddie Harris was born October 20, 1934 in Chicago, Illinois, to a Cuban father and a Mississippi-born mother, and started singing in Baptist churches before he ever picked up a horn. — Source: https://eddieharris.com/about/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- He studied under the legendary Captain Walter Dyett at DuSable High School in Chicago, the same program that produced Nat King Cole, Dinah Washington, Gene Ammons, and Johnny Griffin. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- His 1961 debut LP *Exodus to Jazz* (VeeJay) yielded the title track "Exodus," reportedly the first jazz instrumental to be certified gold. — Source: https://eddieharris.com/about/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- He composed "Freedom Jazz Dance," later canonized by Miles Davis on the 1966 *Miles Smiles* sessions. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- Harris was the first jazz player to record with a Selmer Varitone (1966) and went on to invent the reed trumpet, the saxobone, and the guitorgan. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varitone and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- He died of congestive heart failure in Los Angeles on November 5, 1996, at age 62. — Source: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/11/07/Sax-player-Eddie-Harris-dead-at-60/9950847342800/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Harris
- His 1969 live album *Swiss Movement* with Les McCann is one of the best-selling jazz LPs of all time and earned Grammy attention; partnering with McCann likely seeded Harris's later turn toward singing. — Sources: https://eddieharris.com/about/ and AllMusic note that he was "perhaps inspired by his partner Les McCann's vocal forays" via https://jivetimerecords.com/2020/11/eddie-harris-i-need-some-money-atlantic-1975/

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty. Top candidates:

1. **Jazz-Funk (recommended)** — This is the strongest fit per the book's working definitions. Eddie Harris was a card-carrying jazz musician (post-bop, soul jazz, Atlantic-era electric experimenter) playing funk, with extended track lengths across the LP, room for soloing, and a rhythm section in the funk pocket. The book's definition specifically calls out "funk played by jazz musicians whose earlier careers were in more traditional jazz styles" and "longer track lengths, room for extended improvisation." That's *I Need Some Money* exactly. — Source: book's "On Genres & Regions" matter and AllMusic style tags (Jazz; Hard Bop, Soul Jazz) via https://www.allmusic.com/album/i-need-some-money-mw0000944796

2. **Classic Funk** — Defensible alternative since the track is on a major label (Atlantic), released within the 1969–1977 classic-era window, and the title cut clocks in at a radio-friendly 3:10 with a vocal hook and Sly-style drum-machine groove. The argument against: the track's primary identity feels more like a jazz player making a funk move than a funk record proper, and the LP overall has long-form jazz-funk sprawl. — Source: book's "On Genres & Regions" matter

3. **Deep Funk** — Worth mentioning because despite being on Atlantic, the album was an underperformer commercially and has been rediscovered through crate-digging culture (John Dwyer's "$5 in a used jazz bin" and the "too weird to play" DJ note are textbook deep-funk-rediscovery beats). However, the book's definition of deep funk leans toward regional/private-press and small-label obscurities, so this is a weaker fit than Jazz-Funk. — Source: https://www.ravensingstheblues.com/john-dwyer-on-eddie-harris-i-need-some-money/

**Recommendation:** Jazz-Funk.
