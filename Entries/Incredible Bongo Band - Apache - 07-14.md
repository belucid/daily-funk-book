There are plenty of strange stories in funk history, but the Incredible Bongo Band’s “Apache” is a contender for the wildest.

Start with the drum and percussion break. It helped make "Apache" one of the most sampled recordings ever, appearing in more than 800 tracks. DJ Kool Herc called it the national anthem of hip-hop. The mystery is that even the musicians involved do not know who played drums on the famous break. The track was cut across two sessions, one with Jim Gordon on drums and another with Kat Hendrikse, and no one is certain which take made the final master

Then comes the next twist. The Incredible Bongo Band was not really a band at all. It was assembled by MGM Records executive Michael Viner from a rotating cast of Los Angeles session players. It was originally created to record music for the 1972 B-movie *The Thing with Two Heads*, built around the bizarre premise of a racist white man's head being transplanted onto a Black man’s body. Even the group's personnel list has a mythic quality, with Ringo Starr appearing on its second album.

Finally, back to one of those drummers, Jim Gordon, who was born on this day in 1945. Gordon's life took a tragic turn when he murdered his mother during a psychotic episode linked to schizophrenia. He died while incarcerated in 2023, which adds a sad coda to a song that has been powering hip-hop for forty years.

Is Apache the most sampled song in history?
**Resolved: No.** According to WhoSampled, "Apache" has been sampled in approximately 807 songs, which is impressive but far from the most sampled. The most sampled track in history is "Amen, Brother" by The Winstons (over 7,498 samples), followed by "Think (About It)" by Lyn Collins (4,261 samples). "Apache" is highly sampled but the "most sampled song in history" claim is not accurate. The entry text currently says "over 700 recordings," which is closer to the WhoSampled count of ~807 and is a defensible number. The claim "most sampled" should be softened to something like "one of the most sampled." Sources: [WhoSampled Most Sampled Tracks](https://www.whosampled.com/most-sampled-tracks/), [WhoSampled Apache page](https://www.whosampled.com/Incredible-Bongo-Band/Apache/)

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The break heard round the world"** — The central paradox of "Apache": a throwaway cover of a British surf instrumental, recorded by anonymous LA session musicians for a B-movie producer's vanity project, became the single most important drum break in music history. The gap between the track's humble origins and its seismic impact is the story.

2. **"A ghost band's immortal groove"** — The Incredible Bongo Band never really existed. No fixed lineup, no touring identity, no public faces. The musicians who played on "Apache" didn't even know which drummer's take made the final cut (Jim Gordon or Kat Hendrikse). Yet this phantom ensemble created the most sampled break of all time. The anonymity is part of the story: hip-hop took an uncredited groove and made it the foundation of a culture.

3. **"From cowboys to concrete"** — Jerry Lordan wrote "Apache" to evoke "the courage and savagery of the Indian Apache warrior Massai" after watching a 1954 western. From fake Hollywood cowboys-and-Indians music, to a British guitar instrumental, to a funky bongo-drenched LA session, to the Bronx block parties where Kool Herc spun it into the national anthem of hip-hop. Each hand that touched it stripped away the original meaning and replaced it with something new.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Apache" was not released as a single from *Bongo Rock* and did not chart on its own. It became famous entirely through DJ culture and crate-digging. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- DJ Kool Herc called it "the national anthem of hip-hop." The phrase was popularized by journalist Will Hermes in a 2006 New York Times article about the record's re-release. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_(instrumental)
- Rolling Stone ranked the Incredible Bongo Band's version #31 on its 2022 list of "200 Greatest Dance Songs of All Time." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_(instrumental)
- The track has been sampled in over 700 songs according to some estimates. Notable samples include: Grandmaster Flash's "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" (1981), Nas's "Made You Look" (2002, producer Salaam Remi slowed the break), Goldie's "Inner City Life" (1994, bridging it into drum and bass), Missy Elliott's "We Run This," and C+C Music Factory's "Things That Make You Go Hmmm." — Sources: https://www.ethanhein.com/wp/2010/apache/, https://stereogum.com/2025842/apache-the-samples/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- Kool Herc's technique of mixing two copies of the record to extend the drum break, which he called "the merry-go-round," helped coin the term "breakbeat." — Source: https://www.breaktothebeat.com/classic-break-beats/classic-breaks-part-3a-apache/
- The Sugarhill Gang recorded their own version of "Apache" in 1981, which actually did chart: #53 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #13 on the R&B chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_(instrumental)
- David Bowie borrowed melody elements from "Apache" for "How Does the Grass Grow?" on his 2013 album *The Next Day*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_(instrumental)

#### Album

- *Bongo Rock* peaked at #197 on the Billboard 200 and #58 on the Billboard Top Soul LPs chart. A modest showing that belies its eventual influence. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongo_Rock_(album)
- The album was recorded at Can-Base Studios in Vancouver, Canada. Michael Viner chose the location to take advantage of Canadian content laws (CanCon regulations), which had helped promote the earlier single "Bongo Rock." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- The album was featured in Robert Dimery's *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- "Bongolia" from the album was used in Edgar Wright's *Baby Driver* (2017). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- The entire album is essentially covers of 1950s and 1960s instrumentals reimagined with heavy percussion and funk arrangements, including "In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida" (Iron Butterfly) and "Let There Be Drums" (Sandy Nelson). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongo_Rock_(album)

#### Artist

- The Incredible Bongo Band was never a real band. It was a rotating cast of elite LA session musicians assembled by MGM executive Michael Viner, producer Mike Curb, and arranger Perry Botkin Jr. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- The project originated from the 1972 B-movie *The Thing With Two Heads* (a film about a racist whose head is transplanted onto a Black man's body). Viner was called in to supplement the soundtrack. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- Session musicians on the album included some of the most accomplished players in LA: Jerry Scheff (Elvis's TCB Band bassist, who also played on the Doors' "L.A. Woman"), Steve Douglas (Phil Spector's Wall of Sound regular), Mike Deasy (Beach Boys' *Pet Sounds*), and King Errisson (Bahamian percussionist). — Sources: https://stereogum.com/2025842/apache-the-samples/columns/breaks-with-tradition, https://www.mrbongo.com/blogs/news/9850758-the-incredible-bongo-band-who-were-they
- There is a genuine dispute over whether Jim Gordon or Canadian drummer Kat Hendrikse played the famous drum break on the final recording. Both were in the sessions. The Mr Bongo label notes this remains unresolved. — Source: https://www.mrbongo.com/blogs/news/9850758-the-incredible-bongo-band-who-were-they
- Jim Gordon, the drummer most commonly credited, co-wrote the piano coda to Derek and the Dominos' "Layla." He was later diagnosed with schizophrenia, murdered his mother in 1983, was sentenced to 16 years to life, and died in prison on March 13, 2023. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gordon_(musician)
- Michael Viner's other claim to fame: he helped produce Sammy Davis Jr.'s chart-topping cover of "The Candy Man" (1972). He also produced *The Best of Marcel Marceau* (1970), a novelty album of silence by the famous mime. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Viner
- Ringo Starr played drums on the second album, *The Return of the Incredible Bongo Band* (1974). — Source: https://www.mrbongo.com/blogs/news/9850758-the-incredible-bongo-band-who-were-they
- The 2013 documentary *Sample This*, directed by Dan Forrer and narrated by Gene Simmons (of KISS), chronicles the story of "Apache" and the band's influence on hip-hop. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band
- The Minnesota Lynx (WNBA) adopted "Apache" as their unofficial team anthem in 2007, dancing to it at center court after victories. For their first WNBA Finals appearance in 2011, they brought in The Sugarhill Gang to perform at halftime. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_(instrumental)

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** — The strongest fit. "Apache" was released in 1973, squarely in the classic funk era (1968-1977). It was produced within the commercial pipeline of a major label (MGM/Pride), features a funk groove built on heavy percussion and a locked-in rhythm section, and its primary identity is funk rather than a fusion with jazz, rock, or Latin styles. The track's structure, with its emphasis on rhythm and groove over melody, is textbook classic funk. — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongo_Rock_(album)

2. **Deep Funk** — A strong secondary case. "Apache" was overlooked on release (the album peaked at #197, and "Apache" itself was never even released as a single). It only found its audience later through crate-digging and DJ culture, which is the defining trait of deep funk per the book's definition: "delayed recognition." The track was a commercial nonentity that became one of the most important recordings in popular music history entirely through underground rediscovery. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredible_Bongo_Band

3. **Funk Rock** — A weaker but defensible case. The track features a prominent surf-rock guitar line (played by Mike Deasy, a *Pet Sounds* veteran) layered over the funk percussion. The original "Apache" was a rock instrumental, and the IBB version retains that guitar-driven melodic identity while funking up the rhythm section. It sits at a crossroads of rock instrumentation and funk groove. — Source: https://stereogum.com/2025842/apache-the-samples/columns/breaks-with-tradition
