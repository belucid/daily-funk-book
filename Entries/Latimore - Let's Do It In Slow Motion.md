### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The slow jam that drummers loved"**: This album cut had two unlikely second lives: Levon Helm (The Band's drummer/vocalist) cut a full cover in 1978, and DJ Premier looped it for Gang Starr's "Royalty" twenty years later. Frame the entry around how a quiet, self-penned Latimore slow groove kept getting picked up by rhythm men across rock and hip-hop. ([WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Latimore/Let's-Do-It-in-Slow-Motion/))
- **"A title that means exactly what it says"**: The song is a seduction set to a deliberately unhurried tempo, the antithesis of the disco speeding up the TK family of labels in 1976. Use the slow-motion conceit, both the come-on and the groove, against the four-on-the-floor world it was recorded next to. ([Wikipedia, TK Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TK_Records))
- **"Deep cut, deep reach"**: It was never a single, just track B2 on the LP, yet it has a richer afterlife than most of the charting songs around it. A case for paying attention to album cuts. ([Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/280787-Latimore-2-It-Aint-Where-You-Been-Its-Where-Youre-Goin))

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The whole record is funky, but this one slows the pulse down on purpose. Listen for how Latimore's Fender Rhodes and George "Chocolate" Perry's bass hold the pocket back. What makes a slow tempo still feel like funk and not a ballad?
- DJ Premier looped a piece of this for Gang Starr's "Royalty." Listen for the most loop-ready bar, the riff or vocal phrase that would survive being chopped and repeated, then go hear what Premier did with it. ([WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/3910/Gang-Starr-K-Ci-&-JoJo-Royalty-Latimore-Let's-Do-It-in-Slow-Motion/))
- The Band's Levon Helm liked this enough to record his own version in 1978. Play both back to back. What did a Tennessee-soul singer's slow groove give a roots-rock drummer that he couldn't get from his own catalog? ([Wikipedia, Levon Helm album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Helm_(1978_album)))

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Self-penned by Benjamin Latimore (writer credit "B. Latimore"), produced by Steve Alaimo. It is track B2 on the LP and was not released as a single. Source: [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/280787-Latimore-2-It-Aint-Where-You-Been-Its-Where-Youre-Goin)
- Covered by Levon Helm, drummer and lead vocalist of The Band, on his 1978 self-titled solo album, produced by Donald "Duck" Dunn of Booker T. & the M.G.'s, recorded at Cherokee Studios (Hollywood) and Muscle Shoals Sound. Source: [Wikipedia, Levon Helm (1978 album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Helm_(1978_album))
- Sampled by Gang Starr feat. K-Ci & JoJo on "Royalty," produced by DJ Premier, from the acclaimed 1998 album *Moment of Truth*. The Latimore sample anchors the track. Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/3910/Gang-Starr-K-Ci-&-JoJo-Royalty-Latimore-Let's-Do-It-in-Slow-Motion/)
- Also sampled by French rapper Rohff on "Le Même Quartier." Source: [WhoSampled, Latimore track page](https://www.whosampled.com/Latimore/Let's-Do-It-in-Slow-Motion/)

#### Album

- *It Ain't Where You Been... It's Where You're Goin'*, Glades 7509, released 1976. Album peaked at #181 Billboard Pop / #47 Black Albums (charting January 1977). Sources: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/1447563-Latimore-It-Aint-Where-You-Been-Its-Where-Youre-Goin), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Personnel are the core TK/Miami session crew: George "Chocolate" Perry (bass), Ron "Tubby" Ziegler (drums), Ron Peterson (guitar), Richie Puente (percussion), Latimore on keyboards, with horn and string arrangements by Mike Lewis; produced by Steve Alaimo. Source: [Discogs release](https://www.discogs.com/release/1447563-Latimore-It-Aint-Where-You-Been-Its-Where-Youre-Goin)
- The album's charting singles were "Somethin' 'Bout 'Cha" and a slow blues-soul reworking of KC & the Sunshine Band's "I Get Lifted" (the album closes with the Latimore take of that TK-family disco hit). Source: [Discogs master tracklist](https://www.discogs.com/master/280787-Latimore-2-It-Aint-Where-You-Been-Its-Where-Youre-Goin)

#### Artist

- Born Benjamin Latimore, September 7, 1939, in Charleston, Tennessee. Raised on country radio (Nashville's WLAC), Baptist church choir, and blues; sang his first church solo at 13. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Got his start in Miami as a sideman/pianist (including for Steve Alaimo) before recording for Henry Stone's Dade label around 1965, then moving to Glades in the early 1970s. He doubled as a TK house keyboard player. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Biggest hit: "Let's Straighten It Out" (1974), #1 R&B, #31 Hot 100. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))
- Inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 2017. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latimore_(musician))

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk** (already set): Defensible. It is a 1976 TK/Miami funk-soul album cut with a locked rhythm section and the classic-era house sound. As a slow groove it sits at the soul end of classic funk, but the rhythm-section identity and 1969–1977 era window fit the book's definition.
- **Jazz-Funk** (alternative): Latimore's keyboard work and the song's relaxed, harmonically warm feel push toward the Rhodes-forward jazz-funk territory; a reasonable secondary tag if the keyboard playing becomes the angle.
- Recommendation: keep **Classic Funk**; mention jazz-funk only if the entry foregrounds the Rhodes.
