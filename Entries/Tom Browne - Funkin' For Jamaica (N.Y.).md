The cover of Tom Browne’s *Love Approach* shows him in the pilot’s seat of a small plane, trumpet in hand. “Approach” is aviation jargon for preparing to land. Lest listeners think Browne was merely extending the title’s metaphor, the gatefold explained that he was a licensed commercial pilot who flew the Piper Seneca on the cover.

Browne started piano lessons at eleven, then switched to trumpet before attending Manhattan's High School of Music and Art. He saw music as a hobby, planning to become a professional pilot. While studying physics at Kingsborough Community College, he earned his pilot’s license and became involved in New York’s jazz scene, finding work as a sideman with Weldon Irvine, Woody Shaw, Freddie Hubbard, and the Fatback Band. Guitarist Earl Klugh introduced him to GRP Records, which signed him in 1979.

Today’s song, “Funkin’ for Jamaica (N.Y.),” comes from Browne’s second GRP album. The title refers not to the Caribbean nation, but to Jamaica, Queens, where Browne grew up. The song topped the US R&B singles chart but never crossed over to the *Billboard* Hot 100.

Browne continued recording into the 1980s, then stepped away from music to fly cargo planes for FedEx contractors. He returned to recording in the mid-1990s and has described needing both pursuits: the creative freedom of music and the structure and discipline of aviation.

### Open Research Questions

**OPEN: how, when and why did he learn to fly?**

Resolved on the "why," partially on the "when," thin on the "how" (no source pins the flight-training specifics).

- **Why (the key finding):** Flying was not a post-fame hobby, it was arguably his *first* chosen career. Browne went to Kingsborough (Community) College as a **physics major aiming to become a professional pilot**, and pursued aviation in parallel with music from early on. In his own words to Wax Poetics, he was "gearing towards an aviation career" and "Music was kinda something I did as my cool-out." So the more accurate framing is not "trumpeter who became a pilot" but "aspiring pilot who happened to blow up as a trumpeter first." Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- **Why he kept both:** He's described it as a left-brain/right-brain balance: music lets him be free and expressive, flying is structured and disciplined, and he says he needs both. Source (interview, accessible only via search-engine snippet; article itself is now paywalled): https://godanriver.com/entertainment_lifestyles/hanging-up-pilot-s-wings-tom-browne-making-music-a/article_7c2049ae-a1d9-11e4-9f55-738d7ebe9d44.html
- **When:** The aviation goal predates his 1979 recording debut (it's why he studied physics). He held a commercial license during his hit years, which is why the *Love Approach* (1980) cover shows him in the cockpit of a Piper Seneca. He then made flying his primary career from the mid-1980s. No source gives the date he first soloed or earned his ticket. Sources: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/funkin-for-jamaica-tom-browne/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- **How (unresolved):** No source reviewed (Wax Poetics, Wikipedia, WDET, Blues Boulevard, funknstuff/Truth in Rhythm, godanriver) details where or how he did his flight training. The most likely place to find it is the full **Truth in Rhythm, Episode 14** video interview (July 2017), which several writeups draw from but which isn't transcribed online. Flagging for the author as the best next lead if the "how" matters. Sources checked: https://funknstuff.net/tir-browne/ ; https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/

**OPEN: When did he leave to fly for FedEx? When did he return to music (he has albums in the '90's and 2000's)**

Resolved (with a caveat on the exact departure year).

- **Left music:** After his early-'80s GRP hits, Browne stepped away from the music scene in the **mid-1980s** to fly full time. WDET dates the exit to "the mid-1980s"; the Blues Boulevard Jazz bio pins it at roughly **1986**. No source gives a precise month/day. Sources: https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/ ; https://bluesboulevardjazzgreenville.com/artists/tom-browne/
- **What he flew:** He worked "many years as a charter and airline captain, most recently with a FedEx Feeder Company on ATR72 and Fokker F-27 aircraft." Note the nuance for the author: he flew for a **FedEx Feeder** (a regional contractor operating on FedEx's behalf), not FedEx's mainline jet fleet, and that was the *most recent* of several flying jobs, not necessarily the first. Sources: https://bluesboulevardjazzgreenville.com/artists/tom-browne/ ; https://www.allaboutjazz.com/musicians/tom-browne
- **Returned to recording:** **1994**, on the Hip-Bop label, with the (aptly titled) *Mo' Jamaica Funk*. His post-return discography then runs *Another Shade of Browne* (1996), *R 'n' Browne* (1999), and *S' Up* (2010), confirming the '90s/2000s albums the author flagged. Sources: https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)

**OPEN: how did he get started in music**

Resolved.

- Browne started on **piano at age 11**, then took up trumpet and was classically trained. Source: https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/
- He attended Manhattan's **High School of Music & Art**, winning a scholarship to study under **Murray Karpilovsky**, principal trumpeter of the NBC Symphony Orchestra, and later worked with jazz players including **Woody Shaw** and **Freddie Hubbard**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- He earned a **physics degree from Kingsborough College**, where he discovered jazz, and broke in professionally as a **sideman** for **Weldon Irvine**, **Sonny Fortune**, and the **Fatback Band** before going solo. Source: https://wdet.org/2020/11/16/jazz-trumpeter-tom-browne-makes-his-own-lane/
- Guitarist **Earl Klugh** introduced him to GRP founders **Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen**; Browne signed to the new **GRP** label in **1979** and debuted with *Browne Sugar* that year. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)

Discrepancy to flag: WDET names the school "Manhattan School of Music and Art," but the institution is more reliably the **High School of Music & Art** (the Manhattan public school in the LaGuardia lineage), which is what other sources cite. The two are being conflated; the High School of Music & Art reading is the safer one for print.
### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Funk as hometown memoir."** The title is literally an ode to Jamaica, Queens (the "N.Y." parenthetical exists to head off the Caribbean assumption). A jazz trumpeter turns his old neighborhood into a #1 party record — the entry can trace how place becomes groove.
- **"The trumpet player who became a pilot."** Browne rode this hit to R&B stardom at 25, then quietly left music to fly cargo planes for FedEx feeders. A frame about funk careers that don't follow the expected arc.
- **"The voice you know, the name you don't."** Toni Smith co-wrote and sang the indelible "Funk!" hook but spent her life as a background singer and died in 2020 in relative obscurity. A theme about the uncredited-in-memory voices that carry funk's most famous moments.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is billed as a jazz-funk record by a trumpeter, yet the hook everyone remembers is a shouted vocal chant, not a trumpet line. Count how long you wait before Browne's horn actually enters, and ask whose record this really is.
- Marcus Miller was a teenager when he laid down this bassline. Follow the bass through the whole track and decide whether it, or the vocal, is the real engine of the groove.
- Listen to the opening four bars in isolation. You've heard them sampled dozens of times (Mariah Carey, and others). Try to hear it fresh as a 1980 dance intro rather than a hip-hop building block.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- Reached #1 on the US R&B/Black singles chart (held the top for roughly a month, autumn 1980) yet never charted on the Billboard Hot 100 — a pure crossover-within-Black-radio phenomenon. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkin%27_for_Jamaica_(N.Y.)
- Peaked at #10 on the UK Singles Chart and #9 on the US dance chart. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkin%27_for_Jamaica_(N.Y.)
- The "Jamaica" is not the Caribbean nation but the Jamaica neighborhood of Queens, NYC, where Browne grew up — the "(N.Y.)" was added to the title to make that explicit. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkin%27_for_Jamaica_(N.Y.)
- Heavily sampled in hip-hop and dance; Mariah Carey built her 2001 single "Don't Stop (Funkin' 4 Jamaica)" on its intro, and it has been sampled/interpolated by acts including Tone Loc, Coolio and others. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkin%27_for_Jamaica_(N.Y.)
- Co-writer and lead vocalist Toni Smith (Thomassina Carrollyne Smith), a Bronx native, went on to a long career as an in-demand background singer and died January 28, 2020. Source: https://soultracks.com/story-toni-smith-dies/

#### Album
- The parent album *Love Approach* (1980) was Browne's second solo LP, produced by the GRP team of Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen, catalog GRP 5008 on Arista GRP. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/158930-Tom-Browne-Love-Approach
- The session assembled a remarkable young rhythm section: a teenaged Marcus Miller on bass, Bernard Wright on keys/synth, Omar Hakim and Buddy Williams on drums, Bobby Broom on guitar. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/158930-Tom-Browne-Love-Approach
- Discogs files the album under Jazz-Funk / Funk / Disco. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/158930-Tom-Browne-Love-Approach

#### Artist
- Tom Browne (b. October 30, 1954, Queens, NY) trained classically at the High School of Music & Art under an NBC Symphony trumpeter, and mentored with Woody Shaw and Freddie Hubbard before turning to jazz-funk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)
- After his early-'80s hits (this and 1981's "Thighs High"), Browne largely stepped away from music to become a commercial/cargo airline pilot. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Browne_(trumpeter)

### Genre Suggestions

- **Jazz Funk** (primary recommendation): Browne is a jazz trumpeter and Discogs styles this as Jazz-Funk; the book defines jazz-funk as funk played by jazz musicians, which fits exactly. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/158930-Tom-Browne-Love-Approach
