### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"Dropping the 'Jazz'"** — In 1971, The Jazz Crusaders became The Crusaders, a deliberate move to broaden their audience beyond the jazz room. "Stomp and Buck Dance," the opening track of 1974's Southern Comfort, is the payoff of that decision: Wayne Henderson's trombone-led strut aimed squarely at R&B and dance charts (it hit #41 R&B, #12 Dance). A frame about commercial pivots paying off, not selling out. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
2. **"Texas boys in a Hollywood studio"** — The Crusaders were Wheatley High School friends from Houston's Fifth Ward who moved west and grew into LA session royalty. Southern Comfort was cut at Wally Heider in Hollywood with newcomer Larry Carlton on guitar. The track title nods south, but the address is California. A frame about geographic identity and reinvention.
3. **"The composer behind the groove"** — Wayne Henderson wrote this tune, produced extensively outside the band, then left the Crusaders in 1976. Framing the track as a Henderson showcase (before his exit) gives you a specific, human angle rather than a generic band profile. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Henderson_(musician)

### Listening Mission Suggestions

1. Listen for the first time Larry Carlton's guitar enters. He joined the band as a full member on this album at age 26, and you can hear him feeling out how much room to take. Does he lead, or does he defer?
2. The tune is named for two Black American vernacular dances, "stomp" and "buck." Can you hear the dance in the rhythm, the hard downbeat, the percussive emphasis, or does the groove feel closer to a mid-70s studio strut than a folk step?
3. Count the horn voices. Wayne Henderson's trombone and Wilton Felder's tenor sax work in tight unison for much of the head. Where do they split off, and which voice do you follow when they do?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Stomp and Buck Dance" was released as a single and climbed to #41 on the Billboard R&B chart and #12 on the Dance chart in 1975, broad crossover for an instrumental jazz-funk opener — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Written by trombonist Wayne Henderson, one of his signature compositions in the Crusaders catalog — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- The title references two African American vernacular dance traditions: buck dancing (percussive folk dance with roots traced to enslaved communities, often credited as a forerunner of tap) and the stomp — Source: https://www.ncpedia.org/buck-dancing
- Sampled by Redman on "Let Da Monkey Out," Special Ed on "I'm the Magnificent (The Magnificent Remix)," and Jill Scott feat. Eve on "Shame" — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Crusaders/Stomp-and-Buck-Dance/
- Produced by Stewart Levine, the band's long-running production partner — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)

#### Album
- Southern Comfort is a double LP, released October 1974 on ABC/Blue Thumb, entered Billboard 200 on October 26, 1974, peaked at #31, hit #3 on the Soul LPs chart, and #1 on the Jazz LPs chart — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Recorded at Wally Heider Recording Studios in Hollywood, engineered by Rik Pekkonen and Peter Granet — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- This is the first Crusaders album on which Larry Carlton appears as a full band member (he was 26) — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- Track 10 on the double LP, "Lilies of the Nile" (9:35) by Wilton Felder, is already covered as a separate entry in this book (08-31)

#### Artist
- The band formed as high school friends at Wheatley High in Houston's Fifth Ward in 1954, originally called the Swingsters, then the Modern Jazz Sextet, then the Nite Hawks (briefly the house band at the New Frontier Hotel in Las Vegas), before becoming the Jazz Crusaders on Pacific Jazz in 1961 — Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/crusaders
- The name shortening from Jazz Crusaders to The Crusaders happened in 1971, a deliberate move to signal expansion beyond jazz — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Wayne Henderson left the band in 1976, two years after Southern Comfort, to focus on production work — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Henderson_(musician)

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Jazz-Funk (current)** — Best fit. The Crusaders are the template for jazz players moving into funk in the 1970s: founded as a jazz group (The Jazz Crusaders, 1961), shortened their name in 1971 to signal the shift, and by 1974 were working funk rhythms with jazz heads, unison horns, and extended instrumental passages. "Stomp and Buck Dance" is textbook jazz-funk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
2. **Soul Jazz** — A secondary possibility, but weaker. Soul jazz per the book's definition emphasizes smaller ensembles and the earlier bridge between hard bop and funk; by 1974 the Crusaders had graduated to bigger arrangements and a full funk rhythm section. Better saved for their 1960s Pacific Jazz era.
3. **Classic Funk** — Plausible because the single crossed over to the R&B chart, but the track is an instrumental led by trombone, tenor, and Rhodes with jazz-style unison lines. The jazz-player DNA outweighs the funk-first framing.

**Recommendation:** keep Jazz-Funk.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - California (current suggestion)** — By 1974 the band had been LA-based for 14+ years. Album recorded at Wally Heider Studios, Hollywood. Label (Blue Thumb) was LA-based. Best "where the track was made" answer.
2. **US - Texas** — The founding members (Sample, Felder, Hooper, Henderson) met at Wheatley High School in Houston's Fifth Ward. Their shared musical identity formed there. Best "where the sound came from" answer. Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/crusaders
3. **US - West** — A safe bucket covering LA without committing to California specifically, but less precise than US - California.

**Recommendation:** US - California for the LA session-band identity that defines their 1970s output. If the author prefers to honor the Houston origin story, US - Texas is defensible and sets up the "Texas boys in Hollywood" writing theme.
