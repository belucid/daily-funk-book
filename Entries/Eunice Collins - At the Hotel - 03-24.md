Eunice Collins walked into a small Chicago studio on a whim and recorded this track in a single take. She never signed a contract, never got paid, and then disappeared from the recording world.

Eunice called her song “What’s Your Hurry Baby,” but the studio owner renamed it “At the Hotel” without her input. The background singers refused to participate at first, so Eunice recruited others in the room to sing with her. The single-take, lo-fi session gave the track its intimate, distinctive sound.

Decades later, the track found a second life when Lion Babe built “Treat Me Like Fire” around a sample of it. The new attention led to its use over the end credits of a March 24, 2022 episode of Donald Glover’s *Atlanta*, and to a 7-inch reissue for Record Store Day that same year.

**1. "The one-take mystery woman"**
Collins walked into a studio by chance, recorded the song in a single take, never signed a contract, never got paid, and vanished from the music world for nearly 40 years. The story of how a song can outlive its own commercial neglect, and how the artist herself had no idea until strangers started calling, is a compelling frame for the entry.

**2. "The original title was 'What's Your Hurry Baby'"**
The studio owner changed the title without Collins's input, the backup singers initially refused to participate, and the lo-fi production quality gives the track its signature intimacy. This theme foregrounds the accidental nature of the recording's aesthetic, the idea that its cult status is partly built on qualities no one intended.

**3. "A $700 record she can't afford to buy"**
Collins never signed a contract, never received royalties, and can't afford to purchase the original pressing of her own record. Meanwhile, Lion Babe sampled it for a track with over a million plays, and it appeared in the end credits of Donald Glover's *Atlanta*. This theme highlights the economics of deep funk obscurity and rediscovery.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The original title was "What's Your Hurry Baby," changed by studio owner Chuck Sibit to "At the Hotel" without Collins's input. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- The track was recorded in a single take. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- The backup singers originally recruited by the studio refused to sing, so Collins selected her own. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- The production has been described as "about as lo-fi as R&B ever got," with a detuned guitar that sounds like it's "on cough syrup." — Source: [Paul Pearson blog, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/04/song-of-day-4132013-eunice-collins-at.html)
- The single was released in two parts: Part 1 (vocal) and Part 2 (instrumental with a recorder/flute solo). — Source: [Sounds of the Universe](https://soundsoftheuniverse.com/product/-at-the-hotel-/)
- Lion Babe's "Treat Me Like Fire" (2012) misheard a Collins lyric: Collins says the line is "out of the fire, into the pan," not "into the pain." — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- The track was featured in the end credits of *Atlanta* S3E2 "Sinterklaas Is Coming to Town" (March 24, 2022). — Source: [Tunefind](https://www.tunefind.com/song/360588), [GetYarn](https://getyarn.io/yarn-clip/2914a564-444f-4ab4-90d2-8995b7104255)
- The track has been sampled at least five times: by Lion Babe ("Treat Me Like Fire," 2012), Alkalino ("Take It Easy"), Flamingosis ("For Sale"), Novatore ("At the Hotel," 2020), and Franky Rizardo & Ros T ("Out the Fire (At the Hotel)," 2021). — Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Eunice-Collins/At-the-Hotel/)
- Original pressings now sell for approximately £750. — Source: [Sounds of the Universe](https://soundsoftheuniverse.com/product/-at-the-hotel-/)

#### Album
- This is a standalone 7" single, not an album cut. Catalog number MA-601. — Source: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/2734689-Eunice-Collins-At-The-Hotel)
- It was reissued as a Record Store Day 2022 7" on the original Mod-Art imprint, licensed through Tufamerica Inc. and published by Swing Beat Songs. — Source: [Shuga Records](https://www.shugarecords.com/products/eunice-collins-at-the-hotel-1974-new-7-single-record-store-day-2022-mod-art-uk-vinyl-soul-funk)
- The track was included on the Now-Again Records compilation *Loving On The Flipside: Sweet Funk And Beat-Heavy Ballads 1969-1977* (2012), produced and annotated by Eothen Alapatt with liner notes on Collins by Rob Sevier. — Source: [Now-Again Records](https://www.nowagainrecords.com/various-loving-on-the-flipside/)

#### Artist
- Eunice Collins was born in 1951 in Chicago. This is her only known recording. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She was married at 15 and had a daughter born in 1968. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She started working in a record shop at age 10, unpaid, just to hear music. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She attended Malcolm X Junior College in Chicago and sang with a college band. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- Her influences included Motown, Memphis soul (Carla Thomas), and Philadelphia soul, but she also loves country music, particularly Garth Brooks and George Strait. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She discovered the Mod-Art studio by accident when she parked her car near a roller skating rink where the studio was located and walked in. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She never signed a contract and was never paid for the recording. The label owner gave her copies of the record along with chart position sheets she believes were fabricated. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- She only discovered the song's internet popularity around 2011, roughly two years before her 2013 interview. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)
- Collins was not aware of Lion Babe's sampling of her track until contacted, and she noted that her song also appeared on Now-Again's compilation without her knowledge or compensation. — Source: [Paul Pearson interview, 2013](http://paul-pearson.blogspot.com/2013/12/the-top-song-of-day-2013.html)

### Genre Suggestions

The current genre is **Deep Funk**, which fits well.

**1. Deep Funk (recommended, keep current)**
This is the strongest fit. Deep funk is defined in the book as "less a sound than a commercial fate," centering tracks that were overlooked on release and only found their audience later through crate-digging and DJs. "At the Hotel" is a textbook case: a one-off 7" on a tiny Chicago private press label, no contract, no distribution, no chart presence, rediscovered decades later through collectors, YouTube, and sampling. The delayed recognition is the defining trait. — Source: Genre definition from *On Genres & Regions.md*

**2. Classic Funk**
The recording date (1974) and the raw, stripped-down funk production place it squarely in the classic funk era (1968-1977). However, the book's definition of classic funk emphasizes tracks that were "released into the commercial pipeline of major labels, well-distributed singles and albums, radio play, concert tours, and chart presence." This track had none of those things, making Deep Funk the better label. — Source: Genre definition from *On Genres & Regions.md*

**3. Soul Jazz (weaker fit)**
The lo-fi intimacy and blues-rooted vocal delivery have a kinship with the smaller-ensemble, groove-forward soul jazz tradition, but the track lacks the jazz harmonic language and improvisation that define soul jazz. Not recommended.
