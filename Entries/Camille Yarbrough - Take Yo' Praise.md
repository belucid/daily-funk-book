## Research Notes

### Open Research Questions

**Personnel discrepancy:** The prior research notes stated "Camille plays all the instruments on this except guitar, played by Cornell Dupree." This appears to be incorrect. Discogs credits for the original 1975 LP (VSD 79356) list full session personnel: Cornell Dupree (guitar), James Benjamin (bass), Leopoldo Fleming (congas, percussion), Jimmy Johnson (drums, percussion), and Linda Twine (Clavinet). Yarbrough is credited as writer and vocalist, not as a multi-instrumentalist on this record. The AllMusic credits confirm the same personnel. No source was found attributing instrumental performance on this album to Yarbrough herself. — Sources: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/719973-Camille-Yarbrough-The-Iron-Pot-Cooker), [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-iron-pot-cooker-mw0000254228)

**Birth year discrepancy:** Sources conflict on Yarbrough's birth year. Wikipedia lists January 8, 1934. Encyclopedia.com, AALBC, and WBSS Media list January 8, 1938. The Encyclopedia.com entry appears sourced from the *Contemporary Black Biography* reference series, which is generally well-researched. If born in 1938, she joined Katherine Dunham's company at 17 (consistent with the AALBC profile saying "at age fifteen" she found dance, and "at age twenty" she moved to pursue it professionally). If born in 1934, the Dunham timeline works differently. The 1938 date appears more consistent with the biographical narrative across multiple sources. — Sources: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Yarbrough), [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current writing theme is: *"Songs where the sample is better known than the sampled, seems like a cover at first, cause the sample has the words."* This is a strong hook. Here are alternatives:

1. **"The griot who got sampled"** — Yarbrough was a dancer, actress, professor, children's book author, radio host, and self-described griot before she ever made a record. The Iron Pot Cooker was a stage show years before it was an album. The entry could frame her as someone whose life was the art, and the record was almost incidental, which makes the Fatboy Slim afterlife all the more ironic. She wasn't a recording artist who got sampled; she was a cultural healer whose one album happened to contain four minutes of pure, undeniable soul.

2. **"Praise for the unheard"** — Yarbrough wrote "Take Yo' Praise" for the unnamed people of the civil rights movement. Decades later, millions of people heard those words without ever knowing who sang them or why. The entry could explore that double layer of anonymity: the people she was praising, and Yarbrough herself.

3. **"The iron pot"** — The album title comes from Nigerian female healers called "iron pot cookers." Yarbrough saw herself as a healer. The entry could use this metaphor: the track is something slow-cooked, nourishing, and deceptively simple, a healing administered through groove.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

No listening mission is currently set. Suggestions:

1. **"Fatboy Slim's 'Praise You' used the vocal intro as a loop, and millions of people know those words by heart. Now hear the full song underneath them. What does the groove do that the sample never could?"** — This mission leverages the near-universal familiarity with the Fatboy Slim version to set up a revelation: the original is a completely different animal, warm and live and full-band.

2. **"Cornell Dupree's guitar sits so far back in the mix you might miss it. Find it. What is it doing to the feel of the track?"** — Dupree was one of the great session guitarists of the era. His contribution here is subtle but essential. This gives the listener a specific hunt.

3. **"Listen to the way the song builds. It starts spare and intimate, just Yarbrough's voice and a pulse. At what point does it stop being a quiet prayer and start being a groove?"** — This focuses attention on the track's dynamic arc, encouraging active listening to its structure.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- "Take Yo' Praise" was written for "all the people who had come through the black civil rights movement, who had stood up for truth and righteousness and justice, because human beings need to praise and respect one another more than they do." — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Yarbrough)
- Norman Cook (Fatboy Slim) discovered the track at a Camden Market stall in North London selling vinyl bootleg albums. He heard the isolated vocal introduction and built "Praise You" around it. — Source: [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-fatboy-slim-praise-you)
- To match the vocal to his piano sample (taken from a JBL speaker demonstration record called "Sessions"), Cook played the original record at +7 pitch on his turntable, then chopped the vocal into segments and time-stretched each line individually using an Akai S950 sampler. The vocal rises approximately three semitones and speeds up about 20bpm in the process. — Source: [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-fatboy-slim-praise-you)
- Yarbrough wasn't initially aware of what sampling was when her record company called to tell her about "Praise You." In a 2020/2021 WhoSampled interview, she said she liked the song and felt Cook kept "the soul of it, which was 'praise.'" — Source: [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/news/2020/10/09/camille-yarbrough-interviewed-for-whosampled/)
- "Praise You" hit #1 in the UK (January 16, 1999), reached #36 on the Billboard Hot 100, and #1 on the Billboard Dance Club Songs chart. Its Spike Jonze-directed guerrilla music video, shot for $800 outside a movie theater in Westwood, LA, won three MTV VMAs (Breakthrough Video, Best Direction, Best Choreography) and was voted best music video of all time in MTV's 20th anniversary poll (2001). — Sources: [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/fatboy-slim-praise-you/), [Wikipedia - Praise You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praise_You)
- A "Yo' Praise" CD maxi-single was released in 1999 (capitalizing on the Fatboy Slim attention), and a 12" white label promo followed in 2000. — Source: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/master/2893066-Camille-Yarbrough-Yo-Praise)

#### Album
- The Iron Pot Cooker was based on Yarbrough's one-woman stage show "Tales and Tunes of an African American Griot," first performed in 1971. The album is essentially a studio distillation of a live theatrical piece. — Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Yarbrough)
- The title comes from Nigerian female healers: Yarbrough said, "I consider myself a healer, and thus I too, am the iron pot cooker." — Source: [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- Journalist Kevin Powell called The Iron Pot Cooker "a precursor to Lauryn Hill's best-seller The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill." — Source: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-iron-pot-cooker-mw0000254228)
- Recorded at Vanguard's 23rd Street Studio, New York, NY. Produced by Ed Bland. All songs written by Camille Yarbrough. — Source: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/719973-Camille-Yarbrough-The-Iron-Pot-Cooker)
- The album was reissued on CD in 2000/2005, and received its first vinyl reissue in September 2020 via Craft Recordings as a Record Store Day release (180g all-analog remaster from original tapes by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio). — Source: [Craft Recordings](https://craftrecordings.com/products/camille-yarbrough-the-iron-pot-cooker-180g-lp)
- Session musicians include Cornell Dupree (guitar), James Benjamin (bass), Leopoldo Fleming (congas, percussion), Jimmy Johnson (drums, percussion), and Linda Twine (Clavinet). Cornell Dupree was a legendary session guitarist who played on hundreds of recordings, including Aretha Franklin, King Curtis, and Donny Hathaway sessions. — Source: [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/719973-Camille-Yarbrough-The-Iron-Pot-Cooker)
- Full track listing: "But It Comes Out Mad" (6:19), "Dream/Panic/Sonny Boy the Rip-Off Man/Little Sally the Super Sex Star" (14:06), "Ain't It a Lonely Feeling" (3:55), "Take Yo' Praise" (4:13), "Can I Get a Witness?" (4:14), "All Hid" (6:10). Total runtime: 38:57. — Source: [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-iron-pot-cooker-mw0000254228)

#### Artist
- Born January 8 (1934 or 1938, sources conflict) in Chicago, Illinois. Seventh of eight children. Father from Alabama, mother from Chicago. — Sources: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938), [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- At 18, joined the Katherine Dunham Dance Company and toured internationally for 18 months across the US, Australia, Korea, Japan, New Zealand, Vietnam, and Western Europe. — Source: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938)
- Broadway credits include *Kwamina* (1961), *Trumpets of the Lord*, and *To Be Young, Gifted, and Black* (which toured 56 cities on college campuses). — Source: [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- Had a small role in the film *Shaft* (1971). — Source: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938)
- Spin Magazine named her "the foremother of rap." Her response: "When you go to the old, you see where the new comes from. Everything I did on stage, without music, was spoken word, it was rap." — Source: [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- Taught African dance at City College of New York for twelve years. — Source: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938)
- Co-hosted Bob Law's *Night Talk* on WWRL-AM, a late-night Black talk radio show (12 AM to 5 AM). — Source: [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- Award-winning children's book author: *Cornrows* (1979, Coretta Scott King Award), *The Shimmershine Queens* (1989, Parents' Choice Award). — Source: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/yarbrough-camille-1938)
- The Ga people of Ghana instooled her as Queen Mother, assigning her the historical stool of Harriet Tubman. — Source: [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/articles/her-praise-profile-of-camille-yarbrough-a-renaissance-woman)
- As of 2024, she appears to still be alive and active (documented political contributions in mid-2024). — Background knowledge, corroborated by absence of any obituary in searches.

### Genre Suggestions

The current genre is **Soul Jazz**. This is reasonable but here are considerations:

1. **Soul Jazz** (current) — The track's warm, vocal-centered groove over a jazz-inflected rhythm section with clavinet and congas fits the soul jazz mold. Cornell Dupree's understated guitar work and the overall feel are consistent with the early-to-mid 1970s soul jazz sound. The album was recorded at Vanguard (a jazz/folk label) and AllMusic classifies it under R&B/Soul. This is the strongest fit.

2. **Classic Funk** — "Take Yo' Praise" has a clear funk pulse: the clavinet, the syncopated bass, the congas driving the rhythm. It's not hard funk, but it's undeniably funky. If the project's definition of Classic Funk includes the smoother, more soulful end of the spectrum, this could work as a primary or secondary genre tag.

3. **Deep Funk** — Less likely as a primary genre, but the track's obscurity before the Fatboy Slim sample, its single-album pedigree, and its discovery via crate-digging all fit the deep funk collector aesthetic. The track has the hallmarks of a deep funk 45 discovery even though it was originally an album cut on a relatively well-known label.
