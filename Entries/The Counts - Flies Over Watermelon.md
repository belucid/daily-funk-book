### Writing Theme Suggestions

The existing theme is **"A case for slow funk"** — a great hook given the track's languid 3:00 instrumental groove. The band themselves operated at a more medium-tempo pocket than the JBs or Funkadelic, and "Flies Over Watermelon" deliberately leans laconic. Alternatives worth considering:

- **"The Detroit band that recorded in Atlanta for a porn kingpin"** — leans into the unusual backstory: The Counts left Westbound after being shortchanged for The Ohio Players and Funkadelic, decamped to Atlanta, and signed with Aware Records, bankrolled by Michael Thevis, "The Scarface of Sex," who controlled ~40% of US pornography distribution before launching three soul/funk labels (GRC, Aware, Hotlanta) and the Sound Pit studio. Source: https://theartsdesk.com/node/91381/view
- **"A title that does most of the work"** — "Flies Over Watermelon" is the kind of evocative novelty title that primes the listener. The track itself is mostly instrumental, but the title sets a scene (a Southern porch, summer heat, lazy circling) that the slow groove then earns. AllMusic-adjacent reviews call it "a spacy novelty that the group could switch to in a heartbeat." Source: https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-counts/202361583 (artist bio quote).
- **"Jazz musicians playing funk"** — Dusty Groove and Jazz Music Archives both note The Counts were "basically jazz musicians at heart," and their jazz instincts (long forms, room to breathe, organ-led pocket) come through on Funk Pump. "Flies Over Watermelon" is the album's clearest example. Source: https://www.dustygroove.com/item/369342/Counts:Funk-Pump

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Flies Over Watermelon" is track B4 on Funk Pump, clocking in at 3:00 — one of the album's shortest cuts (only the album's title track is similarly compact). The 5:10 closer "Tecalli" was where the band stretched out; "Flies Over Watermelon" is them showing they could also write tight. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/245586-The-Counts-Funk-Pump
- Recorded at The Sound Pit, Atlanta, GA — a studio owned by Michael Thevis, the Atlanta porn kingpin who was simultaneously bankrolling the GRC/Aware/Hotlanta soul label empire as a money-laundering vehicle. — Source: https://theartsdesk.com/node/91381/view
- The track is published by Act One Music Co., Inc., the publishing arm tied to the Thevis label operation. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/589287-The-Counts-Funk-Pump
- Album notes describe the track as "a spacy novelty that the group could switch to in a heartbeat," contrasting it with the harder-hitting vocal cuts. — Source: https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-counts/202361583

#### Album

- Funk Pump was produced by soul luminary William Bell (the Stax songwriter behind "You Don't Miss Your Water" and co-writer of "Born Under a Bad Sign") together with James McDuffie. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/589287-The-Counts-Funk-Pump
- The album reached No. 45 (some sources say No. 58) on the US Billboard Black Albums chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts
- The album opens with a cover of Carole King and David Palmer's "Jazzman" — a 1974 hit for King — making Funk Pump in part a response to King's commercial moment. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/cover/320306/The-Counts-Jazzman-Carole-King-Jazzman/
- Personnel on the album: Leroy Emmanuel (guitar, percussion, lead vocals), Mose Davis (keyboards, lead vocals), Byron Miller (bass), Leslie Daniels (drums), Ricardo Gunzalas (percussion). Cover design by Ruby Mazur, photography by Suzanne Ayres. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/589287-The-Counts-Funk-Pump
- A 7" promo single of the title track "Funk Pump" was released in 1974 on Aware (AWDJ 046), with mono on the A side and stereo on the B side for radio. "Flies Over Watermelon" was not released as a single — it only exists on the LP. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/2172919-The-Counts-Funk-Pump
- Album was reissued as a CD in 1994 and on 180-gram vinyl by Rhino/Scorpio; further reissued in 2022 by Octave (Japan) as a remaster. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9878551-The-Counts-Funk-Pump

#### Artist

- The Counts started life as The Fabulous Counts, formed in Detroit in 1968 by keyboardist Mose Davis, guitarist Leroy Emmanuel, alto saxophonist Demo Cates, tenor saxophonist Jim White, and drummer Raoul Keith Mangrum. Their 1968 instrumental "Jan, Jan" on Detroit's Moira label launched them, and their third single "Get Down People" hit #32 R&B and #88 pop. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts
- After signing with Westbound Records in 1970, they dropped "Fabulous" from their name. Their 1971 album What's Up Front That Counts hit #193 pop and #35 Black Albums. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts
- The band was famously "shortchanged" by Westbound in favor of The Ohio Players and Funkadelic, prompting most original members to leave. The reduced band moved to Atlanta and signed with Michael Thevis's Aware Records, where they made their final two albums (Love Sign 1973, Funk Pump 1974/75). — Source: https://www.last.fm/music/The+Counts/+wiki
- Recording at The Sound Pit, Atlanta, makes The Counts a rare example of a Detroit band that essentially relocated to the Southern funk/soul axis — a reverse-Motown migration during funk's peak years. — Source: https://dereksmusicblog.com/2017/05/21/the-rise-and-fall-of-a-musical-empire/
- Demo Cates settled permanently in Toronto after recording part of What's Up Front That Counts there, becoming a fixture in the Canadian jazz/soul scene and a two-time Juno Award nominee. He also contributed to Rap the Night Away (1981), credited as Canada's first full-length hip-hop album. — Source: https://citizenfreak.com/artists/110010-cates-demo
- The band split in 1976, and reunited in 2009 with the 1976 lineup: Mose Davis, Leroy Emmanuel, Demo Cates, Jimmy "Junebug" Jackson, and Jimmy Brown. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts
- Leroy Emmanuel went on to appear on more than 20 Bohannon albums and led the Canadian funk band LMT Connection. — Source: https://funknstuff.net/tir-leroy-emmanuel/

### Genre Suggestions

Currently empty.

1. **Classic Funk** — Funk Pump is squarely in the 1969–1977 classic funk era, on a commercial label with national distribution and chart presence (#45 R&B/Black Albums). Even though Aware was a niche label, this fits "Classic Funk" because the track's primary identity is funk, not a fusion. The book's definition of classic funk emphasizes commercial pipeline and the period 1969–1977, and Funk Pump checks both boxes. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts)
2. **Jazz-funk** — Strong case: multiple reviewers explicitly describe The Counts as "basically jazz musicians at heart" and call out the jazz instincts on Funk Pump (longer instrumental forms, room to breathe, organ-led pocket, the 5:10 "Tecalli" closer). Mose Davis was classically trained at the Detroit Conservatory of Music. "Flies Over Watermelon" is a vocal-free instrumental groove with a jazz-funk feel, though at 3:00 it's compressed compared to typical jazz-funk run times. (https://www.dustygroove.com/item/369342/Counts:Funk-Pump)
3. **Deep Funk** — Defensible: Funk Pump was a low-selling album on a tiny label that essentially nobody outside Atlanta noticed at release; the band was rediscovered by crate-diggers in the 1990s, and the album has been reissued multiple times (1994 CD, Rhino/Scorpio LP, 2022 Octave remaster) precisely because deep-funk DJs surfaced it. The book's definition of deep funk emphasizes "delayed recognition" and crate-digger discovery, and Funk Pump fits that arc. The Counts' Aware-era output is firmly in deep-funk reissue territory. (https://www.discogs.com/release/9878551-The-Counts-Funk-Pump)

Strongest single suggestion: **Jazz-funk**, given the instrumental nature of "Flies Over Watermelon" specifically. Runner-up: **Deep Funk** given the album's commercial fate and reissue history. **Classic Funk** is a fallback if the author wants to honor the era and major-funk vocabulary of the album as a whole.

### Region Suggestions

Region was changed from `US - Ohio` (which appears to be incorrect — likely a conflation with The Ohio Players) to `US - Michigan`. The strongest cases:

1. **US - Michigan** (chosen) — The Counts formed in Detroit in 1968 and built their identity, lineup, and reputation as a Detroit band. Even after relocating to Atlanta, their sound is essentially a Detroit funk unit. The "On Regions" rationale allows for "where the band formed" as a valid choice. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fabulous_Counts)
2. **US - Southeast** — Alternative: by 1974, the band was operating out of Atlanta, recording at The Sound Pit, on an Atlanta label (Aware). If the criterion is "where the recording happened" or "where the scene lived" for this specific record, US - Southeast captures the Atlanta soul-funk axis (alongside GRC and Hotlanta artists). (https://theartsdesk.com/node/91381/view)
3. **US - Midwest** — Broader umbrella that captures the band's Detroit origin if Michigan feels too specific given they were a multi-state operation by Funk Pump.

The book's region list includes US - Southeast as a category but not US - Georgia specifically, so if the author wants to honor the Atlanta recording context, US - Southeast is the move. I went with US - Michigan because the band's identity, lineup, and stylistic DNA are Detroit; the Atlanta label/studio was a contract arrangement, not a scene move for most members.
