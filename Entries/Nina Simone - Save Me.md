### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items in this entry. Two questions surfaced during research and are resolved as best possible:

- **Which recording is "Save Me"?** It is a studio recording issued only as the B-side of the "To Be Young, Gifted And Black" 45 (RCA Victor 74-0269), 1969. It is distinct from the live "Save Me" Nina performed in Munich (April 1969) and other live captures that circulate on streaming compilations. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1778404 , https://www.deezer.com/en/track/66789228
- **Year — 1969 or 1970?** Discogs consistently catalogs the single as 1969. Wikipedia notes the A-side charted in January 1970 (No. 8 R&B, No. 76 Hot 100), so some sources cite 1970 as the release year. I used 1969 per the pressing metadata; flag for the author. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Be_Young,_Gifted_and_Black

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The B-side that out-funked the anthem."** "To Be Young, Gifted And Black" became the civil-rights anthem, but the flip side is the one crate-diggers chase — Jazzman reissued the 45 in 2002 for the funk on "Save Me," not the A-side. An angle on how history and the dancefloor value the same record differently.
- **"Nina wrestles Aretha."** Simone covers Aretha Franklin's 1967 gospel-charged "Save Me" (co-written with sister Carolyn Franklin and King Curtis) and, per Aquarium Drunkard, "gets inside the material and wrestles it." A theme about one titan re-reading another.
- **"Weldon Irvine's other job."** The same young arranger who gave Nina the words to "To Be Young, Gifted And Black" arranged this funk B-side; he'd later mentor Q-Tip, Mos Def and Common before his 2002 suicide. A theme threading a single 1969 session to hip-hop.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Play Aretha Franklin's 1967 original "Save Me" back-to-back with Nina's version. Aretha races; Nina digs in. Where does Nina add weight the original doesn't have?
- Ignore Nina's famous voice for one pass and follow only the rhythm section and Weldon Irvine's arrangement. What makes collectors call this a funk record rather than a soul ballad?
- This clocks in at barely 3:20. Notice how quickly it starts and stops — does the brevity make it feel more urgent, or does it leave you wanting a second verse?

### Interest and Surprise

### Track
- "Save Me" is a cover of Aretha Franklin's 1967 recording, written by Aretha Franklin, her sister Carolyn Franklin, and saxophonist King Curtis (Curtis Ousley). The original 1969 Nina single label credits only "Aretha Franklin," while later reissues restore the full three-writer credit. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1778404 , https://www.discogs.com/release/295051-Nina-Simone-Funkier-Than-A-Mosquitos-Tweeter-Save-Me
- It circulated for decades as a rare-groove/funk 45 and was reissued by London's Jazzman label in 2002 (JM.023) — with "Funkier Than A Mosquito's Tweeter" moved to the A-side, effectively re-marketing the two funk cuts to a new dancefloor audience. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/854670-Nina-Simone-Funkier-Than-A-Mosquitos-Tweeter-Save-Me
- Aquarium Drunkard calls Simone's reading "incredibly funky," saying she "gets inside the material and wrestles it." — Source: https://aquariumdrunkard.com/2011/02/08/nina-simone-save-me-aretha-franklin/

### Album
- There is no album to speak of — "Save Me" is a genuine non-album single, one of the RCA-era Nina tracks that never landed on an LP and thus stayed obscure until the reissue/comp era. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/854670-Nina-Simone-Funkier-Than-A-Mosquitos-Tweeter-Save-Me

### Artist
- Simone introduced the A-side "To Be Young, Gifted And Black" live to a crowd of roughly 50,000 at the 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival before the studio single was cut — the same festival later spotlighted in the film *Summer of Soul*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Be_Young,_Gifted_and_Black
- Arranger Weldon Irvine, who shaped this session, went on to mentor Q-Tip, Mos Def, Common and Talib Kweli, inspiring a Madlib tribute album after his 2002 death. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weldon_Irvine
- Three of the song's writers/collaborators met untimely ends: King Curtis was stabbed to death in 1971, Carolyn Franklin died of cancer in 1988, and Weldon Irvine died by suicide in 2002. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Curtis , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weldon_Irvine
