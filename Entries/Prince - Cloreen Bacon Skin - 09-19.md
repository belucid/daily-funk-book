At fifteen-plus minutes, today’s track is one of the longest official Prince releases, and also one of the leanest. It is just Prince on bass and Morris Day on drums. Prince does all the talking. Morris Day stays silent. 

The song has humor and a fat, funky beat, but can you make it through all fifteen-plus minutes of this stripped-down sound? The real question is why Prince decided listeners would want this on the _Crystal Ball_ triple album.

Prince’s comedy comes through his recurring "Jamie Starr" persona, an imitation of the older men he heard at the barbershop growing up. Everything you hear here was made up on-the-spot, including the name "Cloreen" and the unflattering "Bacon Skin" tag. This kind of improvised jamming was a fundamental part of Prince’s creative process. You can hear its afterlife too. Morris’ drum part resurfaced on "Irresistible Bitch," and "Cloreen Bacon Skin" was later self-sampled in "Soul Psychodelicide."

Dirty secret time: this is my favorite Prince song.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

The current `writing theme` is: "Humor in funk, too indulgent to release this given the length and the strip down nature of drums and bass and spoken word"

That's a strong angle. Here are some alternatives:

1. **"The day Prince became his own father"** — Prince's vocal performance channels his "Jamie Starr" alter ego, which engineer Susan Rogers (or more likely Peggy McCreary, see note below) identified as Prince imitating "men of his father's generation, barber shop guys." When Prince says "You can't fuck with me, I'm old," he's reportedly doing an impression of his father, John L. Nelson. This opens up the father-son dynamic in Prince's creative life, and the way he processed it through comedy and funk.

2. **"Molten funk before it cools"** — The track is the raw material that later became "Irresistible Bitch" and, in spirit, "Soul Psychodelicide." Prince himself called "Irresistible Bitch" the coolest of the songs "cut in the same vibe." This angle frames Cloreen Bacon Skin as a glimpse into how Prince's creative forge worked: the jam comes first, the song comes later.

3. **"Two guys, two instruments, fifteen minutes"** — Just Prince on bass and Morris Day on drums in a room in Hollywood. No overdubs, no plan. This is the most stripped-down Prince recording that exists on an official release, and it captures the collaborative chemistry that built The Time.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The track is entirely improvised. Prince wrote in the Crystal Ball liner notes: "Everything U hear is impromptu... even the title was made up a split second before you hear it." — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- Prince acknowledged that "Cloreen Bacon Skin" was the track on Crystal Ball that "needed the most explanation" among the newly released songs. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- The bass playing style Prince used here, a rhythmic "rumble" approach rather than melodic bass lines, was later adopted and mastered by Revolution bassist BrownMark. — Source: https://mookiefantana.com/2020/10/09/daily-prince-10-9-20-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- "Tricky," a 1984 B-side for The Time's "Ice Cream Castles" single, is essentially a remix of the Cloreen Bacon Skin groove with Minneapolis keyboard hits layered on top. Audiences got a taste of this jam fourteen years before Crystal Ball was released. — Source: https://princesongs.org/2021/01/08/cloreen-bacon-skin-tricky/
- The 1989 recording of "Soul Psychodelicide" features a Cloreen Bacon Skin sample at its beginning, showing Prince continued to mine this jam years later. — Source: https://princevault.com/index.php?title=Soul_Psychodelicide
- Prince directly connected this track to "Irresistible Bitch," calling it "the coolest" of the songs "cut in the same vibe." When he revisited "Irresistible Bitch" at Sunset Sound in September 1983, he rebuilt it around the drum groove Morris Day played on "Cloreen Bacon Skin." — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- At 15 minutes and 36 seconds, it is one of the longest tracks Prince ever officially released. — Background knowledge, but no source found for a definitive ranking.
- The vocal performance uses Prince's "Jamie Starr" alter ego. According to (likely) engineer Peggy McCreary or Susan Rogers, Prince was "imitating men of his father's generation... barber shop guys." When Prince says "You can't fuck with me, I'm old," he's doing an impression of his own father. — Source: https://mookiefantana.com/2020/10/09/daily-prince-10-9-20-cloreen-bacon-skin/

#### Album
- Crystal Ball was the first album Prince released entirely independently, sold via a 1-800-NEW-FUNK phone line and his website love4oneanother.com, with no major label distribution. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-crystal-ball-album/
- The original target release was July 4, 1997. It shipped six months late on January 29, 1998, causing significant customer frustration. Some packages arrived without track listings or booklets, and bonus items were inconsistent (some got T-shirts, some got Emancipation lyric books, some got cassettes). — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-crystal-ball-album/
- Despite charting modestly at #62 on the Billboard 200, Prince claimed approximately 250,000 copies sold and emphasized he earned "$7 on every one" versus standard industry royalty rates, making it financially successful. — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-crystal-ball-album/
- The album title reuses the name of an unreleased 1986 triple album that Warner Bros. forced Prince to trim down to the double album Sign o' the Times. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ball_(unreleased_album)
- "Cloreen Bacon Skin" (recorded 1983) is the oldest recording on Crystal Ball. The album spans material from 1983 to 1996. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ball_(box_set)

#### Artist
- Prince used the pseudonym "Jamie Starr" as his production credit for The Time's early albums. The name was inspired by the 1980 film The Idolmaker, about a rock promoter who creates pop stars. Prince played all the instruments himself on early Time records. — Source: https://americansongwriter.com/learn-princes-songwriting-pseudonyms/
- Morris Day later reflected that these impromptu bass-and-drums sessions were how he and Prince created many of The Time's songs: "He'd be on bass, I'd be on drums... That's how we came up with a lot of songs, 'The Walk,' 'Cool' and stuff like that." — Source: https://ultimateprince.com/prince-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- The session took place on March 27, 1983, the day after Prince and Morris Day recorded "Jungle Love" at the same studio, which became one of The Time's biggest hits. — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-cloreen-bacon-skin/
- The 1999 tour was running through LA at this time. Prince played the Universal Amphitheatre the next day, March 28, 1983, meaning this jam happened between tour stops. — Source: https://princevault.com/index.php?title=1999_Tour
- **Engineer discrepancy:** One source (mookiefantana.com) credits Susan Rogers as engineer on this session. However, multiple sources indicate Rogers did not begin working with Prince until mid-1983 in Minneapolis. Peggy McCreary was the staff engineer at Sunset Sound during this period and engineered the Ice Cream Castle sessions that same week. The engineer for the March 27 session was most likely Peggy McCreary. — Sources: https://mookiefantana.com/2020/10/09/daily-prince-10-9-20-cloreen-bacon-skin/ vs. https://audiomediainternational.com/in-the-studio-with-prince-susan-rogers-recalls-her-five-years-as-the-stars-staff-engineer/ and https://prnalumni.org/members/spotlight/spotlight-peggy-mccreary/

### Genre Suggestions

The current genre is **Minneapolis Sound**, which fits well. Here are the cases:

1. **Minneapolis Sound (current, recommended)** — This is the strongest fit. The genre definition in the book describes it as "a lean, high-tempo evolution of 1970s funk-rock, fused with new wave and synth-pop." While Cloreen Bacon Skin predates the synth-heavy Minneapolis sound of Purple Rain and beyond, it captures the core of how Prince and Morris Day built that scene: two Minneapolis musicians jamming on bass and drums in a room. It's the skeletal foundation of the Minneapolis sound before the keyboards arrive.
   - Source: Genre definition from On Genres & Regions.md

2. **Classic Funk** — The track was recorded in 1983, which pushes past the book's 1968-1977 classic funk window. However, the stripped-down bass-and-drums instrumentation owes more to classic funk's raw vocabulary than to the synthesizer-driven Minneapolis sound. If the genre field is meant to reflect what the music sounds like rather than who made it, Classic Funk has a case.
   - Source: Genre definition from On Genres & Regions.md

3. **Deep Funk** — The track sat unreleased in Prince's vault for 15 years before appearing on Crystal Ball. It has the "delayed recognition" hallmark of deep funk. However, Prince is not an obscure artist, and the track appeared on an official Prince release, so this is a weaker fit.
   - Source: Genre definition from On Genres & Regions.md

**Recommendation:** Minneapolis Sound remains the best choice. The track is inseparable from the Prince/Morris Day creative partnership that defined the scene.
