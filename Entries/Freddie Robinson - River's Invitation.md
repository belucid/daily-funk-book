### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were in the entry. Questions the research raised for the author:

- **Vocal or instrumental?** Discogs credits Robinson with "Guitar, Vocals" for the album as a whole, and Shazam flags the track as having lyrics. Neither source settles whether Robinson sings this specific track or plays the melody on guitar, so please confirm by listening before writing.
- **Where was it recorded?** No source gives a studio or session date. The players (Monk Higgins, Joe Sample, Wilton Felder, Harvey Mason, Red Holloway, Darlene Love) were all LA session regulars, which strongly suggests Los Angeles rather than Memphis. Searched Discogs release notes, Sessiondays, Wikipedia, and the Ace "Bluesology" listings. The Ace CD liner notes (Dean Rudland or similar) would be the next place to look.
- **Which name to print?** "Off The Cuff" is credited to "Freddie Robinson" on Discogs, while the album credits list "Freddy Robinson" and most of his other records use Freddy. The Discogs artist profile notes that all but one album credit "Freddy", and this album is the exception. The entry now uses Freddie to match this release. https://www.discogs.com/artist/136142-Freddie-Robinson
- **When did he become Abu Talib?** Wikipedia and Notorious Jazz say he converted to Islam in 1975. The Discogs profile says he used the name Abu Talib "for a few years from 1978". Unresolved.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **A sweet invitation to drown.** Percy Mayfield's lyric is about a man who hears the river calling him to join his lost love at the bottom (background knowledge, but no source found for the lyric summary), and Toppermost notes how the upbeat orchestration and "suicidal lyrics" go hand in hand. A funky, lilting setting of a song about walking into a river is a great tension to open up.
- **The bassline hip-hop kept.** A 1973 album track that sold quietly on a Stax subsidiary became a building block for Diamond D's production on House of Pain's "Word Is Bond" (1994), West Coast gang-rap compilation "Bangin' on Wax", and others. It shows how deep funk gets found: not on the radio, but in a producer's crates.
- **Bessie and the baling wire.** Robinson made his first instrument out of baling wire as a kid in Arkansas and bought a Sears guitar at 13 that he named Bessie and kept all his life. A story about a blues kid who ended up recording with half the Crusaders.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Lock onto Wilton Felder's bass from the first bar. Diamond D built a House of Pain track on this record 21 years later: can you hear the part he wanted?
- This is a song about a man thinking about walking into a river. Does the groove argue with the lyric or comfort it?
- Percy Mayfield cut this song twice, in 1953 and 1963. Play his 1963 Tangerine version next to this one. What did Robinson and Monk Higgins add that turns a blues ballad into something you can nod to?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The song was written by Percy Mayfield, who first recorded it as "The River's Invitation" for Specialty in 1953 (Specialty SP-451, B-side "I Dare You Baby"). Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/1570347-Percy-Mayfield-And-Orchestra-The-Rivers-Invitation-I-Dare-You-Baby
- Mayfield re-recorded it in 1963 for Ray Charles's Tangerine label (TRC 931), and that version reached No. 25 R&B and No. 99 on the Hot 100. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerine_Records_(1962), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Mayfield, https://www.discogs.com/master/987371-Percy-Mayfield-Rivers-Invitation-Baby-Please
  - Counterpoint: KNKX says the song "didn't come along until 1963", ignoring the 1953 Specialty original. Discogs and Toppermost both document the 1953 single, so treat 1963 as the remake. https://www.knkx.org/blues/2023-08-12/poet-of-the-blues-percy-mayfield-birthday
- Toppermost describes the song's mix of "upbeat orchestration and the suicidal lyrics" and says the Tangerine version dropped the "The" from the title. Source: https://www.toppermost.co.uk/?p=5214
- Robinson's version is sampled on House of Pain's "Word Is Bond" (feat. and produced by Diamond D) from "Same as It Ever Was" (June 28, 1994). Wikipedia lists the sample in the album credits. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same_as_It_Ever_Was_(House_of_Pain_album), https://www.whosampled.com/sample/96087/House-of-Pain-Word-Is-Bond-Freddie-Robinson-River%27s-Invitation/
- WhoSampled also lists the bassline as sampled by Bloods & Crips ("C-Sick", 1993, "Bangin' on Wax"), M.A.C. 10 ("L.F.T.C.I.", 1989), Casual ("Follow the Funk", 1994), and Brother Ali ("Uncle Sam Goddamn", 2007). Only WhoSampled, which is user-maintained, supports these, and the Brother Ali credit could not be confirmed elsewhere. Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/407009/Bloods-&-Crips-C-Sick-Freddie-Robinson-River's-Invitation/, https://www.whosampled.com/sample/121375/M.A.C.-10-L.F.T.C.I.-Freddie-Robinson-River's-Invitation/
- The track was picked for the BGP/Ace breakbeat compilation "Super Breaks Vol. 3" (2002), alongside Funkadelic, Sly and the Family Stone, and the Bar-Kays. Source: https://acerecords.co.uk/super-breaks-vol-3
- Japan's P-Vine chose it to close the 1996 compilation "Stax Club Classics", which shows the crate-digger afterlife reached Japan too. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6332443-Various-Stax-Club-Classics
- It was never released as a single. Discogs searches by artist, title, and Enterprise label return no 7" for this track. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/136142-Freddie-Robinson

#### Album

- "Off The Cuff" (Enterprise ENS-1035, 1973, copyright Stax Records) was produced by Monk Higgins, Alex Brown, and Larry Maxwell, with Higgins arranging and conducting. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/978564-Freddie-Robinson-Off-The-Cuff
- The band was strong: Joe Sample (keys) and Wilton Felder (bass) of the Crusaders, Harvey Mason (drums), Red Holloway (tenor sax), George Bohanon (trombone), Oscar Brashear (trumpet), and Darlene Love on backing vocals. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/978564-Freddie-Robinson-Off-The-Cuff, https://sessiondays.com/2024/04/29/1973-freddie-robinson-off-the-cuff/
- Enterprise was a Stax subsidiary founded by Al Bell in 1967 and named after the starship Enterprise from "Star Trek", Bell's favorite show. Its biggest act was Isaac Hayes. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_Records
- The album was reissued on CD only in Japan (P-Vine PCD-5117, 1996), and the original LP does not appear to be on streaming services. The track streams only via the Stax compilation "Blue Monday: The Stax Blues Masters". Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/606994-Freddie-Robinson-Off-The-Cuff, https://www.deezer.com/album/321092407
- No published critical review of the album was found (AllMusic, Dusty Groove searched).

#### Artist

- Born Fred Leroy Robinson in Memphis, Tennessee, on February 24, 1939, and raised in Arkansas. Died of cancer on October 8, 2009, in Lancaster, California. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Talib_(musician), https://www.discogs.com/artist/136142-Freddie-Robinson, https://news.pollstar.com/2009/10/12/bluesman-who-recorded-as-freddy-robinson-dies/
- As a child he made an instrument from baling wire. At 13 he bought a guitar from a Sears catalog and named it Bessie, and he kept it for the rest of his life. Source: https://news.pollstar.com/2009/10/12/bluesman-who-recorded-as-freddy-robinson-dies/
- He played bass and guitar for Little Walter at Chess around 1959 to 1960, and played on Howlin' Wolf sessions including "Spoonful", "Back Door Man", and "Wang Dang Doodle". Sources: https://www.shazam.com/artist/-/164183937 (Bill Dahl bio), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Talib_(musician)
- While in Ray Charles's band in LA he cut the instrumental "Black Fox" (written by Monk Higgins), which reached No. 56 on the Hot 100 and No. 29 R&B per Wikipedia. Ultimate70s lists a Cashbox peak of No. 45 from July 25 to September 19, 1970. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Talib_(musician), https://www.ultimate70s.com/song?id=3553
- He also played on John Mayall's "Jazz Blues Fusion" (1972) and Blue Mitchell's "Bantu Village" (1969). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Talib_(musician)
- Late in life he recorded a Southwest Airlines jingle. Source: https://news.pollstar.com/2009/10/12/bluesman-who-recorded-as-freddy-robinson-dies/
- Caution: the Wikipedia article lists a filmography (1939 to 1948) that cannot belong to someone born in 1939. It is likely a mix-up with the trombonist Fred Robinson. Do not use it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Robinson_(musician)

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is empty.

- **Deep Funk (recommended).** This fits the book's definition almost exactly: a poorly selling album on a well-known label's subsidiary, found later by crate-diggers. US hip-hop producers sampled it (House of Pain, Bloods & Crips), and breaks compilers in the UK (BGP "Super Breaks Vol. 3") and Japan (P-Vine "Stax Club Classics") revived it. Sources: https://acerecords.co.uk/super-breaks-vol-3, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same_as_It_Ever_Was_(House_of_Pain_album)
- **Soul Jazz.** Discogs files the album under Soul-Jazz and Modern Electric Blues. The blues-rooted guitar leader and the Crusaders rhythm section fit the soul jazz bridge. But the large ensemble with horns, strings, and backing vocals pushes past the book's "small-group" soul jazz framing. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/606994-Freddie-Robinson-Off-The-Cuff
- **Jazz-Funk.** The case is the players (Sample, Felder, Mason) and the 1973 date. But a 3-minute vocal blues cover with no extended solos runs against the book's jazz-funk traits.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - California (set).** Monk Higgins, Joe Sample, Wilton Felder, Harvey Mason, and Darlene Love were LA session players. Robinson lived in LA from his Ray Charles years onward. Monk Higgins was LA-based and produced Robinson's earlier LA records. The studio is not documented, so this is a well-supported inference, not a confirmed fact. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/978564-Freddie-Robinson-Off-The-Cuff, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monk_Higgins, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Talib_(musician)
- **US - Southeast.** Enterprise and its parent Stax were in Memphis, and Robinson was born there. This is defensible if the author prefers label or birthplace over recording scene. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_Records
- **US - Illinois.** Robinson's formative years were in Chicago with Little Walter and Howlin' Wolf, but by 1973 that was well behind him. This is the weakest case.
