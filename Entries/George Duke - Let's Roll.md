George Duke was one of the true legends of the 1970s jazz-funk scene. He embraced jazz-funk fusion without reservation, making some of the funkiest jazz of the decade.

*Face the Music* is a 2002 return to that sound. Duke called today's song, "Let's Roll," "a throwback to the music I used to play with Flora Purim, Airto (Moreira), Stanley Clarke and other fusioneers."

After years with Epic, Elektra and Warner Bros., Duke released *Face the Music* on his own BPM Records label. He called the move "definitely a turning point in my career." The album reached #4 on Billboard's Contemporary Jazz chart.

Treat any link as unproven.

- **Track personnel:** Discogs credits the personnel for the whole album, not track by track. Duke's notes name the core band used throughout: McBride (bass), Little John Roberts (drums) and Jef Lee Johnson (guitar). Horns, percussion or vocals on this track can't be confirmed without the liner notes.

#### Track

- Duke described "Let's Roll" as "a throwback to the music I used to play with Flora Purim, Airto, Stanley Clarke and other fusioneers," and told listeners: "Listen to Christian on this one." — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html

- The title arrived in 2002, the year many songs used "Let's roll" for Flight 93: Neil Young, dc Talk, the Bellamy Brothers and others. No source links Duke's instrumental to that wave. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let%27s_roll

#### Album

- It was the first release on Duke's own BPM (Big Piano Music) label. Duke called it "definitely a turning point in my career" and wanted "spiritual continuity" from one core group of players: McBride, Little John Roberts and Jef Lee Johnson. — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- The opener, "The Black Messiah (Part Two)," pays tribute to Cannonball Adderley and uses Adderley's voice, with permission from Blue Note. A separate entry for that track exists in this project. — Source: https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
- The closer, "Ten Mile Jog," was cut down from a 35-minute jam session. — Source: https://jazzusa.com/?p=3998
- The album includes a cover of James Brown's "Ain't It Funky Now." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/283433-George-Duke-Face-The-Music
- It peaked at No. 4 on Billboard's Top Contemporary Jazz Albums, No. 7 on Top Jazz Albums and No. 24 on Top Independent Albums. JazzTimes called it "an album filled with zesty, free-spirited, funk-fusion jams." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Music_(George_Duke_album)
- It was recorded and mixed by Erik Zobler at Le Gonks West, Los Angeles, and mastered by Doug Sax and Robert Hadley at The Mastering Lab. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/3115798-George-Duke-Face-The-Music

#### Artist

- George Martin Duke was born January 12, 1946, in San Rafael, California, and raised in Marin City. He died August 5, 2013, in Los Angeles, of chronic lymphocytic leukemia. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- Frank Zappa and Cannonball Adderley both recruited him. Zappa, who rarely played on other people's records, played guitar solos on Duke's 1974 album *Feel*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- Duke sometimes recorded under the alias "Dawilli Gonga." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke
- The fusion circle the track looks back to: in 1975, while imprisoned at FCI Terminal Island, Flora Purim organized a concert with outside musicians, Duke among them. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Purim
- Duke and Stanley Clarke first met in Finland in 1971 and formed the Clarke/Duke Project in 1981. Purim and Airto were in the original Return to Forever with Clarke. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Clarke
- Duke was musical director of the 1988 Nelson Mandela 70th Birthday Tribute at Wembley Stadium. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Duke

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Jazz Funk (recommended).** The track fits the book's player and music tests. Duke came from Adderley's and Zappa's bands, and the track is an 8-minute groove built for soloists, featuring an acoustic jazz bassist. Discogs styles are "Fusion, Jazz-Funk." Duke himself called it a throwback to 1970s fusion. The weak point is the book's era test, which puts jazz-funk "most at home in the 1970s." Still, the track deliberately recreates that sound. — https://www.discogs.com/master/283433-George-Duke-Face-The-Music ; https://georgeduke.com/2000s.html
2. **Acid Jazz (weak alternative).** It's a post-1980s revival of 1970s jazz-funk. But the book defines acid jazz by club and sampling culture "not extended improvisation," and this track is all improvisation. Not recommended.
3. **Nu-Funk (weak alternative).** It's a retro-funk record made after the mid-1990s, but the book ties nu-funk to bands making 45-style singles. Not a natural fit.
