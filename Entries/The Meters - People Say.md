### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The most sampled band you've never heard of."** "People Say" is a textbook example of why The Meters became hip-hop's deepest well of breakbeats — the entry can hang on the gap between the band's modest commercial profile in 1974 and its enormous downstream influence (Ice-T, Fabolous, and many others sampled this very track).
- **"Toussaint's house band steps into the light."** Rejuvenation was the first Meters album cut at Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn's new Sea-Saint Studios, the band shifting from instrumental session unit to a vocal-forward funk group with their own songwriting. "People Say" opens that album as a statement of arrival.
- **"New Orleans second-line, dressed as funk."** Zigaboo Modeliste's syncopated, behind-the-beat drumming on this track is the connective tissue between Mardi Gras parade rhythm and 1970s funk. A theme exploring how the city's street rhythm hides inside a "national" funk record.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Lock onto Zigaboo Modeliste's drums for the first thirty seconds. He never plays the backbeat where you expect it — try to clap along and notice exactly where the snare lands relative to the "1."
- This is four players and no overdubbed wall of horns. Listen for how much space they leave: where does the groove come from when almost nobody is playing on the beat?
- Compare the album cut (~5:15) with the single edit (~3:07) if you can find both. What did the 7" lose, and does the long version earn its extra two minutes?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "People Say" has been sampled across hip-hop, including Ice-T's "Fried Chicken" and Fabolous's "Right Now & Later On" — part of why The Meters are cited as one of the most-sampled groups in hip-hop and pop. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/People-Say/
- It opens the album as track A1 and was also issued as a Reprise 7" single (RPS 1314) backed with "Loving You Is On My Mind," edited down to roughly 3 minutes. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/556246-The-Meters-People-Say
- All four band members share the writing credit (Neville / Porter / Nocentelli / Modeliste), reflecting the group's collective, groove-built composition approach. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/431938-The-Meters-Rejuvenation

#### Album

- Rejuvenation was the first Meters album recorded at Allen Toussaint's newly built Sea-Saint Studios in New Orleans, produced by Toussaint with the band. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)
- The album was ranked #138 (2003) and #139 (2012) on Rolling Stone's "500 Greatest Albums of All Time," its most prestigious critical recognition. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)
- The album contains the 11:48 epic "It Ain't No Use" and the New Orleans standard "Hey Pocky A-Way," making it widely regarded as the band's creative peak. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/109366-The-Meters-Rejuvenation

#### Artist

- The Meters were Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn's de facto house band, backing New Orleans artists like Lee Dorsey and Dr. John before and during their own recording career. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)
- Keyboardist/leader Art "Poppa Funk" Neville (b. Dec 17, 1937) later co-founded The Neville Brothers; he died July 22, 2019. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Neville
- The classic lineup was Art Neville (keys), Leo Nocentelli (guitar, b. June 15, 1946), George Porter Jr. (bass, b. Dec 26, 1947), and Joseph "Zigaboo" Modeliste (drums, b. Dec 28, 1948). — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Nocentelli , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Porter_Jr. , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Modeliste
