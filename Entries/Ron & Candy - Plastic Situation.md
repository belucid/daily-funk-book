### Open Research Questions

No `open:` items were present in the entry. Questions surfaced during research that the author may want to pursue:

- **1972 or 1973?** Discogs dates every pressing of the single to 1973. The 2003 Luv N' Haight compilation's notes say "Plastic Situation" was "recorded & released on Inner City Records in 1972" (https://www.discogs.com/release/580320-Various-Inner-City-Sounds-Gritty-Soul-Underground-Funk-From-The-Vaults-Of-Inner-City-Records), and Wikipedia says "Lovely Weekend" was "recorded in 1972" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kenoly). Candy Rae's Discogs bio says she was signed in 1972 (https://www.discogs.com/artist/10996514-Candy-Rae). Most likely: recorded 1972, released late 1972 or 1973. The catalog number "ICIM-173" *might* encode "1-73" (January 1973), but that is my speculation with no source. No trade-press (Billboard/Cash Box) listing was found. Next step: search Billboard/Cash Box "new releases" columns from late 1972 to 1973 on americanradiohistory.com (worldradiohistory.com).
- **Was it really a hit?** Wikipedia says the single "sold nearly 200,000 copies", but that sentence carries no inline citation (I checked the wikitext). Candy Rae's Discogs bio calls it a "hit single". No Billboard chart entry was found (https://www.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/1973-02-03 and similar weekly charts were searched with no match). Treat the 200,000 figure as unverified. The number may refer to the A-side "Lovely Weekend", not "Plastic Situation".
- **Sampling claim:** A Record Shack store listing calls the single a "Sampled piece of funky early 70s Soul" (https://recordshack.org/shop/format/7/ron-candy-lovely-weekend-b-w-plastic-situation-inner-city-7/). No WhoSampled or other entry confirming a sample of either side was found. Unverified.
- **Who plays on it?** Discogs credits only Arthur (Art) Woods (arranger, co-writer), George Semper (producer), Ron Kenoly and Candy Rae (performers). The backing band is uncredited. One YouTube upload is titled "RON and CANDY & GROUP PLASTIC SITUATION" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mk7ZAMhSAIE), which hints at a named group, but I could not find one. Semper's Inner City session pool reportedly included Al McKay and James Gadson (Discogs label profile), but nothing ties them to this session.
- **Clark Brown Audio, Crenshaw:** Wikipedia names this as the studio for "Lovely Weekend" (uncited). No independent source was found for the studio.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Before the worship leader."** The male voice on this gritty LA funk B-side belongs to Ron Kenoly, who became one of the best-selling praise-and-worship artists of the 1990s (*Lift Him Up*, Dove Award 1997) and died in February 2026. The entry can frame the track as the secular "before" picture of a sacred career, and ask what of the funk survived into his worship music.
- **The flip side of a sweet A-side.** "Lovely Weekend" is the soul ballad side that collectors and Northern Soul DJs share (several YouTube uploads from Northern Soul channels). "Plastic Situation" is the side the 2003 crate-digger compilation chose as track one. This is a story about how the B-side can become the reason a record matters.
- **One single, two singers, one label's first act.** Ron & Candy were the first act signed to George Semper's Inner City Records, and this was Candy Rae's only known record release. That opens a theme about one-shot artists and the small LA labels that briefly gave them a voice.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Two singers who both learned to sing in church trade lines on a song about phoniness. Listen to how Ron and Candy split the vocal. Who carries the grit, and does it ever sound like a sermon?
- Play this right after Ron Kenoly's "Jesus Is Alive." Can you hear the same voice and the same phrasing habits twenty years apart?
- The producer, George Semper, was known as a Hammond organ player. Listen to the backing band under the vocals: what is doing the funky work here, the rhythm section or the keys?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Plastic Situation" is the B-side of "Lovely Weekend" (Inner City ICIM-173), credited as written by Ron Kenoly and Art Woods, arranged by Arthur Woods, produced by George Semper. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8065517-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend-Plastic-Situation
- When Ubiquity's rare-groove imprint Luv N' Haight and George Semper compiled *Inner City Sounds: Gritty Soul & Underground Funk From The Vaults Of Inner City Records* in 2003, "Plastic Situation" was track one (A1). The A-side was left off. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1194965-Various-Inner-City-Sounds-Gritty-Soul-Underground-Funk-From-The-Vaults-Of-Inner-City-Records
- The same compilation includes a previously unreleased "Tenderness" credited to Funkhouse Express & Ron And Candy. In 2022 Record Shack reissued a "Tenderness" 7" credited to Candy Rae alone, from the George Semper Music Archives. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1194965-Various-Inner-City-Sounds-Gritty-Soul-Underground-Funk-From-The-Vaults-Of-Inner-City-Records ; https://www.discogs.com/release/24259640-Candy-Rae-Tenderness
- The single was finally released digitally on May 5, 2026 by The George Semper Music Archives, LLC, three months after Kenoly's death. — Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/lovely-weekend-single/6766396257 ; https://www.deezer.com/album/974427071
- The single is far more wanted than owned. The main Discogs pressing shows 273 wants against 233 haves, and the variant pressings run about 3 to 5 wants per copy owned (e.g. 101 want / 29 have). — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1910376-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend ; https://www.discogs.com/release/8065517-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend-Plastic-Situation
- The pressing was made at Monarch Record Mfg. Co. in LA. Discogs lists the publisher as Music Forever, and the runouts carry matrix number 2180 (hence the alternate catalog "45-2180" seen on some copies). — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/1910376-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend ; https://www.discogs.com/release/14503021-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend

#### Artist

- Ron & Candy were Ron Kenoly and Rachel L. Holden ("Candy Rae"), based in Los Angeles. Discogs calls them "Inner City Records first act." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/412277-Ron-And-Candy
- Semper on signing them: "The two of them came and sang the song at my house, and I knew straight away I wanted to sign them." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kenoly (quote is uncited on Wikipedia)
- **Ron Kenoly** (Dec 6, 1944, Coffeyville, Kansas to Feb 3, 2026, age 81) became one of the defining voices of 1990s praise and worship music. *Lift Him Up* (1992) was called the fastest-selling worship album to that point, and *Welcome Home* won a 1997 Dove Award. His best-known songs include "Ancient of Days" and "Jesus Is Alive." — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kenoly ; https://www.legacy.com/legacy/notable-deaths/ron-kenoly ; https://assets.christianpost.com/news/ron-kenoly-writer-of-ancient-of-days-dead-at-81.html
- Before and alongside Ron & Candy, Kenoly sang demos of Jimmy Webb songs (including "Up, Up and Away") and recorded R&B for A&M under the stage name "Ron Keith" ("I Betcha I'll Get Ya," "Soul Vaccination"). — Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kenoly ; https://www.legacy.com/legacy/notable-deaths/ron-kenoly ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/372071-Ron-Kenoly
- Kenoly served in the US Air Force from 1965 to 1968, singing in a Top 40 cover band (The Mellow Fellows) that toured military bases. Discogs says he recommitted to Christianity in 1975, and his first Christian album came in 1983. — Sources: https://assets.christianpost.com/news/ron-kenoly-writer-of-ancient-of-days-dead-at-81.html ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/372071-Ron-Kenoly
- **Candy Rae** (Rachel L. Holden) was born in 1946 in Philadelphia and sang in gospel congregations from childhood. She moved to LA in the early 1970s and sang backup for Jimmy McGriff and Kool & the Gang. This single is "her only known record release." She kept performing on the LA club circuit until the 1980s. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/10996514-Candy-Rae
- **George Semper** (Trinidadian-American organist and producer) founded Inner City Records in LA in 1972. He was musical director for Brenton Wood and Wattstax and was called the "Godfather of Bay Area Funk." His own site says he gave Frank Zappa his first professional gig, opening for Little Richard in 1965. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/282008-George-Semper ; https://www.discogs.com/label/53448-Inner-City-Records-3 ; http://www.georgesemper.life/

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Deep Funk (recommended).** A small LA label's first single, a B-side, no confirmed chart presence, then rediscovered by Luv N' Haight / Ubiquity in 2003 and placed as track one of a "Gritty Soul & Underground Funk" crate-digger comp. This matches the book's definition: "overlooked on release and only found their audience later through crate-digging DJs." The Discogs wants-to-haves ratio supports collector-driven demand. — https://www.discogs.com/master/172261-Various-Inner-City-Sounds-Gritty-Soul-Underground-Funk-From-The-Vaults-Of-Inner-City-Records ; https://www.discogs.com/release/1910376-Ron-And-Candy-Lovely-Weekend
2. **Classic Funk.** It is squarely in the 1969 to 1977 window and its primary identity is funk/soul (Discogs styles: Soul, Funk). It weakens because it never had the major-label distribution or chart presence the book's Classic Funk definition calls for. The one counterpoint is the uncited "nearly 200,000 copies" claim, which would point the other way if it could be verified.
3. **Gospel Funk (not recommended, but worth noting).** Both singers came out of church and Kenoly became a worship leader. But the book defines Gospel Funk as gospel music absorbing funk, or funk turning toward praise, and this is a secular record. Better used as a writing theme than as the genre.

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - California (set).** The duo was "based in Los Angeles, California", Inner City Records was an LA label, the single was pressed at Monarch in LA, and Wikipedia places the recording at Clark Brown Audio in Crenshaw, LA. — https://www.discogs.com/artist/412277-Ron-And-Candy ; https://www.discogs.com/label/53448-Inner-City-Records-3 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Kenoly
2. **US - West.** A broader fallback that would also cover Semper's San Diego and Bay Area roots. It is less precise than the California value, which already exists.
3. Not recommended: Kenoly's Kansas birthplace (US - Midwest) or Candy Rae's Philadelphia birthplace (US - Northeast). Both are origins of the singers, not of the record.
