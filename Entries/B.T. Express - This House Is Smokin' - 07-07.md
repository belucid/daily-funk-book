July 7 is B.T. Express Day. At least in Brooklyn, New York. At least in 1977.

The second half of the 1970s was a rough stretch for Brooklyn, so it is easy to imagine city leaders welcoming anything that brought a little borough pride. A free concert by Brooklyn Transit Express, a homegrown act that made it big, must have felt like the right ticket.

B.T. Express’ debut album, *Do It (‘Til You’re Satisfied)*, was a huge success. The title track hit #1 on the R&B chart, #2 on the pop chart, and sold over a million copies. The next single, "Express," followed in 1975 and reached #1 R&B and #4 pop. The album itself reached #1 on the R&B album chart and #5 on the *Billboard* 200.

Brooklyn’s government was not the only one to take notice. B.T. Express was the first funk band to play at the White House. Or were they? The claim sits right on the fault line between funk and disco. “Do It (‘Til You’re Satisfied)” is often cited as an early disco hit, and “Express” points even more clearly toward the disco sound that would soon dominate.

Today’s song, “This House Is Smokin’,” is a two-minute funky burner that closes out the gold-selling album.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The shortest track on a gold record"**: At 2:12, "This House Is Smokin'" is barely a song, more a sprint. On a debut LP that went gold and topped the R&B chart on the strength of two long-form hits, the album closer is a sub-three-minute lung-buster. The hook: how much funk can you cram into 132 seconds, and why didn't they let it run?
- **"The avant-garde sax player hiding in a disco band"**: Alto saxophonist Carlos Ward played with John Coltrane (1965–66) and was a long-running collaborator of Don Cherry. Then he turned up in B.T. Express playing tight, danceable horn lines. The hook: trace what an out-jazz cat brings to a tight pocket band, and what it means that funk could absorb that kind of player without breaking stride.
- **"The album cut that built hip-hop"**: A two-minute throwaway closer turned into raw material for 3rd Bass, Above the Law, IAM, and others fifteen years later. The hook: B.T. Express scored Top 5 pop hits with their singles, but this deep cut may have had a longer afterlife than the A-sides.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- "Clock it. This track is 2:12 long. Once it kicks in, count how many distinct hooks and breaks the band fits into that runtime, then ask whether you wish they'd stretched it to seven minutes or whether the brevity is the whole point."
- "Lock onto the bass line first, then listen for the horns answering it. This is the same band that recorded the seven-minute disco-funk hit 'Express'. What changes when they only give themselves two minutes to work with?"
- "Around the one-minute mark the horns lock into a stab pattern that hip-hop producers couldn't leave alone in the late '80s. See if you can hear what 3rd Bass and Above the Law heard in it."

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "This House Is Smokin'" is the closing track on side B of the LP, the shortest cut on the record at 2:12. Writers credited as George Burton and Joe Constantino; producer Trade Martin. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_It_('Til_You're_Satisfied)_(album)
- The track has been sampled at least seven times in hip-hop, including 3rd Bass "Triple Stage Darkness" (1989, *The Cactus Album*), Above the Law "Menace to Society" (1990), IAM "Total Kheops", World Renown "Roll Wit It", Mr. X & Mr. Z "Silly Rabbit", Positively Black "Nightmare on America Street", and Butta Verses feat. Joell Ortiz "Rock Mics" (2008). — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/B.T.-Express/This-House-Is-Smokin'/sampled/
- Despite being an album cut, it was issued as a B-side on a 1975 Scepter Belgian 7" backing "Once You Get It." — Source: https://rateyourmusic.com/release/single/b_t-express/once-you-get-it-this-house-is-smokin/

#### Album

- *Do It ('Til You're Satisfied)* peaked at #5 on the Billboard 200, hit #1 on the Top R&B Albums chart, and was certified Gold by the RIAA on March 6, 1975. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_It_('Til_You're_Satisfied)_(album)
- Recorded at Ultra-Sonic Recording Studios in Hempstead, Long Island, produced by Jeff Lane and Trade Martin, with arrangements by Randy Muller (who would soon front Brass Construction, also produced by Lane). — Source: https://www.cherryred.co.uk/b-t-express-the-definitive-collection-do-it-til-you-re-satisfied-4cd
- The album spawned the #2 pop / #1 R&B hit "Do It ('Til You're Satisfied)" and the #4 pop / #1 R&B follow-up "Express", both gold singles. — Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/bt-express-bridged-the-gap-between-funk-and-disco/

#### Artist

- B.T. Express stands for Brooklyn Transit Express. They evolved out of the King Davis House Rockers → Madison Street Express → Brothers Trucking → Brooklyn Transit Express; Scepter Records suggested the final rename when they signed. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._T._Express
- Alto sax player Carlos Ward (b. May 1, 1940, Ancón, Panama) had played with John Coltrane in 1965–66 and was a long-running collaborator of Don Cherry, appearing on Cherry's 1973 *Relativity Suite*. He was simultaneously in B.T. Express. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ward
- Keyboardist Michael Jones, who joined later, would leave in 1979 and become the producer Kashif, scoring R&B hits with Whitney Houston, Evelyn King, and others. He died September 25, 2016. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._T._Express
- B.T. Express were the first funk band to play the White House (1977) and received an official "B.T. Express Day" proclamation from the borough of Brooklyn on July 7, 1977. — Source: https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/bt-express-bridged-the-gap-between-funk-and-disco/

### Genre Suggestions

- **Classic Funk (recommended)**: This is textbook classic funk by the book's working definition. Major-label release (Scepter), well-distributed, charted (#1 R&B, #5 pop, gold), arrived at the peak of the classic era (1974), with funk as the track's primary identity, not funk fused into rock, jazz, or Latin styles. The fact that the parent album spawned two crossover hits cements the "central sound in mainstream American music" framing. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_It_('Til_You're_Satisfied)_(album)
- **Deep Funk (possible alternative)**: One could make a narrower case that "This House Is Smokin'" specifically belongs in deep funk territory. The track was never the A-side, was the 2:12 closer of a chart-dominating LP, and found its real audience years later through crate-digging hip-hop producers. That fits the book's deep-funk definition of "delayed recognition" for an obscure album cut. The objection is that the album itself was hardly obscure, so this is a stretch. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/B.T.-Express/This-House-Is-Smokin'/sampled/

My pick: **Classic Funk**.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - New York (recommended)**: B.T. Express formed in Brooklyn, the band was associated with the Brooklyn scene (Brass Construction, the Risbrook brothers' Bedford-Stuyvesant upbringing), and Brooklyn proclaimed "B.T. Express Day" in 1977. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._T._Express
- **No strong alternative**: Recording happened on Long Island (Ultra-Sonic Recording Studios, Hempstead), which is still New York. There's no compelling argument for another tag.
