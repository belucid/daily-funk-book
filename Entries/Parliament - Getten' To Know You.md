### Open Research Questions

The author posed no `open:` questions in this file. Two questions surfaced during research and are documented here:

**1. What is the actual release date of *The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein*?** This could not be resolved to a single authoritative day, and the sources are unusually scattered — four different dates are in circulation:

- **20 July 1976** — Discogs US first pressing (Casablanca NBLP 7034). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein
- **7 July 1976** — Qobuz (℗ 1976, Island Def Jam metadata). Source: https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/clones-of-dr-funkenstein-parliament/vln4a1gr1gamb
- **20 September 1976** — Wikipedia infobox, which carries **no citation** for the date. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- **29 September 1976** — Apple Music album metadata and Albumism's anniversary retrospective. Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/clones-of-dr-funkenstein/1434919629 and https://albumism.com/features/parliament-the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein-turns-45-anniversary-retrospective

Approach taken: I checked Discogs (API), Wikipedia (raw wikitext, to see whether a citation was attached to the infobox date — none is), Apple Music, Qobuz, uDiscoverMusic ("Released in September 1976"), Ace Records ("1976"), and George Clinton's own site (which says only "the summer of 1976"). I could not find a contemporary *Billboard* chart-debut or "Top Album Picks" scan to arbitrate. **Note for the author:** the existing entry `Parliament - Dr. Funkenstein - 07-20.md` already commits the book to 20 July 1976 in its body text, so consistency argues for treating July 20 as the book's working date unless the author wants to revisit it. September remains the more commonly repeated date in secondary press.

**2. Was "Getten' To Know You" ever a single?** No. A Discogs API search restricted to `type=release&format=7"` for artist Parliament + track "Getten' To Know You" returns zero results. The album's only two singles were "Do That Stuff" (b/w "Everything Is On The One") and "Dr. Funkenstein" (b/w "Children of Production"). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein — this is an album cut only, which is part of what makes its sampling afterlife interesting.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The love song hiding in the clone factory.** Every P-Funk album smuggles in one romance number, and on an album entirely about intergalactic cloning, this is it. The angle: what happens when the sweetest song on the record has to share a sleeve with a mad scientist's origin myth — and why Clinton kept insisting on that slot.
- **Diaper Man steps to the mic.** Garry Shider is the most visually famous P-Funk member almost nobody can name a lead vocal by. This is one. Frame the entry around the co-writer/guitarist/musical-director who spent twenty years in a diaper and did the unglamorous work of holding the band together.
- **From Casablanca to the rave.** A non-single album track from 1976 gets flipped by The D.O.C. in 1989, then that flip gets flipped again by British hardcore and jungle producers in 1992. A short, clean case study in how funk travels: not through hits, but through deep cuts that a crate digger happened to reach for.
- **Wesley's half of the record.** Fred Wesley and Bernie Worrell split the album's horn arrangements down the middle, four charts each. This is one of Wesley's. A good hook for writing about what an ex-James Brown arranger does differently with a horn section than a P-Funk keyboardist does.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This is a Fred Wesley horn chart, not a Bernie Worrell one — the album is split four charts each. Listen to the horns here, then go straight to "Everything Is On The One" (Worrell's). Can you hear whose hand is on which?
- The lead vocal here belongs to Garry Shider, the guy who spent twenty years on stage in a diaper. Nothing about this performance is a joke. At what moment do you stop hearing the costume?
- On an album about cloning funkateers for interplanetary distribution, this is the love song. Play it directly after "Children Of Production" and ask what Clinton is doing by putting it there — is it a breather, a bait-and-switch, or the actual point of the record?
- Around the horn break, listen for the two-bar figure that Dr. Dre's crew would later lift for The D.O.C.'s "Whirlwind Pyramid." Once you hear it as a loop, can you still hear it as a song?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Getten' To Know You" is the only song on *The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein* written by George Clinton and Garry Shider alone — every other track on the album adds Bernie Worrell, Bootsy Collins, or Glen Goins to the credit. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein
- Garry Shider sings lead. Albumism's retrospective calls it the album's love song — "rather than a tongue-in-cheek ballad, it's a swirling disco number, with particularly off-kilter horns playing by Wesley and Parker," and credits Shider with "a marvelous vocal performance." — Source: https://albumism.com/features/parliament-the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein-turns-45-anniversary-retrospective
- The horn chart is Fred Wesley's. The album's arrangements were split evenly: Wesley took "Dr. Funkenstein," "Children Of Production," "Getten' To Know You," and "Do That Stuff"; Bernie Worrell took the other four. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein
- It was never released as a single — a Discogs 7" search returns nothing, and the album's singles were "Do That Stuff" and "Dr. Funkenstein." Its entire afterlife is as an album cut. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- **The sample chain is the best story here.** The D.O.C. sampled it on "Whirlwind Pyramid" (from *No One Can Do It Better*, 13 July 1989, Ruthless/Atlantic, produced by Dr. Dre); WhoSampled places the Parliament sample at 0:01 of the D.O.C. track, alongside samples of Kurtis Blow's "AJ Scratch" and Yellow Sunshine. — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/72936/The-D.O.C.-Whirlwind-Pyramid-Parliament-Getten'-to-Know-You/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_One_Can_Do_It_Better
- That D.O.C. track then became raw material for the British breakbeat-hardcore scene: 2 Bad Mice's 1992 remix of Blame's "Music Takes You" samples "Whirlwind Pyramid," and jungle pioneer Bizzy B's "Brainstorm" samples it too. A 1976 Parliament album cut is therefore two removes from the DNA of UK rave. — Sources: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/192954/Blame-Music-Takes-You-(2-Bad-Mice-Remix)-The-D.O.C.-Whirlwind-Pyramid/ and https://www.whosampled.com/sample/415852/Bizzy-B-Brainstorm-The-D.O.C.-Whirlwind-Pyramid/
- SanTown (714)'s "Up 'N' Herd" also samples the track directly. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/604609/SanTown-(714)-Up-'N'-Herd-Parliament-Getten'-to-Know-You/
- A live version circulates as "Getten' to Know You (Live)," credited to Parliament Funkadelic and drawn from the *Live... Madison Square Garden 1977* release (Rox Vox, 2017). It is not on the official *Live: P-Funk Earth Tour* album. — Sources: https://open.spotify.com/track/5KNYscci12tNWMWUdwnoOo, https://www.discogs.com/master/1295165-Parliament-Funkadelic-Live-Madison-Square-Garden-1977, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live:_P-Funk_Earth_Tour
- Modern funk revivalists The Motet covered it with percussionist Jason Hann for their 2020 "Quarantine Comes Alive" series. — Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I5AaGaZSKxA

#### Album

- *The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein* was Parliament's fifth studio album and second gold record, reaching #3 on the *Billboard* R&B Albums chart and #20 on the pop chart. Recorded in 1976 at United Sound in Detroit and Hollywood Sound in Hollywood, produced by George Clinton. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- Two of the horn players are jazz-fusion royalty who had no other business on a P-Funk record: Michael and Randy Brecker sit in the section alongside Fred Wesley, Maceo Parker, and Rick Gardner. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein and https://acerecords.co.uk/the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein
- The record continues the *Mothership Connection* storyline: Starchild turns out to be an agent of Dr. Funkenstein, who is cloning funkateers to seed the Earth. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/parliament-the-clones-of-dr-funkenstein/
- The Casablanca era is written right into the packaging. Some copies shipped with an insert offering Parliament *and Donna Summer* merchandise — T-shirts, a life-size stand-up, a moon mobile, a satin pillowcase — plus a free "BONUS DISCO LP" for returning the questionnaire. On an album whose love song is, per Albumism, a disco number, the label's disco machinery is literally in the sleeve. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein
- Critical reception was strong on release and has held: *Rolling Stone* gave 5 stars, AllMusic 4.5, Robert Christgau a B+. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- Guitarist Michael "Kidd Funkadelic" Hampton, bassist Cordell "Boogie" Mosson, and drummers Jerome Brailey and Gary "Mudbone" Cooper round out one of the deepest P-Funk studio lineups ever assembled. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/70285-Parliament-The-Clones-Of-Dr-Funkenstein

#### Artist

- Garry Marshall Shider was born 24 July 1953 in Plainfield, New Jersey — the same town where Clinton's Parliaments started as a doo-wop group — and died 16 June 2010 in Upper Marlboro, Maryland, of brain and lung cancer. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Shider
- Shider joined P-Funk full time in 1972 and served as musical director of the P-Funk All Stars for most of the band's history. He performed in a diaper, earning the nickname "Diaper Man." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Shider
- Fred Wesley (b. 4 July 1943, Columbus, Georgia) had been James Brown's bandleader and musical director of the J.B.'s; he left Brown in 1975 and moved straight into Clinton's orbit, later leading the spin-off Horny Horns. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Wesley
- Maceo Parker (b. 14 February 1943, Kinston, North Carolina) joined Brown in 1963, left in 1970, and joined Clinton in 1975, reuniting with Wesley. The horn section on this track is, functionally, James Brown's horn section in exile. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maceo_Parker
- George Clinton was born 22 July 1941 in Kannapolis, North Carolina; Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 with sixteen members honored together. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clinton_(funk_musician)

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty. Working from the definitions in `Matter/On Subgenres.md`:

- **Psychedelic Funk — primary recommendation.** The book's own definition names George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic universe as the center of this genre, and this track sits inside the most explicitly science-fictional Parliament concept album there is. The two sibling Parliament entries in the book (`Dr. Funkenstein`, `Give Up the Funk`) both use Psychedelic Funk, and consistency across the P-Funk entries is worth something. The one honest tension: the song itself is the album's *least* psychedelic moment — it's a love song with a straight-ahead horn chart. Its psychedelia is contextual (it lives inside the clone mythology) rather than musical.
- **Classic Funk — genuinely strong secondary case.** 1976 falls squarely inside the book's 1969–1977 classic window; the album was a major-label Casablanca release with chart presence, radio play, gold certification, and a national tour. If the author wants one Parliament entry that represents P-Funk as *mainstream commercial funk* rather than as cosmology, this track — the accessible, danceable, non-conceptual one — is the natural candidate.
- **Electro Funk — not recommended.** Worrell's synthesizers are all over the album, but the book limits Electro Funk to the late 1970s through mid-1980s and to records defined by their electronic texture. This track is defined by horns and voice.

**Suggestion: Psychedelic Funk**, with Classic Funk as a deliberate alternative if the author wants this entry to make the "P-Funk was pop music" argument rather than the mythology argument.

### Region Suggestions

The `region` field has been set to **US - Michigan**, matching the enumeration in `Planning/Field Values.md`.

- **US - Michigan (recommended, and the value written).** The album was recorded at United Sound Systems in Detroit, and the Parliament/Funkadelic operation was Detroit-based through its Westbound and Casablanca years. This also matches both sibling Parliament entries, which matters for the book's internal consistency. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- **US - California (secondary case, specific to this album).** Unusually, *Clones* was recorded in two places: United Sound in Detroit **and** Hollywood Sound in Hollywood, California. Casablanca itself was a Los Angeles label. If a source ever establishes that this particular track was cut in Hollywood, US - California would become defensible — but no source specifies per-track studio assignments, so this stays speculative. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clones_of_Dr._Funkenstein
- **US - Northeast (weaker).** Both Clinton and Shider come out of Plainfield, New Jersey, and the song's two authors are therefore both New Jersey men. This is a biographical claim rather than a claim about where the record was made. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Shider

No new region value needs to be added to `Planning/Field Values.md` for this entry; the existing enumeration covers it.
