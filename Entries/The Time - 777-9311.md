The title of today's song, "777-9311," was the home phone number of Dez Dickerson, Prince's guitarist in The Revolution. Dez suggested it when Prince needed a phone number that would rhyme for a hook. He changed his number within 48 hours of the single's release after receiving crank calls at all hours.

The Linn LM-1 drum machine beat comes from a demo pattern programmed by Art Wood. The pattern was inspired by David Garibaldi's intro to Tower of Power's "Squib Cakes" (see Nov 4th). Garibaldi was not involved in programming it and is puzzled that he keeps getting writing credits.

Prince wrote, played, and produced the track, then handed it to The Time. Nearly everything you hear is Prince except the vocals. Maybe this is one he should have kept. Okayplayer called it "Prince's best song of all time," and Questlove called it one of his most daring funk grooves.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The phone number that rang off the hook"** — The central story of Dez Dickerson's real phone number being turned into a hit single, the calls flooding in, and the number being changed within 48 hours, is a vivid and funny way into the song. It also opens a door into Prince's mischievous control over everyone in his orbit.

2. **"The drum machine that stole from Tower of Power"** — The Linn LM-1 beat on "777-9311" traces back through a surprisingly tangled lineage: Art Wood programmed a demo pattern inspired by David Garibaldi's intro to Tower of Power's "Squib Cakes," which shipped as a stock preset with the LM-1, which Prince then used for this track. Garibaldi himself has no memory of programming it and is puzzled he keeps getting credit. It's a story about how grooves migrate and mutate through technology.

3. **"Prince's best song, given away"** — Prince wrote, played, and produced essentially the entire track, then handed it to The Time, the one band he later admitted he was "afraid of." Okayplayer has called it "Prince's best song of all time." The tension between generosity and competitive control is a rich vein.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The drum beat is a Linn LM-1 stock preset pattern. Art Wood programmed it for Roger Linn, inspired by David Garibaldi's drum intro on Tower of Power's "Squib Cakes" (1974). Garibaldi himself has publicly stated he has no memory of programming the beat and is puzzled he continues to receive credit. — Source: https://reverb.com/news/the-secret-origin-of-princes-most-famous-drum-machine-beat
- Questlove has called "777-9311" "one of the most daring funk songs of all time" and singled out Prince's bass playing as its defining feature, saying "People think of Prince and they think 'vocalist, songwriter, guitarist.' But his bass playing: bar none." — Source: https://www.musicradar.com/artists/singers-songwriters/people-think-of-prince-and-they-think-vocalist-songwriter-guitarist-but-his-bass-playing-bar-none-questlove-on-the-prince-production-with-the-david-garibaldi-lm-1-drum-beat-that-he-calls-one-of-the-most-daring-funk-songs-of-all-time
- The 7" single version runs only 3m 28s, less than half the album version's 8m 05s. The B-side of the 7" was "Grace"; the 12" single featured "The Walk" as B-side. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/777-9311
- The song was blocked from #1 on the R&B chart by Evelyn "Champagne" King's "Love Come Down." — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-time-777-9311/
- The track has been sampled by 2Pac ("Whatz Ya Phone #" on *All Eyez on Me*, 1996), Sir Mix-A-Lot ("Beepers," 1989), Justin Timberlake ("I'm Lovin' It"), 69 Boyz ("Kitty Kitty"), Domino ("Physical Funk"), and Common (referenced in "Puppy Chow"). — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Time/777-9311/sampled/
- The song's title, "777-9311," was the actual home phone number of Dez Dickerson, Prince's guitarist in The Revolution. Prince called Dickerson from the studio saying he needed a phone number that would rhyme for a song hook. Dickerson recalled hearing the groove played over the phone and thinking two things: "one, this groove is just nasty; and, two, I wonder what's going to happen when this comes out." He changed his number within 48 hours of the single's release due to crank calls at all hours. — Source: https://diffuser.fm/prince-time-777-9311/

#### Album
- *What Time Is It?* was recorded at Prince's Kiowa Trail home studio in Chanhassen, Minnesota, between January and June 1982. Prince played virtually all instruments, with Morris Day contributing drums on three tracks (1, 4, and 5), though all band members were credited. — Source: https://princesongs.org/2019/09/20/777-9311/
- The album sessions also produced an early version of "Jerk Out," which was shelved and later re-recorded for The Time's 1990 album *Pandemonium*. A track called "Bold Generation" was recorded but rejected entirely. — Source: https://princesongs.org/2019/09/20/777-9311/
- AllMusic rated the album 90/100, calling it "much more fully developed than its predecessor." — Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/what-time-is-it-mw0000192096

#### Artist
- Prince once said of The Time: "To this day, they're the only band I've ever been afraid of." Their live shows during tours were so strong that Prince removed them from the 1999 tour, reportedly feeling threatened by his own creation. — Source: https://www.okayplayer.com/originals/prince-best-song-band-admired-envied.html
- During the 1982 Controversy Tour, Prince and members of his band threw eggs at The Time from offstage during The Time's set. After the show, guitarist Jesse Johnson was handcuffed to a wall-mounted coat rack. A hotel food fight afterward resulted in Prince holding Morris Day financially responsible for damages. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band)
- When teaching the song to The Time, Prince pushed keyboardist Jimmy Jam intensely: "Jimmy, you're doing the bass with your left hand. You're not using your right hand." He demanded Jam play, sing, and dance simultaneously, and eventually Jam mastered all three. — Source: https://www.okayplayer.com/originals/prince-best-song-band-admired-envied.html
- Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis were fired/departed from The Time in 1983 (accounts vary), and went on to become one of the most successful songwriting and production duos in music history, working with Janet Jackson, Boyz II Men, and many others. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band)
- The Time was assembled by Prince under a clause in his Warner Bros. contract that allowed him to recruit and produce other artists. He drew from the existing Minneapolis band Flyte Tyme, adding Morris Day (his high school bandmate from Grand Central) and Jesse Johnson. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_(band)
- Jellybean Johnson, The Time's drummer, passed away on November 21, 2025, at age 69, just two days after his birthday (November 19, 1956). — Source: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/jellybean-johnson-the-time-prince-dead-obituary-1235471010/

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Minneapolis Sound** (current value) — This is the strongest fit. The Time is one of the defining acts of the Minneapolis Sound alongside Prince. The track features the genre's hallmarks: Linn drum machine, synth layers, lean arrangement, and a fusion of funk with new wave sensibility. This is the right call.

2. **Classic Funk** — A secondary case could be made. The song's DNA is deeply rooted in classic funk: the prominent bassline, the call-and-response vocal style, and the Tower of Power drum lineage. However, the production technology (Linn LM-1, Oberheim OB-X synths) and the 1982 date place it firmly in the Minneapolis Sound's early-80s territory rather than the 1968-1977 classic funk era.

3. **Electro Funk** — A weaker but defensible case. The track is built almost entirely on a drum machine and synthesizers, with Prince as a one-man electronic band. But the warmth of the bass guitar and the live-feeling guitar work keep it from the colder, more mechanical feel typically associated with electro funk.

**Recommendation**: Keep Minneapolis Sound. It's the right genre.
