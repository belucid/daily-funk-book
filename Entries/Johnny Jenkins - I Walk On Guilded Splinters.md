### Open Research Questions

No `open:` questions were present in the entry. Questions surfaced during research that the author may want to resolve:

- **Exact album release date.** Wikipedia gives "April 1970" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton-Ton_Macoute!). Aquarium Drunkard says Jenkins "laid down" the album in late 1969 (https://aquariumdrunkard.com/2009/04/28/johnny-jenkins-walk-on-gilded-splinters). Peace & Rhythm says release was delayed about two years after recording began in 1969 (https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/johnny-jenkins-march-5-1939-june-26-2006), which conflicts with a 1970 release. Discogs gives only "1970" for every original pressing. No day-level date found.
- **Track length discrepancy.** The original LP label lists A1 as 5:23 (https://www.discogs.com/release/9005897-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute). The audio on streaming services runs 5:49–5:50 (Apple 5:50, Deezer 5:49: https://www.deezer.com/track/1763304577), and Wikipedia lists 5:49. The YouTube video on the Discogs master runs 5:51. I used the streaming length (actual audio). The printed 5:23 is likely a label error or a different timing convention. The 7" single edit runs 3:25 (https://www.discogs.com/release/8790379-Johnny-Jenkins-I-Walk-On-Guilded-Splinters).
- **Title spelling.** The original LP prints "I Walked On Guilded Splinters"; the 7" prints "I Walk On Guilded Splinters"; BMI's registered title is "I Walked on Guilded Splinters"; many later reissues use "Gilded" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_on_Gilded_Splinters). The `track` field matches the 7" and the streaming title, so it was left alone.
- **Recording studio.** Discogs credits Capricorn Sound Studios (Macon) plus engineers associated with FAME / Muscle Shoals (Jimmy Johnson) and Ardent / Memphis (Terry Manning). Which studio this specific track was cut in was not confirmed. JamBase does not name the studio (https://www.jambase.com/article/allman-brothers-grateful-dead-dr-john-cover-johnny-jenkins).
- **Day of death.** Most sources say June 26, 2006 (a Monday). Fox News/AP says he died "Monday night" (https://www.foxnews.com/story/musician-johnny-jenkins-dies-at-67), which agrees. Jambands.com says he died "on Tuesday" (https://jambands.com/news/2006/06/28/johnny-jenkins-1939-2006/), which would be June 27. Cause of death: "stroke" (Wikipedia, JamBase) vs. "long illness" (Jambands).
- **Was Ton-Ton Macoute! really a Duane Allman solo project?** Wikipedia flags this "citation needed." It is supported by Wax Poetics, Jambands, and Peace & Rhythm, but none cite a primary source. Gregg Allman's memoir (*My Cross to Bear*) is quoted by JamBase on Duane's playing, and might be the best primary source to check.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The most borrowed drum break you've never heard of."** Butch Trucks' opening shuffle, joined by Jaimoe's timbales, was sampled by Tuff Crew, The Cure, Beastie Boys, Geto Boys, Beck, Oasis, Wu-Tang Clan and NehruvianDOOM. It is a deep-funk story of delayed recognition: a cult blues-rock LP that became hip-hop and alt-rock source material.
- **"The man who gave Otis a ride."** Jenkins led the Pinetoppers, hired Otis Redding as singer and driver, and his unused studio time produced "These Arms of Mine." He then turned down stardom (fear of flying, preference for Macon). This entry is his one big statement, recorded with someone else's band.
- **"A Duane Allman solo album with someone else's name on it."** The record began as Duane's project before the Allman Brothers Band formed, and Jenkins sang over the tracks. That raises a question about whose record this is.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The first 15 seconds are just Butch Trucks' drums, then Jaimoe's timbales slide in. Once you hear it, can you un-hear Beck's "Loser"?
- Dr. John's 1968 original is a seven-and-a-half-minute voodoo chant. Listen for what Duane Allman's dobro adds that turns the haunted ritual into a swamp-funk groove.
- Two Allman Brothers drummers, one Muscle Shoals bassist, and a bluesman from Macon. Focus on the bass and percussion and ask whether this sounds more like Southern rock or like funk.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Opening track of *Ton-Ton Macoute!*, a cover of Dr. John's song from *Gris-Gris* (1968), written by Mac Rebennack under the pseudonym "Dr. John Creaux." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_on_Gilded_Splinters
- Dr. John said the song was based on a traditional voodoo church song, and he changed "Splendors" to "Splinters" because he liked the sound. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_on_Gilded_Splinters
- On Jenkins' version, Butch Trucks plays the opening drum break, and Jaimoe joins on timbales a few bars later. Duane Allman plays dobro. — Source: https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- The break was sampled first by Tuff Crew ("Bound To Ike," 1988), and later by The Cure ("A Forest (Tree Mix)," 1990), Beastie Boys ("Pass the Mic (Pt. 2)" remix, 1992), Geto Boys, New Kingdom, Kid Rock, Wu-Tang Clan ("Gun Will Go," 2007) and NehruvianDOOM ("Caskets," 2014). — Source: https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- Beck's "Loser" (first released March 8, 1993; No. 10 Billboard Hot 100) is built on the drum track sampled from this recording. Producer Carl Stephenson looped Beck's slide guitar over it. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loser_(Beck_song) ; https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- Oasis sampled the Jenkins intro for "Go Let It Out" (2000). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_on_Gilded_Splinters ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Let_It_Out
- Gregg Allman's memoir is quoted calling Duane's dobro on the track "just flat-out evil." — Source: https://www.jambase.com/article/allman-brothers-grateful-dead-dr-john-cover-johnny-jenkins
- Backing vocals (A1, B5) are credited to Southern Comfort, a group that included a pre-Grateful Dead Donna Jean Godchaux (then Donna Thatcher). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9005897-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton-Ton_Macoute! ; https://www.jambase.com/article/allman-brothers-grateful-dead-dr-john-cover-johnny-jenkins
- Issued as a 7" promo on Capricorn C-8000 (mono/stereo, 3:25 edit). C-8000 appears to be the first number in Capricorn's 8000 single series (C-8001 is Sam & Bill, C-8002 is Macon). This is an inference from Discogs catalog numbers. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/8790379-Johnny-Jenkins-I-Walk-On-Guilded-Splinters
- The song was also covered by Cher (1969), Marsha Hunt (1969, UK No. 46), Humble Pie (a 23-minute live version, 1971), and Paul Weller (1995). The Weller version plays at the end of season four of *The Wire*. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walk_on_Gilded_Splinters

#### Album

- *Ton-Ton Macoute!* (ATCO SD 33-331, 1970) was produced by Johnny Sandlin, with Duane Allman co-producing "Down Along the Cove" and "Bad News." — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9005897-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute
- Originally meant as Duane Allman's solo debut; Allman left to form the Allman Brothers Band and Jenkins added vocals over the tracks (Wikipedia flags "citation needed"). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton-Ton_Macoute! ; https://waxpoetics.com/blogs/rock-pop/johnny-jenkins-ton-ton-macoute
- The album features ABB founders Berry Oakley, Butch Trucks and Jaimoe, plus Muscle Shoals players Eddie Hinton, Pete Carr and Robert Popwell, and Paul Hornsby. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/9005897-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute
- The album is made up entirely of covers (Dr. John, Muddy Waters, Bob Dylan, John Lee Hooker, Sleepy John Estes, Chris Kenner, J.D. Loudermilk). — Source: https://waxpoetics.com/blogs/rock-pop/johnny-jenkins-ton-ton-macoute
- Peace & Rhythm suggests "Voodoo In You" may have inspired Stevie Wonder's "Superstition." This is speculative and unsourced there. — Source: https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/johnny-jenkins-march-5-1939-june-26-2006
- Discogs files it under Funk / Soul and Blues, with the styles Bayou Funk, Funk and Delta Blues. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/169001-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute
- AllMusic rates it 4.5 stars; Christgau gave it a B−. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton-Ton_Macoute!
- It was Jenkins' only album for 26 years, until *Blessed Blues* (1996). — Source: https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Jenkins

#### Artist

- Johnny Edward Jenkins was born March 5, 1939, in Bibb County / Macon, Georgia, and died June 26, 2006, in Macon. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Jenkins ; https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/johnny-jenkins-march-5-1939-june-26-2006
- He was left-handed and played his first homemade "guitar" (a matchbox and rubber bands) upside down. — Source: https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/johnny-jenkins-march-5-1939-june-26-2006
- His acrobatic, behind-the-head stage style influenced Jimi Hendrix, who saw him in Macon. They played a few gigs together in the late 1960s. — Source: https://www.foxnews.com/story/musician-johnny-jenkins-dies-at-67 ; https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition
- He led the Pinetoppers, who employed Otis Redding as singer and as driver, because Jenkins had no driver's license. At a 1962 session Jenkins left 40 minutes unused, and Redding cut "These Arms of Mine" with Jenkins on guitar. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Jenkins
- He was the first artist managed by Phil Walden, later co-founder of Capricorn. — Source: https://waxpoetics.com/blogs/rock-pop/johnny-jenkins-ton-ton-macoute
- He declined an offer to join Otis's touring band out of fear of flying, and worked day jobs (ditch digging, logging) for much of his life. — Source: https://www.peaceandrhythm.com/blogs/news/johnny-jenkins-march-5-1939-june-26-2006
- He had an early-1960s regional hit with the instrumental "Love Twist." — Source: https://www.foxnews.com/story/musician-johnny-jenkins-dies-at-67
- He was inducted into the Georgia Music Hall of Fame in 2012. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Jenkins

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is currently empty.

1. **Deep Funk (primary suggestion).** The book defines deep funk as "a commercial fate": tracks overlooked on release that found an audience later through crate-digging DJs hunting for hip-hop samples. That fits this track closely. It is a cult LP that didn't chart, but its drum break became a hip-hop and alt-rock staple, from Tuff Crew in 1988 through Beck, the Beastie Boys and Wu-Tang. Source: https://stereogum.com/2034143/breaks-with-tradition-i-walk-on-guilded-splinters/columns/breaks-with-tradition
2. **Funk Rock.** It was played largely by the Allman Brothers' rhythm section, with Duane on dobro, and Wikipedia calls the album blues rock / Southern rock. The groove is funk and the textures are rock. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton-Ton_Macoute!
3. **Psychedelic Funk.** The voodoo lyrics, the "Night Tripper" source material, and the hypnotic, chanting atmosphere fit the book's definition ("surreal lyrics... alter egos... hallucinatory storytelling"). Discogs tags the album "Bayou Funk." Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/169001-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute

### Region Suggestions

1. **US - Southeast (applied).** Jenkins was born, lived and died in Macon, Georgia. The album came out on Macon's Capricorn imprint and credits Capricorn Sound Studios. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Jenkins ; https://www.discogs.com/release/9005897-Johnny-Jenkins-Ton-Ton-Macoute
2. **US - Southeast (Muscle Shoals angle).** Several players (Eddie Hinton, Pete Carr, Robert Popwell) and engineer Jimmy Johnson come from the Muscle Shoals, Alabama scene. That also maps to US - Southeast, which reinforces the choice.
3. **US - Louisiana (alternate).** The song is New Orleans voodoo music by Dr. John, and Discogs tags the style "Bayou Funk." This is a case for the song's origin, not for this recording, so it is weaker.

There is no case for a new region value. A "US - Georgia" value would be more precise, but US - Southeast covers it adequately.
