### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"A 1997 Rhodes, a 2026 record"** — The groove was born when Kronlund jammed on a gaffer-tape-patched Fender Rhodes Mark I he bought off a guy in Bushwick in the summer of '97, but the track wasn't finished and released until 2026. A near-30-year gestation makes a great hook about how funk ideas wait for their moment. (Source: youandthemusic / ceconline)
- **"Brooklyn funk made in Stockholm"** — The band is named for Brooklyn and born there in 1993, yet "Bust The Bus Stop" was cut at Kapsylen Studio in Stockholm with a largely Swedish-based lineup around the lone original member, Swedish bassist Lati Kronlund. A meditation on a place-name band that long ago left the place.
- **"The last man standing"** — Kronlund is the sole remaining original member of a collective that began as an accident (a no-show drummer at an Al Green session). A theme about authorship and continuity: when is a band still the same band three decades on?

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- This whole groove grew out of one beat-up electric piano. Pick out the Fender Rhodes line and ask: does the rest of the arrangement serve that keyboard riff, or has it been buried under the horn section?
- The lead vocal is Alison Limerick, the same voice on the 1991 house anthem "Where Love Lives." Listen for how a house-diva phrasing sits inside a live funk band — where does she push against the pocket?
- Count the horns: trombone, tenor sax, trumpet, baritone sax. Try to hear when the section plays as one block versus when individual horns answer the vocal. What does that arranging choice do to the "bus stop" feel?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The groove originated on a beat-up Fender Rhodes Mark I that Kronlund bought "from a guy in Bushwick" in the summer of '97, "held together with gaffer tape but sounded really good"; he jammed on it as soon as it reached his studio and that produced the riff. — Source: http://www.youandthemusic.com/2026/01/review-brooklyn-funk-essentials-bust.html
- Released first as a 2-track single (Edit 3:31 / full 5:45) on January 28, 2026, ahead of the album. — Source: https://brooklynfunkessentials.bandcamp.com/album/bust-the-bus-stop
- Recorded at Kapsylen Studio, Stockholm; mixed by Kronlund at Black Plastic Magick; mastered by Hoffe Stannow at Cosmos Mastering. Lead vocal by Alison Limerick, co-lead/guitar Desmond Foster, with a full horn section (Ebba Åsman trombone, Loïc Gayot tenor sax, Jéssica Pina trumpet, Sven Andersson baritone sax). — Source: https://brooklynfunkessentials.bandcamp.com/album/bust-the-bus-stop
- Songwriting credited to Åsman/Kronlund (registered as Lars Kronlund / Ebba Åsman). — Source: https://earone.com/post/558e4dcffb58/brooklyn-funk-essentials-alison-limerick-ebba-asman-desmond-foster-bust-the-bus-stop-radio-date-28-01-2026

#### Album

- "Black Butterfly" is the band's eighth studio album, released May 15, 2026 on Dorado Records (vinyl cat# DOR170LP). — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/37510203-Brooklyn-Funk-Essentials-Black-Butterfly
- "Bust The Bus Stop" is sequenced as track A2; the album spans soul, funk, jazz, hip hop and Latin influences and was produced and co-written by Kronlund. — Source: https://www.jackrussellmusic.net/2026/05/13/brooklyn-funk-essentials-announce-new-album-black-butterfly-out-may-15th-2026/
- The album also reaches back to a Talking Heads cover ("Life During Wartime"), underlining the band's eclectic, genre-hopping habit. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/37510203-Brooklyn-Funk-Essentials-Black-Butterfly

#### Artist

- Brooklyn Funk Essentials formed in 1993 out of an accident: a drummer failed to show for an Al Green session (Baker producing "Don't Look Back"), and Kronlund pulled together musicians to fill the studio time. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Funk_Essentials
- Co-founded by producer Arthur Baker (the electro/dance pioneer behind Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock") and Swedish bassist Lati Kronlund, who is now the sole remaining original member. — Source: https://www.jackrussellmusic.net/lars-lati-kronlund/
- Before BFE, Kronlund produced Alison Limerick's house classic "Where Love Lives" — and three decades later she sings lead on this track, a long-running collaboration. — Source: https://www.jackrussellmusic.net/lars-lati-kronlund/
- Despite the Brooklyn name, the band's creative base is Swedish; "Bust The Bus Stop" was recorded in Stockholm. The band reports 100M+ streams across a three-decade career. — Source: https://www.jackrussellmusic.net/2026/05/13/brooklyn-funk-essentials-announce-new-album-black-butterfly-out-may-15th-2026/

### Genre Suggestions

Current value: **Nu-Funk**.

- **Nu-Funk (keep / reconsider):** The book defines Nu-Funk as a mid-1990s retro revival scene capturing the feel of classic-era 45s. BFE emerged in exactly that mid-90s scene and on a UK label (Dorado) central to it, so the band fits Nu-Funk historically. However, this specific track is a 2026 recording that is lushly produced rather than a lo-fi 45-style homage — so Nu-Funk fits the *band's lineage* more than this particular cut. Defensible, with that caveat.
- **Acid Jazz:** Discogs tags the band's catalog "Acid Jazz," and BFE is routinely described as acid-jazz-adjacent. The book defines acid jazz as a late-80s/90s offshoot reviving 70s jazz-funk for club culture with tight grooves and hooks rather than long improvisation — a strong match for BFE's groove-and-horns approach. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/130915-Brooklyn-Funk-Essentials-Cool-And-Steady-And-Easy
- **Classic Funk (as a feel, not era):** The track itself is straight-ahead horn-driven funk with a Rhodes-led groove. The book's Classic Funk is era-bound (1969–1977), so it does not formally fit, but it's worth noting the track's *sound* is more "classic funk feel" than anything experimental. Recommend keeping Nu-Funk or switching to Acid Jazz.
