Today's song, "Lilies of the Nile," was composed by Wilton Felder, who plays tenor saxophone and electric bass on the track. Felder was born on August 31, 1940, and in the early 1970s, he was one of Motown’s West Coast first-call bass players.

If you’ve been paying close attention, this is our third day with a track from the Crusaders’ *Southern Comfort*. Call me a fanboy if you want, but it is a colossal two-LP jazz-funk monster. In 1974, it hit #31 on the *Billboard* 200, #1 on the jazz chart, and #3 on the soul chart. If you have time, the whole album is worth the trip.

### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The double-LP detour."** Southern Comfort was a sprawling 2-LP set that hit #1 on the Jazz chart and #3 on Soul LPs in late 1974. "Lilies of the Nile" is the 9-minute ballad-groove at the heart of it, the moment the Crusaders slow down and let Felder's tenor breathe. The theme: what a long-form, slow-burn funk ballad does that a 3-minute single can't.
2. **"Larry Carlton, officially."** This album is Carlton's first as a full Crusaders member, at 26 years old. His clean, singing lines preview everything he'd become as a session god in the late '70s. Theme: the sound of a guitarist arriving.
3. **"From Jazz Crusaders to Crusaders."** Four Houston high schoolers in 1954, a Vegas house band, a Pacific Jazz hard-bop outfit, and finally, by dropping "Jazz," a jazz-funk crossover force. "Lilies" is that whole arc in one track: tenor-led melodicism welded to a deep R&B pocket.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

Current mission (keep?): *"This is jazz-funk in a slower, smokey, sultry vein. Can you imagine some Barry White vocals laid over the top of this tune?"* Strong Barry White-era mood cue, invites active comparison. Keep as-is is a fine choice. Alternatives:

1. Felder's tenor carries the melody, but listen for what Wayne Henderson's trombone is doing underneath, and when the two finally braid together. Which one is actually leading by the 6-minute mark?
2. Felder plays both saxophone and electric bass on this track. Try to hear the bass line as the same musician's voice as the sax. Does knowing that change what you hear?
3. Larry Carlton's guitar is the new voice in the Crusaders in 1974. Count how many bars go by before you notice him, then listen again for what he's been doing the whole time.

#### Album

- Released October 1974, debuted on the Billboard 200 on October 26, 1974. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- Peaked at #31 Billboard 200, #3 US Soul LPs, and #1 US Jazz LPs. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Released as a double LP on ABC/Blue Thumb, recorded at Wally Heider Recording Studio, Hollywood, produced by Stewart Levine. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Carlton's first album as a full Crusaders member (he was 26). — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/
- Single "Stomp and Buck Dance" (pulled in 1975) hit #41 R&B and #12 on the US Dance chart. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort_(The_Crusaders_album)
- Closing track "A Ballad For Joe (Louis)" is Joe Sample's tribute to boxer Joe Louis. — Source: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-the-crusaders-southern-comfort/

#### Artist

- Formed in 1954 as the Swingsters at Wheatley High School in Houston, Texas, by Joe Sample, Wilton Felder, and Stix Hooper. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Wayne Henderson joined at Texas Southern University, along with a young Hubert Laws on flute. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Moved to LA in 1958, spent time as the house band at the New Frontier Hotel in Las Vegas as "The Nite Hawks." — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Signed to Pacific Jazz in 1961 as the Jazz Crusaders, dropped "Jazz" from the name in 1971, signaling the shift toward R&B and funk crossover. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group)
- Wilton Felder moonlighted as an in-house electric bassist at Motown's LA operation in the early 1970s. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_Felder
