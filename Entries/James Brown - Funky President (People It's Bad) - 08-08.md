### Writing Theme Suggestions

1. **"The Godfather votes with his feet"** — Brown's tangled political journey from performing "Say It Loud, I'm Black and I'm Proud" at Nixon's 1969 inauguration, to boycotts over his Nixon endorsement in 1972, to rebuking Gerald Ford with this single in October 1974. The song lands as a reckoning: Brown had burned credibility backing Nixon, and "Funky President" reads as a man trying to reclaim his voice as a critic of power, not a friend of it.

2. **"The 'Hey!' heard round the world"** — The single vocal "hey!" at the 0:09 mark became one of hip-hop's most sampled vocal elements, appearing in roughly 200 tracks according to WhoSampled. From Eric B. & Rakim to Kanye West to Childish Gambino, that one syllable became a foundational building block of a new genre. An entry could be framed around how a throwaway vocal tic became immortal.

3. **"Session men in the funky president's cabinet"** — The recording session at Sound Ideas in New York featured a remarkable lineup of jazz-fusion session musicians including David Sanborn (alto sax), Joe Farrell (tenor sax/flute), Pee Wee Ellis (baritone sax), Jon Faddis and Marvin Stamm (trumpet), and Cornell Dupree (guitar). This was not the J.B.'s; Brown was building funk with the cream of New York's jazz-fusion scene.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track
- The distinctive "hey!" vocal at the 0:09 mark has been sampled in approximately 200 songs according to WhoSampled, making it one of hip-hop's most frequently sampled vocal elements. — Source: https://djbooth.net/features/hey-sample-breakdown/
- The drum break became one of rap's most popular samples. Eric B. & Rakim's "Eric B. Is President" (1986) was the first major hip-hop track to sample it, and Eric B. has said he "picked up Fonda Rae's 'Over Like A Fat Rat' and took the bassline and matched it to the 'Funky President' beat." — Source: https://hiphophero.com/the-first-rap-song-to-sample-funky-president-by-james-brown/
- The track has been sampled by Public Enemy ("Fight the Power"), N.W.A. ("Fuck Tha Police"), A Tribe Called Quest ("Oh My God"), DJ Jazzy Jeff & The Fresh Prince ("Summertime"), Salt-N-Pepa, 2Pac, Run-DMC, Kanye West ("Clique," "Runaway," "New God Flow"), Childish Gambino ("3005"), Logic, Run The Jewels, and many more. — Source: https://www.whosampled.com/James-Brown/Funky-President-(People-It%27s-Bad)/sampled/
- Kanye West sampled the "hey!" in multiple productions including "Runaway," "Clique," and "New God Flow." — Source: https://djbooth.net/features/hey-sample-breakdown/
- The track was featured on the Master Sounds 98.3 radio station in *Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas* (2004), and also played inside the Caligula and Four Dragons casinos in the game. It was later removed from some versions due to license expiration. — Source: https://gta-songs.fandom.com/wiki/Funky_President
- The "hey!" sample crossed genre boundaries, appearing in tracks by The Offspring, Everlast, and Calvin Harris. — Source: https://djbooth.net/features/hey-sample-breakdown/

#### Album
- *Reality* was James Brown's 39th studio album, released December 19, 1974 on Polydor Records. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_(James_Brown_album)
- Recorded at three studios: A & R Studios (NYC), United Artists Studios (Los Angeles), and Sound Ideas Studios (NYC), between November 1972 and December 1974. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_(James_Brown_album)
- Rolling Stone listed it as the 46th best album of 1974, but critical reception was mixed. AllMusic gave it 1.5/5 stars, Robert Christgau a B-, and the Rolling Stone Album Guide 2.5/5. NME called it "very well produced" but noted limited progression since 1972. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_(James_Brown_album)
- The album peaked at #56 on the Billboard 200. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_(James_Brown_album)
- The album includes a cover of Cole Porter's "Don't Fence Me In" and Hank Ballard's "The Twist," showing Brown ranging far outside funk on the same LP. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_(James_Brown_album)

#### Artist
- Brown released "Funky President" in October 1974 as a direct rebuke to Gerald Ford, who had pardoned Nixon the previous month (September 8, 1974). Brown said of Ford: "He was a nice man, but he talked a lot and didn't say anything." — Source: https://thebasispoint.com/friday-funk-funky-president-people-its-bad/
- In 1968, Brown backed Democrat Hubert Humphrey for president. When Nixon won and offered an inauguration gig, Brown canceled a Texas show and shipped his 20-person band to Washington. He performed "Say It Loud, I'm Black and I'm Proud" at the inauguration, and some white audience members found themselves singing back the refrain. — Source: https://ghostsofdc.org/2013/01/17/james-brown-said-it-loud-at-nixons-inauguration/
- Brown's endorsement of Nixon in the 1972 election led to boycotts by Black organizations. Protesters held banners reading "James Brown Nixon's Clown." He declined a second inauguration performance in January 1973 due to the backlash. — Source: https://ghostsofdc.org/2013/01/17/james-brown-said-it-loud-at-nixons-inauguration/
- Brown had released "You Can Have Watergate, Just Give Me Some Bucks And I'll Be Straight" in May 1973, responding to the unfolding Watergate scandal. "Funky President" arrived the following year as the political sequel. — Source: https://thebasispoint.com/friday-funk-funky-president-people-its-bad/
- The session band for this recording was not the J.B.'s but a group of elite New York session and jazz-fusion musicians, including David Sanborn (alto sax), Joe Farrell (tenor sax/flute), Pee Wee Ellis (baritone sax), Jon Faddis and Marvin Stamm (trumpet), Joe Beck and Sam Brown (guitar), Gordon Edwards (bass), Allan Schwartzberg (drums), and Johnny Griggs (percussion). Arrangements by David Matthews, Fred Wesley, and James Brown. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_President_(People_It%27s_Bad)
- Born May 3, 1933 in Barnwell, South Carolina. Grew up in Augusta, Georgia. Died December 25, 2006 in Atlanta, Georgia (age 73, pneumonia). — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown

### Genre Suggestions

1. **Classic Funk** (current value, recommended) — This is the definitive fit. "Funky President" was released on a major label (Polydor) in 1974, charted #4 R&B and #44 Hot 100, and sits squarely in the core funk vocabulary of the classic era (1968-1977). Brown is the architect of funk as a genre, and this track is a textbook example of his mid-1970s sound: tight drum pocket, horn stabs, wah guitar, and declamatory vocals. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_President_(People_It%27s_Bad)

2. **Jazz-Funk** (alternative consideration) — The session personnel are overwhelmingly jazz-fusion musicians (David Sanborn, Joe Farrell, Jon Faddis, Marvin Stamm), and the horn arrangements by David Matthews reflect jazz-funk sensibilities. However, the track's structure, vocal style, and groove remain firmly in Brown's classic funk idiom rather than jazz-funk's longer, more improvisatory format. This is classic funk played by jazz musicians, not jazz-funk. — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funky_President_(People_It%27s_Bad)

3. **Deep Funk** (not recommended) — While the track has been rediscovered through sampling, it was a charting hit on release (#4 R&B), so it doesn't fit the "delayed recognition" definition of deep funk.

**Recommendation:** Keep Classic Funk. It's the obvious and correct call.
