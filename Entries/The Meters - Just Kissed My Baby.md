### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"The drummer who sang the riff."** The track started as a Leo Nocentelli guitar lick, but it became a song when drummer Zigaboo Modeliste began improvising a vocal melody and lyrics over it. A piece about the funk band whose rhythm player wrote the hook from behind the kit. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-meters/just-kissed-my-baby
- **"A New Orleans groove that built hip-hop."** Public Enemy mined this one track for two cuts on their 1987 debut; it has gone on to be sampled in dozens of records. An angle on how a deep album cut from New Orleans became raw material for the East Coast rap revolution. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Just-Kissed-My-Baby/
- **"The guest from the swamp."** Little Feat's Lowell George slips in on slide guitar — an unexpected California rock cameo on a New Orleans funk record. A piece about cross-pollination and the swampy guitar tone that ties the two scenes together. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-meters/just-kissed-my-baby

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The song is built on a single repeating guitar riff with almost no chord changes. Track how the band keeps it from getting boring across 4+ minutes — what does Zigaboo's drumming add each time around that a metronome wouldn't?
- There is a guest slide guitarist (Lowell George of Little Feat) layered over the Meters' own guitar. See if you can pick out the two distinct guitar voices, and decide which one is carrying the groove and which is commenting on it.
- Listen to the vocal: it is a drummer singing, not a frontman. How does the phrasing sit against the beat differently than a typical lead-singer-driven funk track?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The song grew out of a Leo Nocentelli guitar riff; Zigaboo Modeliste then started singing over it and supplied the melody and lyrics — the drummer effectively wrote the song's vocal hook. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-meters/just-kissed-my-baby
- Little Feat's Lowell George guested on slide guitar. Source: https://www.songfacts.com/facts/the-meters/just-kissed-my-baby
- Public Enemy sampled it on two tracks from their 1987 debut *Yo! Bum Rush the Show* — "Timebomb" and "Terminator X Speaks with His Hands." Source: https://www.whosampled.com/sample/1914/Public-Enemy-Timebomb-The-Meters-Just-Kissed-My-Baby/
- WhoSampled documents the track being sampled in dozens of later songs (Public Enemy, EPMD's "Never Seen Before," and many more), making it one of the Meters' most-sampled recordings. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Just-Kissed-My-Baby/sampled/
- The track was featured on the *Grand Theft Auto IV* in-game funk radio station "IF99." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)

#### Album

- *Rejuvenation* was the first Meters album recorded at Allen Toussaint and Marshall Sehorn's brand-new Sea-Saint Studios in New Orleans. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)
- Rolling Stone ranked the album #138 on its 2003 list of the 500 Greatest Albums of All Time (and #139 in the 2012 revision). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejuvenation_(The_Meters_album)
- It is widely regarded as the Meters' creative peak, also home to the New Orleans standard "Hey Pocky A-Way." Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/rejuvenation-mw0000608560

#### Artist

- The Meters formed in New Orleans in 1965/68 as Art Neville (keys), Leo Nocentelli (guitar), George Porter Jr. (bass), and Zigaboo Modeliste (drums), and served as Allen Toussaint's house band, backing artists like Lee Dorsey and Dr. John. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meters
- Critic Robert Christgau called Modeliste's drumming "the secret," noting "it's almost as if he's the lead." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zigaboo_Modeliste
- Keyboardist and founder Art Neville (also of the Neville Brothers) died July 22, 2019, at age 81. Source: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/art-neville-dies/

### Genre Suggestions

Current field: **Classic Funk**.

- **Classic Funk (endorse).** Fits the book's definition precisely: a major-label (Reprise) release from the 1969–1977 core era whose primary identity is funk, not funk fused into another genre. The Meters are foundational to funk's core vocabulary. Source: On Subgenres.md definition + https://www.allmusic.com/album/rejuvenation-mw0000608560
- **Deep Funk (secondary case).** While *Rejuvenation* was a notable album, "Just Kissed My Baby" was an album cut, not a hit single, and its enduring fame came largely through later crate-digger sampling (Public Enemy, EPMD). The "delayed recognition via sampling DJs" arc partly matches the book's deep-funk definition. Weaker than Classic Funk because the Meters were never obscure. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/The-Meters/Just-Kissed-My-Baby/sampled/
- Discogs tags the album style as "Bayou Funk / Soul" — useful color but not one of the book's enumerated genres. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/109366-The-Meters-Rejuvenation

Recommendation: keep **Classic Funk**.
