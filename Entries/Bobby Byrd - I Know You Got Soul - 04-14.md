Bobby Byrd founded the group that became the Famous Flames in Toccoa, Georgia, in 1953. If the name means anything to you, it is probably because of its association with the early recordings of James Brown. Brown’s first released single, “Please, Please, Please,” appeared in 1956 under the credit James Brown with the Famous Flames.

Brown had been sent to juvenile detention for armed robbery in 1949, when he was sixteen. Three years later, his reform-school baseball team played against Byrd’s team, and the two young men formed an immediate bond. Byrd’s family sponsored the nineteen-year-old Brown for early release and welcomed him into their home.

Brown did not immediately join Byrd’s group. After a few unsuccessful attempts with other bands, he asked Byrd for a place and came aboard as a drummer. When singer Troy Collins died in a car accident in 1954, Byrd made the career-defining decision to move Brown to the front of the group.

Brown quickly became the star, and the billing shifted from the Famous Flames to James Brown and the Famous Flames. The original group eventually broke apart, and Brown formed other backing bands, but Byrd remained one of Brown’s closest musical partners. On April 14, 2012, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inducted the Famous Flames, helping correct its earlier decision to honor Brown without the group that helped launch him.

Today’s song, “I Know You Got Soul,” was released as a Bobby Byrd single in 1971 on King Records. Byrd, Brown, and Charles Bobbit wrote it, Brown arranged and produced it, and the J.B.’s supplied the groove. Brown may have become the star, but here Byrd steps up to the microphone and reminds you where the story began.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- Released May 1971 as King 45-6378, backed with "It's I Who Love You (Not Him Anymore)"; written by James Brown, Bobby Byrd and Charles Bobbitt, arranged and produced by Brown, with the J.B.'s as the band. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/952529-Bobby-Byrd-I-Know-You-Got-Soul-Its-I-Who-Love-You-Not-Him-Anymore and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- Peaked at #30 on the Billboard R&B chart. Searches found no Hot 100 placement documented. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- Per Wikipedia, the track has been sampled on roughly 150 songs — most famously Eric B. & Rakim's "I Know You Got Soul" (1987, third single from *Paid in Full*), Public Enemy's "Fight the Power," and James Brown's own "Static." Funkatropolis (2019, citing WhoSampled) put the count at 177 and growing. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song) and http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2019/05/i-know-you-got-soul-by-bobby-byrd.html
- The J.B.'s personnel on the session: Fred Thomas (bass), John "Jabo" Starks (drums), Fred Wesley (trombone), Hearlon "Cheese" Martin and Robert Coleman (guitars), Jimmy Parker (alto sax), St. Clair Pinckney (baritone sax), Jerone "Jasaan Sanford" Melson (trumpet), Johnny Griggs (congas), with James Brown on background vocals. Source: http://funkatropolis.blogspot.com/2019/05/i-know-you-got-soul-by-bobby-byrd.html
- A previously unreleased extended version (4:42) appeared on the 1988 compilation *James Brown's Funky People (Part 2)* — this longer edit is the version on most streaming platforms today. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- The track appears on the *Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas* in-game radio station Master Sounds 98.3, introducing it to another generation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)

#### Album

- There was no original album: the track came out only as a 7" single in 1971. It was later anthologized on *James Brown's Funky People (Part 2)* (1988) and *Bobby Byrd Got Soul: The Best of Bobby Byrd* (1995). Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/456602-Bobby-Byrd-I-Know-You-Got-Soul-Its-I-Who-Love-You-Not-Him-Anymore and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- The single's distributor is listed as Starday-King Records Inc., publishing by Dynatone (Brown's publishing arm), BMI. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/952529-Bobby-Byrd-I-Know-You-Got-Soul-Its-I-Who-Love-You-Not-Him-Anymore

#### Artist

- Bobby Howard Byrd, born August 15, 1934 in Toccoa, Georgia; died September 12, 2007 in Loganville, Georgia, of cancer, aged 73. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd founded the gospel group that became the Famous Flames (Gospel Starlighters, 1952) — before James Brown joined. He met Brown at a baseball game at the juvenile detention facility where Brown was serving time for robbery, and the Byrd family helped sponsor Brown's parole. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd on Brown: "I didn't need him in competition, I needed him with me." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Royalties from Jay-Z's "U Don't Know" sampling of Byrd helped him secure a $250,000 mortgage. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd was inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame posthumously as a Famous Flame in 2012 (ceremony April 14, 2012, inducted by Smokey Robinson), received the Rhythm and Blues Foundation Pioneer Award in 1998, and entered the National Rhythm & Blues Hall of Fame in 2020. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Famous_Flames and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
- Byrd was portrayed by Nelsan Ellis in the 2014 James Brown biopic *Get on Up*. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd

- **Classic Funk (recommended).** Squarely inside the book's 1969-1977 classic window: a 1971 King Records single from the James Brown commercial pipeline, with radio play and a #30 R&B chart placement. Its primary identity is funk, not a fusion. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- **Deep Funk (weaker case).** The track's massive sampling afterlife (~150 songs) resembles the deep-funk rediscovery arc — but the book defines deep funk by *delayed* recognition, and this record charted on release and stayed canonical. The sampling story is better told inside a Classic Funk frame. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_You_Got_Soul_(Bobby_Byrd_song)
- **Gospel Funk (long shot).** Byrd's roots are literally gospel (Gospel Starlighters) and the song is a secular call-and-response sermon about soul — but nothing in the track itself is gospel music, so this is a thematic echo, not a label. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Byrd
