### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Nosnibor": the label that is just his name walking backwards.** Robinson got one single onto Ascot, watched the major-label interest evaporate, and responded by building his own label out of his own surname spelled in reverse. Fifteen singles and one LP later he is a collector's holy grail and was never anything but independent. Source: http://acrossthekitchentable.blogspot.com/2020/01/
- **The Cleveland man who hired Los Angeles.** The 1976 LP is a small-city self-released record with a big-money session sheet: Gene Page arranging, H.B. Barnum co-producing, and Elvis Presley's own backing singers on the vocal stack. A one-man Ohio label reaching across the country to buy the best. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5135436-Cleveland-Robinson-Cleveland
- **The long tail of a local 45.** Robinson lived to 91 and died in Cleveland in December 2019, decades after his records had quietly become £250 rarities in British soul clubs he likely never knew about. Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/2310163-Cleveland-Robinson

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The horn line and the bassline are chasing the same phrase but never quite land together. Follow just the bass for one pass, then just the horns. Which one is the song actually built on?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Jungle Dollar" was issued twice on Robinson's own label, first as 7" single Nosnibor 1009 backed with "Understanding," then six years later as B1 on the LP. — Source: http://www.45rpmrecords.com/OH/Nosnibor.php
- The 45 version was arranged by Bert DeCoteaux, a New York arranger who also worked with Ben E. King and The Main Ingredient. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4456760-Cleveland-Robinson-Jungle-Dollar-Understanding
- Nosnibor also issued a 45 numbered 1723, "Jungle Dollar" b/w "No One's Gonna Take Your Place," matching the LP's catalog number N-LP 1723. It reads as the album's promo single, and it means Robinson released "Jungle Dollar" as an A-side twice. — Source: http://www.soulfulkindamusic.net/nosnibor.htm
- I found no evidence of a hip-hop sample, cover version, or licensed compilation appearance for this track. Searches across deep-funk reissue coverage returned nothing. Treat it as genuinely uncomped. — Source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/deep-funk-archival-feature

#### Album

- "Cleveland" (1976) was arranged by Gene Page, the arranger behind Barry White's Love Unlimited Orchestra sound, and co-produced by Robinson with H.B. Barnum. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5135436-Cleveland-Robinson-Cleveland
- The backing vocalists include Kathy Westmoreland and Myrna Smith, both of whom sang behind Elvis Presley, Smith as a member of The Sweet Inspirations. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5135436-Cleveland-Robinson-Cleveland
- Cover photography is credited to FotoArts Inc. of Cleveland, keeping the packaging local even as the music was made with LA session royalty. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5135436-Cleveland-Robinson-Cleveland
- The LP is scarce. At time of research the cheapest copy for sale on Discogs was $321.84, against $5.00 for the 45. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/674288-Cleveland-Robinson-Cleveland

#### Artist

- Robinson was born in Macon, Georgia, raised in Pittsburgh, and moved to Cleveland with his wife in the early 1960s. — Source: http://acrossthekitchentable.blogspot.com/2020/01/
- His first single, "These Are The Hands," was picked up by Ascot and drew major-label interest that never converted into a deal. He founded Nosnibor in response, his surname reversed. — Source: http://acrossthekitchentable.blogspot.com/2020/01/
- He released at least 15 singles and exactly one album, all as singer, writer, producer, and label owner. — Source: https://www.discogs.com/artist/2310163-Cleveland-Robinson
- The label operated from 12428 B St. Clair, Cleveland, later 3919 East 131st St. Both are residential-scale addresses, not office buildings. — Source: http://www.45rpmrecords.com/OH/Nosnibor.php
- He recorded at Schneider's Recording studio in downtown Cleveland. Hank Schneider was the mentor to Thomas Boddie, who founded Cleveland's first Black-owned recording studio and whose archive became a celebrated Numero Group box set. — Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/2310163-Cleveland-Robinson and https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/cleveland-recording-co
- His 45 "Boy" is the prize of the catalog on the UK northern soul circuit, with originals quoted at £250 and up. — Source: http://acrossthekitchentable.blogspot.com/2020/01/
- **Name-collision warning for fact-checkers:** Cleveland Lowellyn Robinson (1914-1995) was a prominent labor leader and administrative chair of the 1963 March on Washington. He is a different person, and he dominates search results. — Source: https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/robinson-cleveland-lowellyn
