Let’s start this odd tale with the musician you won’t hear on today’s track, “Sex In Motion (Instrumental).” The absent singer is the album’s namesake, Marvin Neroes. Born in Texas on December 21, 1955, Neroes grew up in a broken and violent home. His family moved to Las Vegas by way of California, but he ran away at age 15. He spent the next five years homeless, traveling the country by hopping freight trains, before returning to Las Vegas at age 20. Neroes dreamed of a musical career, especially of making a record, but his self-taught falsetto is an acquired taste. I’ve yet to acquire it.

Back in Vegas, Neroes met local record producer Charles “Cholly” Lober Abu Williams, who already had a handful of minor hits to his name. Williams took Neroes under his wing, buying him clothes, booking local gigs, and, always the hustler, eventually finding the money to record an album.

Williams combined his own lyrics with Neroes’ vocals and a trio of talented high school brothers: Clifford Johnson on bass, Richard Thompson on guitar, and Michael Thompson on drums. For reasons lost to history, Williams decided to make a concept album about a ladies’ man named Marvin Whoremonger. The premise made little sense, and was performed by musicians far too young.

The album flopped and disappeared immediately. Collector Geoffrey Weiss stumbled across a copy in the 1980s, and for years rumors, newly located copies, and digital transfers circulated among collectors until Now Again Records painstakingly researched, restored and reissued it in 2016.
