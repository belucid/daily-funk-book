### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **The chicken-coop studio that birthed Americana.** Frame the entry around Wray's three-track studio, a converted chicken shack on his Accokeek, Maryland farm, where the band stomped floors for kick drums and rattled cans of nails for snares. The track's grit is literally the sound of the room. Rationale: a vivid origin story that aligns with "country funk" as fated by circumstance, not branding.
- **Funk from a man with one lung.** Wray lost a lung to tuberculosis before recording, yet delivers a snarling, growling vocal on "Fire and Brimstone." Rationale: pairs physical drama with the song's gospel/preacher imagery and surprises readers who only know "Rumble."
- **The forgotten record that fed crate-diggers and movie supervisors.** Light in the Attic put it on Country Funk 1969-1975 in 2012; Nick Cave and Warren Ellis pulled it for Lawless the same year; the Neville Brothers had already covered it on Yellow Moon (1989). Rationale: a clean way to show how deep-funk rediscovery works across decades.

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- Five seconds in, you hear acoustic guitar and stomped floorboards. Then a snarl. Listen for what is missing: there is no proper drum kit. How do they fake a backbeat for four minutes?
- The 1958 "Rumble" Link Wray was an instrumentalist whose guitar was the voice. Here he sings, growls, and preaches. Decide which Link Wray you find more dangerous, and why.
- Compare the original to The Neville Brothers' 1989 Yellow Moon version. Aaron Neville's falsetto turns brimstone into a hymn; Wray's makes it a threat. Which one scares you more?

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- The song was recorded with no real drum kit at hand: the rhythm comes from musicians stomping the studio floor for the bass drum and shaking a can of nails for the snare — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album)
- It was also released as a 7" single in 1971 on Polydor (UK 2066 120, US PD 2-14084, with German and Australian pressings), backed with "Juke Box Mama" — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4670573-Link-Wray-Fire-And-Brimstone
- The Neville Brothers covered it on Yellow Moon (1989), and Nick Cave and Warren Ellis recut it as The Bootleggers featuring Mark Lanegan for the 2012 Lawless soundtrack — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album) and https://newmusicunited.com/2012/08/26/the-bootleggers-feat-mark-lanegan-fire-and-brimstone-link-wray-cover-version-2012/
- Light in the Attic placed "Fire and Brimstone" on its 2012 Country Funk 1969-1975 compilation, the project that effectively coined and codified the country-funk genre — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5196842-Various-Country-Funk-1969-1975
- The single has been performed live by Phil Lesh with the Terrapin Family Band (2014, 2019), pulling the song into the Grateful Dead orbit — Source: http://www.deaddisc.com/songs/Fire_And_Brimstone.htm

#### Album

- The whole record was cut at "Wray's Shack Three Track," a three-track studio Link's brother Vernon ("Ray Vernon") built inside a converted chicken coop on the family's Accokeek, Maryland farm — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album)
- When the band needed loud amplifiers, they put the speakers outside in the yard and miked the windows — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album)
- The original Polydor LP shipped in a die-cut "unipak" sleeve whose front was cut to follow Link Wray's profile — Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/1390287-Link-Wray-Link-Wray
- Critical reaction split sharply: AllMusic gave 4/5, Robert Christgau gave C+, and Pitchfork's 2017 reissue review awarded 9.1/10 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album)
- The album sold poorly enough on release that "90% of surviving copies are cutouts," before becoming a $30-50 collector item — Source: https://dockellisrecords.wordpress.com/2015/01/24/link-wray-1971-st-lp-polydor-24-4064/

#### Artist

- Born Fred Lincoln Wray Jr. on May 2, 1929, in Dunn, North Carolina; died of heart failure on November 5, 2005, in Copenhagen, Denmark, at 76 — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray
- His mother Lillian Mae (née Coats) was of Shawnee descent; the family faced KKK harassment in the rural South — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray
- Wray's 1958 instrumental "Rumble" pioneered guitar distortion and was banned from radio in New York and Boston for allegedly inciting gang violence, despite having no lyrics — Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray
- He lost a lung to tuberculosis years before recording the 1971 album, which makes the growled vocal on "Fire and Brimstone" especially physical — Source: https://john1b1.substack.com/p/link-wrays-chicken-shack
- The shack sessions produced a "Three Track Shack trilogy" — the 1971 self-titled LP plus Mordicai Jones and Beans & Fatback — later collected on a 2005 two-CD set — Source: https://msmokemusic.com/blogs/mind-smoke-blog/posts/5669157/hot-platters-link-wray-1971

### Genre Suggestions

- **Country Funk (currently set).** Strongest case: the song is on Light in the Attic's Country Funk 1969-1975, the comp the book's genre definition explicitly says "effectively named the genre." Acoustic guitar, dobro, stomped floor rhythm, gospel/preacher lyric — textbook country funk. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/5196842-Various-Country-Funk-1969-1975
- **Gospel Funk (alternative).** Lyric is pure brimstone preacher, the choir-style backing vocals (achieved by men singing falsetto, per the Substack source), and the swampy stomp put it in serious conversation with the book's gospel-funk definition ("funk musicians turning their groove toward praise" / "make a joyful noise for the Lord"). Source: https://john1b1.substack.com/p/link-wrays-chicken-shack
- **Funk Rock (alternative).** Wray is a rock-and-roll guitar pioneer ("Rumble" is rock canon), and Discogs categorizes the single as Rock / Blues Rock. If the book wants to lean into his rock pedigree rather than the Americana revival framing, funk rock fits. Source: https://www.discogs.com/master/568099-Link-Wray-Fire-And-Brimstone

Recommendation: keep Country Funk. It is the single most defensible label and the one the genre-defining 2012 compilation already used.

### Region Suggestions

- **US - Southeast (now set).** The album was recorded in Accokeek, Maryland (just south of the Mason-Dixon line, culturally Mid-Atlantic but often grouped Southeast); Link Wray was born in Dunn, North Carolina; the music is steeped in Southern blues, gospel, and country. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray
- **US - DC (alternative).** Accokeek is in the DC metropolitan area, and the album received its earliest radio and press support from Washington, D.C. media per Wikipedia. If the book treats Accokeek as DC-orbit, "US - DC" is defensible. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray_(album)
- **US - Northeast (weaker alternative).** Maryland sometimes lands in Northeast classification (US Census Bureau groups Maryland in the South), but culturally Wray and this record sit South of any Northeast framing. Not recommended.

Recommendation: hold US - Southeast unless the book prefers DC-area placement for Accokeek records.
