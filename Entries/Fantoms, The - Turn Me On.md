### Open Research Questions

The author left no `open:` items. Questions that surfaced during research, and how far I got:

- **Is the 1973 date firm?** Discogs dates PF-10003 to 1973 and the country to US; MusicBrainz gives the recording a first-release date of 2004 (the compilation), not the 45, so it does not corroborate 1973. The definitive Fantoms history — Dan Phillips' *Home of the Groove* post — walks the discography from "The Mau Mau" (Big Deal, c. 1970) through Power Funksion 1000 (1971/72) and PF-10002 "Junk"/"Get A Little Bit" (1972), then jumps to MAN Records in 1975. It never mentions PF-10003 at all. The catalog-number sequence (10002 in 1972 → 10004 in 1975) makes 1973 plausible and nothing contradicts it, but no second source confirms the year. Approach taken: Discogs label scans, 45cat (no PF-10003 listing found; 45cat's Fantoms page returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch and its indexed entries only show Power Funksion 1000), Home of the Groove, Rate Your Music, eBay/Roots Vinyl Guide sale listings. **Unresolved: a precise release date (month/day) for the 45.**
- **Whose label was it?** AllMusic's review of *Funky Funky New Orleans, Vol. 4* says the Fantoms' "Turn Me On" is one of "several songs from the highly revered JB's label" making their CD debut. Every other source — the 45's label scan, Discogs, Home of the Groove — puts it on the band's own Power Funksion imprint. JB's was a real New Orleans label (part of the Senator Jones JB's/Super Dome/Jenmark family that Tuff City also licensed), so the likeliest explanation is that AllMusic's reviewer generalized across the compilation. I could not resolve this conclusively; see Field Verification.
- **Was Walter "Wolfman" Washington actually on this session?** Discogs credits him on guitar for PF-10003. The Discogs artist profile, however, places him among "a few temporary guitarists" in the band only "from 1969 to 1972," and says the Fantoms "continued without a permanent guitarist" after 1969. His Wikipedia biography does not mention the Fantoms. I could not find a second source confirming his presence on this specific 45. Treat the credit as single-source (Discogs, presumably transcribed from the label).

### Writing Theme Suggestions

- **"Press it yourself."** Power Funksion was not a label that signed the Fantoms — it *was* the Fantoms. They cut the record, put their own imprint on the sleeve, and promoted and distributed the 45 themselves, then watched it vanish. A hook about what it cost a working New Orleans band to be its own A&R, pressing plant, and street team. Source: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html ; https://www.discogs.com/label/94319-Power-Funksion-Records
- **"The colonel on trombone."** Alphonse Davis, the trombonist credited on this record, was one of the members drafted out of the band, spent 27 years in the Marine Corps, and in 1999 was hired to run the Orleans Parish school system — an 80,000-student district — without an education certificate. The horn section of a lost funk 45 as a snapshot of who these men became. Sources: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins ; https://www.asumag.com/facilities-management/maintenance-operations/article/20851185/the-new-breed
- **"Uncle Sam rearranged the band."** Three of five original members were drafted to Vietnam in the late 1960s; the Discogs profile describes the same rupture ("the original line-up splintered in 1969 when Uncle Sam conscripted their bassist, tenor and alto sax players"). The lineup that cut "Turn Me On" is the second or third draft-forced rebuild. Funk personnel history as war history. Sources: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms
- **"The record the historians missed."** Dan Phillips wrote the definitive Fantoms piece and never mentions this 45; the band's own reunion notes skip it. Yet 148 Discogs users want a copy and only 14 have one. A theme about how deep funk canon gets built by collectors rather than chroniclers. Sources: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html ; https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed

### Listening Mission Suggestions

- The Fantoms carried **two** lead singers, Anthony Rainey and Roland Treaudo, and both are credited on this side. Try to hear the handoff. Where does one voice stop and the other start — and would you have known there were two if nobody told you?
- New Orleans funk usually means the Meters: four men, nothing wasted. This is a ten-piece with a four-horn section squeezed onto a 45. Listen for what the horns are actually doing behind the vocal, and decide whether the extra bodies add power or just fill space the Meters would have left empty.
- The guitarist credited here is Walter "Wolfman" Washington, years before he became one of the city's great blues-guitar voices. Listen to the guitar part cold and ask yourself whether you would ever have picked him out of a lineup.
- This is the A-side; the flip is called "Jungle Weed," and the band's previous single was called "Junk." Play both sides back to back and listen for whether the band's idea of a hit sounds any different from its idea of a B-side.

### Interest and Surprise

#### Track

- "Turn Me On" was the A-side of Power Funksion PF-10003, backed with "Jungle Weed"; the 45 was pressed by Houston Records (matrix LH-10763/LH-10764) and distributed by Big Deal Records. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed
- The record is genuinely scarce: 148 Discogs users list it in their wantlist against 14 who own a copy, only 4 copies are for sale, and Discogs' own price suggestion for a Near Mint copy is roughly $300 USD. It carries an average user rating of 4.91/5. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed
- The song sat unheard for three decades until Tuff City's Funky Delicacies imprint put it out as track 6 of *Funky Funky New Orleans, Vol. 4 (Rare And Unreleased New Orleans Funk 1969-1973)* in 2004 — its first appearance on CD. Sources: https://www.discogs.com/master/742140-Various-Funky-Funky-New-Orleans-Volume-4-Rare-And-Unreleased-New-Orleans-Funk-1969-1973 ; https://tuffcity.com/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-vol-4-2
- AllMusic's reviewer singled the band out on a compilation of obscurities: "the consistently excellent Fantoms with 'Turn Me On'," in what the review calls "the most obscure and eclectic" volume in the series. Source: https://www.allmusic.com/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-vol-4-mw0000259638
- Credits on the 45: Anthony Rainey and Roland Treaudo, vocals (A-side); Anthony Rainey, guitar; Walter "Wolfman" Washington, guitar; Robino Barnes, bass; Winston Shy, drums; William Norflin, organ; Ronald Mitchell, trumpet; Alphonse Davis, trombone. Written by the Fantoms; produced by Marty Lewis, Sr.; published by Subit Publishers (BMI). Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed
- I found no evidence the track has ever been sampled or covered — WhoSampled has no Fantoms page for it. Source: https://www.whosampled.com/ (searched; no entry)

#### Album

- There is no Fantoms album carrying this track. The band's only album-length project, *Just Having A Party*, was self-produced at Ultrasonic Studio in 1978 and went unreleased until Funky Delicacies issued it on CD in 2001 — after a near-signing with TK Records collapsed when TK pivoted to disco. Sources: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms
- The compilation that rescued "Turn Me On," *Funky Funky New Orleans, Vol. 4*, was released 2 November 2004 on Funky Delicacies (a division of Tuff City Records) and later expanded to 16 tracks; Tuff City reissued it digitally on 4 March 2021. Sources: https://music.apple.com/us/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-volume-4/969700853 ; https://tuffcity.com/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-vol-4-2
- Both Fantoms sides of the 45 made that compilation — "Turn Me On" at track 6 and "Jungle Weed" at track 9 — meaning the entire 1973 single survives in reissue. Source: https://tuffcity.com/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-vol-4-2

#### Artist

- The Fantoms formed in 1968 in New Orleans as a merger of two young bands — a 15-piece student ensemble led by high-schooler William Norflin and a funk band led by tenor saxophonist Milton Lewis — settling into a ten-piece with a four-horn front line. They rehearsed in the garage beside Milton Lewis' house near Fig and Cherry streets in Hollygrove, "just beside the railroad tracks." Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms ; https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins
- Vietnam broke the original lineup: "three of five original members were drafted to fight in Vietnam," and Discogs puts it more bluntly — "the original line-up splintered in 1969 when Uncle Sam conscripted their bassist, tenor and alto sax players." Sources: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms
- One of those draftees, trombonist Alphonse "Woody" Davis, served 27 years in the U.S. Marine Corps, ran the Marines' officer candidate school, and in 1999 was hired as chief executive officer of the 80,000-student Orleans Parish school system — the board had to get legislative approval to waive the superintendent certification he lacked. Sources: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins ; https://www.asumag.com/facilities-management/maintenance-operations/article/20851185/the-new-breed
- The band won the New Orleans Police Department's annual talent show, which led to a standing weekly gig at the Devil's Den (1968–1971) and steady work at social aid and pleasure club halls — the Autocrat, the ILA, the Labor Union Hall. Sources: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins ; https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html
- Their cover of Stephen Stills' "Love the One You're With" (MAN Records, mid-1970s) is reported to have influenced the Meters and the Neville Brothers; TK Records and London Records both circled the band without signing them. Source: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html
- At least one pressing run misspelled the band's name as "The Fanoms" — Discogs still carries "Fanoms" and "The Fanoms" as name variations. Sources: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html ; https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms
- In 1973, the year of this single, the band split in two: Milton Lewis left to form the Family Underground, taking vocalist Roland Treaudo, trumpeter Ronald Mitchell and drummer Winston Shy with him. Family Underground then released its own records on Power Funksion (PF-10004, PF-10005). Sources: https://www.discogs.com/artist/477473-The-Fantoms ; https://www.discogs.com/label/94319-Power-Funksion-Records
- Drummer Dwight Richards of Chocolate Milk, commenting on the Home of the Groove post, confirmed the Fantoms' local standing and identified bassist Robino Barnes as his high school friend from St. Augustine High School. Source: https://homeofthegroove.blogspot.com/2007/08/fabulous-fantoms-funksion.html
- The surviving members reunited in 2014 as "Fantoms Revisited" after roughly 30 years apart, releasing *The Journey Begins* on 27 December 2014 and using the show to advocate for rebuilding Hollygrove. Vocalists Anthony Rainey and Roland Treaudo had become a minister and a realtor. Source: https://fantomsrevisited.bandcamp.com/album/the-journey-begins

### Genre Suggestions

The `genre` field is empty and is author-only. Against the book's enumeration in `Matter/On Subgenres.md`:

- **Deep Funk** (primary recommendation): This is the textbook case as the book defines it — "less a sound than a commercial fate." A private-press regional 45 on the band's own imprint, self-distributed, commercially invisible, then resurrected 31 years later by a crate-digging reissue label (Tuff City/Funky Delicacies) for an audience of collectors. 148 wants against 14 haves on Discogs is the delayed recognition made numerical. Sources: `Matter/On Subgenres.md` ; https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed ; https://tuffcity.com/album/funky-funky-new-orleans-vol-4-2
- **Classic Funk** (secondary): Musically it sits squarely in the 1969–1977 window the book draws, with a big horn section, two lead vocalists, and no fusion into jazz, rock, or Latin idiom — funk as its primary and only identity. It fails the book's classic-funk test only on the commercial-pipeline clause: no major label, no distribution, no chart. Source: `Matter/On Subgenres.md`
- **Psychedelic Funk** (weak third, listen first): Only worth considering if the recording itself supports it. Discogs files the 45 under the style tag "Bayou Funk," and the flip side "Jungle Weed" plus the band's earlier "Junk" hint at a countercultural streak, but I found no source describing the music as psychedelic. Source: https://www.discogs.com/release/6865795-The-Fantoms-Turn-Me-On-Jungle-Weed

Note for the author: Discogs' own style tag for this record, "Bayou Funk," has no equivalent in the book's enumeration. If a New Orleans/Louisiana subgenre is ever added, this record and the Meters entry would be its anchors.
